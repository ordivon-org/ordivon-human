# HF3 Source Ledger

## Attention / selection

### Simons & Chabris (1999) — inattentional blindness
- DOI: 10.1068/p281059
- PMID: 10694957
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10694957/
- HF3 use: unexpected large objects can fail explicit detection during demanding selective-attention tasks; supports `attention != report` and warns against equating gaze/location with noticing.

### Drew, Võ & Wolfe (2013) — expert observers
- PMID: 23397479
- PMCID: PMC3964612
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3964612/
- HF3 use: 83% of radiologists missed an unexpected gorilla in a lung-nodule search; many fixated its location. Shows expertise and visual fixation do not guarantee explicit detection.

### Awh, Belopolsky & Theeuwes (2012) — selection priority framework
- PMID: 18502468
- DOI: 10.1038/nrn3288
- HF3 use: integrates current goals, physical salience and selection history into attentional priority; supports rejection of a simple top-down/bottom-up dichotomy.

### Theeuwes (2019) — visual selection review
- PMID: 28986770
- PMCID: PMC6634613
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6634613/
- HF3 use: selection can be fast/automatic and history-dependent rather than purely slow/volitional.

### Anderson (2013) — value-driven attention
- PMCID: PMC3630531
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3630531/
- HF3 use: learned reward associations can bias selection independently of current task relevance and physical salience.

## Attentional blink / access

### Dux & Marois (2009) — attentional blink review
- DOI: 10.3758/APP.71.8.1683
- PMID: 19933555
- PMCID: PMC2915904
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2915904/
- HF3 use: rapid serial presentation reveals a 200–500 ms temporal vulnerability for reporting a second target; supports temporally competitive access rather than a global consciousness bit.

### Martens & Wyble (2010) — attentional blink review
- PMID: 20025902
- PMCID: PMC2848898
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2848898/
- HF3 use: AB constrains the rate at which information becomes consciously accessible and links attention to selection dynamics.

## Attention / consciousness boundary

### Noah et al. (2019/2020) — attention and conscious perception
- DOI: 10.1111/nyas.14030
- PMID: 30883785
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30883785/
- HF3 use: review argues attention and consciousness are separable constructs; evidence is compatible with attention being necessary in some contexts but not sufficient, while multiple attention processes matter.

### Tsuchiya et al. (2015) — expanded attention/consciousness taxonomy
- PMCID: PMC6074089
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6074089/
- HF3 use: warns that no-report paradigms can still contain attention/task effects; separates prerequisites, correlates and consequences of conscious perception.

## Access / working memory

### Overgaard (2018) — phenomenal consciousness and cognitive access
- DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2017.0353
- PMID: 30061466
- PMCID: PMC6074085
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6074085/
- HF3 use: reviews the phenomenal-consciousness/cognitive-access debate and shows that claims of overflow are definition- and measurement-sensitive.

### Velichkovsky (2017) — consciousness and working memory
- DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2017.07.005
- PMID: 28756199
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28756199/
- HF3 use: reviews evidence that working-memory processes can occur with limited/no reported awareness.

### Trübutschek et al. (2017) — working memory without consciousness
- PMCID: PMC5589417
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5589417/
- HF3 use: masked targets could influence delayed localization above chance; challenges identifying working memory with conscious sustained activity.

### Soto et al. / related temporal-order evidence (2019)
- PMID: 31019199
- PMCID: PMC6482300
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6482300/
- HF3 use: masked spatial information with nil subjective visibility could be retained and retrieved with temporal order, supporting dissociation between subjective visibility and some working-memory functions.

### D'Angiò et al. (2025) — PRISMA review of VSTM, attention and consciousness
- DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106445
- PMID: 41167442
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167442/
- HF3 use: 42-study systematic review finds multiple VSTM stages and differential effects of spatial/divided/feature attention; reinforces that attention, memory and consciousness interact without collapsing into one stage.

## Metacognition / confidence

### Fleming & Lau (2014) — measuring metacognition
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cortex.2014.03.002
- PMID: 25076880
- PMCID: PMC4097944
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4097944/
- HF3 use: separates metacognitive bias, sensitivity and efficiency; motivates formal measures beyond raw confidence.

### Song et al. / Fleming et al. — neural basis of metacognitive ability
- PMCID: PMC3318765
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3318765/
- HF3 use: empirical dissociations between first-order task performance and metacognitive accuracy.

### Maniscalco, Peters & Lau (2016) — performance/metacognitive dissociation
- DOI: 10.3758/s13414-016-1059-x
- PMID: 26791233
- PMCID: PMC4811689
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4811689/
- HF3 use: task performance can improve while metacognitive sensitivity decreases under specific evidence-use heuristics.

### Fleming (2024) — metacognition and confidence synthesis
- DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-022423-032425
- PMID: 37722748
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37722748/
- HF3 use: confidence is an inferential judgment informed by models of the world and the cognitive system; confidence can diverge from task performance.

### Nature Human Behaviour confidence study (2018)
- DOI: 10.1038/s41562-017-0139
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-017-0139
- HF3 use: confidence and perceptual decisions can rely on partially separable representations; confidence can overweight decision-congruent evidence.

### Maniscalco et al. (2025) — hemianopia/blindsight metacognition
- DOI: 10.1080/02643294.2025.2570940
- PMID: 41123189
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41123189/
- HF3 use: individual patients show distinct objective and metacognitive profiles, reinforcing that metacognitive sensitivity and objective performance are separable dimensions.

## Cognitive control / automaticity

### Lau & Passingham (2007) — unconscious task-set control
- PMID: 17522324
- Related methodological evidence: PMID 17942711
- PMCID: PMC6672767
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6672767/
- HF3 use: masked information influenced preparation for an alternative task and prefrontal activity, challenging the claim that cognitive control is exclusively conscious.

### Kunde, Kiesel & Hoffmann / related cognitive-control review
- PMCID: PMC3303114
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3303114/
- HF3 use: conflict history and task context alter control allocation; control is dynamic and context-sensitive.

### McBride, Boy, Husain & Sumner (2012) — automatic motor activation
- PMID: 22536177
- PMCID: PMC3334842
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3334842/
- HF3 use: visual stimuli can automatically modulate motor responses and executive control without requiring explicit conscious report.

### Ansorge / unconscious inhibition evidence
- PMCID: PMC6670759
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6670759/
- HF3 use: masked No-Go signals can produce later frontocentral inhibitory-control activity, supporting local unconscious control effects.

## Task relevance / nonconscious working memory

### Nonconscious task-relevant information in working memory (2023)
- PMCID: PMC9977358
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9977358/
- HF3 use: nonconscious stimuli could be used for delayed matching, while conscious task-relevance cues were needed for certain prioritization effects; demonstrates dissociation among identification, priority and conscious experience.

## Foundational methodological boundary

### Overgaard / Naccache and colleagues (2018) — access/report debate
- PMCID: PMC6074081
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6074081/
- HF3 use: illustrates a rival strict-access account and confirms that report generation itself is an active inferential chain, not a passive readout.

## Source interpretation rule

These sources are not treated as a vote-counting exercise. HF3 extracts repeated
boundary failures across paradigms. Where studies disagree, the durable result is
the dissociation that survives rather than the preferred theory explaining it.
