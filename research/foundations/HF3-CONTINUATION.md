# Human Foundations Continuation after HF3

## HF3 completed result

HF3 retains:

```text
Priority(x,t | goals, salience, value, history, state, action)
Attention_D(target, process, t)
AvailableFor_D(content, subject, t)
WorkingMemory(content, state, t)
Metacognition_D(process, evaluator, t)
Confidence(decision, evaluator, t)
CognitiveControl(policy/process, context, t)
```

The central HF3 compression is:

```text
Attention selects.
Working memory preserves/manipulates.
Metacognition evaluates.
Confidence expresses/integrates uncertainty about a decision or process.
Control regulates.
Access must specify the downstream function.
None is identical to Experience.
```

## Exact next foundation

HF3 repeatedly invokes variables that determine **why one goal or representation
wins**, rather than merely how it is selected:

```text
Goal
Motivation
Value
Affect
Effort
Reward
Self-Regulation
Agency
```

These cannot safely be collapsed into `Attention`, `Control` or `Confidence`.

Therefore the next round is:

# HF4 — Goals, Motivation, Value, Affect, Effort, Reward and Self-Regulation

## HF4 starting questions

1. What is a goal: desired end-state, policy constraint, representation, or all of
   these under different projections?
2. How do goals differ from preferences, motives, values and intentions?
3. What is motivation beyond a generic “drive” variable?
4. How do reward and value alter attention, effort and action selection?
5. How does affect alter valuation without being reduced to valence?
6. How should effort be represented: cost, control allocation, opportunity cost,
   fatigue, or inferred willingness?
7. How can two people with the same stated goal allocate radically different
   effort because their value, uncertainty or constraints differ?
8. How do intrinsic and extrinsic incentives interact?
9. What is self-regulation when the immediate reward conflicts with a longer-term
   goal?
10. Which failures are failures of goal representation, valuation, control,
    opportunity, capability or resource rather than “motivation”?
11. How should Human agency be distinguished from successful control of action?
12. Which distinctions survive across individual, social and developmental scales?

## Candidate HF4 falsifiers

- temporal discounting and delay of gratification;
- effort-discounting tasks;
- reward-driven attentional capture;
- value-based decision-making under uncertainty;
- affect-congruent attention and memory;
- approach/avoidance conflict;
- implementation-intention and habit paradigms;
- goal shielding and goal neglect;
- preference reversals;
- overjustification / incentive effects;
- self-control under resource and opportunity constraints;
- motivational effects of competence, autonomy and social value;
- agency/volition dissociations.

## Do not precommit

HF3 does not establish that:

- goals are always conscious;
- motivation is one scalar quantity;
- reward equals value;
- affect equals valence;
- effort equals subjective difficulty;
- high effort proves high motivation;
- preference equals intention;
- intention guarantees agency;
- self-control is a single executive faculty;
- delayed reward is always valued more rationally;
- observed behaviour reveals the person's true goals.

## Stop rule

Do not schedule HF5 now. HF4 must expose a repeated neighboring distinction that
cannot be represented by HF0–HF4 without category collapse.
