# HF16 — Population, Aggregation, Future Generations, Non-Identity, Creation, Replication and Existential Loss

## 0. Status and question

HF15 established:

```text
WhoCounts
!= HowManyCount
!= HowClaimsCombine
!= WhichPopulationIsBetter
```

HF16 asks:

> **Once multiple present, future, possible or copied bearers enter moral scope, how
> can their claims and welfare be compared across number, severity, distribution,
> time and identity without hiding the aggregation rule inside one scalar?**

HF16 does not choose total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, prioritarianism,
critical-level utilitarianism, person-affecting ethics, nonaggregation, lexical
priority or any social discount rate as foundation truth.

---

# 1. Aggregation is overloaded

HF16 separates at least:

```text
WelfareAggregation
ClaimAggregation
RiskAggregation
TemporalAggregation
PopulationAggregation
PreferenceAggregation
NormativeTheoryAggregation
```

These are not synonyms.

---

# 2. Population aggregation

Working family:

```text
PopulationAggregation_F(P,C)
= framework-relative procedure for evaluating or ordering population state P, or
  alternatives containing different sets/distributions of bearers, under context C
```

---

# 3. Claim aggregation

Working family:

```text
ClaimAggregation_F({Claim_i},C)

  relevance and uncertainty
```

---

# 4. Welfare aggregation

```text
WelfareAggregation_F({W_i},C)

```


# 16. Many tiny benefits versus one severe harm

This is a core aggregation falsifier.

Voorhoeve's Aggregate Relevant Claims model explicitly targets the combination of:

```text
many substantial claims can outweigh one grave claim
```

while resisting:

```text
arbitrarily many trivial benefits outweigh one death
```

HF16 records this as one competing solution family.

---

# 17. Tiny claims are not irrelevant by definition

```text
ManyTinyBenefits != IrrelevantByDefinition
```

A threshold/relevance rule requires a normative argument.

---

# 18. Tiny claims are not automatically aggregative winners

```text
ManyTinyBenefits != SevereHarmByDefinition
```

No algebraic identity makes enough headaches automatically outweigh death without a
specified rule.

---

# 19. Taurek-style nonaggregation

A competing family rejects simply adding the number of persons saved and instead
emphasizes each person's claim/chance.

HF16 retains this family as pressure against totalizing arithmetic.

---

# 20. Nonaggregation is not numbers-never-matter by definition

Different nonaggregative theories treat ties, lotteries and relevance differently.

---

# 21. Partial aggregation

Working family:

```text
PartialAggregation_F
= some claims aggregate against some competing claims, but not all claims are fully
  intersubstitutable across magnitude/type
```

---

# 22. Relevance threshold

A partial-aggregation theory may define when a claim is sufficiently relevant to
compete with a stronger claim.

```text
RelevantClaimThreshold != HarmDeniability
```

Below-threshold claims can remain genuine harms/benefits while not aggregating in a
particular conflict.

---

```text

  aggregation operation
```

# 64. Non-identity problem

Parfit's identity-affecting cases show that a policy can predictably lead to a lower-
quality future yet make no same future person worse off than the alternative, because
the alternative would contain different persons.

---

# 65. Non-identity is partly an identity problem

It depends on whether the same bearer exists across alternatives.

---

# 66. Non-identity is also a normative problem

Even once identity is specified, it remains open whether worse future conditions are
wrong if no same person is worse off.

# 69. Person-affecting family


# 73. Wide person-affecting family

# 222. Competing aggregation/population models

## M1 — total welfare aggregation

### Strength

Simple, additive, sensitive to every welfare contribution and population size.

### Pressure

Repugnant-type conclusions, interpersonal cardinal comparability, creation neutrality,
distribution and rights.

### Disposition

Retain as explicit model family; reject as hidden default.

## M2 — average welfare

### Strength

Avoids automatic value from population multiplication below current average.

### Pressure

Can favor reducing population in ways that improve average while losing positive
lives; ignores total number in characteristic ways.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M3 — critical-level/generalized utilitarianism

### Strength

Provides explicit social contribution threshold and flexible population-size
sensitivity.

### Pressure

Critical-level choice itself is normative; can create other population dilemmas.

### Disposition

Retain with explicit `c` and welfare transform.

## M4 — prioritarian/generalized utility

### Strength

Makes distribution and worse-off position matter while remaining aggregative.

### Pressure

Variable populations create creation-priority problems; does not by itself solve
identity/non-identity.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M5 — maximin/leximin

### Strength

Strong protection of worst-off bearers.

### Pressure

Can be insensitive to huge benefits to many when worst-off changes little; lexical
structure is demanding.

### Disposition

Retain as distinct family.

## M6 — sufficientarian/threshold

### Strength

Captures threshold crossing and severe deprivation.

### Pressure

Threshold selection and treatment above/below threshold remain unresolved.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M7 — Taurek-style nonaggregation

### Strength

Resists treating persons as containers whose claims can simply be summed; preserves
individual chances.

### Pressure

Numbers and large groups can become too weakly relevant depending on formulation.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M8 — partial/relevant-claims aggregation

### Strength

Allows aggregation among comparable/relevant claims while blocking arbitrarily many
trivial claims from dominating grave claims.

### Pressure

Needs a principled relevance relation/threshold and can produce discontinuities.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M9 — narrow person-affecting

### Strength

Grounds morality in effects on particular persons and avoids some creation-value
claims.

### Pressure

Non-identity cases and different-person populations.

### Disposition

Retain as pressure-test family.

## M10 — wide/conditional person-affecting

### Strength

Can recognize reasons concerning persons who exist in only one alternative and model
procreation asymmetry.

### Pressure

Requires explicit account of existence/nonexistence and conditional reasons.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M11 — impersonal population value

### Strength

Can evaluate identity-affecting policies and whole-population trajectories.

### Pressure

Must justify how possible/new lives contribute; exposed to population paradoxes.

### Disposition

Retain.

## M12 — incomplete/plural ordering

### Strength

Avoids false precision where values/claims are incommensurable or theories conflict.

### Pressure

Real institutions still need action selection.

### Disposition

Retain; this directly exposes HF17.

## M13 — expected-value longtermism

### Strength

Makes low-probability, huge irreversible future losses visible.

### Pressure

Depends strongly on probabilities, population axiology, future quality, standing and
rights/legitimacy constraints.

### Disposition

Retain as explicit conditional model, not default.

## M14 — fixed social discounting

### Strength

Operational simplicity and connection to opportunity cost/growth frameworks.

### Pressure

Long-horizon results are extremely parameter-sensitive; components can be ethically
and empirically distinct.

### Disposition

Never use one rate without decomposition.

## M15 — declining/uncertain discounting

### Strength

Captures uncertainty/persistence in future rates in specified economic models.

### Pressure

Model-dependent; not equivalent to normative declining welfare weight.

### Disposition

Retain as economic uncertainty model.

## M16 — functional replacement/copy counting

### Failure

Treating every identical copy as survival or every process as a new bearer ignores
HF1 identity and HF15 standing.

### Disposition

Reject untyped counting; require identity-first analysis.

# 247. Why the boundary is now unavoidable

Three independent lines converge:

1. **Formal population ethics:** attractive axioms conflict; no theory wins merely by
   avoiding one paradox.
2. **Empirical population ethics:** humans show substantial heterogeneity in total,
   average, inequality and creation judgments.
3. **Institutional reality:** policy cannot wait for unanimous metaethical convergence.

Therefore a system needs a theory of collective choice under plural and sometimes
incomplete normative orderings.
