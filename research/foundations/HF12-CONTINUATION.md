# Human Foundations Continuation after HF12

```text
Agent A state/model/goals             Agent B state/model/goals
        ↓                                      ↓
        └──── perception / prediction / partner models ────┐
                                                           ↓
```

## Exact next foundation

HF12 explains current multi-agent relations but repeatedly encounters social facts
that **pre-exist and outlive the current participants' reciprocal episode**:

```text
conventions
norms
reputation
status
authority
power
sanctions
institutions
```

These can:

- coordinate strangers;
- travel through third-party information;
- persist across membership turnover;
- define roles and permissions before negotiation;
- constrain options through sanctions/resources;
- preserve harmful or beneficial equilibria;
- be encoded in rules, records, infrastructures and organizations.

Therefore the exact next round is:

# HF13 — Social Norms, Conventions, Reputation, Status, Authority, Power, Sanctions and Institutions

## HF13 starting questions

1. What is convention relative to habit, coordination equilibrium and explicit
   agreement?
2. What is social norm relative to statistical regularity, empirical expectation,
   normative expectation, appropriateness judgment and law?
3. What is norm compliance relative to preference, conformity, strategic response
   and fear of sanction?
4. What is reputation relative to direct history, gossip, rating, credential and
   stereotype?
5. What is status relative to prestige, dominance, role and authority?
6. What is authority relative to expertise, influence, permission and coercive
   capability?
7. What is power relative to capability, resource control, dependence and legitimate
   authority?
8. What is sanction relative to punishment, incentive, enforcement and social
   exclusion?
9. How do conventions and norms emerge, persist, tip and decay across networks?
10. How do harmful norms remain stable and how should descriptive prevalence be kept
    separate from normative legitimacy?
11. What is institution relative to repeated interaction, organization, office,
    rule, infrastructure and state?
12. How do institutions externalize memory, permissions, roles and enforcement?
13. How should Human×AI systems observe/enforce institutional rules without treating
    observed convention or automated policy as legitimate by default?
14. What next boundary emerges after persistent social order is rebuilt?

## Candidate HF13 falsifiers

- decentralized convention emergence without a central planner;
- established convention overturned after minority critical mass;
- identical behavior frequency but different appropriateness expectations;
- direct experience versus third-party reputation conflict;
- reputation changing first interaction before direct evidence;
- punishment promoting cooperation under one norm but stabilizing welfare-reducing
  behavior under another;
- institution-generated behavior persisting after institution removal;
- collective-risk manipulation creating norm persistence after incentives change;
- expertise without authority and authority without expertise;
- prestige without coercive power and coercive power without legitimacy;
- rule/office surviving member turnover;
- practical power without legal authority and formal authority without practical
  control;
- AI enforcing prevalent but illegitimate rules;
- machine-readable institutional permissions constraining options before Human–AI
  negotiation.

## Do not precommit

HF12 does not establish that:

- conventions are norms;
- norms are laws;
- common behavior is legitimate behavior;
- punishment is norm enforcement in every case;
- reputation is accurate;
- high status implies competence or moral worth;
- authority implies expertise;
- power implies legitimacy;
- institutions are necessarily formal organizations;
- institutionally stabilized cooperation is welfare-improving;
- AI should infer normative authority from frequency, popularity or historical
  practice.

## Stop rule

Do not schedule HF14 now. HF13 must expose a repeated neighboring distinction whose
absence creates category failures across materially different persistent-social-
order cases.
