# HF6 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF6 concerns **history-dependent persistent change**. Surface improvement is not
enough. Each source is interpreted through at least five questions:

```text
What changed?
How was change induced?
How long did it persist?
How specific/general was it?
Did it change future response, or only current performance?
```

The ledger therefore distinguishes acquisition, retention, transfer,
reversibility/detraining and response trajectory whenever the design permits.

---

# 1. Perceptual learning: acquisition, persistence and specificity

## Karni & Sagi (1993) — time course of visual skill learning

- PMID: 8371779
- DOI: 10.1038/365250a0
- HF6 use: visual discrimination improvement developed with practice and
  continued to evolve across time, providing a canonical case in which learning
  cannot be reduced to momentary within-trial performance.

## Ahissar & Hochstein (1997) — task difficulty and specificity

- PMID: 9163425
- HF6 use: visual perceptual learning generalized more under easier training
  conditions and became more specific under harder conditions. Transfer is a
  property of the learned relation and training regime, not a universal feature.

## Fahle-style task specificity: no transfer between similar stimuli/tasks

- PMID: 8805246
- DOI: 10.1016/S0960-9822(02)00479-7
- HF6 use: substantial improvement on one hyperacuity task did not transfer to a
  closely related task with very similar stimuli at the same retinal location.

## Jeter et al. (2010) — specificity increases with training

- PMID: 20624413
- PMCID: PMC3346951
- HF6 use: brief training showed substantial transfer while extensive training
  produced stronger orientation/location specificity. More practice can improve
  trained performance while narrowing generalization.

## ERP specificity/generalization study

- PMID: 12657891
- HF6 use: behavioral and neural training effects showed different specificity
  patterns; one neural measure generalized while another remained orientation
  specific.

### HF6 inference

```text
TrainingGain != Transfer
Retention != Generalization
MorePractice != BroaderCapability
BehavioralTransfer != NeuralTransferPattern
```

# 2. Motor learning and consolidation

## Brashers-Krug, Shadmehr & Bizzi (1996)

- PMID: 8717039
- DOI: 10.1038/382252a0
- HF6 use: a newly learned motor memory was vulnerable to interference from a
  second task immediately after training but not after a four-hour interval.
  Supports time-dependent stabilization/consolidation distinct from practice
  performance.

## Fischer et al. (2002) — sleep and finger skill

- PMID: 12193650
- PMCID: PMC129381
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.182178199
- HF6 use: post-training sleep was associated with later motor-sequence
  performance gains relative to wake intervals. Retained as one task-specific
  consolidation result, not universal proof that sleep always enhances skill.

## Nettersheim et al. (2015) — stabilization rather than enhancement

- PMID: 25926448
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1236-14.2015
- HF6 use: explicitly separated early post-training boost, wake decay and
  sleep-related stabilization; challenges treating every post-sleep gain as new
  learning.

## McHill et al. (2017) — sleep versus circadian drive

- PMID: 28460138
- PMCID: PMC6084766
- DOI: 10.1093/sleep/zsx036
- HF6 use: experimental circadian misalignment design separated sleep/wake effects
  from circadian phase on motor acquisition and consolidation.

## Cohen et al. / effector-independent transfer after sleep

- PMID: 20691764
- HF6 use: sleep preserved an effector-independent extrinsic representation across
  hands, while intrinsic transfer remained absent. Consolidation and transfer are
  representation-specific.

### HF6 inference

```text
Acquisition != Consolidation
Consolidation != TimePassage
Consolidation != GuaranteedEnhancement
Retention != Transfer
Transfer_extrinsic != Transfer_intrinsic
```

# 3. Extinction, renewal and reinstatement

## Hermans et al. / human reinstatement

- PMID: 15701362
- DOI: 10.1016/j.brat.2004.03.013
- HF6 use: conditioned fear responses can return after extinction through
  reinstatement procedures.

## LaBar & Phelps (2005)

- PMID: 15998188
- DOI: 10.1037/0735-7044.119.3.677
- HF6 use: reinstatement of extinguished fear in humans was context-dependent and
  involved memory systems; reduced response during extinction does not imply
  deletion of the original association.

## Schiller-style immediate-extinction replication pressure

- PMID: 18509113
- PMCID: PMC2414250
- DOI: 10.1101/lm.909208
- HF6 use: spontaneous recovery/reinstatement after apparently successful
  extinction provides direct pressure against simple erasure models.

## Human renewal experiment

- PMID: 20822294
- DOI: 10.1037/a0020519
- HF6 use: conditioned responding recovered outside the extinction context even
  when context effects could not be reduced to a simple inhibitory cue account.

## Thompson et al. (2018)

- PMID: 29981936
- DOI: 10.1016/j.brat.2018.07.001
- HF6 use: different extinction procedures altered spontaneous recovery,
  reacquisition and reinstatement differently across response measures.

### HF6 inference

```text
ExtinctionPerformance != MemoryErasure
ReducedResponseNow != OriginalLearningGone
Renewal != SpontaneousRecovery != Reinstatement != Reacquisition
```

The return-of-response family strongly exposes the next memory/retrieval boundary.

# 4. Habituation and sensitization: repeated stress

## Wüst et al. (2005)

- PMID: 15471617
- DOI: 10.1016/j.psyneuen.2004.07.002
- HF6 use: repeated psychosocial stress produced mean HPA habituation, but only
  about half of participants showed the expected decline and a minority showed
  sensitization. Group mean adaptation is not individual trajectory identity.

## Kudielka et al. (2006)

- PMID: 16236419
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2005.09.001
- HF6 use: exhaustion covaried with reduced cortisol habituation; some individuals
  sensitized across repeated stress sessions.

## Schommer et al. / HPA versus sympathetic dissociation

- PMID: 12764219
- HF6 use: endocrine and sympathetic/cardiovascular components showed different
  repeated-stress trajectories. `Habituation_systemA` does not imply whole-body
  habituation.

## Thoma et al. (2017) — simultaneous habituation and sensitization

- PMID: 27916659
- DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2016.11.030
- HF6 use: the same repeated stress exposures produced cortisol habituation while
  inflammatory IL-6 responses sensitized; the strength of the two changes was
  inversely related in this small sample.

## Turan et al. (2015) — anticipatory sensitization

- PMID: 25497480
- DOI: 10.1016/j.psyneuen.2014.11.014
- HF6 use: repeated stress sessions produced progressive anticipatory cortisol
  sensitization in some participants, modulated by attention/emotion training.

## Petrowski et al. — timescale and recovery of stress habituation

- PMID: 22318453
- DOI: 10.1007/s10484-012-9183-4
- HF6 use: cortisol response decreased across 24-hour repeated sessions but after a
  ten-week interval was comparable to the earlier challenge, showing timescale and
  recovery matter for calling a response change persistent.

### HF6 inference

```text
Habituation_D != Habituation_E
Habituation != PermanentChange
Sensitization_D != Sensitization_E
SameExposure can produce Habituation_A + Sensitization_B
GroupMeanTrajectory != IndividualTrajectory
```

# 5. Startle habituation and dishabituation

## Abel et al. (1998)

- PMID: 10065906
- DOI: 10.1177/026988119801200402
- HF6 use: repeated human startle testing directly measures decrement across
  repeated stimuli and retest sessions.

## Carlsen et al. / startle dishabituation in action context

- PMID: 12904937
- DOI: 10.1007/s00221-003-1575-5
- HF6 use: startle response that normally habituates can be dishabituated under a
  reaction-time task context. Response decrement is not equivalent to exhausted
  response machinery.

## startle modality comparison

- PMID: 8132414
- DOI: 10.3109/00207459308987207
- HF6 use: acoustic and electrocutaneous startle showed comparable habituation
  patterns, useful for testing response-system specificity and supramodal claims.

### HF6 inference

```text
Habituation != EffectorFatigue
Dishabituation is evidence that response capacity can remain available
ResponseDecrement != SensoryReceptorFailure
```

# 6. Tolerance is not habituation

## Robertson et al. (1981) — caffeine tolerance

- PMID: 7009653
- PMCID: PMC370671
- DOI: 10.1172/JCI110124
- HF6 use: repeated caffeine administration produced near-complete tolerance in
  several humoral/hemodynamic endpoints over days.

## Denaro et al. (1991)

- PMID: 2060564
- DOI: 10.1007/BF00315208
- HF6 use: repeated dosing produced endpoint-specific tolerance; some effects were
  incompletely compensated at high exposure.

## Evans & Griffiths-style caffeine choice/tolerance

- PMID: 1410146
- HF6 use: subjective caffeine effects developed tolerance while reinforcing choice
  effects did not show the same pattern.

## conditioned tolerance in humans

- PMID: 1384078
- HF6 use: former opiate-dependent participants showed context/cue-dependent
  attenuation of drug effects, supporting a learned component of tolerance.

### HF6 inference

```text
Tolerance_D != Tolerance_E
Tolerance != Habituation
Tolerance can include pharmacodynamic + learned/contextual mechanisms
RepeatedDoseEffectDecline != generalized response habituation
```

# 7. Physiological adaptation and detraining

## continuous heat acclimation

- PMID: 3396564
- HF6 use: repeated heat exposure changed sweating and circulatory responses across
  days. Physiological adaptation involved several effectors with different time
  courses.

## repeated mild heat and skeletal-muscle mitochondria

- PMID: 30024339
- HF6 use: six days of repeated local heat exposure altered heat-shock proteins,
  mitochondrial proteins and respiratory capacity in human skeletal muscle.

## short-term heat acclimation

- PMID: 33846088
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2021.03.020
- HF6 use: acclimation reduced heat strain in one subsequent exposure context but
  benefits did not simply generalize to every consecutive heat challenge.

## adaptation specificity in cutaneous vasomotor function

- PMID: 29156142
- DOI: 10.1139/apnm-2017-0569
- HF6 use: adaptation became evident when measured at matched deep-body
  temperatures, illustrating how test conditions determine whether an adaptation
  is detected.

## Coyle et al. (1984) — detraining

- PMID: 6511559
- DOI: 10.1152/jappl.1984.57.6.1857
- HF6 use: different endurance-training adaptations decayed at different rates;
  some remained above sedentary-control levels after 84 days of detraining.

## mitochondrial training/detraining

- PMID: 1474078
- DOI: 10.1152/jappl.1992.73.5.2004
- HF6 use: mitochondrial ATP-production/enzyme changes increased with training and
  partially reversed with detraining.

## modern mixed training/detraining experiment

- PMID: 33867497
- HF6 use: after several forms of exercise training, lean mass/strength and aerobic
  adaptations showed different retention/decay during detraining.

### HF6 inference

```text
Adaptation_D != Adaptation_E
AcquisitionRate_D != DecayRate_D
Detraining != InstantReturnToBaseline
Persistence_D != Persistence_E
```

# 8. Recovery, restitution and compensation after injury

## Kitago et al. (2013)

- PMID: 22798152
- DOI: 10.1177/1545968312452631
- HF6 use: improved clinical arm use after constraint-induced therapy did not by
  itself identify whether normal motor control was restored or task-specific
  compensation occurred.

## Corti et al. (2012)

- PMID: 22357633
- DOI: 10.1177/1545968311433426
- HF6 use: different rehabilitation regimes produced improvements through
  different mixtures of normalized movement and compensatory strategies.

## high-intensity stepping after stroke

- PMID: 30595090
- DOI: 10.1177/1545968318817825
- HF6 use: improved walking speed was supported by both more normal and compensatory
  movement strategies.

## chronic stroke cortical map plasticity

- PMID: 9696052
- HF6 use: constraint-induced therapy improved motor performance and enlarged motor
  output maps in chronic stroke, demonstrating adult injury/training-induced
  plastic change.

### HF6 inference

```text
FunctionalImprovement != Restitution
FunctionalImprovement != CompensationOnly
Compensation != Failure
NeuralReorganization != automatically FunctionalRestitution
```

# 9. Sensitive periods and development

## Maurer et al. (1999) — rapid visual improvement after cataract removal

- PMID: 10506555
- DOI: 10.1126/science.286.5437.108
- HF6 use: infants deprived of patterned visual input showed rapid acuity
  improvement after visual input was restored; developmental state alters both
  damage and recovery dynamics.

## Birch/critical timing in congenital unilateral cataract

- PMID: 8675395
- HF6 use: acuity outcomes showed a sharp change in relation to timing of surgical
  treatment, with intervention before roughly six weeks associated with better
  outcomes in that cohort.

## early visual deprivation and later spatial/temporal vision

- PMID: 10615511
- HF6 use: different visual functions showed different long-term effects after
  early deprivation; sensitive-period constraints are function-specific.

## global-form deficits after early visual deprivation

- PMID: 11934447
- HF6 use: later global-form sensitivity depended on early monocular/binocular
  deprivation history.

## biological-motion sparing after early deprivation

- PMID: 22709397
- HF6 use: despite major deficits in some global-motion/form functions, biological
  motion sensitivity could be relatively spared. Developmental deprivation does
  not produce uniform visual-system loss.

### HF6 inference

```text
SensitivePeriod_D != SensitivePeriod_E
EarlyDeprivationEffect_D != Effect_E
DevelopmentalConstraint != TotalLaterImmutability
```

# 10. Adult plasticity after atypical development

## Polat et al. (2004) — adult amblyopia training

- PMID: 15096608
- PMCID: PMC404107
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0401200101
- HF6 use: targeted perceptual learning improved contrast sensitivity and letter
  recognition in adults with amblyopia, demonstrating meaningful adult plasticity
  after development outside normal visual conditions.

## Zhou et al. (2006) — adult amblyopia retention/generalization

- PMID: 16153674
- DOI: 10.1016/j.visres.2005.07.031
- HF6 use: adult training gains generalized differently depending on training
  regimen; in a tested subset, visual-acuity gains showed high retention over at
  least one year.

### HF6 inference

```text
SensitivePeriod != AbsoluteAdultNoPlasticity
AdultPlasticity != ChildDevelopmentalPlasticity
PotentialForChange != EqualEase/ExtentOfChangeAcrossAge
```

# 11. Development across the lifespan is not monotonic

## Janacsek et al. (2012)

- PMID: 22709399
- PMCID: PMC3383816
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-7687.2012.01150.x
- HF6 use: probabilistic sequence learning across ages 4–85 showed pronounced
  age-related changes, with results depending partly on whether raw reaction time,
  transformed RT or accuracy was used.

## Lukács & Kemény (2015)

- PMID: 25039658
- DOI: 10.1111/cogs.12143
- HF6 use: three implicit-skill paradigms across ages 7–87 produced different
  lifespan trajectories from the simple `children best, then continuous decline`
  story; adolescence/adulthood were highly efficient in their measures.

### HF6 inference

```text
Development != MonotonicImprovement
AgeCurve_D != AgeCurve_E
DevelopmentalConclusion depends on task + metric + stage
```

The two studies are useful partly because they prevent a single generic
`learning ability by age` curve from becoming ontology.

# 12. Aging and retained plasticity

## Rodrigue, Kennedy & Raz (2005)

- PMID: 15980284
- DOI: 10.1093/geronb/60.4.P174
- HF6 use: mirror-tracing skill was partially retained across five years; older
  adults required more training at follow-up but still learned and could reach a
  similar asymptote under relevant conditions.

## motor/mental training in older adults

- PMID: 27450266
- HF6 use: six weeks of motor and imagery training in older adults improved
  performance with distinct functional brain response patterns; combining training
  modes did not automatically add benefit and could introduce interference.

## older-adult inhibition training

- PMID: 22182362
- HF6 use: older adults improved on trained inhibitory processing but did not show
  broad transfer to other tasks.

## aging, gait learning and interlimb transfer

- PMID: 29981399
- HF6 use: older adults retained meaningful gait learning and transfer, but age
  affected learning/retention dynamics.

## experimentally induced versus use-dependent M1 plasticity (2025)

- PMID: 40445383
- HF6 use: young and older adults differed after paired-associative stimulation,
  while age differences were not observed in the same way after actual motor skill
  acquisition. `Plasticity` depends on induction paradigm.

### HF6 inference

```text
Aging != NoLearning
Aging != NoPlasticity
AgeEffect_D != AgeEffect_E
InducedPlasticityAssay != WholePlasticCapacity
TrainingGain != FarTransfer
```

# 13. Resilience trajectories

## deRoon-Cassini et al. (2010) — severe physical injury

- PMID: 20175630
- HF6 use: longitudinal rehabilitation data distinguished resilient, recovery and
  persistent-distress trajectories after severe injury.

## Galatzer-Levy / police prospective stress physiology

- PMID: 24952936
- HF6 use: resilient/recovery trajectories over years were not defined by absent
  cortisol response; blunted laboratory cortisol response predicted less favorable
  later distress trajectories in that cohort.

## dynamic resilience under background stress exposure

- PMID: 39531708
- DOI: 10.1037/amp0001315
- HF6 use: resilience trajectory estimation changed when background stressors were
  explicitly modeled, reinforcing that resilience is relative to exposure and
  outcome trajectory rather than a standalone trait score.

## cross-domain resilience after major events

- PMID: 40097913
- DOI: 10.1002/jts.23140
- HF6 use: resilience in depressive-symptom trajectory did not automatically imply
  equivalent positive adaptation across psychological, functional and health
  domains.

### HF6 inference

```text
Resilience != NoResponse
Resilience != FastRecoveryOnly
Resilience_D != Resilience_E
Resilience requires Exposure + Outcome + Timescale + Trajectory
```

# 14. Aging: maintenance versus reserve

## Montemurro et al. (2025)

- PMID: 40095141
- PMCID: PMC11914246
- DOI: 10.1007/s40520-025-03000-z
- HF6 use: higher estimated cognitive reserve was associated with more stable
  longitudinal cognitive trajectories in an older clinical cohort.

## longitudinal brain-maintenance / cognitive-reserve modeling (2026)

- PMID: 41863706
- DOI: 10.1007/s11357-026-02157-3
- HF6 use: explicitly separates brain maintenance (less age-related brain change)
  from cognitive reserve (better-than-expected cognition given brain change) in a
  longitudinal design.

### HF6 inference

```text
Maintenance != Reserve
Reserve != PerformanceLevel
StableFunction can arise through different mechanisms
```

HF6 retains reserve/maintenance as useful trajectory modifiers, not fixed global
Human traits.

# 15. Source-level synthesis

Across domains, the stable empirical separations are:

```text
CurrentStateChange != PersistentAdaptation
Practice != Learning
TrainingPerformance != RetainedLearning
Acquisition != Consolidation
Consolidation != Retention
Retention != Transfer
Transfer_D != Transfer_E
Specificity != LackOfPlasticity

PlasticityCapacity != PlasticChange
BehavioralChange != NeuralMechanismIdentity

Habituation != Fatigue
Habituation != SensoryAdaptation
Habituation_D != Habituation_E
Sensitization_D != Sensitization_E
Habituation_A can coexist with Sensitization_B
Tolerance != Habituation

Extinction != Erasure
SpontaneousRecovery != Renewal != Reinstatement != Reacquisition

PhysiologicalAdaptation_D != Adaptation_E
Detraining_Decay_D != Decay_E
FunctionalImprovement != Restitution
Compensation != Restitution

SensitivePeriod_D != SensitivePeriod_E
SensitivePeriod != AdultImmutability
Development != MonotonicImprovement
ChronologicalAge != DevelopmentalMechanism
Aging != UniformDecline
Aging != NoPlasticity

Resilience != NoResponse
Resilience_D != Resilience_E
Maintenance != Reserve
```

The repeated residual is not merely `change`. It is **what persists, what is
retrieved, what decays, what transfers, and what returns after apparent loss**.
That residual points toward memory/retention/forgetting/retrieval/generalization as
the next foundation boundary.
