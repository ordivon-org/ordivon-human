# HF2 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF2 studies a phenomenon whose defining evidence problem is unusual: conscious
experience is first-personal, whereas most scientific measurements are
third-personal. HF2 therefore separates:

```text
phenomenon
!= elicitation method
!= report
!= behaviour
!= neural/physiological marker
!= memory trace
```

A source can establish a dissociation, improve an inference, constrain a theory,
or expose a measurement confound without becoming a universal consciousness
detector.

---

# 1. Sleep and dreaming

## Siclari et al. (2017) — neural correlates of dreaming

- Citation: Francesca Siclari et al., *The neural correlates of dreaming*, Nature
  Neuroscience 20:872–878.
- DOI: 10.1038/nn.4545
- PMID: 28394322
- PMCID: PMC5462120
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462120/
- HF2 use: serial awakenings and high-density EEG show that reports of conscious
  experience can occur in REM and NREM sleep, while some awakenings yield no
  reported experience. Dream contents can be related to localized posterior
  cortical activity.
- Boundary: retrospective report is the best available access to many dream
  contents but is not an infallible record; failure to recall/report does not by
  itself establish absence of earlier experience.

## Massimini et al. (2005) — breakdown of cortical effective connectivity during sleep

- Citation: Marcello Massimini et al., *Breakdown of cortical effective
  connectivity during sleep*, Science 309(5744):2228–2232.
- DOI: 10.1126/science.1117256
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16195466/
- HF2 use: perturbational evidence shows major changes in cortical effective
  connectivity between wake and sleep while the same Human is tracked across
  state transitions.
- Boundary: this is a neural-state correlate, not a direct readout of subjective
  experience and not a universal consciousness metric.

## HF2 sleep inference

Sleep pressure-tests three collapses:

```text
wakefulness = consciousness
sleep stage = experience/no-experience
later dream report = complete record of prior experience
```

All three fail as universal identities.

# 2. Anaesthesia, connectedness and recall

## Sanders et al. (2012) — Unresponsiveness ≠ Unconsciousness

- Citation: Robert D. Sanders et al., *Unresponsiveness ≠ Unconsciousness*,
  Anesthesiology 116(4):946–959.
- PMID: 22314293
- PMCID: PMC3311716
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3311716/
- HF2 use: provides the useful distinction between consciousness and
  **connectedness** to the external environment. Dreaming can be conscious yet
  disconnected; anaesthesia aims to prevent connected experience of surgery,
  whether by unconsciousness or disconnection.
- Boundary: this review synthesizes multiple studies and does not provide one
  definitive physiological marker of connectedness.

## Sanders et al. (2017) — connected consciousness after intubation

- Citation: *Incidence of Connected Consciousness after Tracheal Intubation: A
  Prospective, International, Multicenter Cohort Study of the Isolated Forearm
  Technique*, Anesthesiology 126(2).
- PMID: 27984262
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27984262/
- HF2 use: 12 of 260 participants (4.6%) responded to command with the isolated
  forearm after tracheal intubation during intended general anaesthesia; five
  responders signalled pain. No explicit postoperative recall of the event was
  reported in the follow-up sample.
- Boundary: an isolated-forearm response is strong evidence of connected command
  processing/volitional response, not proof that every other unresponsive patient
  lacks experience.

## Gaskell et al. (2017) — frontal alpha-delta EEG does not preclude volitional response

- Citation: *Frontal alpha-delta EEG does not preclude volitional response during
  anaesthesia: prospective cohort study of the isolated forearm technique*.
- PMID: 29121278
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29121278/
- HF2 use: a common anaesthesia EEG pattern can coexist with volitional isolated-
  forearm responses, demonstrating that a candidate physiological state marker
  need not determine connected responsiveness.

## Young-adult multicentre isolated-forearm cohort (2023)

- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10375493/
- HF2 use: command responses after intubation occurred in 37/338 young adults in
  this cohort, reinforcing the dissociation among intended anaesthesia, ordinary
  behavioural unresponsiveness and connected command following.
- Boundary: incidence is cohort/protocol dependent and must not be generalized as
  a universal rate of consciousness under anaesthesia.

## HF2 anaesthesia inference

```text
intended anaesthesia
!= unconsciousness
!= disconnection
!= behavioural unresponsiveness
!= amnesia
```

An anaesthetic can change several dimensions simultaneously, but their empirical
association is not identity.

# 3. Disorders of consciousness and covert command following

## AAN / ACRM / NIDILRR Disorders of Consciousness guideline

- Authority: American Academy of Neurology with the American Congress of
  Rehabilitation Medicine and NIDILRR.
- Guideline: September 2018; reaffirmed July 12, 2024.
- URL: https://www.aan.com/Guidelines/Home/GuidelineDetail/926
- HF2 use: prolonged disorders of consciousness require careful standardized
  assessment and repeated evaluation; clinical diagnosis and prognosis cannot be
  reduced to one casual bedside observation.
- Boundary: a clinical diagnostic framework is not a complete scientific theory
  of consciousness.

## Monti et al. (2010) — willful modulation in disorders of consciousness

- Citation: *Willful Modulation of Brain Activity in Disorders of Consciousness*,
  New England Journal of Medicine 362:579–589.
- DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa0905370
- URL: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0905370
- HF2 use: 5/54 severely brain-injured patients showed willful, task-specific fMRI
  modulation; in two, voluntary behaviour could not be detected at bedside, and
  one patient used the paradigm to answer yes/no questions.
- Boundary: failure to show an imaging response is not symmetric evidence of
  unconsciousness; task performance requires perception of instruction,
  sustained attention, comprehension and imagery capacity in addition to any
  conscious experience.

## Bodien et al. (2024) — Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness

- Citation: *Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness*, New
  England Journal of Medicine 391:598–608.
- DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2400645
- URL: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645
- HF2 use: in a six-centre prospective cohort of 353 adults, cognitive motor
  dissociation was detected by task-based fMRI and/or EEG in 60 of 241 (25%)
  participants without an observable command response at bedside. Conversely,
  imaging command responses were also absent in many participants who did show
  observable command following.
- Boundary: this is evidence that motor/behavioural output and covert task
  performance dissociate; task positivity is not equivalent to the full contents
  of consciousness, while task negativity does not prove absence of experience.

## HF2 DoC inference

```text
bedside responsiveness
!= covert command following
!= conscious contents as a whole
```

and diagnostic evidence should be represented as **asymmetric and
uncertainty-bearing**, not as a binary consciousness oracle.

# 4. Infant / nonverbal evidence

## Kouider et al. (2013) — neural marker of perceptual consciousness in infants

- Citation: Sid Kouider et al., *A neural marker of perceptual consciousness in
  infants*, Science 340(6130):376–380.
- DOI: 10.1126/science.1232509
- PMID: 23599498
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23599498/
- HF2 use: infant event-related responses to briefly presented faces showed a
  late slow-wave pattern with similarities to adult signatures associated with
  perceptual consciousness, with much longer latencies in younger infants.
- Boundary: the study provides convergent neural evidence under nonverbal
  conditions; it does not make the late slow wave a universal consciousness
  detector or establish adult-like report/access architecture in infants.

## HF2 infant inference

```text
absence of verbal report
!= absence of potentially conscious perceptual processing
```

but:

```text
adult-like neural marker
!= direct first-person proof
```

The correct result is graded evidential support with explicit model dependence.

# 5. Objective performance and reported awareness

## Blindsight and visual awareness

### Weiskrantz / Cowey tradition

- Example primary study: *Is blindsight like normal, near-threshold vision?*
- PMID: 9391175
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9391175/
- HF2 use: some patients can discriminate stimuli in a clinically blind field
  above chance while reporting no ordinary visual awareness, even after response
  bias is addressed.

### Consciousness of the first order in blindsight

- PMID: 21078979
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21078979/
- HF2 use: blindsight itself is heterogeneous; the classic distinction between
  type I (discrimination without acknowledged awareness) and type II (some
  feeling/awareness without ordinary seeing) shows that `awareness` is not a
  single clean binary label.

### V1 and feedback are not necessary for all reported conscious vision

- PMID: 21097490
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21097490/
- HF2 use: patients with V1 damage can report degraded but visual awareness in
  blind fields under some stimulus conditions. This pressure-tests simplistic
  anatomical equations such as `V1 activity = visual consciousness`.

## HF2 blindsight inference

```text
stimulus sensitivity / discrimination
!= ordinary visual phenomenology
!= subjective confidence
!= metacognitive access
```

Blindsight does not prove that every above-chance response is fully unconscious;
it proves that objective performance and reported/experienced visual qualities
must be measured separately.

# 6. Report and post-perceptual processing

## Pitts, Metzler & Hillyard (2014)

- Citation: *Isolating neural correlates of conscious perception from neural
  correlates of reporting one's perception*.
- PMID: 25339922
- PMCID: PMC4189413
- DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01078
- HF2 use: task relevance and awareness were crossed; consciously perceived but
  task-irrelevant stimuli showed an earlier posterior negativity, whereas access
  for report added later components including P3b.
- Boundary: ERP component interpretation remains theory- and paradigm-dependent;
  this study does not alone establish one universal NCC.

## Cohen et al. (2020) — P3b tracks report/postperceptual processing

- Citation: *Distinguishing the Neural Correlates of Perceptual Awareness and
  Postperceptual Processing*, Journal of Neuroscience 40(25):4925–4935.
- PMID: 32409620
- PMCID: PMC7326348
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0120-20.2020
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7326348/
- HF2 use: visible stimuli generated a robust P3b in report conditions but not
  no-report conditions, supporting the claim that P3b is strongly associated
  with post-perceptual/report processes rather than awareness itself.

## Pitts et al. (2014) — gamma/P3 post-perceptual processing

- PMID: 25063731
- PMCID: PMC4169212
- DOI: 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.07.024
- HF2 use: further pressure against treating late gamma/P3 responses as pure
  consciousness markers independent of task/report demands.

## Hesse & Tsao (2020) — no-report binocular rivalry

- PMID: 33174836
- PMCID: PMC7676863
- DOI: 10.7554/eLife.58360
- HF2 use: macaque inferotemporal populations tracked the inferred conscious
  percept during binocular rivalry without active report, while also encoding
  suppressed stimulus information.
- Boundary: the inferred percept depends on a validated behavioural proxy; no-
  report does not eliminate all cognitive/oculomotor confounds.

## Kingir et al. (2025) — no-report rivalry confound

- PMID: 40493983
- DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2025.103891
- HF2 use: fixation shifts/saccades in a no-report rivalry paradigm induced a
  meaningful fraction of perceptual switches, showing that removing overt report
  can introduce or reveal new confounds rather than produce direct unmediated
  access to consciousness.

## Block (2019) — limits of no-report inference

- Citation: Ned Block, *What Is Wrong with the No-Report Paradigm and How to Fix
  It*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 23(12):1003–1013.
- PMID: 31676213
- DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2019.10.001
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31676213/
- HF2 use: argues that removing overt report need not remove post-perceptual or
  cognitive processing, so a no-report condition is not automatically a
  no-cognition condition. HF2 retains this as a methodological rival/critique,
  not as proof that all no-report paradigms fail.

## HF2 report inference

```text
Report
= an action produced from experience/access/decision/memory/motor processes
```

so:

```text
NeuralCorrelate(ReportCondition)
may contain
NCC-prerequisites + NCC + post-perceptual access + decision + memory + action
```

No-report paradigms reduce some confounds but replace direct trial-by-trial report
with an inferential proxy.

# 7. Awareness does not guarantee immediate reportability or memory

## Chen & Wyble (2015) — attribute amnesia

- Citation: *Amnesia for object attributes: failure to report attended
  information that had just reached conscious awareness*.
- PMID: 25564523
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25564523/
- HF2 use: participants could fail an unexpected immediate report of an attribute
  that had just been attended and used successfully for target selection.
- Boundary: the interpretation that the attribute was consciously available
  relies on the task structure and does not prove unlimited phenomenal overflow.

## HF2 memory inference

```text
contemporaneous experience
!= durable encoding
!= immediate unexpected reportability
!= later recall
```

Memory failure can destroy evidence about a prior experience without logically
entailing that the experience never occurred.

# 8. Consciousness, access and report as an open empirical boundary

## Cohen et al. no-report results

- HF2 use: removing report can eliminate candidate late markers such as P3b,
  indicating that post-perceptual access/report processes contaminate some
  classical contrasts.

## Hatamimajoumerd et al. (2022) — no-report fMRI masking

- PMID: 35981538
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cub.2022.07.068
- HF2 use: univariate frontal activation for visible stimuli was robust in report
  conditions and disappeared in no-report conditions, yet multivariate decoding
  of awareness remained possible across the brain including frontal regions.
- Boundary: absence of univariate activation does not imply absence of relevant
  information; decoding does not establish causal necessity.

## Mudrik et al. (2023) — phenomenal consciousness without online access?

- PMID: 37393795
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2023.105529
- HF2 use: experimentally probes the debated possibility that qualitative
  experience may exceed online access, using retrospective judgments under
  conditions designed to remove online access.
- Boundary: the paradigm does not settle the philosophical phenomenal/access
  distinction; retrospective judgment itself requires later access and memory.

## HF2 access inference

HF2 does **not** freeze `phenomenal consciousness` and `access consciousness` as
separate ontological substances. It instead retains a measurable downstream-
availability question:

```text
AvailableFor_D(content, system, t)
```

where `D` might be:

- discrimination;
- working memory;
- decision;
- metacognitive evaluation;
- verbal report;
- long-term memory;
- action planning.

Different downstream availabilities need not be identical.

# 9. One-dimensional consciousness level is under pressure

## Bayne, Hohwy & Owen (2016) — Are There Levels of Consciousness?

- DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2016.03.009
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27101880/
- HF2 use: argues that global states of consciousness are not well represented by
  one degree/level and proposes a multidimensional alternative involving how
  conscious contents are gated and what functional roles they can play.
- Boundary: this is a conceptual/theoretical analysis, not an empirical proof
  that one specific multidimensional state-space is canonical.

## HF2 state-space inference

HF2 therefore treats scalar `level of consciousness` as a potentially useful
clinical shorthand, not a foundation primitive.

A safer research representation is:

```text
ConsciousStateProfile(t) =
[
  Wakefulness/Arousal,
  ExperienceEvidence,
  ExternalConnectedness,
  ContentBreadth/Complexity,
  Access/Availability,
  Responsiveness,
  ReportChannel,
  Encoding/Recall,
  MetacognitiveEvidence
]
```

The coordinates are not assumed independent or exhaustive.

# 10. Competing consciousness theories as pressure tests

## Cogitate Consortium (2025) — adversarial test of GNWT and IIT

- Citation: *Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated
  information theories of consciousness*, Nature, published 30 April 2025.
- DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1
- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1
- HF2 use: preregistered, multimodal adversarial collaboration in 256 human
  participants directly tested divergent predictions of Global Neuronal Workspace
  Theory and Integrated Information Theory. Results supported some predictions
  while substantially challenging key tenets of both.
- Boundary: the study does not establish a final consciousness theory; it is
  especially valuable to HF2 because it demonstrates that mature theories can
  generate sharp predictions and still remain partially underdetermined by data.

## HF2 theory rule

Retain theories as model families that propose relations among:

```text
content
availability/broadcast
recurrence/integration
neural substrate
report/action consequences
```

Do not translate a theory's preferred mechanism directly into the definition of
conscious experience.

# 11. Source-level synthesis

Across the source families, the strongest stable separations are:

```text
Experience != Wakefulness
Experience != ExternalConnectedness
Experience != BehaviouralResponsiveness
Experience != Report
Experience != Memory

Wakefulness != ExternalConnectedness
Connectedness != Responsiveness
Responsiveness != Reportability
Reportability != LaterRecall

ObjectiveDiscrimination != ReportedVisualExperience
NeuralMarker != Phenomenon
TaskPerformance != CompleteConsciousState
ReportConditionActivity != NCC by default
NoReportProxy != DirectFirstPersonAccess

CurrentConsciousState != one scalar level by default
TheoryPrediction != ontology authority
AbsenceOfPositiveEvidence != evidence of absence at equal strength
```

HF2 therefore proceeds with a **state/content/evidence/availability grammar**
rather than a one-word or one-score consciousness model.
