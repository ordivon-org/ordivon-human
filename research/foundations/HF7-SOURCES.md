# HF7 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF7 studies a latent-history problem: memory is often inferred from present
performance even though present performance depends on encoding, retention,
retrieval cues, competition, context, confidence and response channel.

The ledger therefore distinguishes:

```text
history / exposure
encoding process
persistent availability / retention
retrieval conditions
retrieval operation
current expression
confidence / report
```

No failed test proves physical deletion, and no successful test proves exact
replay of an unchanged record.

---

# 1. Retrieval practice: remembering changes later memory

## Roediger & Karpicke (2006) — test-enhanced learning

- PMID: 16507066
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/
- HF7 use: repeated retrieval/testing produced better delayed retention than
  repeated study under the reported conditions even though repeated study could
  support stronger immediate performance.

### HF7 inference

```text
Retrieval != PassiveMeasurement
ImmediatePerformance != DelayedRetention
```

Testing can change the future memory state that it appears to measure.

---

# 2. Retrieval-induced forgetting

## Anderson, Bjork & Bjork (1994)

- PMID: 7931095
- DOI: 10.1037//0278-7393.20.5.1063
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7931095/
- HF7 use: selective retrieval practice enhanced practiced exemplars while later
  recall of related nonpracticed exemplars was impaired relative to baseline.

## Anderson, Bjork & Bjork follow-up / recall-specific mechanism

- PMID: 11082860
- DOI: 10.3758/bf03214366
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11082860/
- HF7 use: competitive retrieval, rather than matched reexposure, produced the
  impairment of competitors under the experiment.

### HF7 inference

```text
RetrievalTargetStrengthening
can coexist with
CompetitorAccessibilityReduction
```

and:

```text
Remembering_A
can cause later forgetting-like behavior_B
```

This does not by itself prove permanent deletion or one universal inhibitory
mechanism.

# 3. Amnesia: explicit access versus skill/priming

## Cohen & Squire (1980) — mirror-reading skill

- PMID: 7414331
- DOI: 10.1126/science.7414331
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7414331/
- HF7 use: amnesic patients acquired and retained a pattern-analysis/mirror-reading
  skill despite severe declarative-memory impairment.

## Gabrieli et al. / mirror tracing including H.M.

- PMID: 8136066
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8136066/
- HF7 use: Alzheimer and global-amnesia groups acquired/retained mirror-tracing
  skill despite poor recall/recognition of the training experience; H.M. retained
  the skill over a long interval in the reported study.

## Shimamura & Squire (1984) — priming

- PMID: 6240522
- DOI: 10.1037//0096-3445.113.4.556
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6240522/
- HF7 use: severe explicit-memory impairment coexisted with preserved priming
  effects under several conditions.

## Cave & Squire (1992) — long-lasting picture priming

- PMID: 1534352
- DOI: 10.1037//0278-7393.18.3.509
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1534352/
- HF7 use: amnesic patients showed long-lasting repetition priming after single
  exposure while recognition remained impaired.

### HF7 inference

```text
ExplicitRecall != SkillMemory
Recognition != Priming
NoEpisodicRecollection != NoHistoryDependentChange
```

`Memory` cannot be retained as one unitary faculty defined by conscious report.

# 4. Recognition: recollection and familiarity are not one thing

## Yonelinas & Levy (2002)

- PMID: 12412899
- DOI: 10.3758/bf03196315
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12412899/
- HF7 use: recollection- and familiarity-based recognition showed different
  short-interval forgetting patterns under the task/model.

## Bastin & Van der Linden (2003)

- PMID: 12597069
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12597069/
- HF7 use: recognition task format and aging changed estimated contributions of
  recollection and familiarity.

## Staresina/independent contribution experiment

- PMID: 22623670
- PMCID: PMC6622307
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.6396-11.2012
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22623670/
- HF7 use: recollection and familiarity made separable contributions to recognition
  judgments under the experiment.

### HF7 inference

```text
Recognition != RecallWithMoreCueing
RecognitionJudgment != Recollection
Familiarity != Recollection
```

Dual-process interpretations remain model-sensitive; HF7 retains the empirical
process distinction without canonizing one final architecture.

# 5. Context-dependent retrieval: classic result plus failed direct replication

## Godden & Baddeley (1975)

- DOI: 10.1111/j.2044-8295.1975.tb01468.x
- Primary publisher record:
  https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2044-8295.1975.tb01468.x
- HF7 use: original diver experiment reported better free recall when learning and
  retrieval environment matched (land/underwater).

## Murre et al. (2021) — direct replication

- PMID: 34760281
- PMCID: PMC8568063
- DOI: 10.1098/rsos.200724
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34760281/
- HF7 use: a close replication did not reproduce the original context-dependent
  recall effect in its small sample.

### HF7 inference

HF7 retains only the scoped principle:

```text
RetrievalAccessibility
can depend on cue/context compatibility
```

not:

```text
same physical context always improves memory
```

Replication-sensitive boundaries are part of the foundation.

# 6. Internal state can gate retrieval

## Petersen (1979) — scopolamine state dependence

- PMID: 116273
- DOI: 10.1007/BF00427515
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/116273/
- HF7 use: in some uncued recall tasks, matching drug state between encoding and
  retrieval improved performance relative to changed drug state; effects did not
  appear identically across all task formats.

## Kenealy (1997) — mood-state-dependent retrieval experiments

- PMID: 9225625
- DOI: 10.1080/713755711
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9225625/
- HF7 use: reports several mood-induction experiments and methodological constraints
  around mood-state-dependent retrieval.

### HF7 inference

```text
CurrentAccessibility
can depend on InternalState_t
```

but:

```text
StateMismatch != StorageLoss
```

and state-dependent retrieval is not assumed universal.

# 7. Extinction return: nonexpression is not erasure

HF7 inherits the strongest HF6 extinction falsifiers.

## LaBar & Phelps (2005)

- PMID: 15998188
- DOI: 10.1037/0735-7044.119.3.677
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15998188/
- HF7 use: extinguished fear could be reinstated in context-sensitive fashion.

## Human spontaneous recovery / reinstatement experiment

- PMID: 18509113
- PMCID: PMC2414250
- DOI: 10.1101/lm.909208
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18509113/

### HF7 inference

```text
NoCurrentExpression
!= NoRetainedHistory
```

The data constrain simple erasure accounts without proving one single hidden-memory
architecture.

# 8. Reminder-driven updating / reconsolidation pressure

## Hupbach et al. (2007)

- PMID: 17202429
- PMCID: PMC1838545
- DOI: 10.1101/lm.365707
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17202429/
- HF7 use: reminder before learning a second object list produced later intrusions
  from List 2 into recall of List 1, consistent with reminder-dependent updating.

## Sederberg et al. (2011) — temporal-context alternative

- PMID: 21512839
- PMCID: PMC3432313
- DOI: 10.3758/s13423-011-0086-9
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21512839/
- HF7 use: demonstrates that behavioral reminder/updating patterns can be modeled
  with a temporal-context account, providing direct theoretical pressure against
  treating one behavioral result as unique proof of cellular reconsolidation.

### HF7 inference

```text
Reactivation
can alter later expression / content integration
```

but:

```text
BehavioralUpdatingEffect != UniqueReconsolidationMechanismProof
```

# 9. False memory: retrieval is constructive and fallible

## Roediger & McDermott (1995)

- Citation: *Creating False Memories: Remembering Words Not Presented in Lists*.
- Journal: J Exp Psychol Learn Mem Cogn 21(4):803–814.
- DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.21.4.803
- HF7 use: semantically related word lists produced frequent false recall and
  high-confidence false recognition of a nonpresented critical lure.

## Stadler, Roediger & McDermott (1999) norms

- PMID: 10355238
- DOI: 10.3758/bf03211543
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10355238/
- HF7 use: false-memory rates varied widely across semantic lists, showing that
  reconstructive error is stimulus/association dependent rather than one fixed
  propensity.

### HF7 inference

```text
SubjectiveRecollection != HistoricalVeridicality
HighConfidence != TruthGuarantee
SuccessfulRecall != LiteralReplay
```

### Boundary

Word-list false-memory paradigms do not license direct inference about contested
real-world autobiographical or trauma memories. Domain transfer must be separately
validated.

# 10. Prospective memory

## Chasteen, Park & Schwarz (2001)

- PMID: 11760131
- DOI: 10.1111/1467-9280.00385
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11760131/
- HF7 use: implementation intentions improved prospective-memory performance in
  older adults under the experiment.

## Henry et al. (2020)

- PMID: 32730052
- DOI: 10.1037/pag0000563
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32730052/
- HF7 use: different implementation-intention/imagery strategies and cue type were
  tested in younger and older adults, showing prospective memory depends on cue and
  strategy architecture rather than retained retrospective content alone.

### HF7 inference

```text
RememberContent
!= RememberToAct
```

Prospective memory requires retaining an intention plus detecting/using a future
cue and initiating the action.

# 11. Cognitive offloading / external memory

## Gilbert (2015) — delayed intentions

- PMID: 25404057
- PMCID: PMC4448673
- DOI: 10.1080/17470218.2014.972963
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25404057/
- HF7 use: participants strategically altered the environment to reduce the burden
  of remembering delayed intentions.

## Gilbert et al. (2020) — reminder use

- PMID: 31448938
- DOI: 10.1037/xge0000652
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31448938/
- HF7 use: reminder decisions reflected objective memory ability, metacognitive
  confidence and reminder cost; people could over- or under-use external support.

## Current prospective/retrospective offloading experiment (2026)

- PMID: 42352705
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42352705/
- HF7 use: reminder/scheduling conditions improved prospective-task success while
  retrospective recall of contextual video content did not differ significantly in
  the reported experiment.

### HF7 inference

```text
ExternalReminderSuccess
!= InternalRetrospectiveMemoryGain
```

and:

```text
JointMemoryCapability
can increase without identical change in InternalRetention
```

Externalization changes the causal architecture of remembering.

# 12. Aging: memory processes dissociate

## Fabiani & Friedman (1997)

- PMID: 9025117
- DOI: 10.1016/S0028-3932(96)00073-5
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9025117/
- HF7 use: older adults showed marked impairment in temporal-order/recency memory
  while recognition for the pictorial stimuli was relatively preserved in the
  experiment.

## Bastin & Van der Linden (2003)

- PMID: 12597069
- HF7 use: recollection/familiarity contributions to recognition varied with age
  and test format.

## Millar & Balota (2022)

- PMID: 36552069
- PMCID: PMC9775546
- DOI: 10.3390/brainsci12121609
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36552069/
- HF7 use: wakeful rest improved later free recall but not recognition in their
  younger/older-adult paradigm, another direct dissociation between memory tests.

### HF7 inference

```text
AgingEffect_recall
!= AgingEffect_recognition
!= AgingEffect_temporal-order
!= AgingEffect_recollection
```

`Memory decline` without process/task qualification is too coarse.

# 13. Savings / relearning as hidden persistence

HF7 retains Ebbinghaus's historical `savings` logic as a method rather than one
mechanism:

```text
performance appears lost
+
relearning is faster than initial acquisition
→ evidence of retained history not visible in current unaided performance
```

Modern methodological discussion:

- PMID: 36069971
- PMCID: PMC9971077
- DOI: 10.3758/s13423-022-02172-3
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36069971/

HF7 uses savings as a falsifier of:

```text
CurrentPerformanceBaseline
= CompleteErasure
```

not as proof of one storage location.

# 14. Source-level synthesis

The cross-source result is:

```text
Memory != CurrentRecall
Encoding != Retention
Retention != Retrieval
Retrieval != Expression
Expression != Truth
Confidence != Veridicality

Retrieval != PassiveMeasurement
RetrievalPracticeBenefit_A can coexist with RetrievalInducedForgetting_B

ExplicitRecall != SkillMemory
Recognition != Priming
Recognition != Recollection
Familiarity != Recollection

Cue/Context/State can modulate accessibility
StateMismatch != StorageLoss
ContextMismatch != StorageLoss

ExtinctionPerformance != Erasure
ForgettingBehavior != PhysicalDeletion

Reactivation can update later memory expression
UpdatingEffect != UniqueReconsolidationMechanismProof

RememberingContent != RememberingSource
RememberContent != RememberToAct

ExternalMemorySupport != InternalMemoryGain
JointMemoryCapability != IndependentInternalMemory

Aging_D != Aging_E across memory processes
```

The repeated residual is **what the remembered content is and how it relates to
truth, source, concept, belief and knowledge**. False memories, semantic
associations, skill without recollection, source/order failures, prospective
intentions and generalization all require a representation/knowledge layer that
HF7 should expose rather than silently assume.
