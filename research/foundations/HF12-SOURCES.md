# HF12 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF12 is a technical foundation round, so this ledger privileges **primary empirical
studies** rather than treating reviews or philosophical taxonomies as empirical
authority.

Claims distinguish where relevant:

```text
co-presence / one-way influence / reciprocal interaction
actual reciprocity / believed live-social context
parallel action / coordination / synchrony / joint action
individual goal / joint outcome criterion / shared goal
joint intention / joint commitment / persistence
signal / message / interpretation / grounding / repair
common-ground history / common-ground estimate / actual overlap
subtask / role / authority / responsibility
coordination / cooperation / competition / prosociality
trust report / reliance / compliance / monitoring / dependence
individual capability / nominal aggregation / interaction synergy / joint capability
tool / delegation / supervision / collaboration / teaming
```

A relation measured on one surface is never treated as proof of all neighboring
social constructs.

# 1. Reciprocal interaction and mutual adaptation

## Konvalinka et al. (2010) — continuous mutual prediction and adaptation

- PMID: 20694920
- DOI: 10.1080/17470218.2010.497843
- Use: pairs performed joint tapping while hearing another person or computer.
  Bidirectionally coupled partners became mutually adaptive rather than behaving as
  two independent followers.

### HF12 inference

```text
BidirectionalCoupling != TwoParallelUnidirectionalResponses
MutualAdaptation != IndividualTrackingOnly
```

## Demos et al. (2017) — responsive versus prerecorded music partner

- PMID: 28261123
- PMCID: PMC5306131
- DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00149
- Use: live performance permitted bidirectional adaptation whereas prerecorded
  partner performance forced unidirectional coordination; asynchrony increased under
  the unresponsive condition.

### HF12 inference

```text
LiveReciprocity changes coordination capability
UnresponsivePartner != ResponsivePartner despite similar signal content
```

## Era et al. (2018) — causal perturbation of mutual adjustment

- PMID: 29660090
- PMCID: PMC6007351
- DOI: 10.1093/scan/nsy022
- Use: brain stimulation to one member impaired complementary mutual adaptation in
  a dyadic grasping task, with compensation depending on pair-level adaptation
  skills.

### HF12 inference

```text
DyadicPerformance depends on reciprocal adaptation
OneMemberPerturbation can be compensated at dyad level
```

## Fairhurst et al. virtual partner interaction / coordination dynamics

- PMID: 19492044
- Use: reciprocal Human–virtual-partner coupling produced stable, intermittent and
  newly discovered collective movement strategies not specified in instructions.

### HF12 inference

```text
ReciprocalCoupling can create emergent joint dynamics
```

- PMID: 36182055

# 3. Synchrony is neither necessary nor sufficient for joint action/shared goal

## spontaneous interpersonal synchrony without instructed shared goal

- PMID: 36852275
- PMCID: PMC9958360
- Use: spontaneous cross-person movement synchrony emerged under visual contact
  without an explicit joint motor task.

### HF12 inference

```text
BehavioralSynchrony != SharedGoal
Synchrony != JointAction by definition
```

## Mogan, Bulbulia & Fischer (2019)

- PMID: 30697183
- PMCID: PMC6340984
- DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02790
- Use: synchrony/asynchrony and shared-group versus individual goal were manipulated
  separately. Shared-goal effects were not reducible to synchrony.

### HF12 inference

```text
SharedGoal != Synchrony
Synchrony and goal relation are separable causal dimensions
```

## Wallot et al. — complex production beyond synchrony

- PMID: 27997558
