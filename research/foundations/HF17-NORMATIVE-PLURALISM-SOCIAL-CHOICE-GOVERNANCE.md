# HF17 — Normative Pluralism, Moral Uncertainty, Incommensurability, Social Choice, Collective Decision, Voting, Deliberation and Robust Governance

## 0. Status and question

HF16 established:

```text
AggregationAcrossBearers
!= AggregationAcrossPreferences
!= AggregationAcrossNormativeTheories
!= LegitimateCollectiveChoice
```

HF17 asks:

> **When people, groups and moral theories disagree about what should be done, how can
> a collective form an actionable decision without treating preference, majority,
> consensus, expertise, one social-choice theorem or one AI-mediated summary as moral
> truth or legitimate authority by definition?**

HF17 does not choose majority rule, plurality, Borda, approval, Condorcet methods,
randomization, deliberation, consensus, technocracy, epistocracy, expected moral
choiceworthiness or constitutional rights absolutism as foundation truth.

# 238. Competing collective-choice models

## M1 — simple majority

### Strength

Transparent, anonymous/neutral under binary conditions, responsive and widely
understood.

### Pressure

Multi-alternative cycles, strategic/agenda effects, minority rights, information
quality and legitimacy beyond counting.

### Disposition

Retain as procedure family, not truth criterion.

## M2 — Condorcet/epistemic majority

### Strength

Can aggregate independent weakly competent truth judgments extremely effectively in
classical conditions.

### Pressure

Competence/correlation/truth-object assumptions; value conflicts are not binary truth
tasks.

### Disposition

Retain as epistemic model only with assumptions explicit.

## M3 — scoring/plurality/approval procedures

### Strength

Operationally resolve multi-option choice with different information demands.

### Pressure

Each has distinct strategic, expressive and spoiler/agenda properties.

### Disposition

Retain as procedure families; no universal winner.

## M4 — Arrow-style ordinal social ordering

### Strength

Provides a precise language for collective preference aggregation and property
trade-offs.

### Pressure

Universal domain/IIA/Pareto/non-dictatorship tension; ordinal input limitations.

### Disposition

Use theorem as boundary map, not anti-democracy conclusion.

## M5 — strategy-proof revelation

### Strength

Makes incentive compatibility explicit.

### Pressure

Gibbard-Satterthwaite on unrestricted deterministic multi-option domains.

### Disposition

Retain as design desideratum with domain restrictions explicit; directly exposes
mechanism-design residual.

## M6 — rights-constrained collective choice

### Strength

Protects domains/claims from ordinary majoritarian override.

### Pressure

Sen-style Pareto/liberty conflicts; rights scope/threshold/interpretation contested.

### Disposition

Retain with typed rights and constitutional authority.

## M7 — judgment aggregation

### Strength

Handles logically connected collective propositions/reasons rather than only option
rankings.

### Pressure

Discursive/doctrinal paradox and systematicity/independence trade-offs.

### Disposition

Retain as distinct problem family.

## M8 — deliberative transformation

### Strength

Allows information gain, perspective taking, reason exchange and preference revision.

### Pressure

Design, power, polarization, conformity and persuasion effects.

### Disposition

Retain as intervention, not neutral aggregation.

## M9 — consensus

### Strength

Can reduce coercive loser/winner structure and create stable shared commitments.

### Pressure

Coercion, conformity, minority erasure, veto power, high transaction costs.

### Disposition

Use qualifier-required consensus, never truth identity.

## M10 — epistocracy/expert rule

### Strength

Makes domain knowledge/accuracy salient.

### Pressure

Who defines expertise, bias, domain limits, equal standing and authorization.

### Disposition

Separate advisory epistemic weight from political authority.

## M11 — moral-uncertainty expected choiceworthiness

### Strength

Integrates theory credences when scales are sufficiently structured/comparable.

### Pressure

Ordinal theories, scale normalization, intertheoretic comparison and fanaticism.

### Disposition

Use conditionally, not universal default.

## M12 — incomplete/plural robust choice

### Strength

Preserves genuine incomparability and seeks cross-theory dominance/low regret.

### Pressure

Can leave unresolved choices while institutions must act.

### Disposition

Retain with revision/reversibility/appeal.

## M13 — AI-mediated deliberation

### Strength

Scalable synthesis, minority incorporation and common-ground generation demonstrated
in controlled large-scale studies.

### Pressure

Persuasion, factuality, agenda, compression, representativeness, hidden objective and
legitimacy risks.

### Disposition

AI may facilitate; authority must remain explicitly assigned.
