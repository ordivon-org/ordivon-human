# Human Foundations Continuation after HF1

## HF1 completed result

HF1 rejects untyped identity and replaces it with projection-relative continuity:

```text
Same_K(x_t1, x_t2)
```

where `K` declares what persistence is being asserted.

The default Human research referent is:

```text
HumanIndividual
≈ one biologically continuing Homo sapiens organism
```

This is descriptive biological tracking, not a moral/legal personhood test.

## Exact next foundation

HF1 cannot complete its `Subject` boundary without decomposing:

```text
Experience
Consciousness
Awareness
Wakefulness
Connectedness
Responsiveness

```

Sleep and anaesthesia directly falsify their equivalence, and body-ownership,
agency, infancy and amnesia show why no single behavioural/report/memory surface
can substitute for experience.

Therefore the next round is:

# HF2 — Experience, Consciousness, Awareness, Wakefulness, Connectedness, Responsiveness, Reportability and Recall

## HF2 starting questions

1. Is `experience` the broadest operational term, or does it already assume a
   theory of consciousness?
2. How do consciousness state/level, conscious contents and access differ?
3. What is `awareness`, and when is the word merely relabeling consciousness?
4. Why can wakefulness and conscious contents dissociate?
5. What exactly is connected consciousness under anaesthesia?
6. Which behaviours require consciousness, and which can occur without it?
7. When does reportability fail despite possible experience?
8. How does later recall differ from contemporaneous experience?
9. What can first-person report establish that neural/behavioural evidence cannot,
   and vice versa?
10. How should uncertainty about consciousness be represented rather than
    collapsed into a binary label?
11. How does HF2 connect to Media MF2 perception without equating perception and
    consciousness?
12. What boundaries matter for nonverbal Humans, infants and impaired
    responsiveness without turning one test into a personhood gate?

## Candidate HF2 falsifiers

- wakeful but reduced/unreportable awareness conditions;
- NREM reports of experience and NREM reports of no experience;
- REM without later dream report;
- anaesthesia command response without later recall;
- neurophysiological patterns that do not perfectly predict response;
- nonverbal infant experience inference;
- perception/action dissociations where mature evidence supports them;
- disorders-of-consciousness evidence, only with careful measurement and ethical
  boundaries.

## Do not precommit

HF1 does not establish that:

- report is necessary for consciousness;
- report is sufficient for infallible access to experience;
- wakefulness equals consciousness;
- responsiveness equals consciousness;
- memory is required for contemporaneous experience;
- one EEG/fMRI/behavioural metric is a consciousness detector;
- one current scientific theory of consciousness is the final ontology;
- consciousness level is one scalar variable;
- lack of evidence of consciousness proves absence of experience;
- uncertainty about consciousness authorizes removal of personhood or rights.

## Stop rule

Do not schedule HF3 now. HF2 must expose a neighboring Human distinction whose
absence repeatedly breaks explanations across materially different cases.
