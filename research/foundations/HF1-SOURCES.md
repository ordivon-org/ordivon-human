# HF1 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF1 does not search for one authority that defines `Human`, `Self`, `Person` or
`Agent`. It uses primary empirical research, official biological/legal sources
and original theoretical works as **boundary pressure tests**.

A source may establish an experimental dissociation, an operational definition,
a legal use, or a competing theory without being allowed to define the complete
Human ontology.

---

# 1. Human biological membership and organism individuality

## NCBI Taxonomy — Homo sapiens

- Authority: US National Center for Biotechnology Information Taxonomy.
- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?id=9606
- HF1 use: `Homo sapiens` provides a taxonomic biological referent for `Human`.
- Boundary: taxonomy identifies species membership; it does not define
  consciousness, selfhood, personhood, agency, rights or moral status.

## Bich (2024) — physiological integration and biological individuality

- Citation: Leonardo Bich, *Integrating Multicellular Systems: Physiological
  Control and Degrees of Biological Individuality*, Acta Biotheoretica 72, 1.
- DOI: 10.1007/s10441-023-09476-4
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10441-023-09476-4
- HF1 use: physiological individuality depends on organized integration,
  interdependence, control and coordination among components contributing to
  maintenance of the system. The paper also emphasizes that biological
  individuality admits several criteria and, in general biology, degrees.
- Boundary: HF1 does not infer that ordinary adult human-organism identity is
  itself vague in every practical context. The source instead falsifies the
  simplistic rule `organism = exact material enclosed by skin`.

## HF1 biological inference boundary

HF1 uses the working descriptive convention:

```text
HumanIndividual
≈ a biologically continuing Homo sapiens organism
```

This is a tracking convention for Human research, not a definition of moral or
legal personhood and not a settlement of disputed beginning/end-of-life cases.

# 2. Consciousness, sleep, anaesthesia and responsiveness

## Siclari et al. — neural correlates of dreaming / conscious experience in sleep

- Open literature family: serial-awakening and high-density EEG studies of
  conscious experience during sleep.
- Example record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462120/
- HF1 use: conscious experience can occur in both REM and NREM sleep, while some
  awakenings yield no reported experience. Behavioural sleep stage therefore
  does not map one-to-one onto the presence or contents of consciousness.
- Boundary: retrospective report is evidence about experience, not perfect
  access to every preceding conscious state.

## Massimini et al. (2005) — cortical effective connectivity during sleep

- Citation: *Breakdown of cortical effective connectivity during sleep*, Science
  309(5744):2228–2232.
- DOI: 10.1126/science.1117256
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16195466/
- HF1 use: perturbational evidence shows that effective cortical interactions
  change substantially across wake/sleep state transitions while the same Human
  organism is tracked across those states.
- Boundary: a neural connectivity pattern is not a Human-identity test and does
  not by itself establish the presence or absence of experience.

## Sanders et al. (2017) — connected consciousness during intended general anaesthesia

- Citation: *Incidence of Connected Consciousness after Tracheal Intubation: A
  Prospective, International, Multicenter Cohort Study of the Isolated Forearm
  Technique*, Anesthesiology 126(2).
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27984262/
- HF1 use: 12 of 260 participants (4.6%) gave an isolated-forearm response to
  command after intubation during intended general anaesthesia; five responders
  signalled pain. No participant reported explicit postoperative recall of the
  intraoperative event in the follow-up sample.
- Boundary: isolated-forearm responsiveness is an operational measure of
  connected consciousness, not a complete measure of all consciousness.

## Gaskell et al. (2017) — EEG pattern does not preclude volitional response

- Citation: *Frontal alpha-delta EEG does not preclude volitional response during
  anaesthesia: prospective cohort study of the isolated forearm technique*.
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29121278/
- HF1 use: a commonly observed frontal anaesthesia EEG pattern can coexist with
  volitional isolated-forearm responses. A physiological proxy cannot therefore
  be treated as identical to connected conscious responsiveness.

## Russell et al. / young-adult multicentre cohort (2023)

- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10375493/
- HF1 use: a later prospective international cohort found command responses
  after intubation in 37/338 young adults (11%), again showing that intended
  anaesthesia, overt whole-body unresponsiveness and connected consciousness are
  not interchangeable descriptions.
- Boundary: cohort-specific incidence is not a universal anaesthesia rate.

## HF1 retained separation from this source family

```text
Wakefulness
!= behavioural responsiveness
!= connected consciousness
!= conscious experience
!= reportability
!= later recall
```

and none of those is identical to Human-organism identity.

# 3. Development of explicit body/self representation

## Brownell et al. — body self-awareness in toddlers

- Citation: *“So Big”: The Development of Body Self-awareness in Toddlers*.
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3351035/
- HF1 use: 57 children at 18, 22 and 26 months performed tasks requiring an
  explicit objective representation of their own body. Errors declined with age
  and most children still made at least one error. The authors interpret
  objective body self-awareness as emerging gradually in this period.
- Boundary: failure on a body-awareness task does not establish absence of all
  forms of bodily self-processing, consciousness, agency or personhood.

## Filippetti & Tsakiris (2018) — before explicit mirror self-recognition

- Citation: *Just Before I Recognize Myself: The Role of Featural and
  Multisensory Cues Leading up to Explicit Mirror Self-Recognition*.
- DOI: 10.1111/infa.12236
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6001620/
- HF1 use: among 18-month-old infants, explicit mirror-test recognition was not
  universal; nonrecognizers nevertheless showed differential attention to their
  own facial features. This pressure-tests the idea that mirror-test success is
  the birth of a unitary Self.
- Boundary: mirror recognition is one operational measure of explicit
  self-recognition, not a universal selfhood or personhood criterion.

## Infant body-representation work

- Example primary record:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10462757/
- HF1 use: representations of bodies and body configuration have developmental
  trajectories across infancy. `Body representation` therefore cannot be
  assumed to be one fixed adult structure present in mature form from birth.

# 4. Autobiographical memory and self-continuity

## Grilli, Wank & Verfaellie — life stories in medial temporal lobe amnesia

- Citation: *The Life Stories of Adults with Amnesia: Insights into the
  Contribution of the Medial Temporal Lobes to the Organization of
  Autobiographical Memory*.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.03.013
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5592132/
- HF1 use: medial-temporal-lobe amnesia disrupts important aspects of
  autobiographical memory organization and temporal relations among life
  chapters. Autobiographical/narrative self-organization can therefore change
  while the biological Human under study remains the same research participant.

## Grilli & Verfaellie — autobiographical facts in amnesia

- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4738052/
- HF1 use: autobiographical facts contribute to self-related cognition but are
  not supported by one unitary memory mechanism. Memory and self-representation
  are internally decomposable.

## Stable personality self-representation under severe anterograde amnesia

- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8907494/
- HF1 use: patients with severe anterograde amnesia maintained highly stable
  self-ratings over a year, while failing to update those self-ratings to reflect
  post-injury personality changes reported by caregivers. Maintaining a self
  representation and updating it can dissociate.
- Boundary: personality self-ratings are not the whole Self and caregiver
  reports are not ground-truth identity labels.

## Severe autobiographical-memory loss case evidence

- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7781873/
- HF1 use: near-total autobiographical-memory loss can coexist with retained
  procedural learning and ongoing organismic/social continuity. This is a strong
  counterexample to `same Human iff continuous autobiographical memory`.
- Boundary: individual case reports do not establish population prevalence or a
  complete identity theory.

# 5. Body ownership, agency and prosthetic embodiment

## Kalckert & Ehrsson (2012) — moving rubber hand

- Citation: *Moving a Rubber Hand that Feels Like Your Own: A Dissociation of
  Ownership and Agency*.
- DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3303087/
- HF1 use: passive movement abolished sense of agency while ownership remained;
  anatomically incongruent placement diminished ownership while agency remained.
  The experiment therefore supplies a direct double dissociation between
  experienced body ownership and sense of agency.
- Boundary: neither illusion changes biological parthood of the rubber hand.

## Robotic/prosthetic limb ownership and agency

- Primary literature shows that ownership and agency can be manipulated or
  reported for artificial effectors, including EMG-controlled robotic limbs.
- HF1 use: control of an artificial effector can contribute to agency without
  requiring biological incorporation.

## Bekrater-Bodmann (2020) — Prosthesis Embodiment Scale

- Citation: *Perceptual correlates of successful body–prosthesis interaction in
  lower limb amputees: psychometric characterisation and development of the
  Prosthesis Embodiment Scale*.
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7450092/
- HF1 use: data from 118 unilateral lower-limb prosthesis users supported a
  multidimensional structure including Ownership/Integrity, Agency and
  Anatomical Plausibility. `Embodiment` is therefore not one binary variable.
- Boundary: questionnaire factors operationalize phenomenology and do not settle
  organismal parthood or personal identity.

## HF1 retained separation from this family

```text
Biological parthood
!= body representation
!= body ownership
!= control locus
!= sense of agency
!= prosthetic embodiment
```

# 6. Tool coupling, cognitive offloading and extended cognition

## Clark & Chalmers (1998) — The Extended Mind

- Citation: Andy Clark & David Chalmers, *The Extended Mind*, Analysis 58:7–19.
- DOI: 10.1111/1467-8284.00096
- Publisher record:
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8284.00096
- HF1 use: original argument for active externalism and the possibility that
  tightly coupled external resources can be constitutive of some cognitive
  processes rather than merely causal aids.
- Boundary: this is a contested philosophical thesis. HF1 retains it as a rival
  model, not a universal empirical conclusion that ordinary tools become literal
  parts of the person.

## Gilbert (2015) and cognitive-offloading experiments

- Research family: externally setting reminders can improve intention
  fulfillment; external storage can improve task performance under memory load;
  offloading is sensitive to metacognitive confidence.
- Primary record: Sam J. Gilbert, *Strategic offloading of delayed intentions
  into the external environment*, DOI 10.1080/17470218.2014.972963.
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4448673/
- HF1 use: external representations can become functionally important parts of a
  task-performing cognitive system.
- Boundary: improved joint performance does not establish internal learning,
  biological parthood or personal-identity extension.

## Offloading feedback effects

- Recent experimental work also shows that reliance on an external store can
  alter what remains available in internal memory.
- HF1 use: tool coupling can feed back into the Human, so `external` does not mean
  causally irrelevant.
- Boundary: feedback does not collapse the distinction between internal state and
  coupled-system capability.

# 7. Actor and software Agent

## Wooldridge & Jennings (1995) — Intelligent Agents: Theory and Practice

- Original author-hosted record:
  https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/ker95/ker95-html.html
- Journal: Knowledge Engineering Review 10(2), 1995.
- HF1 use: the weak notion of an agent is explicitly applicable to hardware or
  software systems and emphasizes autonomy, social ability, reactivity and
  pro-activeness. Thus `Agent` is already a functional system role in mature AI
  literature and cannot be reserved as a synonym for Human.
- Boundary: stronger AI notions may add beliefs, desires, intentions and other
  mentalistic concepts; HF1 does not assume those attributions imply
  consciousness or personhood.

## Franklin & Graesser — agent taxonomy

- Original theoretical paper: *Is it an Agent, or just a Program? A Taxonomy for
  Autonomous Agents*.
- Author-hosted record:
  https://www.cs.memphis.edu/~franklin/AgentProg.html
- HF1 use: reinforces that `Agent` has multiple engineering taxonomies and is a
  role/concept for systems, not a species identifier.

# 8. Legal personhood and legal capacity

## 1 U.S.C. § 1 — statutory use of `person`

- Official/statutory text mirrored by Cornell Legal Information Institute:
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/1/1
- HF1 use: for Acts of Congress unless context indicates otherwise, the term
  `person` includes corporations, companies, associations, firms, partnerships,
  societies and joint stock companies as well as individuals. This supplies a
  direct counterexample to treating legal `person` as a universal synonym for a
  biological Human.
- Boundary: statutory interpretation in one jurisdiction is not a universal
  theory of moral or psychological personhood.

## UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 12

- Official UN record:
  https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-12-equal-recognition-before-the-law.html
- HF1 use: Article 12 states equal recognition as persons before the law and
  equal legal capacity, while also requiring access to support that may be
  needed to exercise legal capacity. Therefore `legal capacity` is not defined
  as unaided cognitive/action independence.
- Boundary: HF1 does not infer a complete metaphysical account of personhood from
  a rights convention. The source constrains the normative/legal boundary.

# 9. Historical competing model: Locke on man and person

## John Locke — An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Book II, Chapter XXVII

- Original-work public text:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Essay_Concerning_Human_Understanding/Book_II/Chapter_XXVII
- HF1 use: Locke explicitly separates identity of a living `man` from identity of
  a `person`, and links personal identity to consciousness. This is historically
  important because it makes the sortal/identity question explicit.
- Boundary: HF1 treats Locke as a competing theory, not settled empirical fact.
  Sleep, anaesthesia, amnesia, development and modern rights law expose reasons
  not to collapse all Human persistence into conscious-memory continuity.

# 10. Source-level synthesis

The cross-source evidence does not select one universal persistence relation.
It instead supports several durable separations:

```text
species membership != legal personhood
organismal continuity != exact material continuity
Human identity != current consciousness
consciousness != responsiveness != later recall
biological body != body representation != felt ownership
ownership != agency
reflective self-recognition != biological Human identity
autobiographical memory != organismal persistence
joint/tool-mediated capability != internal skill
cognitive coupling != personal-identity extension by default
Actor != Agent
Agent != Human
legal person != biological Human
legal capacity != unsupported independence
```

HF1 therefore treats `identity` as a **typed relation** rather than one scalar or
single universal equality predicate.
