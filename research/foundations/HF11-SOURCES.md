# HF11 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF11 studies the conversion of a selected policy/plan into realized situated
effect. Every claim therefore distinguishes, where relevant:

```text
action goal / intended effect
action selection
motor preparation / initiation
motor command / control policy
effector trajectory / movement
sensory state estimate
online feedback correction
task outcome / environmental effect
reward / utility consequence
sense of agency / report
skill / calibration history
tool / partner / autonomy allocation
```

The same task outcome may be produced by many movement configurations, and the same
movement can have different action meanings. Therefore outcome, movement and action
are never treated as interchangeable measurements.

# 1. Action-effect / ideomotor theory

## Shin, Proctor & Capaldi (2010)

- PMID: 20822210
- DOI: 10.1037/a0020541
- Use: major review of contemporary ideomotor theory. Anticipated perceptual effects
  can acquire control over actions after action-effect learning.

## Waszak et al. / binding in voluntary action control review

- PMID: 20036685
- DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.12.013
- Use: action planning can bind intended-effect codes and selected motor features.

## Hoffmann et al. (2009)

- PMID: 19526438
- DOI: 10.1080/17470210902922079
- Use: separates movement-related and target-related structure in action-effect
  learning; what constitutes the represented action is not trivial.

## Massen & Prinz (2009)

- PMID: 19620106
- PMCID: PMC2865071
- DOI: 10.1098/rstb.2009.0059
- Use: distinguishes movement, target and action-rule contributions in action and
  tool-use imitation.

## Vogel-Blaschka et al. (2024)

- PMID: 38346045
- DOI: 10.1037/rev0000460
- Use: contemporary computational action-effect framework; retained as one model
  family rather than final action ontology.

## mechanistic challenge to associative ideomotor account

- PMID: 35377704
- DOI: 10.1037/xhp0000994
- Use: rapid reversal/update of action-effect relations can also fit propositional/
  causal-inference accounts; simple bidirectional association is not uniquely
  identified by classic ideomotor effects.

## 2026 event-file synthesis

- PMID: 42347771
- DOI: 10.1037/rev0000634
- Use: current theoretical attempt to integrate stimulus-response and action-effect
  binding. Treated as an emerging synthesis, not settled ontology.

### HF11 inference

```text
Action can be represented by anticipated effects
but
Action != ActionEffectRepresentation
IdeomotorEffect != UniqueAssociativeMechanismProof
MovementRepresentation != Goal/TargetRepresentation
```

# 2. Volition, preparation and initiation

## Hallett (2007)

- PMID: 17466580
- PMCID: PMC1950571
- DOI: 10.1016/j.clinph.2007.03.019
- Use: movement initiation and conscious experience of willing can be manipulated/
  analyzed separately; volition is not a simple conscious trigger variable.

## Haggard / volition review (2017)

- PMID: 29118213
- Use: internally generated action requires separating spontaneity, conscious
  volition, goal-directedness and action generation.

## StartReact preparation review

- PMID: 22033029
- Use: startling stimuli can release prepared movements rapidly, demonstrating that
  motor preparation and movement release/initiation are separable stages.

## current readiness-potential review (2026)

- PMID: 42013765
- Use: readiness potential is a scoped neurophysiological marker influenced by
  movement/task methodology; it is not a direct scalar measure of free will or
  intention.

## voluntary-decision motor-preparation study (2026)

- PMID: 41924032
- PMCID: PMC13037662
- DOI: 10.1162/IMAG.a.1184
- Use: voluntary decisions and motor preparation can be tracked as interacting but
  distinct temporal processes.

### HF11 inference

```text
Intention != MotorPreparation
MotorPreparation != Initiation
Initiation != ConsciousSenseOfVolition
ReadinessPotential != Intention itself
```

# 3. Clinical dissociations: apraxia

## Heilman (2010)

- PMID: 22810515
- DOI: 10.1212/01.CON.0000368262.53662.08
- Use: ideational, conceptual, ideomotor, dissociation and limb-kinetic apraxias
  separate sequence knowledge, mechanical/tool knowledge and skilled production.

## cognitive rehabilitation/apraxia review

- PMID: 22324430
- Use: limb apraxia can occur without elementary sensory or motor-output deficit and
  contains automatic-voluntary dissociations.

## progressive ideomotor apraxia case

- PMID: 7772334
- Use: preserved conceptual/tool-action knowledge alongside severe spatiotemporal
  movement-production deficits.

## ideomotor apraxia goal-directed movement study

- PMID: 16298401
- Use: impaired gesture imitation can coexist with relatively preserved visually
  guided goal-directed movement; no-vision aiming reveals increased dependence on
  online visual information.

### HF11 inference

```text
ActionConceptKnowledge != SkilledActionProduction
GestureImitation != GoalDirectedExecution
ElementaryStrength != PraxisCapability
```

# 4. Clinical dissociations: initiation / freezing / akinesia

## Parkinson freezing/inhibitory-control study

- PMID: 26354102
- DOI: 10.1007/s00702-015-1454-9
- Use: freezing concerns failures to initiate/continue movement and interacts with
  inhibitory-control demands.

## gait-freezing management review

- PMID: 26018593
- DOI: 10.1016/S1474-4422(15)00041-1
- Use: freezing episodes often arise during gait initiation/turning; intention to
  walk does not guarantee effective stepping.

## preparatory postural adjustment intervention

- PMID: 33129334
- Use: increasing preparatory weight shift can rescue otherwise unsuccessful step
  initiation in Parkinson freezing.

## current neural-dynamics study

- PMID: 38954651
- Use: dopamine/DBS can accelerate neural dynamics preceding self-initiated
  movements; initiation latency is a mechanistic variable, not simply motivation.

## gait-initiation plurality study (2024)

- PMID: 39088931
- DOI: 10.1016/j.gaitpost.2024.07.296
- Use: multiple motor/cognitive/limbic components contribute to impaired gait
  initiation.

### HF11 inference

```text
Commitment/Intention != MovementInitiation
InitiationFailure != LackOfGoal by definition
Initiation != Continuation
```

# 7. Predictive and feedback motor control

## Shadmehr et al. / error correction and forward models

- PMID: 20367317
- Use: delayed/noisy feedback and changing body/environment mappings motivate
  internal predictive models combined with sensory state estimation/adaptation.

## functional taxonomy of sensory feedback

- PMID: 27378546
- Use: sensory feedback enters motor control at multiple latency/function levels;
  voluntary/reflexive control boundaries blur in goal-directed action.

## optimal feedback control systematic review

- PMID: 34920414
- DOI: 10.1123/mc.2021-0041
- Use: optimal-feedback-control/minimal-intervention ideas explain useful phenomena
  but generalization is limited by task homogeneity; retained as a major model
  family, not final ontology.

## adaptive feedback control in force-field learning

- PMID: 35027886
- PMCID: PMC8751623
- DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2021.742608
- Use: feedforward and feedback adaptation need not be independent parallel systems;
  adaptive feedback can explain online corrections and trial-by-trial changes.

### HF11 inference

```text
MotorControl != FeedbackOnlyServo
MotorControl != FeedforwardOpenLoopProgram
Feedforward != Feedback
but both can be coupled/adaptive
```

# 10. Error signals are typed

## sensory versus reward prediction error

- PMID: 21423711
- PMCID: PMC3053313
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002012
- Use: sensory and reward prediction errors can drive different components of
  motor adaptation/learning.

## prediction-based adaptation error

- PMID: 21123665
- PMCID: PMC3074418
- DOI: 10.1152/jn.00394.2010
- Use: adaptation can follow predicted-versus-observed movement outcome rather than
  final retinal error alone.

## task-error-driven savings

- PMID: 32029533
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1506-19.2020
- Use: strategic task-error correction can form memories that improve later
  adaptation even when classic sensory-prediction-error accounts are insufficient.

## motor-learning interaction perspective

- PMID: 35058767
- Use: sensory-prediction-error, explicit and reinforcement components interact and
  should not be treated as independent black boxes.

## 2026 perceptual prediction error model

- PMID: 41985001
- PMCID: PMC13102310
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014196
- Use: current evidence proposes perceptual movement-prediction error rather than
  visual performance error as a parsimonious driver of implicit adaptation; treated
  as current rival refinement.

### HF11 inference

```text
Sensory/PerceptualPredictionError != TaskError
TaskError != RewardPredictionError
RewardPredictionError != MovementPredictionError
```

# 13. Automaticity

## Wulf, McNevin & Shea (2001)

- PMID: 11765737
- DOI: 10.1080/713756012
- Use: external versus internal attentional focus changes dual-task cost and motor
  automaticity in a balance task.

## automaticity neural study

- PMID: 15930384
- PMCID: PMC6725010
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3880-04.2005
- Use: extensive practice reduces dual-task interference and changes neural
  recruitment; automaticity is operationally separable from raw movement success.

## implicit-versus-explicit automatization systematic review

- PMID: 30183763
- PMCID: PMC6124806
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0203591
- Use: implicit-learning interventions do not uniformly produce superior
  automatization; evidence is mixed and methodology-sensitive.

## 2026 motor sequence study

- PMID: 41844636
- DOI: 10.1038/s41539-026-00412-y
- Use: extensive sequence learning can coexist with persistent/increased dual-task
  attentional costs in some conditions, falsifying a simple practice→automaticity
  monotonic law.

### HF11 inference

```text
Automaticity != Skill
Practice != AutomaticityGuarantee
Automaticity != AbsenceOfAgency
DualTaskCost is evidence, not ontology
```

# 27. Strong residual: from coordination to social interaction

## shared-goal/joint-action perspective

- PMID: 26283986
- PMCID: PMC4519671
- Use: a shared goal distinguishes reciprocal joint interaction from merely acting
  alongside another person in many experimental contexts.

## spontaneous interpersonal coordination systematic review

- PMID: 34707533
- Use: spontaneous mutual attunement and explicit goal-oriented joint action are
  often conflated and must be separated.

## predictive joint-action/communication framework (2026)

- PMID: 41831389
- DOI: 10.1016/j.plrev.2026.03.001
- Use: emerging model of joint action/communication through predictive multi-agent
  models and dynamic role allocation; retained as a current rival, not final
  theory.

### HF11 residual

In one body, coordination can often be modeled around a single task goal/control
architecture. In joint action, each agent can possess distinct:

```text
beliefs / percepts
goals and subgoals
attention
capabilities
roles
authority
communication channels
```

and can strategically signal to, predict, misunderstand or defect from another.
Sensorimotor coordination alone cannot explain those relations.

This repeatedly exposes a new foundation boundary around:

```text
SocialInteraction
JointAction
Communication
SharedGoal / CommonGround
Role
Cooperation
```

rather than another motor-control taxonomy.
