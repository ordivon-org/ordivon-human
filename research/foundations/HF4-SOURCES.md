# HF4 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF4 studies terms that are especially vulnerable to circular explanation:

```text
He did it because he was motivated.
How do we know he was motivated?
Because he did it.
```

The source ledger therefore privileges **experimental dissociations** over
self-labels. A task can operationalize willingness to work, effort cost, outcome
sensitivity, goal maintenance, reward learning, confidence or subjective affect,
but none becomes `motivation itself`.

---

# 1. Official construct pressure: NIMH RDoC Positive Valence Systems

## Current RDoC structure

- Authority: US National Institute of Mental Health.
- Positive Valence Systems separates:
  - Reward Responsiveness;
  - Reward Learning;
  - Reward Valuation.
- Reward Valuation further separates probability/ambiguity, delay and effort.
- URLs:
  - https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/definitions-of-the-rdoc-domains-and-constructs
  - https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/positive-valence-systems
  - https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/reward-valuation
  - https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/effort

### HF4 use

The framework itself is a useful anti-collapse: reward receipt, reward learning,
prospective valuation, delay and effort are not one construct. Historical RDoC
workshops explicitly debated whether `motivation` was too cross-cutting to be one
positive-valence construct and reorganized the domain to reduce construct overlap.

### Boundary

RDoC is a research framework for mental-health science, not a complete Human
ontology. HF4 uses its separations as pressure tests only.

# 2. Reward prediction and reinforcement learning

## Pessiglione et al. (2006) — dopamine-dependent prediction errors in humans

- Citation: *Dopamine-dependent prediction errors underpin reward-seeking
  behaviour in humans*, Nature 442:1042–1045.
- PMID: 16929307
- PMCID: PMC2636869
- DOI: 10.1038/nature05051
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636869/

### HF4 use

Pharmacological manipulation altered striatal reward-prediction-error signals and
subsequent reward-seeking choices. This supports a learning-signal role distinct
from simple consummatory pleasure.

### Boundary

Dopamine is not therefore `reward`, `pleasure` or `motivation` in one universal
sense. The experiment concerns one learning/action-selection mechanism.

## Colas et al. (2017) — distinct state/action prediction errors

- PMID: 29049406
- HF4 use: even within reinforcement-learning accounts, value and prediction error
  can be typed by state/action architecture rather than represented as one scalar
  signal with one role.

# 3. Wanting, liking and learning

## Hobbs, Remington & Glautier (2005) — alcohol wanting/liking

- PMID: 15517194
- DOI: 10.1007/s00213-004-2026-0
- HF4 use: alcohol priming increased wanting without increasing liking; adulterating
  alcohol reduced liking without a corresponding reduction in wanting.
- Boundary: operational measures are imperfect and substance-specific; the result
  supports dissociation, not one universal two-system model.

## Finlayson, King & Blundell (2007) — food wanting/liking

- PMID: 17052736
- DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2006.08.020
- HF4 use: hungry versus satiated state altered wanting and liking in partially
  dissociable ways across food categories.

## Berridge & Robinson / reward-component literature

- Review record: PMID 19162544, PMCID PMC2756052.
- Human measurement review: PMID 26851575.
- HF4 use: distinguishes hedonic impact (`liking`), incentive salience (`wanting`)
  and learned prediction/cognition.
- Boundary: human operationalizations often confound expected pleasantness with
  wanting or liking, so HF4 does not treat questionnaire labels as process truth.

## Depression reward-effort dissociation

- PMID: 21842963
- HF4 use: consummatory liking and willingness to exert effort can dissociate,
  reinforcing `pleasure != action allocation`.

# 4. Effort-based decision making

## Treadway et al. (2009) — EEfRT

- PMID: 19672310
- PMCID: PMC2720457
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0006598
- HF4 use: operationalizes willingness to choose harder actions for larger or more
  probable rewards; trait anhedonia related to reduced willingness to expend
  effort.
- Boundary: EEfRT measures one form of effort-based choice, not total motivation.

## Hartmann et al. / effort versus delay discounting

- PMID: 25816114
- HF4 use: physical effort and temporal delay produce dissociable reward
  devaluation profiles in human choice.

## effort-discounting model comparison

- PMID: 28759631
- PMCID: PMC5536267
- HF4 use: physical and cognitive effort discount prospective rewards, with model
  form depending on task/data; there is no foundation justification for assuming
  one universal effort-cost function.

## Lim et al. (2016) — reward and sustained attention

- PMID: 27498294
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2016.07.019
- HF4 use: higher reward increased sustained-attention performance and pupil-indexed
  effort, while participants also discounted rewards by vigilance duration.
- Boundary: reward can justify more effort; it does not erase effort cost.

## Lin et al. (2024) — learning to value effort

- DOI: 10.1038/s41562-024-01842-7
- HF4 use: rewarding effortful choices produced limited/mixed transfer to later
  unrewarded effort preferences. Effort valuation can be learned but transfer is
  not automatic.

## effort can alter later reward evaluation

- PMID: 23202663
- PMID: 24308956
- HF4 use: effort can sometimes increase sensitivity to or subsequent valuation of
  obtained outcomes. Therefore `effort` is not universally only a subtractive cost.

# 5. Internal state changes valuation and effort

## fasting / satiation

- PMID: 24454949
- PMCID: PMC3894194
- HF4 use: 24-hour fasting altered wanting, liking, relative reinforcing value and
  intake compared with a fed state.

## sickness: effort sensitivity without reward-sensitivity change

- PMID: 28948979
- HF4 use: experimental endotoxemia altered effort-based willingness while reward
  sensitivity in the task was not comparably altered. This is a direct falsifier
  for one motivational scalar.

## sleep/state: wanting versus work output

- PMID: 28089706
- HF4 use: sleep deprivation could increase reported wanting while reducing
  work-for-reward output in the study paradigm.

## fatigue and physical effort choice

- PMID: 39713470
- HF4 use: cognitive fatigue increased the subjective cost of prospective physical
  effort in later choices.

### HF4 inference

```text
Same external reward
+ different internal state
→ different wanting / cost / effort allocation / choice
```

This strongly motivates the HF5 boundary around bodily regulation and internal
state.

# 6. Delay, uncertainty and current decision value

## Green et al. — two delayed rewards

- PMID: 16248754
- DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.31.5.1121
- HF4 use: preferences among sooner/smaller and later/larger rewards depend on
  delays; common temporal aspects alter choice weighting.

## delay and effort are dissociable

- PMID: 25816114
- HF4 use: delay and physical effort should not be treated as one generic `cost`.

## magnitude effects and experiential delay

- PMID: 26377438
- PMID: 24037826
- HF4 use: discounting varies with reward magnitude and task implementation.

## volatility as a component of delayed value

- PMID: 40162322
- HF4 use: learned volatility of delayed outcomes can alter intertemporal choice,
  showing that apparent impatience can contain uncertainty about the future.

## theoretical boundary

- PMID: 23356243
- HF4 use: even the standard hyperbolic account of preference reversal is not a
  settled universal explanation. HF4 retains context/time-dependent valuation
  without canonizing one discount function.

# 7. Goals, maintenance and implementation

## Kane & Engle (2003) — goal neglect

- PMID: 12656297
- DOI: 10.1037/0096-3445.132.1.47
- HF4 use: participants can know task rules yet fail to actively maintain the goal
  under contexts that encourage neglect.

## Iveson et al. (2017) — goal maintenance limits

- PMID: 28315594
- DOI: 10.1016/j.actpsy.2017.03.003
- HF4 use: goal neglect varies with instruction load, processing constraints and
  context; `having understood a goal` is not identical to `goal currently controls
  behaviour`.

## implementation intentions and prospective memory

- PMID: 11760131
- PMID: 20173194
- PMID: 20496982
- PMID: 21317315
- HF4 use: if-then implementation policies can improve future goal execution or
  weaken habitual response advantage without simply increasing goal value.
- Boundary: effects are context-dependent; implementation intentions do not make
  every response fully automatic or override weak goal commitment in every case.

## goal shielding through implementation intentions

- PMID: 18272806
- HF4 use: if-then plans can protect ongoing goal pursuit from cravings or
  disruptive internal states.

### HF4 inference

```text
GoalContent
!= GoalActivation
!= GoalMaintenance
!= GoalCommitment
!= ImplementationPolicy
!= GoalAttainment
```

# 8. Goal-directed and habitual action

## slips-of-action conflict

- PMID: 30310863
- HF4 use: learned stimulus-response tendencies and goal-directed outcome-sensitive
  control can conflict; cognitive control is an additional bottleneck.

## symmetrical outcome revaluation

- PMID: 35867208
- HF4 use: provides evidence for stimulus-driven inflexibility under some
  conditions, while longer training did not simply produce a monotonic behavioural
  flexibility loss.

## 2026 devaluation-method critique

- PMID: 42414812
- PMCID: PMC13342024
- DOI: 10.3758/s13428-026-03099-6
- HF4 use: apparent habit-like responding can arise when outcome devaluation itself
  is ineffective; this is a strong measurement warning.

### HF4 inference

HF4 retains `goal-directed` versus `habit-like` as useful control modes but does
not freeze a simple two-box ontology for all Human action.

# 9. Approach–avoidance conflict and affect

## Aupperle et al. (2011) — human approach–avoidance conflict task

- PMID: 21843556
- HF4 use: the same option can combine reward and aversive outcomes, producing
  measurable conflict rather than one positive/negative scalar.

## Bach et al. (2014) — human hippocampus in approach–avoidance conflict

- PMID: 24560572
- HF4 use: behavioural inhibition and avoidance adapt to threat level while reward
  approach remains in competition.

## conflict can itself generate avoidance tendency

- PMID: 25931151
- HF4 use: conflict has motivational consequences beyond the original reward or
  threat value.

## affect and reward history

- PMID: 33417595
- PMID: 34477879
- HF4 use: trial-by-trial reward/punisher outcomes can change reported affect and
  subsequent decision parameters, while affective valence/arousal do not map
  simply onto one choice tendency.

### HF4 inference

```text
PositiveValence != Approach
NegativeValence != Avoidance
Affect != Motivation
```

although affect can modulate valuation, attention and action allocation.

# 10. Intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, autonomy and control

## Vansteenkiste et al. (2004)

- PMID: 15301630
- HF4 use: experimentally manipulated intrinsic/extrinsic goal framing and
  autonomy-supportive/controlling context affected learning, performance and
  persistence; effects were mediated by autonomous motivation in that research
  program.

## Williams et al. (2006)

- PMID: 16448302
- HF4 use: randomized health-behaviour intervention linked autonomy support to
  greater autonomous and competence motivation and later cessation outcomes.

## control and effort costs

- PMID: 28515705
- HF4 use: actual control over effort costs had different motivational effects
  from merely illusory choice/control.

## competence-support field experiment

- PMID: 28730741
- HF4 use: competence-support manipulation changed competence satisfaction,
  intrinsic motivation and performance in a team-sport context.

## monetary reward undermining effect

- PMID: 21078974
- PMCID: PMC3000299
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1013305107
- HF4 use: performance-contingent monetary reward could reduce later free-choice
  task engagement and alter reward-related neural responses in an interesting task.

## meta-analytic boundary

- PMID: 10589297
- HF4 use: reward effects depend on reward type/contingency and outcome measure;
  tangible/expected contingent rewards can reduce free-choice intrinsic motivation
  in some conditions while positive feedback can enhance it.

### HF4 inference

```text
MoreExternalReward
!= monotonically MoreMotivation
```

and:

```text
ChoiceProvided
!= ExperiencedAutonomy
```

without evidence that choice changes actual control and motivational meaning.

# 11. Self-control, effort cost and precommitment

## Kool et al. / subjective self-control cost

- PMID: 34446546
- PMCID: PMC8536396
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2018726118
- HF4 use: participants were willing to pay to avoid situations requiring
  self-control, with costs scaling with temptation and changing with motivational
  incentives/stress.

## precommitment for effortful goals

- PMID: 30966912
- HF4 use: participants used precommitment to remove easier immediate alternatives,
  increasing attainment of effort-requiring larger rewards. Model comparison in
  that paradigm fit motivation-maximization better than a simple anticipated
  `willpower failure` account.

## deadline effort and opportunity cost

- PMID: 35511565
- HF4 use: effort can increase near deadlines despite prior fatigue; opportunity
  structure and alternative activities can explain dynamic effort allocation.

## competing self-control theories

- PMID: 36329599
- HF4 use: registered tests comparing limited-resource and motivational accounts
  produced results that did not cleanly validate a simple single-mechanism theory.

### HF4 inference

Self-control should not be canonized as an expendable substance. It is better
represented as a conflict-regulation problem involving current goals, alternatives,
costs, effort, strategies, state and environment.

# 12. Internal-state/allostatic boundary exposed by HF4

## Sterling (2012) — predictive regulation

- PMID: 21684297
- DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2011.06.004
- HF4 use: allostasis is retained as a competing regulatory model emphasizing
  anticipatory adjustment rather than simple fixed-point correction.

## Schulkin & Sterling (2019)

- PMID: 31488322
- HF4 use: review of allostasis as brain-centered predictive physiological
  regulation.

## motivational allostasis model

- PMID: 31788441
- HF4 use: proposes coupling of anticipated physiological needs, effort and action
  allocation; retained as a rival mechanism, not settled Human ontology.

## fatigue/state evidence

- PMID: 29738794
- PMID: 39713470
- PMID: 28948979
- HF4 use: fatigue and sickness alter willingness/effort valuation and sometimes
  cross from cognitive to physical effort domains.

### HF4 conclusion from this source family

HF4 repeatedly requires bodily-state variables to explain why the same external
outcome has a different current value or cost. This creates the next foundation
boundary rather than resolving it inside `Motivation`.

# 13. Source-level synthesis

The cross-source result is:

```text
Reward != Pleasure
Reward != Value
Reward != ReinforcementLearningSignal
Wanting != Liking
PredictionError != RewardReceipt
Goal != Intention != ImplementationPolicy
GoalKnowledge != GoalMaintenance
Goal != Habit
CurrentChoice != StablePreference
Preference != EndorsedValue
EffortDemand != SubjectiveEffort != EffortCost != EffortExpenditure
EffortCost != Fatigue
Effort != Motivation
Motivation != one scalar
PositiveAffect != Approach
NegativeAffect != Avoidance
SelfControl != fixed depletable resource by foundation assumption
SelfRegulation != successful outcome
Agency != goal attainment
```

The strongest residual is state regulation:

```text
Need / deprivation
Satiety
Interoception
Homeostasis / allostasis
Stress
Fatigue
Recovery
```

because these repeatedly transform wanting, reward responsiveness, effort cost,
priority and self-regulation even when the external goal is unchanged.
