# Human Foundations Continuation after HF15

## HF15 completed result

HF15 starts from HF14's bearer problem:

```text
Who can be harmed?
Who can have welfare?
Who can hold rights?
Who can bear duties?
Who can be responsible?
Who deserves direct moral consideration?
```

and establishes a typed standing architecture rather than one moral-worth scalar.

Minimum grammar:

```text
Entity E
  ↓
Descriptive identity / substrate / life / organization
  ↓
Capacity & evidence profile
 ├─ experience / valence / nociception
 ├─ agency / planning / control
 ├─ preferences / goals / valuing
 ├─ needs / functioning / capabilities
 ├─ dependence / vulnerability
 └─ social/legal relations
  ↓
Evidence layer
 report ≠ behavior ≠ physiology ≠ neural evidence ≠ legal status
  ↓
Candidate standing models F
 ├─ species/humanity
 ├─ life
 ├─ sentience
 ├─ interests/welfare
 ├─ rational/moral agency
 ├─ dignity/status
 ├─ relational/care
 └─ plural/layered
  ↓
StandingProfile_F(E)
 ├─ direct/derivative relevance
 ├─ moral patienthood
 ├─ moral agency/responsibility capacity
 ├─ rights eligibility
 ├─ duty eligibility
 ├─ legal personhood
 ├─ political membership
 └─ uncertainty
  ↓
ScopeOfConcern_F
  ↓
[HF16 boundary]
multiple bearers / generations / copies / possible persons
  ↓
aggregation / population / temporal choice
```

## Disorders of consciousness

Large multicenter and earlier command-imagery studies support:

```text
NoObservableCommandResponse != NoVolitionalCognitiveEvidence
```

but not:

```text
AllUnresponsivePatientsAreConscious
```

Therefore overt behavior is an imperfect evidence channel, not the standing
criterion.

## Infants

Noxious-evoked cortical activity, reflex and facial/behavioral measures can
systematically dissociate.

Retain:

```text
LackOfVerbalReport != LackOfNoxiousBrainEvidence
BehavioralPainScore != NociceptiveBrainActivity
ReducedPainBehavior != ReducedNociceptiveProcessing
```

Infant evidence is indirect evidence about pain/sentience; it is not direct
phenomenological access.

## Nonhuman animals


## Exact next foundation

HF15 can now answer more precisely:

```text
who/what may count,
in which role,
with what capacity evidence,
under which normative framework,
and with what uncertainty.
```

But it cannot answer:

```text
How do many claims combine?
How do number and severity trade off?
How should current and future bearers be compared?
What if choices change which people exist?
What if they change population size?
How should possible persons enter evaluation?
How should copied/forked digital bearers be counted?
Is replacement equivalent to survival?
What is lost in extinction beyond current-person death?
```

The decisive firewall is:

```text
WhoCounts
!= HowManyCount
!= HowClaimsCombine
!= WhichPopulationIsBetter
```

Therefore the exact next round is:

# HF16 — Population, Aggregation, Future Generations, Non-Identity, Creation, Replication and Existential Loss

## HF16 starting questions

1. What is aggregation relative to individual standing and claim weight?
2. How do welfare aggregation, rights aggregation and claim aggregation differ?
3. Can many small benefits outweigh one severe harm, and under which framework?
4. What is temporal discounting relative to pure time preference, uncertainty and
   opportunity cost?
5. What makes future generations morally relevant now?
6. What exactly is the non-identity problem?
7. What is a person-affecting principle relative to impersonal population value?
8. Can creating a good life count as a benefit, and to whom?
9. What is the normative status of non-creation?
10. How should population size enter evaluation?
11. How do identity-affecting policies compare populations containing different
    people?
12. How should digital copies, forks, pauses, merges and restorations be counted?
13. Does functional replacement compensate for death/loss?
14. What is extinction/existential loss relative to current-person harms?
15. How should deep uncertainty about future populations and standing be represented?
16. What next boundary emerges after population/temporal aggregation is rebuilt?

## Candidate HF16 falsifiers

- one severe harm versus thousands/millions of tiny benefits;
- present certain life versus many probabilistic future lives;
- same aggregate welfare with different distributions/population sizes;
- identity-affecting climate or reproductive policy;
- future population that would not have existed under the alternative;
- more barely-good lives versus fewer excellent lives;
- preventing a possible life versus killing an existing bearer;
- replacement of a dead bearer by an indistinguishable copy;
- fork one digital patient before distributing benefit/harm;
- merge/restoration with shared pasts;
- catastrophe killing current persons versus identical current deaths plus permanent
  extinction;
- temporal moral-circle bias toward near versus distant generations.

## Do not precommit

HF15 does not establish that:

- total welfare is the correct population objective;
- average welfare is correct;
- person-affecting ethics is correct or false;
- zero pure-time discount is required;
- future persons have exactly current-person claim structure;
- creating a good life equals benefiting an existing person;
- non-creation is harm;
- more lives are always better;
- fewer better lives are always better;
- copies count one-for-one;
- functional replacement cancels death;
- extinction matters only through current deaths;
- existential-risk priority can be derived without population assumptions.

## Stop rule

Do not schedule HF17 now. HF16 must expose a repeated neighboring distinction across
materially different population/temporal cases.
