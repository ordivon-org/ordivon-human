# HF22 — Persistent Relationships, Attachment, Care, Relational Models and Dyad-Specific Social State

## 0. Admission history

HF22 is not a scheduled chapter after HF21.

HD3 tested two candidate residuals:

```text
A. OtherMind / TheoryOfMind mechanism
B. Persistent participant-specific relationship state
```

Candidate A proved scientifically deep but representable through existing HF8/HF9/HF12/
HF21 surfaces once typed mechanism depth was supplied.

Candidate B could not be reduced without hidden choices to either:

```text
HF12 current/repeated interaction
```

or:

```text
HF13 population/institution-level persistent social order
```

Therefore:

```text
NextFoundationAdmissionCondition(persistent relations) = true
FoundationReopenCondition(HF0–HF21) = false
```

HF22 is the thin canonical extraction; HD3 remains the full evidence/model/falsifier owner.

---

# 1. The missing scale

HF22 establishes:

```text
CurrentInteraction
!= PersistentRelationship
!= PersistentSocialOrder
```

A friendship, attachment or care relation can persist while no current interaction occurs.

A convention, law or institution can persist beyond any particular dyad and coordinate
strangers.

These are different persistence scales.

---

# 2. Persistent relationship working grammar

Use:

```text
RelationState_D(A,B,t) = {
  identified participants,
  relation domains/types,
  shared history,
  familiarity,
  attachment/security,
  care/concern policy,
  trust profiles,
  commitment/persistence expectations,
  dependence/interdependence,
  perceived responsiveness,
  conflict/betrayal/repair history,
  shared conventions/resources,
  role/power asymmetries,
  A's relation model,
  B's relation model,
  institutional embedding,
  uncertainty,
  current accessibility/contact
}
```

No field is universally mandatory at the same resolution.

---

# 3. Relation state is dynamic

```text
RelationState_{t+1}
= U(RelationState_t,
    Interaction_t,
    NonInteraction_t,
    LifeEvents_t,
    ThirdPartyInformation_t,
    InstitutionalChange_t,
    Memory/Interpretation_t)
```

This is a typed history-dependent grammar, not a complete quantitative model.

---

# 4. Relationship is not repeated interaction

```text
RepeatedInteraction != PersistentRelationshipByDefinition
```

Repeated transactions can remain purely transactional.

Persistent relationship state requires question-relative evidence that participant-specific
history/expectations/attachment/care/commitment or other relational state survives and
changes future interaction.

---

# 5. Relationship is not current interaction state

```text
RelationshipState_t != CurrentInteractionState_t
```

A friendship can persist during silence; a parent-child relation can matter before the next
encounter.

---

# 6. No interaction is not no relationship dynamics

```text
NoInteraction != NoRelationshipDynamics
```

Absence, delayed response, separation, migration, availability and nonresponse can change a
persistent relation.

---

# 7. Persistent is not static

```text
Persistent != Static
```

A persistent relation can strengthen, weaken, repair, become ambivalent or end.

---

# 8. Relationship is not institution

```text
PersistentRelationship != Institution
```

A friendship may have no formal institutional encoding.

An office, law or professional role may persist without a close personal relationship.

HF13 remains institutional/persistent-social-order owner.

---

# 9. Relationship state is participant-specific

HF22 is keyed to identified participants:

```text
RelationState_D(A,B)
```

rather than a population-wide norm or reputation state.

Third-party reputation can initialize the relation but does not replace direct dyadic state.

---

# 10. Relationship can be asymmetric

```text
RelationState_D(A→B) != RelationState_D(B→A)
```

Attachment, trust, dependence, commitment and perceived closeness need not be reciprocal.

Therefore:

```text
PerceivedRelationship != SymmetricRelationshipByDefinition
```

---

# 11. Relationship can be multiplex

The same pair can simultaneously be:

```text
kin
friends
coworkers
competitors
caregiver/care-recipient
creditor/debtor
romantic partners
```

Therefore:

```text
OneDyad != OneRelationType
```

Use domain-qualified relation layers.

---

# 12. Relationship state is not one closeness scalar

Potential coordinates include:

```text
familiarity
attachment/security
trust by domain
care
commitment
interdependence
shared history
shared identity
responsiveness
conflict/repair history
status/power asymmetry
exchange pattern
contact opportunity
```

Therefore:

```text
ClosenessRating != PersistentRelationshipByDefinition
```

---

# 13. Trust is one coordinate, not relationship totality

HF12 remains trust owner and already separates:

```text
Trust != Reliance != Dependence != Predictability
```

HF22 adds:

```text
TrustRelation_D != RelationshipStateTotality
```

A pair can have high trust in one domain and low attachment, or attachment with low trust
in another domain.

---

# 14. Familiarity is not trust or attachment

```text
Familiarity != Attachment
Familiarity != Trust
```

Repeated exposure alone does not establish security, care or reliance.

---

# 15. Attachment is relationship-specific

```text
AttachmentTo_A != AttachmentTo_B
AttachmentSecurity != PersonTraitByDefinition
```

Attachment can differ across caregivers/partners and change through relational history.

---

# 16. Attachment is not love

```text
Attachment != Love
```

Love can involve attachment together with care, attraction, valuation, commitment,
identity and cultural meaning.

---

# 17. Attachment is not dependence

```text
Attachment != Dependence
SecureAttachment != MaximumClinging
```

Instrumental dependence can occur without attachment; secure attachment can support
exploration rather than maximal proximity.

---

# 18. Attachment is not physiology

Attachment/caregiving can alter stress regulation, but:

```text
AttachmentSecurity != CortisolProfile
```

A physiological consequence is not the relationship itself.

---

# 19. Relationship can regulate the organism

Primary intervention and dyadic-stress evidence supports:

```text
Relationship/CaregivingStructure
→ RegulatoryState / Recovery
```

as a real causal pathway in some contexts.

Therefore:

```text
EmotionRegulation != IntraIndividualOnly
CoRegulation != ChildhoodOnly
```

HF5/HF21 remain regulation/affect owners.

---

# 20. Relationship can scaffold capability

A persistent relation can provide:

```text
co-regulation
information
care
backup
shared memory
joint monitoring
safe exploration
specialized coordination
```

Thus:

```text
IndependentCapability != RelationSupportedCapability
```

But:

```text
RelationalCapability != RelationshipHealthByDefinition
```

A highly coupled relation can be harmful.

---

# 21. Care is not empathy

```text
Care != Empathy
```

Care concerns another's welfare and can involve sustained policies/actions even without
strong affect sharing.

---

# 22. Care is not altruism

```text
Care != AltruismByDefinition
```

Care may be reciprocal, kin-based, role-based, professionally compensated or mixed-motive.

HF12 remains cooperation/prosociality owner.

---

# 23. Caring feeling is not care practice

```text
CaringFeeling != CarePracticeTotality
```

Care may include monitoring, protection, maintenance, resource provision, advocacy,
availability and burden sharing.

---

# 24. Care does not confer authority

```text
CaresAbout(A,B) != AHasAuthorityOver(B)
Care != Ownership
```

Care evidence cannot silently authorize paternalistic control.

---

# 25. Attachment does not confer authority

```text
Attachment != Authorization
Love != Authority
```

Closeness and accurate understanding do not create decision rights.

HF14/HF17 institutional/normative authority boundaries remain intact.

---

# 26. Commitment is not attachment

```text
Commitment != Attachment
```

Commitment can come from promise, norm, contract or strategic choice despite low
attachment; attachment can exist without explicit commitment.

---

# 27. Relationship persistence is not normative goodness

```text
RelationshipPersistence != RelationshipHealth
Attachment != NormativeGood
```

Persistent relationships can be supportive, ambivalent, exploitative, abusive or coercive.

HF14 remains normative owner.

---

# 28. Relationship quality is endpoint-specific

Possible endpoints include:

```text
security
trust
support
responsiveness
conflict
commitment
satisfaction
welfare impact
autonomy impact
```

Therefore:

```text
RelationshipQuality_D != RelationshipQuality_E
```

---

# 29. Relationship resilience is not absence of conflict

```text
RelationshipResilience != NoConflict
```

Persistence can depend on repair/reconfiguration after rupture, separation or role change.

---

# 30. Exit is a valid relation transition

A relation trajectory must allow:

```text
continuation
repair
downgrade
boundary change
separation
exit
```

Exit is not universal failure; persistence is not universally desirable.

---

# 31. Communication repair, trust repair and relationship repair differ

HF12 already separates communication and trust repair.

HF22 adds:

```text
CommunicationRepair != TrustRepair != RelationshipRepair
```

Relationship repair can require longer-horizon changes to attachment, commitment,
obligation expectations or boundaries.

---

# 32. Forgiveness is not restored trust

```text
Forgiveness != TrustRestoration
Forgiveness != RelationshipContinuation
```

A person can forgive while ending the relationship or maintaining monitoring.

HF22 records only the boundary.

---

# 33. Relationship representation is not relationship reality

Each participant may hold:

```text
RelationModel_A(A,B)
RelationModel_B(B,A)
```

These models can diverge from each other and from observable causal/institutional facts.

Therefore:

```text
PerceivedCloseness != MutualCloseness
RelationRepresentation != RelationStateTotality
```

---

# 34. Relationship history is not episodic memory only

Persistent relational state can be encoded through:

```text
expectations
habits
attachment scripts
shared conventions
external records/messages/photos
roles/material arrangements
```

Therefore:

```text
RelationshipHistory != EpisodicMemoryOnly
```

HF7/HF8 remain memory/representation owners.

---

# 35. Attachment relation is not attachment representation

```text
AttachmentRelation != AttachmentRepresentation
```

Relational history can shape secure-base scripts or autobiographical representation, but
the representation is not the relation itself.

---

# 36. Other-mind modeling is not a separate HF22 ontology

HD3 finds:

```text
ObservedEvidence + Context + PriorPartnerModel + RelationHistory
→ OtherMindModel_D(A→B)
→ Prediction / Communication / Action
→ PartnerResponse
→ ModelUpdate
```

This is a specialized composition of HF8 representation, HF9 inference, HF12 interaction
and HF21 affective attribution.

Therefore:

```text
OtherMindModel != OtherMind
Mindreading != MindAccess
TheoryOfMind_D != TheoryOfMind_E
```

but no standalone ToM foundation is introduced.

---

# 37. Mentalizing is not empathy

HF22 inherits HD3's key social-cognition boundary:

```text
Mentalizing != Empathy
```

and:

```text
EmpathicAccuracy != AffectiveSharing != EmpathicConcern
```

These mechanisms can affect relationships without defining relation state.

# 38. Joint attention is not relationship state

```text
GazeFollowing != JointAttention
JointAttention != SharedBelief
JointAttention != PersistentRelationship
```

Joint attention is an interaction/developmental mechanism that can support learning and
relationship formation.

HF12 remains current interaction owner.

---

# 39. Social understanding is relationally conditioned

Mindreading accuracy can depend on:

```text
observer model
partner signal style
shared conventions
neurotype/culture fit
history
context
```

Therefore:

```text
MindreadingAccuracy != ObserverTraitOnly
```

Persistent relation history can improve prediction or create bias.

```text
MoreRelationshipHistory != MoreAccurateMindreadingByDefinition
```

---

# 40. Partner-specific prior is not stereotype

```text
PartnerSpecificHistory != CategoryStereotype
```

HF22 owns identified dyad history; HF13 owns broader reputation/status/norm/category
structures.

---

# 41. Kinship is multi-coordinate

```text
SocialKinship != GeneticRelatedness
```

Human kin relations may be organized through genetic relation together with descent,
marriage/affinity, adoption/fostering, clan, household and social/ritual recognition.

Genetic relatedness can be causally relevant without defining kinship totality.

---

# 42. Family is not household

```text
Family != Household
```

Household co-residence can occur without family relation; family relations can persist
across separate households.

---

# 43. Family is not genetic kin set

```text
Family_D != GeneticKinSet
```

Adoption, marriage, stepfamilies and social parenthood force the separation.

---

# 44. Romantic relationship is not attachment only

```text
RomanticRelationship != AttachmentOnly
```

Romantic relations can involve attachment, intimacy, passion, sexuality, commitment,
jealousy, exchange and cultural/institutional meanings.

---

# 45. Loneliness is not social isolation

```text
Loneliness != SocialIsolation
```

Loneliness is a subjective deficit/mismatch in desired meaningful connection; social
isolation concerns objective network/contact structure.

---

# 46. More contacts is not better relationship

```text
MoreContacts != BetterRelationships
```

Quantity does not uniquely determine support, trust, responsiveness, belonging or
attachment.

---

# 47. Relationship type is projection-relative

Cross-cultural/historical evidence supports recurrent dimensions/categories while also
showing variation.

Therefore:

```text
RelationshipType_D
= projection over relation features for question D
```

and:

```text
UsefulRelationshipEmbedding != CompleteRelationshipOntology
```

---

# 48. Relationship can contain dyadic power asymmetry

```text
DyadicPowerAsymmetry != InstitutionalAuthorityByDefinition
```

Dependence/capability/control asymmetry can exist inside a caregiver-child or intimate
relation even before/without formal institutional authority.

HF13 remains generalized power/authority owner.

---

# 49. Relationship can be mediated

```text
PersistentRelationship != PhysicalCopresence
```

Text, video, asynchronous communication and shared digital environments can maintain a
relationship.

```text
HumanPerceivedConnectionToAI
```

A Human relational state can be psychologically real even when counterpart experience is

# 52. AI social fluency is not experience

Human closeness/trust can change depending on whether identical/similar responses are
attributed to a Human or AI partner.

# 58. Stop rule

Do not expand HF22 into a complete social psychology, family science, attachment catalogue,
romantic-relation taxonomy or AI-companion ontology.

Reopen HF22 only if its own minimum scale/boundary distinctions fail repeatedly across
materially different relation contexts.

The strongest next deep residual concerns social learning, teaching and cultural
inheritance. That residual does not automatically imply HF23.
