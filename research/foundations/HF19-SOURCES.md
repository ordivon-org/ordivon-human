# HF19 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF19 separates four evidence roles:

```text
Statistical/measurement standard
= operational boundary for comparable observation; not universal ontology

Formal economic model
= conditional explanation under declared production/contract/information assumptions;
  not a universal theory of work, firms, ownership or value

Historical/institutional organization theory
= explanatory projection over a particular organization problem; not firm essence

Empirical technology/AI evidence
= observed effect in a specific task, workforce, model and workflow configuration;
  not a universal automation law
```

Never infer:

```text
OutsideStatisticalProductionBoundary -> NotRealWork
EmploymentStatus -> WholeWorkRelation
LaborProductivity -> WorkerTrait
MFP -> PureTechnology
CapitalStock -> HomogeneousProductiveService
FirmTheory_M -> UniversalFirmOntology
OwnershipEfficiency_M -> OwnershipLegitimacy
AIProductivityGain_D -> UniversalHumanCapabilityGain
```

# 1. ILO — work statistics / forms of work

## 19th International Conference of Labour Statisticians (2013), Resolution I

- Institution: International Labour Organization / ILOSTAT.
- Title: *Resolution concerning statistics of work, employment and labour
  underutilization*.
- Use: operationally separates `work` from `employment`. The framework admits own-use
  production work, employment work, unpaid trainee work, volunteer work and other work
  activities; employment is work for pay or profit.

### HF19 inference

```text
Work != Employment
Paid != ProductiveByDefinition
Unpaid != Nonwork
StatisticalBoundary != HumanOntology
```

---

# 2. ILO — employment relationship

## ILO (current 2025 legal/institutional guidance)

- Topic: employment relationship.
- Use: distinguishes an employee-employer legal/institutional relation, with reciprocal
  rights and obligations, from the wider statistical concept of work for pay or profit.
  It is useful pressure against treating all paid work as one authority relation.

### HF19 inference

```text
Employment_Statistical != EmploymentRelation_LegalInstitutional
PaidWork != EmployeeStatusByDefinition
```

---

# 3. ILO — ICSE-18 / status in employment

## 20th ICLS / ICSE-18 manual and ILOSTAT implementation

- Institution: International Labour Organization.
- Framework: International Classification of Status in Employment (ICSE-18), embedded
  in the broader International Classification of Status at Work.
- Use: classifies work relationships along distinct organizing dimensions including
  **authority** and **economic risk**, admitting employers, independent workers,
  dependent contractors and employees as different configurations.

### HF19 inference

```text
SameTask != SameWorkRelationship
Authority != EconomicRisk
Contractor != IndependentByDefinition
DependentContractor is a coherent category
```

# 4. United Nations et al. — System of National Accounts 2025

## *System of National Accounts 2025*

- Institutions: United Nations, European Commission, IMF, OECD and World Bank.
- Use: current international national-accounting production/asset/value-added boundary;
  useful for distinguishing measured production, intermediate consumption, value added,
  gross capital formation and consumption of fixed capital.

### HF19 inference

```text
AccountingProductionBoundary != UniversalWorkBoundary
ValueAdded_SNA != MoralValue
GrossCapitalFormation != EveryCapabilityInvestment
GDPGrowth != WelfareGrowthByDefinition
```

# 5. OECD — productivity measurement

## OECD productivity manuals, indicators and *OECD Compendium of Productivity Indicators 2026*

- Institution: OECD.
- Use: labor productivity and multifactor productivity are ratio/index/residual
  constructs whose interpretation depends on output and input measurement. OECD notes
  that MFP can reflect management practices, organizational change, knowledge,
  network effects, scale, market structure, adjustment costs and measurement error.

### HF19 inference

```text
Productivity != Effort
LaborProductivity != WorkerTrait
MFPResidual != PureTechnology
Residual != CausalFactor
```

# 6. OECD — capital measurement and capital services

## OECD, *Measuring Capital — OECD Manual*

- Institution: OECD.
- Use: separates asset stocks, depreciation/consumption of fixed capital and flows of
  capital services into production. This is important when productive contribution is
  a service flow rather than the nominal value of an owned asset.

### HF19 inference

```text
CapitalAssetStock != CapitalServiceFlow
AssetPrice != ProductiveContribution
InstalledAsset != EffectiveUtilization
```

# 7. World Bank — human capital measurement

## World Bank Human Capital Project / Human Capital Report 2026

- Institution: World Bank.
- Use: current economic measurement projection relating health, knowledge, skills and
  experience to productive capability and future outcomes. HF19 uses it as evidence
  that `human capital` is a model/measurement family rather than the human person.

### HF19 inference

```text
HumanCapital_D != Human
HumanCapitalMeasure != CompleteCapability
HumanCapital != PropertyRightOverPerson
```

Transport limit: this source supports an economic measurement projection; HF1/HF14/HF15
continue to own personhood, authority, moral standing and value boundaries.

- Title: *The Nature of the Firm*.
- Journal: Economica 4(16):386–405.
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x

# 31. Source-level synthesis

The operational-statistics corpus establishes that measurement already needs several
non-equivalent boundaries:

```text
Work != Employment
EmploymentStatistic != EmploymentRelation
Authority != EconomicRisk
Output/Productivity/CapitalStock/CapitalService are boundary-qualified measures
```

The production/growth corpus establishes:

```text
ProductionFunction != ProductionOntology
GrowthResidual != DirectlyObservedTechnology
Production can generate learning
Technology can be endogenous
```

The organization corpus does **not** converge on one definition of `firm`. Instead it
supplies different explanatory projections:

```text
Coase                → market-contracting versus internal-organization cost
Simon                 → bounded employment authority/discretion
Alchian-Demsetz      → team production / monitoring / attribution
Jensen-Meckling      → agency / contractual claims
Williamson           → comparative market-hybrid-hierarchy governance
Grossman-Hart/Moore  → incomplete contracts / asset ownership / residual control
Prescott-Visscher    → persistent organization-specific productive information
Benkler              → peer-production alternative
```

Their differences are evidence **for plural question-relative views**, not a defect to
be erased by choosing one winner.

The specialization/complementarity corpus establishes that productivity gains can be
system properties with coordination and dependency costs rather than additive worker
traits.

Finally, the task/AI corpus establishes only the narrower conclusion:

```text
Technology/AI role is task-, skill-, workflow- and organization-dependent;
automation, augmentation, learning and recomposition can coexist.
```

It does not establish a universal trajectory for jobs, wages, welfare or Human
capability.
