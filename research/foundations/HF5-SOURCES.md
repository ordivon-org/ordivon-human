# HF5 External Evidence and Source Ledger

## Evidence rule

HF5 studies internal regulation where the same surface word often mixes:

```text
physical variable
sensor / afferent signal
central representation
regulatory controller
behavioral effector
subjective feeling
motivation / action allocation
clinical marker
long-term adaptation
```

Sources therefore constrain local links. No biomarker, feeling or theory becomes
the whole regulatory process.

---

# 1. Homeostasis and allostasis

## Ramsay & Woods (2014) — clarification of regulatory roles

- PMID: 24730599
- PMCID: PMC4166604
- DOI: 10.1037/a0035942
- Open record: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166604/
- HF5 use: argues that homeostasis and allostasis should be distinguished by the
  interacting regulatory loops and mechanisms rather than by the simplistic claim
  that homeostasis has a fixed setpoint and allostasis merely changes it.
- Boundary: conceptual review; it does not settle one universal architecture.

## Goldstein & McEwen (2002)

- PMID: 12171767
- DOI: 10.1080/102538902900012345
- HF5 use: emphasizes multiple interacting effectors and homeostats; different
  challenges recruit different patterns of response.

## McEwen (1998) — allostasis and allostatic load

- PMID: 9629234
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1749-6632.1998.tb09546.x
- HF5 use: separates adaptive activation from the cumulative cost of frequent,
  prolonged, inadequately terminated or inadequately compensated activation.

## McEwen & Wingfield (2010)

- PMID: 19786032
- PMCID: PMC2815096
- HF5 use: emphasizes predictable life-cycle demands plus unpredictable challenges
  and the need for supplemental allostatic terminology around homeostasis/stress.

## Sterling (2012) — predictive regulation model

- PMID: 21684297
- DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2011.06.004
- HF5 use: strong rival model in which efficient regulation anticipates future
  needs and coordinates multiple effectors before error grows large.
- Boundary: HF5 retains predictive regulation as a useful rival, not as proof that
  feedback control is unimportant.

## Schulkin & Sterling (2019)

- PMID: 31488322
- HF5 use: brain-centered predictive-allostasis model linking regulation,
  resource allocation and learned anticipatory behavior.

### HF5 inference

```text
Homeostasis != perfect constancy
Allostasis != any state change
Feedback != only possible regulation
Prediction != proof of one allostatic theory
```

# 2. Interoception

## Garfinkel et al. (2015)

- PMID: 25451381
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004
- HF5 use: empirically separates interoceptive accuracy, interoceptive sensibility
  and metacognitive awareness in heartbeat tasks.

## Forkmann et al. (2016)

- PMID: 27702644
- HF5 use: further tests the dissociation among physiological state, objective
  accuracy, sensibility and awareness; argues physiological state itself should be
  explicitly represented beneath performance measures.

## Murphy et al. (2019)

- PMID: 31270764
- PMCID: PMC6797703
- DOI: 10.3758/s13423-019-01632-7
- HF5 use: highlights classification/measurement problems in treating interoception
  as one domain-general trait.

## Schulz & Vögele (2015) — interoception and stress

- PMCID: PMC4507149
- HF5 use: reviews bidirectional brain–body communication and shows that stress and
  interoception interact rather than occupying a one-way ascending signal path.

### HF5 inference

```text
InternalPhysiology
!= AfferentSignal
!= NeuralRepresentation
!= ObjectiveInteroceptivePerformance
!= SelfReportedSensibility
!= MetaInteroceptiveAccuracy
!= BodilyExperience
```

# 3. Hunger, satiation and satiety

## Stevenson et al. / individual differences in hunger-fullness-thirst interoception

- PMID: 26119812
- DOI: 10.1016/j.appet.2015.06.008
- HF5 use: demonstrates large individual differences in how visceral,
  motivational, affective and cognitive information is used to describe hunger,
  fullness and thirst.

## Heisler & Lam (2017) — hunger and satiety circuitry

- PMID: 29107871
- HF5 use: appetite integrates multiple interoceptive signals of nutritional state
  with circuits controlling food seeking and consumption.

## Appetitive interoception / hippocampal contextual model

- PMID: 35067848
- DOI: 10.1007/s11154-021-09698-2
- HF5 use: satiety can function as contextual information altering retrieval and
  appetitive control; it is not merely a scalar opposite of hunger.

## Psychologically induced hunger cues (2025)

- PMID: 39798932
- DOI: 10.1016/j.appet.2025.107855
- HF5 use: experimentally induced interoceptive-like hunger cues can alter food
  desire independent of their ordinary physiological cause, pressure-testing a
  direct signal→feeling model.

## Gut–brain signaling review (2026)

- PMID: 42014861
- DOI: 10.1038/s41575-026-01203-x
- HF5 use: distinguishes food seeking, consumption and non-prandial phases and
  separates hunger, satiation and satiety in gut–brain control.

### HF5 inference

```text
Hunger != EnergyDeficit
Satiation != Satiety
Satiety != Liking
Satiety != Wanting
FoodSeeking != FoodConsumption
```

# 4. Thirst and body-fluid regulation

## Szczepanska-Sadowska (2025)

- PMID: 40869169
- PMCID: PMC12386622
- DOI: 10.3390/ijms26167850
- HF5 use: thirst participates in body-fluid regulation through multiple neural,
  hormonal and behavioral processes; the conscious sensation is one component of
  a wider system.

## Body-fluid / thermoregulation interaction

- PMID: 30454604
- HF5 use: dehydration/hyperosmolality and cardiovascular constraints interact
  with sweating and heat dissipation; one regulated domain changes another.

### HF5 inference

```text
ThirstExperience != Osmolality
DrinkingBehavior != ThirstExperience
FluidHomeostasis != one-variable controller
```

# 5. Thermoregulation

## Schlader et al. (2010) — behavioral thermoregulation

- PMID: 20006632
- DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2009.12.003
- HF5 use: behavioral responses can regulate heat before/alongside autonomic
  responses; thermal comfort/sensation and perceived effort can trigger behavior.

## Romanovsky / behavioral thermoregulation architecture

- PMID: 20711785
- DOI: 10.1007/s00421-010-1602-8
- HF5 use: human thermal regulation relies heavily on behavior, while thermal
  sensation and discomfort have different regulatory roles.

## Recent psychology of thermoregulation (2025)

- PMID: 40020894
- DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2025.114865
- HF5 use: thermal state coordinates cognition, affect and motivation; useful as a
  broad hypothesis generator rather than settled mechanism.

### HF5 inference

```text
ThermalSensation != ThermalDiscomfort
ThermalDiscomfort != CoreTemperature
BehavioralThermoregulation != AutonomicThermoregulation
```

# 6. Sleep pressure and circadian control

## Borbély / two-process model reappraisal

- PMID: 26762182
- DOI: 10.1111/jsr.12371
- HF5 use: separates sleep-wake-dependent homeostatic Process S from circadian
  Process C.

## Two-process model origins/outlook

- PMID: 35502706
- HF5 use: sleep deprivation raises slow-wave activity and recovery sleep dynamics;
  Process S and circadian timing cannot be collapsed.

## Human-data perspective (2026)

- PMID: 42331996
- HF5 use: current human evidence includes both support and tensions for assumptions
  of the two-process framework; useful falsification against treating Process S as
  complete sleep ontology.

## Adenosine and sleep homeostasis

- PMID: 18384754
- HF5 use: convergent human evidence supports adenosinergic involvement in NREM
  sleep homeostasis, without making adenosine identical to sleep need.

## Individual dynamics of sleep homeostasis

- PMID: 20394318
- PMCID: PMC2849788
- HF5 use: Process-S buildup/decay parameters vary substantially among individuals.

### HF5 inference

```text
SleepNeed != CircadianTime
SleepPressure != SubjectiveFatigue
SlowWaveActivity != SleepNeed itself
Adenosine != SleepNeed itself
```

# 7. Pain and nociception

## IASP revised definition task force (2020)

- PMID: 32694387
- DOI: 10.1097/j.pain.0000000000001939
- HF5 use: pain is an unpleasant sensory/emotional experience associated with or
  resembling that associated with actual/potential tissue damage; pain and
  nociception are distinct phenomena.

### HF5 inference

```text
Nociception != Pain
TissueDamage != Pain
PainReport != PainExperience
```

Pain is a strong falsifier of `internal physiological signal = subjective bodily
state`.

# 8. Stress

## McEwen (2007) — stress physiology and adaptation

- PMID: 17615391
- DOI: 10.1152/physrev.00041.2006
- HF5 use: stress involves bidirectional brain–body regulation; acute stress
  mediators can be adaptive while repeated/chronic dysregulation can be damaging.

## McEwen & Gianaros (2011)

- PMID: 20707675
- PMCID: PMC4251716
- HF5 use: stress responses remodel brain/body systems and can be adaptive or
  maladaptive depending on trajectory/context.

## Allostatic-load measurement review (2024)

- PMID: 39132278
- PMCID: PMC11316529
- HF5 use: allostatic-load studies use heterogeneous marker bundles; there is no
  single universally standardized allostatic-load meter.

## Early-life stress terminology review (2024)

- PMID: 39087715
- HF5 use: stress, adversity, trauma, toxic stress, chronic stress and allostatic
  load are frequently conflated and require explicit definitions.

### HF5 inference

```text
Stressor != Appraisal != StressResponse != StressExperience
!= StressMediator != AllostaticLoad
```

and:

```text
AcuteStressResponse != Damage
```

# 9. Fatigue

## HF4 state-to-effort evidence retained

- sickness/effort: PMID 28948979
- cognitive fatigue→physical effort valuation: PMID 39713470
- sleep-deprivation wanting/work dissociation: PMID 28089706
- deadline/opportunity cost: PMID 35511565

HF5 use: subjective fatigue, willingness to work and objective performance need not
move together.

## Sustained recovery from mental fatigue (2026)

- PMID: 42121328
- DOI: 10.1142/S0129065726500383
- HF5 use: rest and acute exercise can produce different temporal recovery profiles
  after mental fatigue, showing recovery is intervention- and endpoint-specific.

### HF5 inference

```text
FatigueExperience
!= PerformanceDecrement
!= SleepPressure
!= EffortCost
!= ResourceDepletion
```

Fatigue may participate in regulation without being a direct gauge of one depleted
substance.

# 10. Recovery, habituation and resilience

## repeated-stress recovery/habituation

- PMID: 27543044
- HF5 use: repeated stress allows separate measurement of response magnitude,
  recovery and habituation; these vary with trait resilience.

## stress habituation review

- PMID: 18667167
- PMCID: PMC2773683
- HF5 use: reduced HPA response to repeated stress satisfies many but not all
  criteria for habituation; negative feedback, circuitry and associative learning
  also contribute.

## resilience systems review (2024)

- PMID: 38483288
- PMCID: PMC11381009
- DOI: 10.1152/physrev.00042.2023
- HF5 use: resilience is a dynamic, multi-system process rather than one stable
  biomarker or trait.

## acute stress and neuroplastic adaptation (2024)

- PMID: 39448004
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2024.10.016
- HF5 use: acute stress responses can themselves drive plasticity/adaptation;
  resilience can involve maintenance or rapid recovery rather than no response.

## recovery as active process — emerging model (2026)

- PMID: 42071106
- DOI: 10.1007/s10522-026-10445-w
- HF5 use: proposes recovery as an active bioenergetically constrained phase.
- Boundary: recent theoretical synthesis; retained as an emerging rival, not a
  settled universal recovery ontology.

### HF5 inference

```text
Recovery != RestDuration
Recovery != ReturnOfOneMarkerToBaseline
Habituation != Recovery
Resilience != NoStressResponse
Adaptation != ReturnToPreviousState
```

This evidence exposes plasticity/history as the next boundary.

# 11. Biological need versus psychological need

## Ryan & Deci (2000) — self-determination theory

- PMID: 11392867
- HF5 use: SDT explicitly proposes autonomy, competence and relatedness as basic
  psychological needs whose satisfaction supports self-motivation and well-being.

## Sheldon & Gunz (2009) — unmet psychological needs and motive strength

- PMID: 19678877
- HF5 use: unmet autonomy/competence/relatedness predicted corresponding desires,
  providing evidence relevant to the `need` claim while also showing the dynamics
  do not simply mirror classic homeostatic satiation.

## large interpersonal-support meta-analysis (2024)

- PMID: 38635183
- HF5 use: support for autonomy/competence/relatedness is strongly associated with
  need satisfaction and well-being across a very large evidence base.

### HF5 inference

Psychological `need` is a theoretically and empirically substantive use of the
word, but it is not automatically the same causal form as osmoregulation,
thermoregulation or nutrient deficit.

Therefore:

```text
PhysiologicalRegulatoryNeed
!= PsychologicalBasicNeed
```

unless a specific common mechanism is independently established.

# 12. Source-level synthesis

The strongest cross-domain separations are:

```text
Need != Desire
Need != Deficit alone
PhysiologicalNeed != PsychologicalBasicNeed

RegulatedVariable != Setpoint
Setpoint != fixed universal constant
Homeostasis != perfect constancy
Allostasis != all regulation
Feedback != prediction

PeripheralState != AfferentSignal
AfferentSignal != CentralRepresentation
CentralRepresentation != BodilyExperience
InteroceptiveAccuracy != Sensibility != MetaAwareness

Hunger != EnergyDeficit
Satiation != Satiety
Satiety != Liking/Wanting
Thirst != Osmolality
ThermalSensation != ThermalDiscomfort

SleepPressure != CircadianDrive
SleepPressure != SubjectiveFatigue
Pain != Nociception

Stressor != StressResponse
StressResponse != Damage
StressMediator != StressExperience
StressResponse != AllostaticLoad

FatigueExperience != PerformanceDecline
Fatigue != EffortCost
Fatigue != one resource meter

Recovery != Rest
Recovery != one-marker baseline return
Recovery != Habituation
Resilience != absence of response
```

The repeated residual is **history-dependent change of the regulatory system
itself**: habituation, sensitization, learning, plasticity, adaptation, resilience,
development and aging alter future responses even after the immediate perturbation
has ended.
