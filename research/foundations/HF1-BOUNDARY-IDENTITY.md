# HF1 — Human Boundary, Organism, Body, Subject, Self, Person, Actor and Agent

## 0. Status

HF1 continues directly from HF0. It is a **foundation research record**, not a
metaphysical verdict, consciousness test, identity score, legal standard,
clinical assessment or rights-ranking system.

HF0 established that one Human reality can require several non-equivalent
projections. HF1 asks what happens when some of the most overloaded projections
are treated as if they were synonyms:

```text
Human
Organism
Body
Subject
Self
Person
Actor
Agent
```

HF1's central question is:

> **Which of these terms track biological continuity, bodily organization,
> first-person experience, self-representation, social/normative status or
> functional action roles—and what persists when consciousness, memory, bodily
> ownership, social role, tools or agency change?**

The answer is not one better noun. It is a typed boundary and persistence model.

---

# 1. Starting falsifier: the ordinary word `same` is underspecified

Consider the following questions:

```text
Is this the same Human?
Is this the same living organism?
Is this the same body?
Is this the same subject of experience?
Is this the same self?
Is this the same person?
Is this the same actor?
Is this the same agent?
```

Ordinary language often answers all of them with one word: `same`.

HF1 finds that materially different cases force different answers. A sleeping
Human can remain the same organism while current conscious experience changes.
A person with severe autobiographical amnesia can remain the same biological and
legal individual while narrative self-organization is disrupted. A rubber or
prosthetic limb can gain experienced ownership or agency without becoming
biological tissue. A corporation can be a legal person without being a Human.
Software can be modeled as an Agent without being a Human or a subject of
experience.

Therefore the first HF1 law is:

```text
Identity requires a declared identity relation.
```

Formally:

```text
Same_K(x_t1, x_t2)
```

where `K` identifies what continuity is being asserted.

Examples:

```text
K = HumanIndividual
K = Organism
K = BiologicalBody
K = ExperientialSubject
K = BodilySelfModel
K = AutobiographicalSelf
K = SocialIdentity
K = LegalPerson
K = ActorRole
K = AgentSystem
```

HF1 rejects unqualified `same person` as sufficient technical language when the
answer affects explanation or authority.

# 2. Competing persistence models

HF1 begins by allowing several identity theories to compete rather than choosing
one in advance.

## M1 — exact material continuity

```text
same entity iff sufficiently same physical matter
```

### Strength

It respects physical realization and can be useful for artifacts whose identity
is literally byte/material bound.

### Failure

Living organisms continually exchange matter while maintaining organized life.
Exact material sameness is too strong for ordinary organismic persistence.

**Disposition:** reject as general Human persistence criterion.

## M2 — organismic continuity

```text
same Human individual
≈ continuity of one living, physiologically integrated Homo sapiens organism
```

### Strength

- survives sleep, temporary unconsciousness and many psychological state changes;
- tracks development, ageing, injury and recovery;
- gives Human research a stable biological referent.

### Failure

Organismic continuity alone does not answer:

- whether the current individual is conscious;
- what body parts feel owned;
- whether autobiographical identity is preserved;
- what social/legal identity applies;
- whether an artificial system is an Agent;
- what moral status or rights should be assigned.

**Disposition:** retain as the default descriptive `HumanIndividual` tracking
relation, not as total Person/Self ontology.

## M3 — current-consciousness continuity

```text
same subject/person iff consciousness remains continuous
```

### Strength

It captures the intuition that first-person experience matters to subjectivity.

### Failure

Humans persist through ordinary intervals where awareness or reportable
experience changes dramatically. Sleep and intended general anaesthesia also
show that consciousness, responsiveness and later recall can dissociate.

**Disposition:** reject as Human-organism identity criterion; retain a separate
`ExperientialSubject` relation.

## M4 — psychological continuity

```text
same person/self iff beliefs, intentions, personality or other psychological
organization remains suitably continuous
```

### Strength

Highly relevant to responsibility, relationships, self-understanding and life
planning.

### Failure

Psychological properties change through development, injury, illness, learning,
conversion, ageing and social transformation. No one psychological feature
survives all Human persistence cases.

**Disposition:** retain as a family of psychologically typed continuity relations,
not one Human identity predicate.

## M5 — autobiographical-memory continuity

```text
same self/person iff autobiographical memory links present to past
```

### Strength

Autobiographical memory strongly contributes to narrative identity, future
projection and self-explanation.

### Failure

Amnesia can severely disrupt autobiographical memory and life-story organization
without terminating the living Human, the ongoing social relationship network or
legal identity.

**Disposition:** retain for `AutobiographicalSelf`, reject as sole Human
persistence criterion.

## M6 — narrative continuity

```text
same self iff a sufficiently coherent life narrative persists
```

### Strength

Useful for meaning, commitment, authorship, social explanation and long-horizon
identity.

### Failure

Infants, people with severe memory disorders, nonverbal people and people in
states without active narrative construction remain Humans. Narratives can also
be externally imposed, revised or false.

**Disposition:** retain as a high-level self-organization projection only.

## M7 — social/relational continuity

```text
same person/self iff recognized as continuous by relations, roles and communities
```

### Strength

Names, relationships, roles, reputation and commitments often persist across
large internal changes. Social recognition can preserve practical continuity
when memory or capacity is impaired.

### Failure

Social attribution can be mistaken, oppressive or institution-specific. A Human
does not cease biologically to exist because a role or community recognition
changes.

**Disposition:** retain `SocialIdentity`, reject social recognition as sole Human
identity.

## M8 — legal continuity

```text
same legal person iff the relevant legal system preserves juridical identity
```

### Strength

Operationally important for rights, obligations, ownership, contracts and
institutional records.

### Failure

Legal `person` is jurisdiction- and context-dependent and can include non-human
organizations. It is neither a biological nor a phenomenological ontology.

**Disposition:** retain as explicit `LegalPerson`, never silently import into
biological/psychological claims.

## M9 — functional-agent continuity

```text
same Agent iff one policy/state/control system persists under the domain's agent
identity rules
```

### Strength

Useful for software, robots, organizations, game subjects and Human×AI systems.

### Failure

A Human can remain the same Human while not actively selecting actions; software
can be an Agent without being Human; the same Human can instantiate several
agent roles over time.

**Disposition:** retain as functional/system identity, not Human identity.

# 3. Main result: there is no single universal persistence relation

The nine models are not merely competitors for one crown. They answer different
questions.

HF1 therefore replaces:

```text
Identity(x_t1, x_t2)
```

with:

```text
Identity(K, x_t1, x_t2, Boundary, Timescale, Context)
```

or compactly:

```text
Same_K(x_t1, x_t2)
```

This is the strongest HF1 compression.

For one concrete Human:

```text
SameHumanIndividual = true
SameCurrentConsciousState = false
SameAutobiographicalSelf = partial / uncertain
SameSocialRole = false
SameLegalPerson = true
SameAgentRole = context-dependent
```

can be a coherent state of affairs.

`Same_X != Same_Y` is therefore a foundation anti-law.

---

# 4. Human

HF1 uses `Human` in two different but related ways and requires them to be
explicit.

## 4.1 Human as species/class predicate

```text
Human(x)
≈ x is a member/instance of Homo sapiens in the relevant biological sense
```

This is a taxonomic/descriptive projection.

## 4.2 Human individual as tracked organism

For ordinary Human research:

```text
HumanIndividual(x_t)
≈ one biologically continuing Homo sapiens organism at time t
```

The word `≈` is deliberate. HF1 does not resolve every disputed edge case in
embryology, death determination, transplantation, conjoined individuality or
chimera/mosaic biology.

The purpose is narrower: provide a stable default referent that does not vanish
when consciousness, memory, language, role or tool access changes.

## 4.3 What `Human` does not mean by itself

```text
Human != currently conscious
Human != rational
Human != autonomous
Human != linguistically competent
Human != reflective self-recognizer
Human != utility maximizer
Human != Agent
Human != LegalPerson as a universal equivalence
```

Some Humans instantiate many of these properties or statuses. None is the
species identity predicate in HF1.

---

# 5. Organism

HF1 treats `Organism` as a biological organization concept rather than a bag of
matter.

A useful working characterization is:

```text
Organism
= living organization whose components are sufficiently integrated,
  coordinated and mutually dependent to maintain the whole across time
```

This is intentionally broad. Biological-individuality research shows that unity,
parthood, integration, demarcation and temporality can become difficult in many
biological systems.

For ordinary adult Human analysis, however, the relevant lesson is not that the
organism boundary is useless. It is:

> **Organismic continuity tracks organized living integration, not the exact set
> of atoms present at one moment.**

## 5.1 Organism anti-collapses

```text
Organism != exact material set
Organism != currently conscious subject
Organism != body image
Organism != social role
Organism != legal person
```

## 5.2 Organism and environment

An organism depends causally on:

- oxygen;
- food;
- temperature range;
- microbiota;
- caregivers/social support in some states;
- tools or medicine in some states.

Dependency does not automatically convert every necessary external support into
organismal parthood.

Therefore:

```text
Causal necessity != parthood
```

becomes an HF1 boundary rule.

# 6. Body is not one thing

`Body` is among the most overloaded terms in Human research.

HF1 separates at least five body-related projections.

## B1 — Biological / organismal body

The living biological structures and material organized as part of the organism.

Question:

> What is physiologically part of this organism?

## B2 — Body representation

Internal sensorimotor/perceptual representations used to locate, predict and
coordinate the body.

Question:

> What does the nervous/cognitive system represent as the body's structure,
> location, posture and action possibilities?

HF1 does not yet freeze the classic `body schema` versus `body image`
decomposition; that may require a later perception/representation round.

## B3 — Body ownership

The phenomenal sense that a body or body part is `mine`.

Question:

> What is experienced as belonging to my body/self?

## B4 — Control locus / effector set

The bodily or external effectors through which an Actor/Agent's intention or
command influences the world.

Question:

> Through what can I currently exercise control?

## B5 — Embodiment relation

A multidimensional relation among an individual and an organic/artificial
structure involving some combination of:

- anatomical plausibility;
- sensorimotor coupling;
- ownership;
- agency;
- incorporation into habitual action;
- self-location;
- affective identification;
- practical dependence.

Embodiment is therefore not a binary synonym for biological parthood.

---

# 7. Body-ownership falsification

The moving rubber-hand paradigm is a particularly high-information HF1
falsifier.

Its important result is not merely that an illusion exists. It shows two
otherwise entangled properties can be manipulated apart:

```text
BodyOwnership
and
SenseOfAgency
```

Passive movement can preserve ownership while reducing agency. Anatomical
incongruence can reduce ownership while leaving agency over the movement.

Therefore:

```text
BodyOwnership != SenseOfAgency
```

and because the model hand does not become living tissue:

```text
BodyOwnership != BiologicalParthood
```

The same logic extends to prosthetic embodiment. Prosthesis users can report
ownership/integrity, agency and anatomical plausibility as distinguishable
components.

Thus:

```text
Embodiment != BiologicalIncorporation
```

# 8. Subject

HF1 uses `Subject` narrowly because the word becomes dangerous when used as a
synonym for Human or Person.

## 8.1 Experiential subject

Working definition:

```text
ExperientialSubject(x, t)
= x is the subject to whom some experience is present at time t
```

This is a **time-indexed experiential role**.

It does not by itself assert:

- reflective self-awareness;
- autobiographical memory;
- agency;
- language;
- autonomy;
- legal personhood.

## 8.2 Subject is not responsiveness

Anaesthesia provides the strongest boundary case. The isolated-forearm technique
can reveal command-following/connected consciousness when overt whole-body
behaviour and later explicit recall do not reveal it.

Therefore:

```text
ExperientialSubject
!= ResponsiveWholeBodyActor
```

More carefully:

```text
ConsciousExperience
!= ConnectedConsciousness
!= BehaviouralResponsiveness
!= Reportability
!= LaterRecall
```

HF1 does not claim these are fully independent. It claims they cannot be
identified without evidence.

## 8.3 Persistence of Human without active subject role

If an interval contains no current conscious experience, HF1 does **not** infer
that the Human individual disappeared and a numerically new Human appeared
later.

Thus:

```text
HumanPersistence
does not require
continuous current ExperientialSubject activation
```

The exact ontology of a `subject of experience` remains open and becomes part of
the next foundation frontier.

---

# 9. Wakefulness, consciousness and identity

Sleep breaks another ordinary-language collapse.

A sleeping person can:

- remain the same Human organism;
- be behaviourally unresponsive to ordinary interaction;
- have dream experience at some moments;
- report no experience after some awakenings;
- later resume ordinary action and autobiographical life.

Therefore:

```text
Wakefulness != HumanExistence
ConsciousState != HumanIdentity
```

and a behavioural state label is not itself the experience.

HF1 therefore refuses definitions such as:

```text
Person = currently awake rational responder
```

as Human foundations.

# 10. Self is a family of self-relations, not one hidden object

HF1 finds that `Self` is too overloaded to remain one primitive.

Rather than posit five metaphysical selves, HF1 models several **self-related
functions/relations**.

## S1 — bodily self

Includes dimensions such as:

- self-location;
- body ownership;
- body boundary;
- first-person bodily orientation.

## S2 — agentive self

Concerns:

- authorship;
- sense of agency;
- control attribution;
- distinction between self-caused and externally caused effects.

## S3 — reflective self

Concerns explicit representation such as:

- `this is me`;
- self-recognition;
- self-concept;
- metacognitive reflection about one's own states/capacities.

## S4 — autobiographical / narrative self

Concerns organization of:

- personal memories;
- life chapters;
- past/future continuity;
- explanations of who one has been and is becoming.

## S5 — social / role self

Concerns:

- names;
- relationships;
- roles;
- memberships;
- status;
- reputation;
- socially recognized commitments.

## 10.1 These are not five independent entities

The correct interpretation is:

```text
SelfRelatedState
= a vector/family of relations and representations
```

not:

```text
Human contains five separate selves
```

The dimensions can support and update one another while dissociating under
specific conditions.

---

# 11. Development falsifies `reflective self = Human`

Explicit mirror self-recognition and objective body self-awareness develop
gradually in toddlerhood. Infants can exhibit self/other-sensitive processing
before they reliably pass an explicit mirror test.

This creates a direct HF1 deletion witness:

```text
ReflectiveSelfRecognition
cannot be the criterion for
HumanIndividual identity
```

Otherwise the same living child would cross from non-Human to Human merely by
passing one behavioural task.

HF1 therefore retains:

```text
BiologicalHuman continuity
precedes mature explicit ReflectiveSelf
```

without claiming that newborns or infants lack every form of bodily or
experiential self-organization.

---

# 12. Amnesia falsifies `autobiographical self = Human`

Autobiographical memory is deeply important for self-concept and narrative
continuity. HF1 does not minimize it.

The key distinction is instead:

```text
importance to one identity projection
!=
criterion for every identity projection
```

Medial-temporal-lobe amnesia can disrupt episodic autobiographical memory and the
organization/temporal relations of life chapters. Severe cases can involve
near-total autobiographical memory loss. Yet the same living biological
individual remains under study, retains other capacities, remains embedded in
relationships, and remains institutionally tracked as the same legal/social
individual.

Therefore:

```text
AutobiographicalContinuity
!= OrganismicContinuity
```

and:

```text
AutobiographicalSelf
!= HumanIndividual
```

Some amnesic patients can also retain a stable self-description while failing to
update it to reflect post-injury changes. This further separates:

```text
SelfModelMaintenance
!= SelfModelUpdating
```

# 13. Person

`Person` is the most dangerous HF1 term because it carries several descriptive
and normative traditions at once.

HF1 therefore forbids unqualified technical use when stakes are high.

## P1 — psychological/descriptive person

A family of philosophical/psychological concepts involving some combination of:

- consciousness;
- memory;
- rationality;
- agency;
- self-awareness;
- psychological continuity.

There is no HF1-selected universal criterion.

## P2 — social person / recognized individual

The Human as addressed, named, related to and held in a network of social roles
and expectations.

## P3 — moral person

A normative status concerning moral standing, responsibility, claims or duties.

HF1 does not derive this status from one empirical threshold.

## P4 — legal person

A status/entity recognized by a legal system for legal purposes.

Legal language supplies decisive evidence that:

```text
LegalPerson != BiologicalHuman
```

as a universal equivalence. For example, US statutory interpretation can include
corporations and other organizations within `person` depending on context.

## 13.1 Supported legal capacity breaks another false equivalence

The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities explicitly combines
recognition of legal capacity with duties to provide support that may be needed
to exercise that capacity.

Therefore HF1 rejects:

```text
LegalCapacity = unsupported independent cognitive performance
```

and retains:

```text
NeedForSupport != absence of LegalPerson status
```

This boundary is particularly important because Human foundation concepts must
not become covert eligibility tests for rights.

---

# 14. Actor

HF1 defines `Actor` more weakly than `Agent`.

Working definition:

```text
Actor(entity, event)
= an entity represented as a causal/action participant or source in an event
```

Examples may include:

- a Human moving an object;
- a person signing a document;
- an organization issuing a policy;
- a software process executing a command;
- a team jointly carrying out a task.

`Actor` is often **event-indexed**.

It does not by itself establish:

- persistent autonomy;
- goals;
- consciousness;
- ownership of the outcome;
- moral responsibility;
- legal personhood.

Thus:

```text
Actor != Agent
Actor != Author
Actor != ResponsiblePerson
```

without additional structure.

---

# 15. Agent

HF1 uses `Agent` as a functional/system role, consistent with mature AI and
multi-agent literature.

A minimum Ordivon working model is:

```text
Agent
= a system that maintains relevant state or policy,
  receives/constructs information about an environment,
  and selects or initiates actions with some domain-relevant autonomy
  across more than one isolated event
```

Depending on the domain, stronger agent models may add:

- explicit goals;
- beliefs;
- planning;
- learning;
- social communication;
- commitments;
- self-modeling.

HF1 does not make those universal.

## 15.1 Agent is not a species predicate

Software agents provide the cleanest falsifier:

```text
Agent != Human
```

## 15.2 Human is not always an active Agent

A sleeping or anaesthetized Human remains Human even if current action selection
or autonomous interaction is greatly reduced.

Therefore:

```text
Human != currently active Agent
```

## 15.3 Agent, agency and sense of agency differ

```text
Agent
= system/role

Agency
= capacity/relation for initiating or controlling consequential action

SenseOfAgency
= experience of authorship/control
```

These may correlate without identity.

Thus:

```text
Agent != Agency != SenseOfAgency
```

---

# 16. Agency is not autonomy

A system/person can have causal control over an action while acting under:

- coercion;
- manipulation;
- severe option restriction;
- delegated instructions;
- institutional compulsion;
- habit;
- partially automated control.

So HF1 preserves HF0's distinction:

```text
ObjectiveControl != SenseOfAgency != Autonomy
```

`Agency` itself must later be decomposed more carefully across control,
intentionality and normative autonomy. HF1 only prevents them from collapsing.

---

# 17. Tool extension is multidimensional

The question:

> “Is the tool part of the Human?”

is too coarse to be a foundation question.

HF1 replaces it with an extension profile.

For a Human `H` and external system/tool `T`:

```text
Extension(H,T) =
[
  CausalIntegration,
  CapabilityIntegration,
  CognitiveIntegration,
  ControlIntegration,
  AgencyIntegration,
  BodyRepresentationIntegration,
  OwnershipIntegration,
  SelfLocationIntegration,
  BiologicalParthood,
  PersonalIdentityIntegration,
  LegalIdentityIntegration
]
```

Each dimension requires its own evidence.

# 18. Extension examples

## 18.1 Calculator

Likely profile in ordinary use:

```text
CausalIntegration            medium
CapabilityIntegration        high for calculation task
CognitiveIntegration         variable / debated
ControlIntegration           low-medium
AgencyIntegration            low
OwnershipIntegration         near zero bodily ownership
BiologicalParthood           no
PersonalIdentityIntegration  no default inference
```

## 18.2 External reminder / notebook

Can become highly integrated into remembering/planning and improve joint task
performance.

Clark & Chalmers supply a competing philosophical argument that sufficiently
reliable coupling may count as constitutive cognition. Cognitive-offloading
experiments establish functional reliance and performance effects but do not by
themselves settle the constitutive-metaphysical claim.

Therefore:

```text
CognitiveFunctionalIntegration
may be high
without
BiologicalParthood or PersonalIdentityIntegration
```

## 18.3 Prosthetic limb

Possible profile:

```text
ControlIntegration           high
AgencyIntegration            high
BodyRepresentation           high
Ownership                    low → high, person/context dependent
BiologicalParthood           not implied
PersonalIdentityIntegration  not implied by ownership alone
```

## 18.4 AI copilot

Possible profile:

```text
CapabilityIntegration        high
CognitiveTaskIntegration     high
DelegatedActionIntegration   variable
ControlTopology              distributed
AgencyAttribution            potentially ambiguous
BodyOwnership                usually irrelevant
BiologicalParthood           no
PersonalIdentityIntegration  no default inference
```

The important Human×AI boundary is therefore not `inside/outside` alone. It is
which **capability, cognition, control, authorship and responsibility relations**
are distributed across the coupled system.

# 19. Extension law

HF1 retains a general non-implication:

```text
Extension_D(H,T)
does not imply
Extension_E(H,T)
```

For example:

```text
Capability extension
!= body ownership extension

Agency extension
!= biological parthood

Cognitive integration
!= personal identity merger

Control integration
!= legal identity extension
```

This is one of HF1's highest-yield laws for Ordivon Human×Agent research.

---

# 20. Coupling and feedback without boundary collapse

Rejecting tool=person does **not** mean external systems are irrelevant.

The coupled process can be:

```text
Human State
+ Tool State
+ World State
        ↓
Coupled Task Process
        ↓
Joint Output
+ Changed Human State
+ Changed Tool/World State
```

External memory or AI may:

- increase immediate performance;
- change what is internally encoded;
- alter strategy;
- change future dependence;
- increase accessible options;
- redistribute verification burden;
- change agency/control topology.

Therefore:

```text
External != causally irrelevant
```

while still preserving:

```text
CoupledSystem != HumanIndividual
```

unless a specific projection explicitly justifies stronger incorporation.

# 21. Cross-context falsifier matrix

HF1 applies the candidate models to materially different cases.

| Case | Naive equivalence falsified | HF1 surviving distinction |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary sleep | Human = awake subject | Human identity != wakefulness |
| dreaming NREM/REM variation | sleep stage = consciousness state | behavioural state != experience |
| anaesthesia with isolated-forearm response | unresponsive = unconscious | responsiveness != connected consciousness |
| no later recall after command response | recall = prior consciousness | later recall != connected consciousness |
| infant before reliable mirror recognition | Human = reflective self | reflective self develops within continuing Human life |
| toddler body-size errors | bodily action = explicit body model | action/body use != mature objective body representation |
| severe autobiographical amnesia | Human/person = life-story memory | organism/legal/social continuity can survive memory disruption |
| stable old self-ratings under anterograde amnesia | self model = accurate current self | self-model maintenance != updating |
| moving rubber-hand illusion | ownership = agency | body ownership != sense of agency |
| agency over incongruent external model hand | agency = bodily ownership | external agency can occur without ownership |
| prosthetic embodiment | embodiment = biological parthood | ownership/agency/plausibility can extend without tissue incorporation |
| calculator/reminder use | capability = internal skill | situated/joint capability != unaided skill |
| extended-mind rival | cognition necessarily ends at skull | cognitive boundary is an open constitutive question in some coupled cases |
| AI delegate | agent action = Human action simpliciter | actor/agent/control/authorship topology must be declared |
| software agent | Agent = Human | functional agency != species identity |
| corporation under statutory `person` | Person = Human | legal person != biological Human |
| supported legal capacity | capacity = unsupported independence | legal status/capacity can coexist with support needs |
| coercion | action = autonomous endorsement | Actor/action != autonomy/value endorsement |
| team action | one outcome = one individual agent | collective actor/agent role != individual Human identity |
| major social-role change | same Human = same social self | social identity can change without organism replacement |

No single persistence model survives all rows as a universal identity relation.

# 22. Boundary topology

HF1's minimum positive model is not a stack of nested circles where every layer
contains the next.

A better representation is a typed relation graph:

```text
                 ┌──────────── SocialIdentity
                 │
HumanIndividual ─┼──────────── LegalPerson (jurisdiction/context dependent)
   │             │
   │             └──────────── Actor / Agent roles (event/system dependent)
   │
   ├─ OrganismalBody
   │      │
   │      ├─ BodyRepresentation
   │      └─ experienced BodyOwnership
   │
   ├─ may instantiate ExperientialSubject at t
   │
   ├─ supports multiple Self-related representations
   │      ├─ BodilySelf
   │      ├─ AgentiveSelf
   │      ├─ ReflectiveSelf
   │      ├─ AutobiographicalSelf
   │      └─ Social/RoleSelf
   │
   └─ couples to Tools / Other Humans / Institutions
          └─ may create JointCapability / DistributedControl / Agent systems
```

Arrows express relations, not identity.

# 23. Human boundary versus task-system boundary

One reason Human×AI discussions become confused is that several legitimate
boundaries coexist.

For a task:

```text
TaskSystemBoundary
may include
Human + AI + notebook + network + institution
```

For organismic tracking:

```text
HumanOrganismBoundary
is narrower
```

For legal responsibility:

```text
LegalActorBoundary
may cut the system differently again
```

For cognitive explanation:

```text
CognitiveProcessBoundary
may be disputed and task-dependent
```

Therefore:

```text
Useful system boundary != personal identity boundary
```

and:

```text
Boundary is question-relative,
not arbitrary.
```

The question and evidence burden constrain which boundary is legitimate.

# 24. Boundary criteria

HF1 proposes that every claim that changes the boundary should name at least:

```text
1. BoundaryType
2. Inclusion criterion
3. Persistence criterion
4. Timescale
```
