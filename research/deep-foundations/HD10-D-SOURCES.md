# HD10-D — Evidence Ledger

## Rule

```text
PositiveManifold != CausalExplanation
Factor != Mechanism
GeneralScore != CompleteProfile
AbilityTaxonomy != ProcessOntology
Prediction != CausalIdentity
StaticScore != LearningTrajectory
SupportedPerformance != IndependentAbility
```

---

# S01 — HCP positive manifold / bifactor g

Julien Dubois, Paola Galdi, Lynn K. Paul & Ralph Adolphs. “A distributed brain network predicts general intelligence from resting-state human neuroimaging data.” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 373:20170284 (2018). PMCID: `PMC6107566`.

The study used Human Connectome Project cognitive data. In `N=1181` participants with cognitive scores, all selected task scores correlated positively. A bifactor model with general `g` plus crystallized, speed, visuospatial and memory group factors fit substantially better than a single-factor model; the general factor accounted for a large proportion of common variance.

HD10-D use:

```text
PositiveManifold is real and strong in a modern battery.
GeneralFactor can provide powerful statistical compression.
SingleFactorTotality is unnecessary.
```

---

# S02 — 2026 distributed network architecture of g

Ramsey R. Wilcox, Babak Hemmatian, Lav R. Varshney & Aron K. Barbey. “The network architecture of general intelligence in the human connectome.” *Nature Communications* 17(1):2027 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-68698-5`. PMID: `41582154`.

`N=831` healthy young adults from HCP were used to test network-neuroscience predictions. Reported results implicated distributed multiple networks, long-range connections, network-control regions and small-world/global coordination.

HD10-D use:

```text
NeuralCorrelate(g) can be distributed/network-level.
General factor != one localized brain module.
```

This does not prove that network architecture is the unique cause of psychometric g.

# S03 — structural-functional coupling and general cognitive ability

Kirsten Hilger et al. “Structural-functional brain network coupling predicts human cognitive ability.” *NeuroImage* (2024). DOI: `10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120563`. PMID: `38492685`.

HCP `N=1030`; general cognitive ability was modeled as a latent g factor from 12 cognitive tasks.

Use:

Brain organization can carry predictive information about general ability, while the latent cognitive factor remains a measurement construct rather than direct biological identity.

# S04 — Carroll/CHC structural replication and clarification

Kevin S. McGrew. “Carroll’s Three-Stratum (3S) Cognitive Ability Theory at 30 Years: Impact, 3S-CHC Theory Clarification, Structural Replication, and Cognitive–Achievement Psychometric Network Analysis Extension.” *Journal of Intelligence* 11(2):32 (2023). DOI: `10.3390/jintelligence11020032`. PMID: `36826930`. PMCID: `PMC9959556`.

The article combines historical clarification with a previously unpublished Carroll exploratory factor analysis and a bifactor analysis of 46 cognitive/achievement tests.

HD10-D use:

```text
Carroll Three-Stratum and Cattell/Horn traditions are related but not identical.
`CHC` is better treated as a family/integration than one timeless theory.
Hierarchical broad/narrow ability structures have strong psychometric utility.
```

# S05 — lifespan differentiation using WJ-III / CHC structure

“Differentiation of Cognitive Abilities across the Lifespan.” PMCID: `PMC2855504`.

Analysis used the Woodcock-Johnson III standardization sample and explicitly modeled CHC-style broad abilities and higher-order common variance across age.

Use:

```text
Broad abilities have different developmental/age relations.
General score != no domain differentiation.
```

---

# S06 — Hebb/Cattell origin of fluid/crystallized distinction

“The Genesis of the Theory of Fluid and Crystallized Intelligence.” PMCID: `PMC5156710`. PMID: `28018191`.

Archival analysis of correspondence and historical papers reconstructs how Hebb's Intelligence A/B distinction fed into Cattell's fluid/crystallized terminology.

HD10-D use:

The original distinction involved current/direct intellectual power versus persistent organization acquired through development/experience, not literal two-substance brain ontology.

# S07 — working memory, STM and fluid intelligence

Randall W. Engle, Stephen W. Tuholski, James E. Laughlin & Andrew R. A. Conway. “Working memory, short-term memory, and general fluid intelligence: a latent-variable approach.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 128(3):309–331 (1999). DOI: `10.1037/0096-3445.128.3.309`. PMID: `10513398`.

Use:

Latent working-memory capacity is strongly related to fluid intelligence while remaining separable from short-term memory.

```text
WMC contributes strongly to Gf-related performance.
```

not:

```text
WMC = Intelligence.
```

# S08 — generality of working-memory capacity

Michael J. Kane, David Z. Hambrick, Stephen W. Tuholski, Oliver Wilhelm, Tabitha W. Payne & Randall W. Engle. “The generality of working memory capacity: a latent-variable approach to verbal and visuospatial memory span and reasoning.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 133(2):189–217 (2004). DOI: `10.1037/0096-3445.133.2.189`. PMID: `15149250`.

`N=236`, with multiple verbal/visuospatial WM/STM and reasoning/Gf tasks.

Use:

Supports substantial domain-general WMC variance and relations with reasoning, while leaving room for domain/process specificity.

# S09 — WMC versus processing speed / STM for Gf

“Latent variable analysis of working memory capacity, short-term memory capacity, processing speed, and general fluid intelligence.” *Intelligence* 30(2):163–183 (2002). DOI: `10.1016/S0160-2896(01)00096-4`.

`N=120` healthy young adults; WMC was a stronger predictor of Gf than STM or processing-speed latent constructs in the reported model.

Use:

```text
WMC is an important explanatory candidate.
```

but predictive dominance in one model is not mechanism identity.

# S10 — Thomson bonds model revival

David J. Bartholomew, Ian J. Deary & Martin Lawn. “A new lease of life for Thomson’s bonds model of intelligence.” *Psychological Review* 116(3):567–579 (2009). DOI: `10.1037/a0016262`. PMID: `19618987`.

Modernizes Thomson's sampling/bonds model and argues that positive covariance can be generated without a single general causal factor.

HD10-D use:

```text
Psychometric g structure does not uniquely identify single-general-cause architecture.
```

---

# S11 — direct Bonds versus Spearman model comparison

David J. Bartholomew, Mark Allerhand & Ian J. Deary. “Measuring mental capacity: Thomson’s Bonds model and Spearman’s g-model compared.” *Intelligence* 41(4):222–233 (2013). DOI: `10.1016/j.intell.2013.03.007`.

The paper develops/fits a contemporary Thomson model and emphasizes that Spearman-style and bonds models can be statistically very difficult to distinguish while carrying radically different causal interpretations.

Use:

```text
SameObservedCovariance != UniqueCausalWorld
```

---

# S12 — Process Overlap Theory

Kristof Kovacs & Andrew R. A. Conway. “Process Overlap Theory: A Unified Account of the General Factor of Intelligence.” *Psychological Inquiry* 27(3):151–177 (2016). DOI: `10.1080/1047840X.2016.1153946`.

POT proposes that tests overlap in domain-general executive processes plus domain-specific processes; broadly shared executive requirements generate much of positive covariance.

Use:

```text
g can be statistical index of overlapping processes rather than one process.
```

---

# S13 — empirical POT pressure: WMC/Gf/executive processes

Tengfei Wang et al. “How Executive Processes Explain the Overlap between Working Memory Capacity and Fluid Intelligence: A Test of Process Overlap Theory.” *Journal of Intelligence* 9(2):21 (2021). DOI: `10.3390/jintelligence9020021`. PMID: `33917495`. PMCID: `PMC8167629`.

Reported results did not support the tested specific POT prediction that proposed specific executive processes explained the shared WMC/Gf variance; a common executive factor played a larger role.

Use:

```text
POT family can survive while one process assignment fails.
```

# S14 — further POT/process pressure

Gidon T. Frischkorn & Claudia C. von Bastian. “In Search of the Executive Cognitive Processes Proposed by Process-Overlap Theory.” *Journal of Intelligence* 9(3):43 (2021). DOI: `10.3390/jintelligence9030043`. PMID: `34449666`. PMCID: `PMC8395920`.

Use:

Specific executive-process factors had limited/inconsistent relations with fluid-intelligence common variance, while processing-speed factors showed stronger relations in the reported models.

```text
OneNamedExecutiveProcessFamily != EstablishedGfMechanism
```

# S15 — mutualism

Han L. J. van der Maas, Conor V. Dolan, Raoul P. P. P. Grasman, Jelte M. Wicherts, Hilde M. Huizenga & Maartje E. J. Raijmakers. “A dynamical model of general intelligence: the positive manifold of intelligence by mutualism.” *Psychological Review* 113(4):842–861 (2006). DOI: `10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.842`. PMID: `17014305`.

The model demonstrates how reciprocal positive interactions among cognitive processes during development can generate a positive manifold and hierarchical structure without one underlying g cause.

Use:

```text
Developmental reciprocal causation is a genuine alternative explanation of g-like covariance.
```

# S16 — network models integrating sampling/mutualism/central processes

Han L. J. van der Maas et al. “Network Models for Cognitive Development and Intelligence.” *Journal of Intelligence* 5(2):16 (2017). DOI: `10.3390/jintelligence5020016`. PMCID: `PMC6526461`.

The paper develops network approaches combining mutualistic development with sampling and possible central processes/environmental interactions.

HD10-D use:

```text
Multiple causes of positive manifold can coexist in one formal architecture.
```

# S17 — fractionating human intelligence

Adam Hampshire, Roger R. Highfield, Beth L. Parkin & Adrian M. Owen. “Fractionating Human Intelligence.” *Neuron* 76(6):1225–1237 (2012). DOI: `10.1016/j.neuron.2012.06.022`. PMID: `23259956`.

A very large online cognitive battery plus neuroimaging analyses recovered separable reasoning, short-term memory and verbal components associated with different networks; simulations suggested higher-order g-like covariance could arise when tasks co-recruit multiple systems.

Use:

```text
High-g task performance can reflect multiple-system recruitment.
```

not one indivisible process.

Florian Schmiedek, Martin Lövdén, Timo von Oertzen & Ulman Lindenberger. “Within-person structures of daily cognitive performance differ from between-person structures of cognitive abilities.” *PeerJ* 8:e9290 (2020). DOI: `10.7717/peerj.9290`. PMID: `32551201`. PMCID: `PMC7292017`.

`101` adults, `9` cognitive tasks, about `100` occasions.

# S19 — dynamic learning potential in children

“Learning Potential in high IQ children: The contribution of dynamic assessment to the identification of gifted children.” *Learning and Individual Differences* 21(2):176–181 (2011). DOI: `10.1016/j.lindif.2010.11.025`.

`127` children (`64` high-IQ, `63` average-IQ) received multiple dynamic-test measures; learning-potential measures differentiated groups and provided predictive information in the reported design.

Use:

```text
Static score and response-to-instruction are distinguishable evidence surfaces.
```

# S20 — dynamic analogical reasoning and planning

Bart Vogelaar, Wilma C. M. Resing, Femke E. Stad & Sophie W. Sweijen. “Is planning related to dynamic testing outcomes? Investigating the potential for learning of gifted and average-ability children.” *Acta Psychologica* 196:87–95 (2019). DOI: `10.1016/j.actpsy.2019.04.004`. PMID: `31005781`.

Use:

Dynamic-test outcomes can reveal instructional need/modifiability beyond a one-shot current-performance score.

# S21 — 2026 computerized dynamic analogical reasoning

“The Spanish version of the computerized dynamic test of analogical reasoning.” PMID: `41812397` (2026).

The mediated-instruction group showed greater pre/post progress than controls in nonverbal analogical reasoning; the instrument was designed to capture learning potential and support needs.

Use:

Current dynamic-assessment evidence continues to support:

```text
CurrentPerformance != ModifiabilityProfile
```

while remaining protocol/domain specific.

# S22 — 2026 ARC-AGI Human fluid-intelligence bridge — preprint

Jasmin Thelen & Oliver Wilhelm. “Bringing Back Rule Induction to Fluid Intelligence Research? An Initial Validation of the ARC-AGI Benchmark in Humans.” arXiv:`2607.11263` (2026 preprint).

`N=100`; a compiled ARC-AGI Human score had good initial psychometric properties and correlated substantially (`rho≈.63`) with a figural fluid-intelligence test in the reported first study.

HD10-D use:

```text
AI-origin benchmark can be embedded in Human cognitive nomological networks.
```

But the study is initial/preprint evidence and does not establish ARC as intelligence totality.

# S23 — ARC-AGI-3 interactive agentic benchmark — 2026 preprint

ARC Prize Foundation. “ARC-AGI-3: A New Challenge for Frontier Agentic Intelligence.” arXiv:`2603.24621` (2026).

The benchmark shifts to interactive novel environments requiring exploration, inferred goals, internal-model construction and planning; construction/calibration used extensive Human testing.

HD10-D use:

Agent-era benchmarks expose a wider adaptive-capability surface involving learning efficiency, exploration and sequential action rather than static answer accuracy alone.

This source concerns artificial-system benchmarking and Human calibration, not proof of Human cognitive ontology.

# S24 — 2026 ARC-AGI-3 Human dataset

ARC Prize Foundation. “Measuring Human Performance on ARC-AGI-3” (2026 dataset/report), controlled study of `458` participants.

Use:

Human performance distributions can provide calibration for interactive novel-task benchmarks and make Human/artificial-system comparisons empirically testable.

CommonTaskSurface != CommonMechanism

# Evidence synthesis

The evidence supports a plural layered conclusion:

```text
Positive manifold is robust.
General factors are useful.
Broad/narrow ability structure is useful.
None of those facts uniquely identifies a common causal substance.

Working memory/executive processes matter but are insufficient as totality.
Sampling and process overlap can generate common covariance through shared demands.
Mutualism can generate common covariance developmentally.
Multiple neural/cognitive systems can jointly produce high-g task performance.
Within-person cognitive architecture can diverge from group-level factor structure.
Static performance and learning response are distinct.
Tool/Agent support changes the task-demand and attribution boundary.
```

Therefore Ordivon retains statistical ability coordinates while refusing to turn them into Human essence.
