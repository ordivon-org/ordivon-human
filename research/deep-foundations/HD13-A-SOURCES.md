# HD13-A — Primary Evidence Ledger

## S01 — Wynn 1990 — counting procedure vs cardinal principle

`Children's understanding of counting.`
Cognition 36(2):155–193.
PMID: 2225756. DOI: 10.1016/0010-0277(90)90003-3.

Children could recite/use portions of counting routines before understanding that the final count word answers `how many`; smaller number-word meanings emerged before broad cardinal-principle induction.

Use:

```text
CountingProcedure != CardinalPrincipleKnowledge
CountListKnowledge != ExactCardinalityConcept
```

---

## S02 — Sarnecka & Carey 2008 — successor/unit change

`How counting represents number: what children must learn and when they learn it.`
Cognition 108(3):662–674.
PMID: 18572155. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2008.05.007.

Cardinal-principle knowers, unlike subset-knowers, showed stronger understanding that adding/subtracting items maps onto movement through the numeral list and that +1 maps to exactly the next numeral.

Use:

```text
CountSequenceFamiliarity != SuccessorKnowledge
```

---

## S03 — Sarnecka & Wright 2013 — cardinality, succession, equinumerosity

`The idea of an exact number: children's understanding of cardinality and equinumerosity.`
Cognitive Science 37(8):1493–1506.
PMID: 23672476. DOI: 10.1111/cogs.12043.

For tested exact numbers, cardinality/equinumerosity/succession form related conceptual achievements.

Use:

```text
ExactNumberConcept includes relations beyond word recitation.
ExactEquality != NumberNaming
```

## S04 — Cheung et al. 2022 — gradual counting-principle acquisition

`Acquisition of the counting principles during the subset-knower stages: Insights from children's errors.`
Developmental Science 25(4):e13219.
PMID: 34935245. DOI: 10.1111/desc.13219.

Secondary analysis of Give-N errors suggests component counting principles can emerge gradually and that implementation/coordinating procedures can lag underlying knowledge.

Use:

```text
GiveNFailure != NoCardinalityKnowledgeByDefinition
```

## S05 — Davidson et al. 2012 — no single semantic induction over count list

`Does learning to count involve a semantic induction?`
Cognition.
PMID: 22245033. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2011.12.013.

Even cardinal-principle knowers often lacked generalized knowledge of greater-than relations and exact +1 successor relations across all known count words; knowledge could remain item-specific.

Use:

```text
CardinalPrincipleStatus != CompleteNaturalNumberTheory
```

## S06 — Wagner et al. 2015 — bilingual mapping vs general counting logic

`Why is number word learning hard? Evidence from bilingual learners.`
Cognitive Psychology.
PMID: 26413888. DOI: 10.1016/j.cogpsych.2015.08.006.

Small number-word meanings were learned partly language-specifically while broader counting competence showed different cross-language structure.

Use:

```text
NumberWordMapping != NumberConceptIdentity
```

## S07 — Brannon 2002 — ordinal numerical knowledge in infancy

`The development of ordinal numerical knowledge in infancy.`
Cognition 83(3):223–240.
PMID: 11934402. DOI: 10.1016/S0010-0277(02)00005-7.

11-month-olds discriminated increasing vs decreasing numerosity sequences in the tested paradigm while 9-month-olds did not; nonnumerical order performance showed a different developmental pattern.

Use:

```text
OrdinalNumericalRelation != CardinalityByDefinition
```

## S08 — Vogel et al. 2014 — symbolic magnitude vs order

`Differential processing of symbolic numerical magnitude and order in first-grade children.`
PMID: 25240153.

Both magnitude and order tasks showed numerical distance effects, but individual differences in task performance/distance effects were uncorrelated and related differently to arithmetic achievement.

Use:

```text
MagnitudeProcessing != OrderProcessingByDefinition
DistanceEffect != OneMechanismProof
```

## S09 — Dehaene & Cohen 1994 — subitizing vs counting

`Dissociable mechanisms of subitizing and counting: neuropsychological evidence from simultanagnosic patients.`
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 20(5):958–975.
PMID: 7964531. DOI: 10.1037/0096-1523.20.5.958.

Severe serial-counting impairments did not imply equivalent small-set enumeration loss.

Use:

```text
Subitizing != Counting
```

## S10 — Demeyere et al. 2012 — lesion mapping of subitizing vs counting

`The neuroanatomy of visual enumeration: differentiating necessary neural correlates for subitizing versus counting in a neuropsychological voxel-based morphometry study.`
Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience.
PMID: 22220729. DOI: 10.1162/jocn_a_00188.

Small-set and large-set enumeration impairments were associated with distinguishable lesion patterns after controlling visual deficits.

Use:

```text
SmallSetEnumeration != LargeSetCounting
```

## S11 — Piazza et al. 2011 — subitizing and object individuation

`Subitizing reflects visuo-spatial object individuation capacity.`
PMID: 21679934.

Individual subitizing capacity covaried with visual object-individuation capacity, supporting an object-indexing account over a simple Weberian continuation account in the tested experiments.

Use:

```text
Subitizing can depend on object individuation.
Subitizing != ANSByDefinition
```

## S12 — Burr et al. 2011 — shared subitizing/MOT resources

`Evidence for a shared mechanism used in multiple-object tracking and subitizing.`
PMID: 21968785.

Concurrent multiple-object tracking reduced subitizing capacity roughly with tracked-object load, unlike an equally difficult nonvisual working-memory control.

Use:

```text
Subitizing shares visual-object resources with tracking.
```

## S13 — van Oeffelen et al. 2014 — pattern recognition in small exact enumeration

`The role of pattern recognition in children's exact enumeration of small numbers.`
PMID: 24862903. DOI: 10.1111/bjdp.12032.

Enumeration performance in young children depended on presentation duration/configuration; canonical patterns could support exact small-set labeling.

