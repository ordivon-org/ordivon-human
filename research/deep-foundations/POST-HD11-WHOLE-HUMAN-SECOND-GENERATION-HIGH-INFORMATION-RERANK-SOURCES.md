# Post-HD11 Second-Generation Re-Ranking — Evidence Ledger

## T01 — Sadibolova et al. 2025 — latent structure of interval timing

`Uncovering the latent structure of human time perception.`
Cognition 257:106078 (2025).
PMID: 39938399.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106078.

Two experiments, N=302 each, using duration reproduction over subsecond/suprasecond intervals.

Result used:

```text
GeneralTimingFactor
+
CentralTendencyBiasFactor
```

provided a better account than a simple clean short-clock/long-clock discontinuity.

Pressure:

```text
TimingPerformance != PureClockReadout
ObservedShortLongDifference != DistinctClockSystemsByDefinition
```

---

## T02 — Mohammad Alipour, Butler & Grahn 2026 — duration/sequence/beat across modalities

`Duration, Sequence and Beat Perception across Modalities.`
Multisensory Research (2026), online ahead of print.
PMID: 42155997.
DOI: 10.1163/22134808-bja10194.

Participants completed single-duration, nonbeat-sequence and beat-sequence timing tasks in auditory and visual modalities.

Result used:

```text
Auditory modality
→ supported a duration → sequence → beat hierarchy

Visual modality
→ same hierarchy did not hold
```

Pressure:

```text
TimingAbility != one modality-invariant scalar
BeatPerception != DurationPerceptionByDefinition
```

---

## T03 — Weng et al. 2026 — perceptual vs motor temporal rhythm learning

`Perceptual Temporal Structure Supports Rhythm Learning and Enhances Theta Oscillations When Perception and Action Are Dissociated.`
Brain Sciences 16(5):489 (2026).
PMID: 42192800.
DOI: 10.3390/brainsci16050489.

Three experiments separated rhythm in visual input from rhythm in motor response.

Result used:

```text
visuomotor rhythm → learning
visual-only rhythm → learning
motor-only rhythm → no reliable learning
```

Pressure:

```text
TemporalRhythmLearning
!= RepeatedMotorTimingByDefinition
```

and supports a perceptual temporal-structure contribution independent of motor repetition.

## T04 — Gundogdu et al. 2026 — duration-specific timing in adolescent ADHD

`Duration-specific time perception impairments in adolescents with ADHD: links to executive function and symptom dimensions.`
Child Neuropsychology (2026), online ahead of print.
PMID: 42447042.
DOI: 10.1080/09297049.2026.2703819.

N=54 adolescents, comparing duration discrimination, multiple-interval reproduction and 53-second estimation.

Result used:

```text
ADHD group showed selective reproduction differences
without uniform impairment across all timing tasks.
```

Pressure:

```text
TemporalProcessingDeficit != one global timing loss
TimingTask_D != TimingTask_E
```

## T05 — Chen et al. 2025 — attention and time perception around saccades

`Attention modulates subjective time perception across eye movements.`
Vision Research (2025).
PMID: 39778360.
DOI: 10.1016/j.visres.2025.108540.

Use:

```text
Attention can perturb temporal judgment;
attention therefore must be separated as contributor from timing target.
```

## T06 — sleep restriction and subjective time 2025

`Altered subjective time perception and cognitive performance following three days of sleep restriction in healthy adults.`
PMID: 41313081.
DOI: 10.1080/13803395.2025.2596925.

N=31 healthy adults in regular-sleep versus three-night sleep-restriction conditions.

Result used only as pressure:

```text
organismic/sleep state can shift subjective timing and memory performance
```

without implying sleep owns timing.

## P01 — Gillan-group / transdiagnostic cognitive arbitration 2025

`Cognitive arbitration between candidate dimensions of psychopathology.`
PMID: 41094062.

Nine datasets, N=7,565, comparing psychiatric symptom factor solutions against cognitive outcomes including model-based planning and metacognition.

Use:

```text
Psychopathology dimensions can be evaluated by external cognitive correlates;
diagnostic/symptom structure need not map to one mechanism.
```

Supports psychopathology as a falsifier/perturbation continent rather than automatic Foundation.

## P02 — Luther et al. 2026 — effort decision mechanisms across severe mental illness

`Computational phenotypes underlying effort-based decision-making and negative symptoms in a transdiagnostic severe mental illness sample.`
Molecular Psychiatry (2026).
PMID: 41691110.
DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03474-x.

N=920 across schizophrenia, first-episode psychosis, bipolar disorder, depression, clinical-high-risk, other clinical and healthy groups.

Use:

```text
similar clinical outcomes need not arise from identical decision mechanisms.
```

Pressure against `diagnosis = mechanism = new Foundation`.

## A01 — Human–GenAI collaboration experiments 2025

`Human-generative AI collaboration enhances task performance but undermines human's intrinsic motivation.`
PMID: 40301425.

Four online experiments, total N=3,562.

Result used:

```text
GenAI collaboration improved immediate task performance;
that augmentation did not persist as an equivalent solo-performance gain;
transition to solo work changed control, intrinsic motivation and boredom measures.
```

Pressure:

```text
JointPerformanceGain != HumanIndependentCapabilityGain
```

and supports existing HOC1/HOC3/HF4 attribution rather than a new owner.

---

## A02 — longitudinal social-chatbot use 2026

`How Does Turning to AI for Companionship Predict Loneliness and Vice Versa?`
Psychological Science (2026).
PMID: 41870975.
DOI: 10.1177/09567976261427747.

12-month longitudinal study with more than 2,000 adults across four Western countries.

Use:

```text
social chatbot use and loneliness/social connection can have bidirectional longitudinal relations.
```

This is strong evidence that persistent Agent interaction can alter Human trajectories, while ownership still maps to HF22/HF12/HF21/HF6 rather than a new generic Human-Agent state.

## A03 — GenAI cognitive offloading 2026

`When Thinking Is Outsourced: Cognitive Offloading and the Heterogeneity of Critical Thinking Among Chinese University Students Using Generative Artificial Intelligence.`
PMID: 42505927.

N=353 questionnaire study.

Use cautiously because constructs are self-reported.

It supports heterogeneity of Human-AI collaboration/offloading profiles, not a causal Foundation claim.

## G01 — identity fusion and intergroup cooperation 2025

`Identity fusion can foster intergroup trust and willingness to cooperate.`
PMID: 40817154.
DOI: 10.1038/s44271-025-00303-9.

Cross-regional samples included the Bangsamoro region plus Gambia, Pakistan, Tanzania and Uganda.

Use:

```text
IdentityFusion != OutgroupHostilityByDefinition
```

because contextual appraisal moderated relations with trust/cooperation.

Supports HF1/HF12/HF13 decomposition.

## L01 — long-term grief trajectories 2025

`Grief trajectories and long-term health effects in bereaved relatives: a prospective, population-based cohort study with ten-year follow-up.`
PMID: 40786154.

N=1,735 bereaved relatives with repeated grief measurement and long-term health follow-up.

Use:

```text
BereavementOutcome != one universal trajectory.
```

Supports grief as a heterogeneous life-course/domain process spanning HF22/HF21/HD9 rather than one Foundation.

# Synthesis

The ranking consequence is asymmetric:

```text
Temporal cognition
→ sparse current Ordivon ownership + strong ordinary-function dissociations
→ highest new deep-route information

Psychopathology
→ stronger falsifier pressure than owner signal

Human–Agent adaptation
→ very high Agent-era importance, but current outcomes map to existing Human owners

Sleep / collective identity / grief
→ high domain value with stronger existing decomposition
```

No source in this ledger is treated as sufficient evidence for HF24 admission.
