# HD2 — Primary Evidence Ledger

## Evidence rule

HD2 uses primary empirical work to discriminate constructs and model families. A study can
support one separation without establishing a universal emotion theory. PubMed IDs are
preferred below because some publisher/Crossref metadata disagree on secondary identifiers;
the PMID resolves the exact indexed article.

## S01 — Emotion meaning is not culturally fixed by posed faces

Gendron et al. (2014), **Perceptions of emotion from facial expressions are not culturally
universal: evidence from a remote culture.** Emotion.

PMID: `24708506`

Use: U.S. and Himba participants sorted posed expressions differently when emotion-concept
cues were absent; conceptual/task context increased similarity but substantial cultural
variation remained.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24708506/

## S02 — `Fear` gasping face can signal threat/anger

Crivelli et al. (2016), **The fear gasping face as a threat display in a Melanesian
society.** PNAS.

PMID: `27791137`

Use: Trobriand adolescents mapped the Western fear-gasping face to anger/threat and selected
it as threatening, unlike Spanish participants. Strong falsifier of a universal one-face ↔
one-emotion dictionary.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27791137/

## S03 — Dynamic facial signaling has shared and culture-specific structure

Jack and collaborators (2024), **Cultural facial expressions dynamically convey emotion
category and intensity information.** Current Biology.

PMID: `38141619`

Use: East Asian and Western European perceivers use temporally structured movement
components for category and intensity, with both commonalities and cultural differences.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38141619/

## S04 — Posed and spontaneous expressions are not perceptually equivalent

2025 preregistered multi-study paper, **Emotions are perceived differently from posed and
spontaneous facial expressions.** Emotion.

PMID: `40014508`

Use: 2,408 perceivers judging 1,244 dynamic expressions showed much higher recognition for
posed than spontaneous expressions and different valence/recognition patterns across three
studies, including cross-cultural testing.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014508/

## S05 — Emotion concepts can shape perceptual representation

2024, **Emotional concepts shape the perceptual representation of body expressions.**
Human Brain Mapping.

PMID: `39185719`

Use: semantic/conceptual emotion structure predicted body-expression representations; EEG
and fMRI analyses found effects on perceptual/neural representation, including around
~170 ms. Supports concept→perception influence without proving concept sufficiency.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39185719/

## S06 — Dimensional and discrete structure unfold dynamically

2020, **Neural signatures of dynamic emotion constructs in the human brain.**
Neuropsychologia.

PMID: `29037506`

Use: MEG/RSA showed valence/arousal and several discrete emotion ratings explained neural
representations with different temporal signatures. Supports non-exclusive category and
dimension projections.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29037506/

## S07 — Category + dimension can outperform either alone

2015, **Multivariate neural biomarkers of emotional states are categorically distinct.**
Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience.

PMID: `25813790`

Use: emotion categories could be decoded across film/music inductions; model comparison
favoured combining categorical and dimensional information rather than simple
valence/arousal alone.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25813790/

## S08 — Distributed category-specific patterns across induction methods

2016, **Discrete Neural Signatures of Basic Emotions.** Cerebral Cortex.

PMID: `25924952`

Use: MVPA classified six induced emotion categories and generalized across movie versus
imagery induction. Supports distributed discriminability, not one-module-per-emotion.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25924952/

## S09 — High-dimensional event features organize inferred emotion

Skerry & Saxe (2015), **Neural representations of emotion are organized around abstract
event features.** Cerebral Cortex.

PMID: `26212878`

Use: mentalizing-region patterns represented fine-grained situational emotion distinctions;
a high-dimensional space of event/appraisal features outperformed simpler affective
valence/arousal accounts for those inferences.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26212878/

## S10 — Emotion representation can generalize across face and situation cues

Skerry & Saxe (2014), **A common neural code for perceived and inferred emotion.** Journal
of Neuroscience.

PMID: `25429141`

Use: medial prefrontal patterns generalized emotional valence across facial-expression and
situational-inference stimuli, supporting abstraction beyond raw facial features.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25429141/

## S11 — Amygdala is not fear

Feinstein et al. (2013), **Fear and panic in humans with bilateral amygdala damage.**
Nature Neuroscience.

PMID: `23377128`

Use: 35% CO2 evoked fear and panic in three people with bilateral amygdala damage,
distinguishing internal/interoceptive fear routes from canonical external-threat/amygdala
accounts.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23377128/

## S12 — Negative valence does not imply withdrawal

Harmon-Jones & Allen (1998), **Anger and frontal brain activity: EEG asymmetry consistent
with approach motivation despite negative affective valence.** Journal of Personality and
Social Psychology.

PMID: `9599445`

Use: dispositional anger related to left-anterior activity consistent with approach
motivation despite negative valence.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599445/

## S13 — Caregiver buffering is a developmental affect-regulation mechanism

Gee et al. (2014), **Maternal buffering of human amygdala-prefrontal circuitry during
childhood but not during adolescence.** Psychological Science.

PMID: `25280904`

Use: maternal stimuli suppressed amygdala reactivity and altered amygdala-prefrontal
connectivity/regulation in children but not adolescents. Supports relational/scaffolded
regulation and developmental change.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25280904/

## S14 — Shame and guilt can dissociate

2021, **Feelings of shame and guilt are associated with distinct neural activation in
youth.** Biological Psychology.

PMID: `33484753`

Use: shame and guilt ratings during social moral evaluation modulated distinct neural
patterns, supporting separation of social-emotion categories and the need for relational
appraisal coordinates.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33484753/

## S15 — Interoceptive processing changes under homeostatic perturbation

2021, **Perceptual insensitivity to the modulation of interoceptive signals in depression,
anxiety, and substance use disorders.** Scientific Reports.

PMID: `33483527`

Use: healthy participants improved heartbeat-perception performance under perturbation more
than several clinical groups. Supports adaptive/contextual interoceptive processing rather
than a fixed body-awareness scalar.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33483527/

## S16 — Interoceptive precision updating can differ transdiagnostically

Smith and collaborators (2020), **A Bayesian computational model reveals a failure to adapt
interoceptive precision estimates across depression, anxiety, eating, and substance use
disorders.** PLoS Computational Biology.

PMID: `33315893`

Use: computational fits to heartbeat/breath-hold behaviour supported reduced adaptation of
interoceptive precision in several clinical groups, without strong evidence for altered
priors in that paradigm.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315893/

## S17 — Similar physiology can accompany different affective response

2020, **Heightened affective response to perturbation of respiratory but not pain signals
in eating, mood, and anxiety disorders.** PLoS ONE.

PMID: `32667951`

Use: clinical groups reported greater stress/suffocation fear during breath holding without
corresponding group differences in measured respiratory/cardiovascular or behavioural
responses. Strong `physiology != feeling` falsifier.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32667951/

## S18 — Interoceptive performance and emotional interpretation can dissociate

2022, **Disentangling interoceptive abilities in alexithymia.** Psychological Research.

PMID: `34097132`

Use: higher alexithymia related differently to interoceptive accuracy/sensibility versus
metacognitive awareness, pressure-testing `body accuracy = emotion insight`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34097132/

## S19 — Preschool interoception is not identical to emotion recognition/regulation

2019, **Interoceptive accuracy, emotion recognition, and emotion regulation in preschool
children.** International Journal of Psychophysiology.

PMID: `30731102`

Use: adapted cardiac perturbation task showed age-related changes and non-equivalence among
interoceptive performance, emotion recognition and regulation.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30731102/

## S20 — Momentary affect integrates expectations and prediction errors

Rutledge et al. (2014), **A computational and neural model of momentary subjective
well-being.** PNAS.

PMID: `25092308`

Use: momentary happiness in a probabilistic reward task depended on recent reward
expectations and prediction errors rather than current earnings alone; replicated at large
scale within the study programme.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25092308/

## S21 — Mood manipulation can alter learning

2014, **Effects of positive mood on probabilistic learning: behavioral and
electrophysiological correlates.** Biological Psychology.

PMID: `25265572`

Use: induced positive mood increased estimated learning rate while not simply increasing
all performance, supporting mood→learning feedback.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25265572/

## S22 — Sleep state changes emotional reactivity

Yoo et al. (2007), **The human emotional brain without sleep—a prefrontal amygdala
disconnect.** Current Biology.

PMID: `17956744`

Use: sleep deprivation changed emotional brain reactivity/connectivity, providing an
altered-state falsifier for trait-like emotion accounts.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17956744/

## S23 — Pharmacological perturbation separates neural/behavioral/affective endpoints

2025 randomized clinical trial, **Negative Affect Circuit Subtypes and Neural, Behavioral,
and Affective Responses to MDMA.**

PMID: `40305021`

Use: within-participant pharmacological manipulation measures separable neural,
behavioural and affective responses, supporting endpoint-specific perturbation analysis.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40305021/

## S24 — Spontaneous expression can differ without implying absent emotion

2025, **Positive emotional valence in spontaneous facial expressions of autistic
adolescents.**

PMID: `40316592`

Use: autistic and non-autistic adolescents differed in spontaneous expression duration and
valence patterns. Supports separating expression production from internal experience and
perceiver interpretation.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40316592/

## S25 — Threat detection is distributed beyond cortex-centric emotion stories

2026, **Human superior colliculus pathways represent the form and motion of looming
objects.** Cell Reports.

PMID: `41758648`

Use: superior colliculus encoded looming-object form/motion and interacted with distributed
networks; responses covaried with fear/valence/arousal measures. Supports conserved
defensive processing without identifying one `fear center`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41758648/

## Evidence boundary

HD2 does not treat any single induction method, facial-expression task, fMRI classifier,
interoceptive task or self-report scale as emotion ontology. The evidence set is designed
to force category separations and preserve multiple mechanistic families. Later deep work
may revise model details without automatically reopening the thin canonical foundation.
