# HD10-B — Evidence Ledger

## Rule

```text
Reliability != Validity
Validity != Mechanism
Prediction != Explanation
Invariance != Ontology
NonInvariance != ConstructUnreality
GroupEffect != IndividualDifferenceReliability
MoreDataPoints != BroaderContextCoverage
Neural != PrivilegedTruth
```

# S01 — Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing

American Educational Research Association, American Psychological Association, National Council on Measurement in Education. *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*, 2014 edition.

Official open-access portal:

`https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html`

Use:

The joint standards are the principal US professional reference for evidence supporting test-score interpretation/use, reliability/precision, fairness and appropriate testing practice.

HD10-B retains the general principle:

```text
Validity concerns the evidential/theoretical support for a proposed score interpretation/use,
not a magical property attached to an instrument name.
```

# S02 — Hedge, Powell & Sumner: reliability paradox

Craig Hedge, Georgina Powell & Petroc Sumner. “The reliability paradox: Why robust cognitive tasks do not produce reliable individual differences.” *Behavior Research Methods* 50:1166–1186 (2018; online 2017). DOI: `10.3758/s13428-017-0935-1`. PMCID: `PMC5990556`. PMID: `28726177`.

Three studies examined seven classic paradigms including Flanker, Stroop, stop-signal, go/no-go, Posner cueing, Navon and SNARC. Many individual-difference measures had low test–retest reliability despite robust experimental effects.

Use:

```text
ReplicableWithinPersonEffect != ReliableBetweenPersonMeasure
```

The paper also demonstrates that low between-person variability can produce the paradox rather than simple large measurement error.

# S03 — Robison et al. 2026: positive modern cognitive-psychometrics counterexample

Matthew K. Robison, Stephen Campbell, Lauren D. Garner, Ciara Sibley & Joseph Coyne. “A comprehensive psychometrics of cognitive ability measures: Reliability, practice effects, and the stability of latent factor structures across retesting.” *Behavior Research Methods* 58, 56 (2026). DOI: `10.3758/s13428-025-02897-8`. PMID: `41612063`. PMCID: `PMC12855318`.

Design:

```text
N = 255 young adults
24 cognitive measures
2 repeated batteries separated by ~2 weeks
7 broad cognitive construct families
```

Reported evidence included generally adequate/high intrasession and intersession reliability, high construct-level reliability, measurement-structure invariance across retest, and substantial stable trait-like variance in latent state-trait models. Some task labels nevertheless failed clean discriminability.

Use:

```text
CognitiveAbilityMeasurement can be psychometrically strong under suitable task batteries
```

while preserving:

```text
ReliableLatentStructure != OneCausalMechanism
LabelSame != VarianceSame
```

# S07 — Simms et al.: self-informant agreement and method construction

Leonard J. Simms, Kerry Zelazny, Wern How Yam & Daniel F. Gros. “Self-informant Agreement for Personality and Evaluative Person Descriptors: Comparing Methods for Creating Informant Measures.” *European Journal of Personality* 24(3):207–221 (2010). DOI: `10.1002/per.763`. PMCID: `PMC3084005`. PMID: `21541262`.

Sample: 303 undergraduate friendship dyads.

Use:

Self/informant agreement depends partly on trait observability/evaluativeness and measurement wording; alternative informant formulations captured incremental information.

```text
SelfInformantAgreement != Perfect
InformantMethod != NeutralByDefinition
```

# S08 — Oltmanns et al.: longitudinal self-other change evidence

Joshua R. Oltmanns et al. “Personality Change: Longitudinal Self–Other Agreement and Convergence with Retrospective-Reports.” PMCID: `PMC6732056`. PMID: `30843725`.

Representative community sample of 1,630 older adults with informants, three waves ~6.5 years apart.

Use:

Personality change measurement benefits from longitudinal multi-method evidence; retrospective reports are not interchangeable with prospective longitudinal change.

```text
RetrospectiveChangeReport != LongitudinalChangeMeasurement
```

# S09 — self/informant measurement invariance

“Psychometric Comparison of Self- and Informant-Reports of Personality.” PMCID: `PMC5248555`. PMID: `25612626`.

The study tested configural, metric and scalar invariance across self- and informant-report versions of several personality dimensions and found substantial comparability for the tested scales.

Use:

```text
DifferentRaterPerspective can still support comparable measurement structure
```

without implying identical information content.

“Cross-cultural examination of the Big Five Personality Trait Short Questionnaire: Measurement invariance testing and associations with mental health.” PMCID: `PMC6917272`.

Sample included US, Argentina and Spain participants and tested language/country/gender measurement invariance.

Use:

Positive evidence that some personality measurement structures can transport across declared samples.

```text
SomeTransportSuccess != UniversalOntology
```

# S11 — Tsimane Big Five transport falsifier

Michael Gurven et al. “How Universal Is the Big Five? Testing the Five-Factor Model of Personality Variation Among Forager–Farmers in the Bolivian Amazon.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* (2013). PMCID: `PMC4104167`. PMID: `23245291`.

Use:


# S12 — WISC-V cross-national measurement-invariance counterpressure

“Cross-National Generalizability of WISC-V and CHC Broad Ability Constructs across France, Spain, and the US.” PMCID: `PMC10455271`. PMID: `37623542`.

Use:


# S13 — Stachl et al.: smartphone digital traces predict questionnaire personality

Clemens Stachl et al. “Predicting personality from patterns of behavior collected with smartphones.” *PNAS* 117(30):17680–17687 (2020). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1920484117`. PMCID: `PMC7395458`.

Design:

```text
624 volunteers
30 days
~25.3 million smartphone logging events
```

Cross-validated behavior features predicted Big Five domain/facet questionnaire scores with moderate correlations.

HD10-B use:

```text
DigitalBehavior can predict questionnaire-assessed trait standing
```

but:

```text
PredictionOfTraitScore != DirectObservationOfPersonalityEssence
```

# S14 — Elliott et al.: poor task-fMRI person-level reliability

Maxwell L. Elliott et al. “What Is the Test-Retest Reliability of Common Task-Functional MRI Measures? New Empirical Evidence and a Meta-Analysis.” PMCID: `PMC7370246`.

Meta-analytic and new-dataset evidence found poor person-level test–retest reliability for many common task-fMRI activation measures, despite their utility for group-level cognitive neuroscience.

Use:

```text
RobustGroupNeuralEffect != ReliableIndividualBiomarker
```

# S17 — recent multiverse reliability evidence

“Impact of analytic decisions on test–retest reliability of individual and group estimates in functional magnetic resonance imaging: A multiverse analysis using the monetary incentive delay task.” PMCID: `PMC12290630`.

Across 240 analysis pipelines, individual-level ICC estimates remained generally low and varied with analytic choices; some reliability gains traded off against interpretability.

Use:

```text
AnalysisPipeline is part of MeasurementProcedure
HigherReliability != BetterInterpretationAutomatically
```

# S20 — Wu et al. 2025 Human–GenAI collaboration

Suqing Wu, Yukun Liu, Mengqi Ruan, Siyu Chen et al. “Human-generative AI collaboration enhances task performance but undermines human’s intrinsic motivation.” *Scientific Reports* 15, 15105 (2025). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-025-98385-2`.

# Evidence synthesis

The full evidence set supports neither psychometric nihilism nor psychometric realism-by-default.

```text
Some instruments are highly reliable.
Some famous tasks are poor for person ranking.
Some latent structures replicate across retest/groups.
Some translated structures fail transport.
Self/informant methods overlap without identity.
Dense repeated measures expose person dynamics hidden by cross-sectional scores.
Digital/neural/physiological channels add evidence but have their own reliability and construct problems.
AI/tool support makes attribution boundaries experimentally visible.
```

Therefore the durable HD10-B rule is:

```text
No measure earns ontology by being standardized, biological, predictive,
reliable, invariant, popular or high-dimensional.

No construct is erased merely because one instrument is noisy or non-invariant.

Evidence must be typed to the exact claim.
```
