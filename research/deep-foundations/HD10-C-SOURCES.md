# HD10-C — Evidence Ledger

## Evidence rule

```text
DescriptiveFactor != CausalMechanism
LexicalReplication != NaturalKind
TraitStability != Immutability
WithinPersonVariability != NoTrait
Person×SituationPattern != NoPersonStability
LatentTraitVariance != Mechanism
IdiographicPattern != Mechanism
CrossCulturalFailure != ConstructUnreality
```

---

# S01 — Goldberg: Big Five as phenotypic trait structure / taxonomy

Lewis R. Goldberg. “The structure of phenotypic personality traits.” *American Psychologist* 48(1):26–34 (1993). DOI: `10.1037/0003-066X.48.1.26`. PMID: `8427480`.

Use:

The paper traces the Big-Five factor structure as a taxonomy rooted in the lexical tradition.

```text
BigFive = powerful organization of phenotypic/person-descriptive trait space
```

not automatic proof of five mechanisms.

---

# S02 — McCrae & John: Five-Factor Model overview

Robert R. McCrae & Oliver P. John. “An Introduction to the Five-Factor Model and Its Applications.” *Journal of Personality* 60(2):175–215 (1992). DOI: `10.1111/j.1467-6494.1992.tb00970.x`. PMID: `1635039`.

Use:

Defines the FFM as a hierarchical organization of personality traits in five broad dimensions and summarizes lexical/questionnaire/observer/cultural evidence.

```text
FFM structural taxonomy != complete theory of origins/operation
```

---

# S03 — McCrae & Costa: cross-instrument / cross-observer FFM convergence

Robert R. McCrae & Paul T. Costa Jr. “Validation of the five-factor model of personality across instruments and observers.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 52(1):81–90 (1987). DOI: `10.1037/0022-3514.52.1.81`. PMID: `3820081`.

Use:

Five broad factors were recovered across adjective/questionnaire and self/peer measurement surfaces.

```text
CrossMethodConvergence supports reusable coordinates
```

without identifying mechanism.

---

# S04 — Saucier & Goldberg: lexical cross-language limits

Gerard Saucier & Lewis R. Goldberg. “Lexical studies of indigenous personality factors: premises, products, and prospects.” *Journal of Personality* 69(6):847–879 (2001). PMID: `11767821`.

Use:

Review of English and 12 other languages reported uneven reproduction of the Anglo-Germanic Big Five and considered alternative broad structures.

```text
LexicalBigFiveTransport != Guaranteed
```

The lexical hypothesis is a discovery premise, not ontology.

---

# S05 — Ashton et al.: six-factor psycholexical structure across seven languages

Michael C. Ashton, Kibeom Lee, Marco Perugini, Piotr Szarota, Reinout E. de Vries, Lisa Di Blas, Kathleen Boies & Boele De Raad. “A six-factor structure of personality-descriptive adjectives: solutions from psycholexical studies in seven languages.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 86(2):356–366 (2004). DOI: `10.1037/0022-3514.86.2.356`. PMID: `14769090`.

Use:

Similar six-factor solutions were reported in Dutch, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Korean and Polish lexical data, including Honesty–Humility and reorganized Emotionality/Agreeableness content.

```text
FiveFactors != unique lexical closure
```

---

# S06 — Lee & Ashton: HEXACO inventory

Kibeom Lee & Michael C. Ashton. “Psychometric Properties of the HEXACO Personality Inventory.” *Multivariate Behavioral Research* 39(2):329–358 (2004). DOI: `10.1207/S15327906MBR3902_8`. PMID: `26804579`.

Use:

Provides a measurement implementation of six broad factors and 24 facets with internal-structure and convergent-validity evidence.

```text
ReliableHEXACOMeasurement != SixMechanismOntology
```

---

# S07 — Ashton & Lee: HEXACO explanatory proposals

Michael C. Ashton & Kibeom Lee. “Empirical, theoretical, and practical advantages of the HEXACO model of personality structure.” *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 11(2):150–166 (2007). DOI: `10.1177/1088868306294907`. PMID: `18453460`.

Use:

Argues for HEXACO as an alternative broad structure and proposes links to reciprocal/kin altruism and other phenomena.

HD10-C retains those as testable explanatory hypotheses, not mechanism identity.

---

# S08 — Five-Factor Theory causal architecture

Robert R. McCrae & Angelina R. Sutin. “A Five-Factor Theory Perspective on Causal Analysis.” *European Journal of Personality* 32(3):151–166 (2018). DOI: `10.1002/per.2134`. PMCID: `PMC6101665`. PMID: `30140117`.

Use:

FFT separates basic tendencies from characteristic adaptations and describes broad causal pathways from biology/basic tendencies through adaptations and environment to behavior.

HD10-C use:

```text
BasicTendency != CharacteristicAdaptation
```

is useful, while strong biological/environmental insulation claims require falsification.

---

# S09 — intervention-induced personality trait change

Brent W. Roberts, Jing Luo, Daniel A. Briley, Philip I. Chow, Rong Su & Patrick L. Hill. “A systematic review of personality trait change through intervention.” *Psychological Bulletin* 143(2):117–141 (2017). DOI: `10.1037/bul0000088`. PMID: `28054797`.

The review identified 207 intervention studies and reported meaningful personality-trait measure change over interventions, including persistence beyond treatment in follow-up samples.

Use:

```text
TraitStability != EnvironmentalInsulation
```

---

# S10 — randomized digital personality-change intervention

Mirjam Stieger, Christoph Flückiger, Dominik Rüegger, Tobias Kowatsch, Brent W. Roberts & Mathias Allemand. “Changing personality traits with the help of a digital personality change intervention.” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 118(8):e2017548118 (2021). DOI: `10.1073/pnas.2017548118`. PMID: `33558417`. PMCID: `PMC7923371`.

Randomized controlled digital intervention with `N=1,523`, waitlist comparison and repeated self/informant Big-Five measurement.

Use:

Targeted volitional/interventional change provides additional pressure against fixed-essence trait models.

# S11 — Rothbart / Posner developmental temperament framework

Mary K. Rothbart, Brad E. Sheese, Michael I. Rueda & Michael I. Posner. “Developing Mechanisms of Self-Regulation in Early Life.” PMCID: `PMC3164871`. PMID: `21892360`.

The paper uses a temperament framework of constitutionally based differences in reactivity and self-regulation and describes developmental change in orienting/executive attention regulation.

Use:

```text
Temperament includes reactivity + regulation
Constitutional != Immutable
Development changes regulatory architecture
```

---

# S12 — temperament longitudinal reactive/regulatory evidence

Johanna Nielsen et al. “Reactive and Regulatory Temperament: Longitudinal Associations with Internalizing and Externalizing Symptoms through Childhood.” PMCID: `PMC6812628`. PMID: `31076976`.

Prospective longitudinal `N=536` with laboratory observation and reactive/regulatory dimensions.

Use:

Temperament is measurable through multiple methods and has longitudinal predictive structure, but exact factorization is not singular across studies.

# S13 — temperament to personality developmental continuity

Whitney R. Ringwald, Katherine M. Lawson, Aleksandra Kaurin & Richard W. Robins. “Linking Temperament and Personality Traits from Late Childhood to Adulthood by Examining Continuity, Stability, and Change.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 130(5):1047–1061 (2026 issue; final publication 2025). DOI: `10.1037/pspp0000576`. PMCID: `PMC12802452`. PMID: `40965930`.

Use:

```text
Temperament/Personality continuity can coexist with developmental mean-level change
```

so continuity does not establish identity.

# S14 — CAPS theory

Walter Mischel & Yuichi Shoda. “A cognitive-affective system theory of personality: reconceptualizing situations, dispositions, dynamics, and invariance in personality structure.” *Psychological Review* 102(2):246–268 (1995). DOI: `10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.246`. PMID: `7740090`.

Use:

Person differences are proposed in accessibility/organization of cognitive-affective units interacting with psychological features of situations, producing characteristic if–then patterns.

```text
PersonalityConsistency can reside in conditional patterns
rather than constant behavior
```

# S15 — stable if–then behavioral signatures

Yuichi Shoda, Walter Mischel & Jack C. Wright. “Intraindividual stability in the organization and patterning of behavior: Incorporating psychological situations into the idiographic analysis of personality.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 67(4):674–687 (1994). DOI: `10.1037/0022-3514.67.4.674`.

Naturalistic camp behavior showed cross-situational consistency depended on psychological similarity of situations and that individuals had distinctive stable situation–behavior profiles.

Use:

```text
StablePersonality != SameBehaviorAcrossAllSituations
```

# S16 — Whole Trait Theory

William Fleeson & Eranda Jayawickreme. “Whole Trait Theory.” *Journal of Research in Personality* 56:82–92 (2015). DOI: `10.1016/j.jrp.2014.10.009`. PMID: `26097268`. PMCID: `PMC4472377`.

Core separation:

```text
Trait_DES = density distribution of corresponding personality states
Trait_EXP = social-cognitive explanatory mechanisms
```

Use:

Whole Trait is retained as the strongest integrative template in C, while HD10 adds explicit situation-exposure and equifinality guards.

# S17 — actor/situation contribution to trait-enactment distributions

William Fleeson & Mary Kate Law. “Trait Enactments as Density Distributions: The Role of Actors, Situations, and Observers in Explaining Stability and Variability.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 109(6):1090–1104 (2015). DOI: `10.1037/a0039517`. PMID: `26348598`. PMCID: `PMC4673017`.

Use:

The study directly tests actor and situation contributions to stable/variable trait enactment, supporting the need to type actual situation exposure rather than treat aggregated behavior as person-only evidence.

---

# S18 — latent state-trait revised theory

Rolf Steyer, Axel Mayer, Christian Geiser & David A. Cole. “A Theory of States and Traits—Revised.” *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* 11:71–98 (2015). DOI: `10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-032813-153719`. PMID: `25062476`.

Use:

LST-R formally defines state/trait concepts probabilistically and links them to statistical models.

```text
LST = rigorous measurement decomposition
```

not a full causal personality theory.

---

# S19 — LST person × situation decomposition

Christian Geiser et al. “Analyzing person, situation and person × situation interaction effects: Latent state-trait models for the combination of random and fixed situations.” *Psychological Methods* 20(2):165–192 (2015). DOI: `10.1037/met0000026`. PMID: `25528499`.

Use:

Demonstrates that person-specific stable variance, situation effects, person×situation effects and measurement error can be separated under explicit models.

```text
VarianceDecomposition != MechanismIdentification
```

---

# S20 — idiographic momentary personality profiles

Colin J. Lee & Emorie D. Beck. “Idiographic Momentary Profiles of Personality Facets.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 130(1):160–185 (2026 issue; final publication 2025). DOI: `10.1037/pspp0000568`. PMID: `40658546`. PMCID: `PMC12262167`.

Two experience-sampling samples (`N=245`, `15,833` surveys) showed that most participants expressed multiple person-specific multivariate personality-state profiles and that situation characteristics predicted profile expression.

Use:

```text
OnePerson != OneStaticPersonalityPoint
PersonSpecificProfiles != PopulationAverageStructure
```

---

# S21 — Tsimane Big Five falsifier

Michael Gurven et al. “How Universal Is the Big Five? Testing the Five-Factor Model of Personality Variation Among Forager–Farmers in the Bolivian Amazon.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. PMID: `23245291`. PMCID: `PMC4104167`.

Use:

Expected Big-Five structure was not robustly recovered under the authors' tests in the studied Tsimane sample.

```text
BigFiveTransport != UniversalByDefinition
```

---

# S22 — longitudinal personality change / life events

Wiebke Bleidorn, Christopher J. Hopwood & Richard E. Lucas. “Life Events and Personality Trait Change.” *Journal of Personality* 86(1):83–96 (2018). DOI: `10.1111/jopy.12286`. PMID: `27716921`.

Use:

Evidence for life-event-related trait change is heterogeneous and still incomplete, but some event-related change exists.

This prevents both:

```text
EnvironmentNeverChangesTraits
```

and the equally bad:

```text
EveryLifeEventRewritesPersonality
```

---

# S23 — person-centered life-event trajectories

“The associations between life events and person-centered personality consistency.” DOI: `10.1111/jopy.12802`. PMID: `36537588`.

Across four datasets (`N=24,491`), event-associated effects were often small/inconsistent but showed substantial idiosyncratic trajectories.

Use:

```text
EnvironmentalEffectOnPersonality != UniformPopulationShift
```

# Evidence synthesis

No existing model is eliminated entirely.

The evidence instead supports division of labor:

```text
Lexical/Big-Five/HEXACO
→ shared description

CAPS
→ conditional response organization

Whole Trait
→ distribution + mechanism bridge

LST
→ measurement decomposition

Idiographic dynamics
→ person-specific realization

Temperament
→ developmental reactivity/regulation constraints
```

The central new HD10-C inference is:

```text
ObservedStateDistribution
= function(PersonConditionalStructure, SituationExposureDistribution, state/history)
```

so observed trait description cannot uniquely identify either internal mechanism or counterfactual response architecture.
