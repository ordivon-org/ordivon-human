# HD3 — Social Cognition, Other-Mind Modeling, Attachment, Care and Persistent Relations

## 0. Status and decision

HD3 began from the strongest residual left by HD2/HF21:

```text
observed partner / social situation
→ what does the other perceive, know, believe, want, intend or feel?
→ what is my relation to that other?
→ how does that relation persist and alter regulation/action across episodes?
```

HF8 already owns representation/belief/mental models. HF12 already owns reciprocal
interaction, partner models, common ground, trust, joint attention/joint action and
relationship history as interaction variables. HF13 owns persistent population-level
norms, conventions, reputation, status, authority and institutions. HF21 owns affective
coordination and emotion attribution.

HD3 therefore sets a stricter test than `social cognition is missing`.

The result is asymmetric:

```text
OtherMindModeling
= real deep mechanism
but can be represented as a typed relation among HF8 + HF12 + HF21 without reopening them.

PersistentDyadicRelationshipState
= repeated neighboring structure not reducible to current interaction or population-level
  social order without hidden choices.
```

Therefore:

```text
NextFoundationAdmissionCondition(persistent relational state) = true
FoundationReopenCondition(HF0–HF21) = false
```

HF22 should be a **thin persistent-relations foundation**, while HD3 remains the owner of
full social-cognition/other-mind evidence and model comparison.

---

# 1. The first error: `Social cognition` is not one capacity

At minimum separate:

```text
SocialCuePerception
AttentionToOther
PerspectiveTaking
OtherMindAttribution
BeliefAttribution
Goal/IntentionInference
EmotionAttribution
EmpathicAffectSharing
SelfOtherDistinction
EmpathicConcern / Compassion
JointAttention
TrustInference
RelationshipRepresentation
Attachment/Care
SocialPrediction
```

These can correlate but need not share one latent mechanism.

Therefore:

```text
SocialCognition != OneAbility
```

---

# 2. Other-mind modeling

Use a typed relation:

```text
OtherMindModel_D(A→B,t)
= A's task-relative uncertain representation of selected latent states of B at t
```

Possible modeled states include:

```text
perceptual access
attention
knowledge
belief
uncertainty
goal
preference
intention
emotion
future action policy
```

No complete model of B is required.

---

# 3. Other-mind model is not the other mind

```text
OtherMindModel_D(A→B)
!= B's actual mental state
```

Communication, behavior, context and history provide evidence, not transparent access.

Thus:

```text
Mindreading != MindAccess
```

---

# 4. Mentalizing / Theory of Mind are umbrella terms

`Theory of Mind`, `mentalizing` and `mindreading` are used for overlapping families of
abilities. HD3 does not treat any unqualified term as a single primitive.

Specify the target:

```text
PerceptualPerspectiveAttribution
KnowledgeAttribution
BeliefAttribution
FalseBeliefAttribution
GoalInference
IntentionInference
AffectiveMentalizing
TraitInference
```

Therefore:

```text
ToM_D != ToM_E
```

---

# 5. False-belief success is not Theory of Mind totality

False-belief tasks are powerful because they separate:

```text
RealityState
from
AgentBeliefAboutReality
```

But:

```text
FalseBeliefPerformance != SocialCognitionTotality
```

It does not directly establish empathy, trust, attachment, social prediction accuracy or
naturalistic relationship competence.

---

# 6. Mental-state attribution is not necessarily deliberate

Acquired left posterior TPJ damage can selectively impair spontaneous use of another's
belief while leaving explicitly instructed belief reasoning more available.

Therefore:

```text
SpontaneousBeliefUse != ExplicitBeliefReasoning
```

This makes `mentalizing` process- and demand-sensitive.

---

# 7. Efficient and flexible mindreading can dissociate

Cross-cultural work in Chinese adults and Semai children found anticipatory false-belief
tracking with signature limits that differed from direct explicit prediction.

HD3 therefore retains as a live model family:

```text
Fast/EfficientSocialPrediction
!= FlexibleExplicitMentalStateReasoning
```

without assuming exactly two neural systems universally.

---

# 8. Other-mind inference can be modeled as inverse planning

A strong computational family treats observed action as evidence about latent:

```text
goal
belief
utility
cost
```

under an approximate rational-action model.

In compact form:

```text
P(MentalState | Action, Context)
∝ P(Action | MentalState, Context) × P(MentalState)
```

This is a useful computational model, not a claim that people explicitly run Bayesian
algebra.

---

# 9. Rational-action priors are not universal truths about agents

Inverse-planning models depend on what counts as:

```text
cost
reward
available action
knowledge
rationality/noise
```

Therefore:

```text
GoodInversePlanningFit != CompleteMindreadingOntology
```

and systematic departures from approximate rationality can mislead a model.

---

# 10. Infants infer latent value from costly action

Ten-month-old infants expect an agent to prefer goals that it previously attained at higher
physical cost, consistent with abstract action-cost/value inference.

This supports early structured action interpretation while not establishing adult-like
propositional Theory of Mind.

```text
GoalValueInference != FullBeliefTheory
```

---

# 11. Comparative pressure: false-belief anticipation is not uniquely human by default

Great apes have shown anticipatory looking consistent with predicting action from a false
belief in declared paradigms.

Therefore:

```text
SomeFalseBeliefSensitivePrediction != HumanUniqueByDefinition
```

But:

```text
AnticipatoryLooking != HumanLanguageBasedExplicitMentalizing
```

Comparative claims must retain task/evidence level.

---

# 12. Cross-cultural timing is neither perfectly fixed nor arbitrary

Standardized five-culture work found similar approximate onset of classic false-belief
performance, while larger cross-cultural work finds meaningful timing differences across
communities and languages.

Thus:

```text
DevelopmentalRegularity != IdenticalTiming
CulturalVariation != NoSharedDevelopmentalConstraint
```

---

# 13. Family discourse can shape mental-state reasoning

Cross-cultural parent-child studies find parental `mind-mindedness` associated with child
Theory-of-Mind performance and capable of explaining some between-sample differences.

Therefore:

```text
ToMDevelopment != MaturationOnly
```

Social experience and language/family environment are plausible developmental inputs.

---

# 14. Mentalizing is not empathy

This is one of HD3's strongest firewalls.

Large-task fMRI work dissociates:

```text
Empathy / affect sharing
from
TheoryOfMind / mental-state reasoning
```

with distinct behavioral relations and partially separable networks.

Therefore:

```text
Mentalizing != Empathy
```

---

# 15. Strong empathizers are not necessarily strong mentalizers

Large-sample evidence shows behavioral capacities can be approximately independent; in
highly emotional contexts, stronger empathic sharing can even interfere with mentalizing
performance in the tested paradigm.

Thus:

```text
MoreEmpathy != BetterMindreadingByDefinition
```

---

# 16. Empathy is itself multi-component

At minimum separate:

```text
AffectiveSharing
EmpathicAccuracy
AffectiveMentalizing
PerspectiveTaking
PersonalDistress
EmpathicConcern
SelfOtherDistinction
```

Therefore:

```text
Empathy != OneScalar
```

---

# 17. Affect sharing is not concern / compassion

A person can share another's distress yet become self-focused or withdraw.

Compassion/concern is other-oriented motivation regarding another's welfare.

Thus:

```text
AffectiveSharing != EmpathicConcern
EmpathicConcern != CompassionByDefinition unless operationalized
```

and neither is identical to helping behavior.

---

# 18. Empathy is not prosociality

Experimental work separating empathy and concern finds they can predict prosocial behavior
differently.

Therefore:

```text
Empathy != Prosociality
Compassion/Concern != ProsocialActionByDefinition
```

HF12 still owns cooperation/prosocial action structure.

---

# 19. Compassion is not empathic distress

Training studies demonstrate dissociable changes in subjective affect and neural responses
after empathy-focused versus compassion-focused practice.

Thus:

```text
Compassion != EmpathicDistress
```

---

# 20. Self-other distinction is required for accurate social attribution

A social perceiver must distinguish:

```text
my state
my estimate of your state
your actual state
```

Thus:

```text
SharedAffect != MergedIdentity
```

and:

```text
SelfOtherDistinction != NoEmpathy
```

---

# 21. Self-other distinction is not one all-purpose process

Causal stimulation and perspective-taking work indicates different social tasks recruit
partly distinct mechanisms for suppressing egocentric interference, transforming
perspective or integrating social information.

Therefore:

```text
SelfOtherControl_D != SelfOtherControl_E
```

---

# 22. Empathic accuracy is not self-reported empathy

One can report high concern or emotional resonance while poorly tracking another person's
actual reported state.

Likewise accurate inference does not guarantee shared feeling.

```text
EmpathicAccuracy != AffectiveSharing != EmpathicConcern
```

---

# 23. Autism is a high-information falsifier against one-dimensional empathy stories

Primary work shows several dissociations:

```text
autistic traits / diagnosis
alexithymia
cognitive mentalizing
affective empathy
interaction partner neurotype
```

can contribute differently across tasks.

Therefore:

```text
Autism != GlobalAbsenceOfEmpathyByDefinition
```

---

# 24. Alexithymia is not autism

Adult empathy-for-pain work found insula responses associated with alexithymia rather than
autism status in that paradigm, while later adolescent work finds some ToM/emotion-
recognition differences track autistic traits rather than alexithymia.

The correct conclusion is not one universal confound.

```text
Autism != Alexithymia
Effect_Alexithymia,D != Effect_Alexithymia,E
```

---

# 25. The double-empathy problem is relational, not a reverse deficit slogan

Recent primary studies find non-autistic observers can be less accurate when interpreting
autistic narrators or mixed-neurotype interactions, while other datasets show weak or
context-sensitive matching effects.

Therefore:

```text
SocialUnderstandingFailure
can be Relation(PersonA, PersonB, Context)
```

not merely a trait deficit of one side.

But:

```text
DoubleEmpathyHypothesis != UniversalSymmetricFailure
```

It remains task- and sample-sensitive.

---

# 26. Social understanding is an interaction between model and signal ecology

Accuracy depends on:

```text
observer model
partner expression style
shared conventions
context
task demands
language
history
neurotype/culture fit
```

Thus:

```text
MindreadingAccuracy != ObserverTraitOnly
```

This is the social analogue of HF20's organism-environment perception relation.

---

# 27. Joint attention is not simple gaze following

Gaze following can be:

```text
A follows B's gaze to object X
```

Joint attention additionally concerns coordinated attention/engagement around a referent or
shared focus.

Therefore:

```text
GazeFollowing != JointAttention
```

---

# 28. Joint attention is not joint action

HF12 already preserved this.

```text
JointAttention != JointAction
```

Two people can attend jointly without jointly changing the world.

---

# 29. Joint attention is not shared belief

Coordinated orientation to one object does not imply identical:

```text
knowledge
belief
interpretation
value
```

Thus:

```text
JointAttention != SharedBelief
```

---

# 30. Joint attention is multi-cue and developmental

Naturalistic infant work shows joint attention can be established through dynamically
coordinated gaze and manual actions rather than one fixed eye-contact template.

2025 work also shows that older infants can use proximal touch as a cue of third-party
jointness where younger infants rely more strongly on eye contact.

Therefore:

```text
JointAttentionCue_D != JointAttentionCue_E
Adult/OlderInfantCueUse != YoungerInfantCueUse
```

---

# 31. Autism pressure shows joint attention is modality-sensitive

Prospective work finds autistic toddlers may show fewer visual/auditory initiations of
joint attention while showing more tactile initiation in declared tasks.

Therefore:

```text
LowVisualJointAttention != NoJointAttentionByDefinition
```

and assessment can be modality-biased.

---

# 32. Joint attention can scaffold learning without being learning itself

Joint attention is associated with later attention/language/social learning surfaces.

But:

```text
JointAttention != Learning
```

It changes evidence/relevance structure for learning.

This exposes a later social-learning/cultural-transmission residual.

---

# 33. Trust is already partly solved by HF12

HF12 correctly established:

```text
Trust != Reliance
Trust != Dependence
Trust != Predictability
Trust != OneScalar
```

HD3 therefore does not create a second trust ontology.

The deeper question is how trust becomes one component of a persistent relation.

---

# 34. Trust updates through interaction history

Multi-round trust-game evidence shows reciprocity changes future trust and produces
partner-specific learning/reputation signals.

Thus:

```text
Trust_t+1 = Update(Trust_t, PartnerAction_t, Context_t, PriorHistory)
```

as a model family.

---

# 35. Trust history is not relationship totality

Two people can:

```text
trust professionally but dislike one another
love one another but distrust one domain
be attached while recognizing unreliability
```

Therefore:

```text
TrustRelation_D != RelationshipStateTotality
```

This becomes a key foundation-admission pressure.

---

# 36. Relationship is not repeated interaction

Repeated transactions with a cashier, opponent or API can occur without the same persistent
relationship structure as:

```text
parent-child
friendship
romantic partnership
care relation
mentor-student
```

Therefore:

```text
RepeatedInteraction != PersistentRelationshipByDefinition
```

---

# 37. Relationship is not current interaction state

A friendship can persist while no interaction is occurring.

A parent-child relation can shape behavior before current contact.

Thus:

```text
RelationshipState_t != CurrentInteractionState_t
```

This state is dyad-specific but can persist across gaps in interaction.

---

# 38. Relationship is not institution

A marriage has institutional/legal surfaces, but a friendship can persist without formal
institutional encoding.

Likewise institutional roles can exist without a close interpersonal relationship.

Therefore:

```text
PersistentRelationship != Institution
```

HF13 remains social-order owner.

---

# 39. Relationship state is not one scalar closeness score

Potential relation coordinates include:

```text
familiarity
attachment/security
trust by domain
care/concern
commitment
interdependence
dependence
shared history
shared identity
obligation expectation
conflict/repair history
status/power asymmetry
exchange pattern
contact opportunity
perceived responsiveness
```

No coordinate is the relation itself.

---

# 40. Relationship state can be asymmetric

```text
RelationState(A→B) != RelationState(B→A)
```

A may feel attached to B while B does not.

A may trust B more than B trusts A.

A may depend on B while B has alternatives.

Therefore relationship modeling cannot require symmetry.

---

# 41. Relationship representation is not relationship reality

Each participant can hold a model:

```text
RelationModel_A(A,B)
RelationModel_B(B,A)
```

while observable causal/institutional relation differs.

Thus:

```text
PerceivedCloseness != MutualCloseness
PerceivedRelationship != SymmetricRelationshipByDefinition
```

---

# 42. Attachment is not love

Attachment describes a security/proximity/secure-base relation family, especially visible
under threat, separation, reunion and exploration contexts.

Love can include attachment but also valuation, sexual/romantic attraction, care,
commitment and cultural meaning.

Therefore:

```text
Attachment != Love
```

---

# 43. Attachment is not dependence

A person can depend instrumentally on someone without attachment.

Secure attachment can support exploration/autonomy rather than maximal proximity.

Thus:

```text
Attachment != Dependence
SecureAttachment != MaximumClinging
```

---

# 44. Attachment is not one immutable trait

Attachment is relationship- and developmental-history sensitive.

An infant can have different attachment relations with different caregivers; daycare work
also shows attachment-like behavior can develop toward professional caregivers over time.

Therefore:

```text
AttachmentTo_A != AttachmentTo_B
AttachmentSecurity != PersonTraitByDefinition
```

---

# 45. Attachment has regulatory consequences

A randomized attachment-based parenting intervention increased maternal sensitivity, which
in turn improved infants' cortisol recovery after mild stressors.

This is causal pressure for:

```text
Relationship/CaregivingStructure
→ OrganismicRegulation
```

without reducing attachment to cortisol.

---

# 46. Attachment is not cortisol regulation

Physiological findings vary across task, temperament, baseline state and family
configuration.

Therefore:

```text
AttachmentSecurity != CortisolProfile
```

A regulatory consequence is not construct identity.

---

# 47. Relationship can scaffold capability

Secure-base and caregiver-support relations can alter:

```text
exploration
stress recovery
attention
learning opportunity
social risk taking
```

Thus capability can be relationally supported.

```text
IndependentCapability != RelationSupportedCapability
```

This extends the relational capability grammar from HF0/HF12.

---

# 48. Adult partners can also co-regulate

Experimental psychosocial-stress work in couples finds positive dyadic coping predicts
faster cortisol recovery, moderated by attachment-related individual differences.

Therefore:

```text
CoRegulation != ChildhoodOnly
```

---

# 49. Care is not empathy

Care concerns another's welfare and often includes action/resource allocation or sustained
responsibility.

A person can care without strongly sharing another's current feeling.

Therefore:

```text
Care != Empathy
```

---

Care can be:

```text
professionally compensated
mixed-motive
```

Thus:

```text
Care != AltruismByDefinition
```

# 51. Care is not benevolent feeling only

Care can involve:

```text
monitoring

protection
maintenance

availability

```

Therefore:

```text
CaringFeeling != CarePracticeTotality
```

---

# 52. Commitment is not attachment

A person can remain committed by norm, contract, promise or strategic choice despite low
attachment; attachment can exist without explicit commitment.

Thus:

```text
Commitment != Attachment
```

---

# 53. Familiarity is not attachment or trust

Repeated exposure can produce familiarity without care, trust or attachment.

```text
Familiarity != RelationshipSecurity
Familiarity != Trust
```

---

# 54. Closeness is not relationship identity

Self-reported closeness is one evidence surface.

It can change rapidly and can be induced in short structured interactions.

Thus:

```text
ClosenessRating != PersistentRelationshipByDefinition
```

---

# 55. Relationship type is not one universal taxonomy

Large 2025 cross-cultural/historical work identifies robust shared dimensions/categories in
relationship concepts while also finding cultural variation.

HD3 therefore retains:

```text
RelationshipType_D = projection over relation features
```

not a universal list of family/friend/romantic/work relations.

---

# 56. Shared relationship dimensions need not be relationship essences

Dimensions such as:

```text
formality
activeness
valence
exchange
equality
```

can organize conceptual similarity across many cultures.

But:

```text
UsefulRelationshipEmbedding != CompleteRelationshipOntology
```

because attachment, care, legal status, biological kinship and causal dependence can be
orthogonal or differently represented.

---

# 57. Kinship is not genetic relatedness

Human kinship can be organized through:

```text
descent
marriage/affinity
adoption/fostering
clan
household
ritual/social recognition
```

in addition to genetic relation.

```text
SocialKinship != GeneticRelatedness
```

# 58. Genetic relatedness can matter without defining kinship

relatedness, cultural kin categories and residence.

Ancient-genomic work also finds burial/social units can include genetically unrelated

Thus:

```text
GeneticRelatedness = one potential relation variable
!= SocialKinshipTotality
```

---

# 59. Family is not household

People can share a household without family relation, and family members can live apart.

Therefore:

```text
Family != Household
```

---

# 60. Family is not genetic kin only

```text
Family_D != GeneticKinSet
```

# 61. Romantic relation is not attachment alone

Cross-cultural romantic data span:

```text
RomanticRelationship != AttachmentOnly
```

# 62. Loneliness is not objective social isolation

Loneliness is a subjective mismatch/deficit in desired meaningful connection; social
isolation concerns objective contact/network structure.

Therefore:

```text
Loneliness != SocialIsolation
```

One can be lonely in a crowd or socially isolated without high loneliness.

---

# 63. Relationship quantity is not relationship quality

Network size/contact frequency does not uniquely determine:

```text
support

responsiveness
attachment
```

```text
MoreContacts != BetterRelationships
```

---

# 64. Perceived empathy can alter social risk behavior

Large field work shows people can underestimate peers' empathy; changing these beliefs can

Thus:

```text
BeliefAboutOthers'Care
can become
SocialBehaviorIntervention
```

This is dyadic/social reflexivity, not proof of actual relationship quality.

---

# 65. Relationship state can alter future evidence about itself

```text
Trust / Attachment / Expectation_t
→ how A approaches B

```

Therefore:

```text
RelationshipModel
```

---

# 66. Betrayal is not generic negative outcome

A bad outcome caused by a trusted/committed partner can update:

```text
trust
predictability
benevolence model

commitment
future monitoring
```

more than the same material loss caused by chance.

Thus:

```text
RelationshipViolation != OutcomeLossOnly
```

# 67. Repair is multi-layered

HF12 separated communication repair and trust repair.

HD3 adds:

```text
RelationshipRepair
```

which may require updating longer-horizon expectations, obligations, attachment or

Therefore:

```text
CommunicationRepair != TrustRepair != RelationshipRepair
```

---

# 68. Forgiveness is not restored trust by definition

A person can forgive while continuing to monitor or end the relationship.

Thus:

```text
Forgiveness != TrustRestoration
Forgiveness != RelationshipContinuation
```

HD3 records the boundary without creating a forgiveness theory.

---

# 69. Relationship persistence is not invariance

Persistent relation state can change continuously.

```text
Persistent != Static
```

A friendship can become distant; attachment security can change; trust can recover or
collapse.

---

# 70. A persistent relationship is a trajectory

Use:

```text
RelationState_D(A,B,t+1)
= U(RelationState_D(A,B,t),
```

# 71. Non-interaction can itself update relationship state

Absence, delayed response, separation, migration or prolonged availability can matter.

Therefore:

```text
NoInteraction != NoRelationshipDynamics
```

---

# 72. Relationship memory is distributed across more than episodic recall

Persistent relations can be encoded in:

```text
expectations
habits

```

Thus:

```text
RelationshipHistory != EpisodicMemoryOnly
```

---

# 73. Attachment representation is not attachment interaction itself

Longitudinal work finds secure-base script knowledge can emerge from caregiving history and
relate to later autobiographical representations.

Therefore:

```text
AttachmentRelation
!= AttachmentRepresentation
```

while relation history can shape representation.

---

# 74. Social relation can be multiplex

The same pair may simultaneously be:

```text
friends
coworkers
competitors
kin
caregiver/care-recipient
creditor/debtor
```

Thus:

```text
OneDyad != OneRelationType
```

Use domain-qualified relation layers.

---

# 75. Relationship can include power asymmetry without being an institution

A caregiver-child relationship can be asymmetrical in capability, dependence and control.

HF13 owns generalized status/authority/power structures; HF22 must still record dyad-level
asymmetry because it changes care, trust and attachment dynamics.

```text
DyadicPowerAsymmetry != InstitutionalAuthorityByDefinition
```

---

# 76. Relationship can be mediated

A persistent relation may be maintained through:

```text
text
video
shared digital spaces
asynchronous communication
AI mediation
```

Physical co-presence is not required.

```text
PersistentRelationship != PhysicalCopresence
```

---

# 77. AI is a novel relational perturbation

Human participants can report:

```text
closeness
connection
trust
self-disclosure
attachment-like responses
```

toward artificial systems.

This demonstrates properties of Human relational cognition and interaction.

It does not establish reciprocal machine feeling or personhood.

---

# 78. Human felt connection to AI is not machine felt connection

```text
HumanPerceivedConnectionToAI
!= MutualExperiencedRelationship
```

2025 experiments show anthropomorphism moderates feelings of connection after chatbot
interaction.

Thus subjective relational state can be real for the Human even when counterpart
phenomenology is unestablished.

---

# 79. Partner label changes relationship formation

Randomized `Fast Friends`-style studies find the same AI-generated responses can produce
different closeness depending on whether the partner is labelled Human or AI.

Therefore:

```text
PartnerOntologyBelief
can change
HumanRelationshipState
```

without changing response text.

---

# 80. AI warmth is not AI benevolence

2026 trust-game work shows perceived warmth/competence of AI agents influences Human trust.

But:

```text
PerceivedWarmth != ActualBenevolentExperience
PerceivedEmpathy != AffectiveEmpathy
```

These are Human partner-model variables unless independently evidenced on the AI side.

---

# 81. AI relational success does not imply Human-equivalent relationship structure

Current human-agent evidence shows some social alignment/trust can approach human-human
levels while agency/responsibility/prosocial/moral engagement can differ.

HD3 therefore insists:

```text
SimilarClosenessScore != SameRelationshipOntology
```

and:

```text
HumanAttachmentLikeResponseToAI
!= AIAttachment
```

---

# 82. Asymmetry is foundational for Human×AI relationships

A Human may possess:

```text
memory
attachment
expectation
```
