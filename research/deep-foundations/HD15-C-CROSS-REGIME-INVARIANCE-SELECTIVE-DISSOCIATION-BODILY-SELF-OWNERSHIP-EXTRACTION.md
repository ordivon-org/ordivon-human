# 3. R1 process reality — body ownership is more than raw sensation

Clinical and experimental evidence establishes that disturbed ownership is not reducible to one raw sensory deficit.

Rare patients can show disturbed sense of ownership separately from anosognosia for hemiplegia; larger lesion studies explicitly distinguish motor awareness from disturbed ownership.

Covert disownership after stroke can be detected even when ordinary verbal interview misses it, and can concern different body parts with distributed network disconnections.

Therefore:

```text
BodyOwnershipTarget
= scientifically real

BodyOwnershipDisturbance
!= GenericMotorAwarenessDeficit
!= NeglectByDefinition
!= OneRawSensoryLoss
```

This supports a typed ownership projection.

It does not yet establish a peer owner.
