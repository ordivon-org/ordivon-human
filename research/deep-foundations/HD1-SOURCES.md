# HD1 — Evidence Ledger

## Evidence rule

HD1 privileges primary empirical/formal papers. A source may support one mechanism or
falsifier without owning the full perceptual ontology.

## S01 — Percept-dependent active olfactory sampling

Sagar, V. et al. (2026 issue; published 2025). **The human brain modulates sniffs
according to fine-grained perceptual features of odours.** Nature Human Behaviour 10,
137–147. DOI `10.1038/s41562-025-02327-x`.

Use: fine-grained odour properties and identity can be decoded from sniff dynamics;
olfactory regions participate in percept-driven sniff modulation. Strong evidence that
sampling action is endogenous to perception.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02327-x

## S02 — Sub-sniff temporal information

Wu and collaborators (2024). **Human olfactory perception embeds fine temporal resolution
within a single sniff.** Nature Human Behaviour.

Use: Human observers can exploit temporal ordering within a single sniff at fine temporal
resolution, pressure-testing static odour-vector descriptions.

## S03 — Hierarchical Bayesian causal inference dynamics

Rohe, T., Ehlis, A.-C. & Noppeney, U. (2019). **The neural dynamics of hierarchical
Bayesian causal inference in multisensory perception.** Nature Communications 10, 1907.
DOI `10.1038/s41467-019-09664-2`.

Use: audiovisual integration/segregation evolves over time in a manner consistent with
Bayesian causal-inference model averaging; supports `CueCombination != AlwaysFuse`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09664-2

## S04 — Multisensory causal-inference benchmark

Körding and collaborators. **Causal inference in multisensory perception.** Primary
behavioural/formal study.

Use: formalizes common-cause versus separate-cause arbitration and demonstrates that cue
integration depends on inferred causal structure, not reliability alone.

## S05 — Feedforward network can approximate multisensory causal inference

Primary computational study: **How multisensory neurons solve causal inference.**

Use: a visual–vestibular neural network can reproduce causal-inference behaviour and
neural response classes, supporting `ComputationalFunction != OneImplementation`.

## S06 — No-report measurement can perturb rivalry

Kingir, E., Segawa, R., Hesse, J. K., Kagan, I. & Wilke, M. (2025). **Fixation shifts in
a novel “no-report” binocular rivalry paradigm induce saccade-related perceptual
switches.** Consciousness and Cognition 133, 103891. DOI
`10.1016/j.concog.2025.103891`.

Use: estimated ~14% of saccades triggered a switch and ~24% of perceptual transitions
were attributable to saccade-related effects in the paradigm. Supports
`NoReportProxy != PassiveMeasurement`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40493983/

## S07 — Mesoscale conflict resolution in binocular rivalry

Qian, C. et al. (2026 issue; published 2025). **Mesoscale cortical mechanisms of
perceptual conflict resolution in binocular rivalry.** Nature Human Behaviour 10,
403–416. DOI `10.1038/s41562-025-02320-4`.

Use: 7T fMRI finds V1 ocular-column competition plus parietal feedback that biases and
synchronizes local competitions. Supports multi-level recurrent conflict resolution.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02320-4

## S08 — Causal recurrent contour integration

Li and collaborators (2019). **Recurrent Processing of Contour Integration in the Human
Visual Cortex as Revealed By fMRI-Guided TMS.** Cerebral Cortex 29, 17–26. DOI
`10.1093/cercor/bhx296`.

Use: V3B critical disruption at 90–110 ms precedes V1/V2 disruption at 120–140 ms,
providing causal evidence for task-specific feedback in contour integration.

Source: https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/29/1/17/4637597

## S09 — Recurrence changes representational geometry

Primary 2025 EEG/fMRI backward-masking study: **Recurrence affects the geometry of visual
representations across the cortical hierarchy.**

Use: masking alters temporally evolving early and later visual representations; supports
recurrent refinement without equating recurrence with consciousness.

## S10 — Blindsight / visibility dissociation under perturbation

Primary TMS/masking and lesion studies of visual discrimination without ordinary reported
seeing.

Use: objective sensitivity and action-related processing can survive conditions that
remove ordinary visibility; chronic-lesion and transient-perturbation mechanisms need not
be identical.

## S11 — Sensory-substitution qualia after occipital TMS

Kupers and collaborators (2006). **Transcranial magnetic stimulation of the visual cortex
induces somatotopically organized qualia in blind subjects.** Proceedings of the National
Academy of Sciences. DOI `10.1073/pnas.0602925103`.

Use: blind people trained with a tongue-display unit reported tongue-referred tactile
sensations after occipital TMS. Supports multi-coordinate modality identity and rejects
`CorticalArea = FixedPhenomenalModality`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16916936/

## S12 — Rapid reversible cross-modal recruitment

Merabet and collaborators (2008). **Rapid and Reversible Recruitment of Early Visual
Cortex for Touch.** PLoS ONE.

Use: five days of blindfolding plus tactile training recruited occipital cortex for touch;
rTMS impaired Braille discrimination on day 5 and the recruitment reversed after vision
returned. Supports history-dependent routing.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18728773/

## S13 — Visual-haptic development in children

Gori, M., Del Viva, M., Sandini, G. & Burr, D. C. (2008). **Young children do not
integrate visual and haptic form information.** Current Biology 18, 694–698. DOI
`10.1016/j.cub.2008.04.036`.

Use: before ~8 years, task-dependent modality dominance replaces adult-like optimal
integration; 8–10-year-olds approach adult patterns.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18450446/

## S14 — Multisensory integration after prolonged early visual deprivation

Senna and collaborators (2021). **Development of multisensory integration following
prolonged early-onset visual deprivation.** Current Biology. PMID `34534443`.

Use: after late cataract removal, visual-haptic weighting and precision can develop over
weeks/months toward typical statistically efficient patterns. Supports experience-
dependent development and rejects a simple irreversible age rule.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34534443/

## S15 — Visuo-proprioceptive integration versus recalibration

Debats, N. B., Heuer, H. & Kayser, C. (2021). **Visuo-proprioceptive integration and
recalibration with multiple visual stimuli.** Scientific Reports 11, 21640. DOI
`10.1038/s41598-021-00992-2`.

Use: multiple visual stimuli can exert superposed influences; current integration and
persistent recalibration are distinct outcomes.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-00992-2

## S16 — Active versus passive tactile recognition

Vega-Bermudez, F., Johnson, K. O. & Hsiao, S. S. (1991). **Human tactile pattern
recognition: active versus passive touch, velocity effects, and patterns of confusion.**
Journal of Neurophysiology 65, 531–546. DOI `10.1152/jn.1991.65.3.531`.

Use: under controlled letter-recognition conditions, mean active and passive performance
was similar. Strong falsifier of `ActivePerception > PassivePerception` as a universal.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2051193/

## S17 — Movement changes tactile bias and precision differently

D'Onofrio Pacheco, P. N. & Zimmermann, E. (2026). **Movement enhances tactile sensitivity
through prediction.** Journal of Neurophysiology 135, 888–895. DOI
`10.1152/jn.00558.2025`.

Use: movement reduced perceived intensity while predictable active/passive movement could
increase discrimination precision. Supports `PerceptualBias != PerceptualPrecision`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843461/

## S18 — Haptic spatial reconstruction

Primary active-haptic studies combining tactile and movement information.

Use: local skin input is transformed with limb/trunk/body state into external spatial
estimates, pressure-testing one-coordinate tactile models.

## S19 — Action-scaled affordance benchmark

Warren, W. H. Jr. (1984). **Perceiving affordances: visual guidance of stair climbing.**
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 10, 683–703. DOI
`10.1037//0096-1523.10.5.683`.

Use: climbability judgments scale with organism-environment dynamics/body scale, not stair
geometry alone.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6238127/

## S20 — Tool-integrated affordance / sensory substitution

Primary studies of stair/door/obstacle affordances with crutches, action feedback and
sensory-substitution devices.

Use: task affordances can scale to a person-plus-tool or substituted-information system,
while tool integration does not imply biological identity.

## S21 — Domain-specific interoception

Banellis, L. et al. (2026). **Interoceptive ability is uncorrelated across respiratory
and cardiac axes in a large scale psychophysical study.** Communications Psychology 4,
43. DOI `10.1038/s44271-026-00404-z`.

Use: in a large psychophysical sample, cardiac and respiratory sensitivity/precision/
metacognitive efficiency were largely uncorrelated apart from modest confidence overlap.
Supports `InteroceptiveAbility_D != InteroceptiveAbility_E`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00404-z

## S22 — Bayesian/efficient decoding of visual material perception

Storrs, K. R., Anderson, B. L. & Fleming, R. W. (2021). **Unsupervised learning predicts
human perception and misperception of gloss.** Nature Human Behaviour 5, 1402–1417. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-021-01097-6`.

Use: unsupervised generative representations predict specific patterns of human gloss
success and misperception better than ground truth or supervised controls in the tested
world, illustrating latent-cause/statistical-learning accounts without proving mechanism
identity.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01097-6

## S23 — Motion illusions as optimal percepts

Weiss, Y., Simoncelli, E. P. & Adelson, E. H. (2002). **Motion illusions as optimal
percepts.** Nature Neuroscience.

Use: combines noisy measurements with environmental priors to account for classes of
motion illusions. Supports Bayesian-model usefulness but not universal Bayesian closure.

## S24 — Decoding can generate perceptual bias

Jazayeri, M. & Movshon, J. A. (2007). Primary study of decoding and motion-direction
perception.

Use: demonstrates that systematic perceptual errors can arise from decoding structure,
not only altered sensory encoding.

## S25 — Illusion and imagery have distinct laminar profiles

Primary 2024 laminar-fMRI study of V1 responses to illusory versus imagined content.

Use: non-veridical/currently absent content can arise through different cortical-layer
profiles. Supports `Imagery != Illusion`.

## S26 — Predictive audiovisual speech signal

Arnal and collaborators (2011). Primary Nature Neuroscience study of audiovisual speech
prediction and mismatch-related oscillatory responses.

Use: compatible with hierarchical prediction-error processing in a declared paradigm,
not evidence that all perception is predictive coding.

## S27 — Active inference under visuo-proprioceptive conflict

Limanowski, J. & Friston, K. (2020). **Active inference under visuo-proprioceptive
conflict: Simulation and empirical results.** Scientific Reports 10, 4010. DOI
`10.1038/s41598-020-61097-w`.

Use: a predictive-coding/active-inference model captured behaviour and reported
attentional allocation in a VR hand conflict task. Retained as model-family evidence, not
universal ontology.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-61097-w

## S28 — Human-machine shared adversarial influence

Veerabadran, V. et al. (2023). **Subtle adversarial image manipulations influence both
human and machine perception.** Nature Communications 14, 4933. DOI
`10.1038/s41467-023-40499-0`.

Use: adversarial signals designed for ANNs can bias human choices in the same direction,
but the human effect is much smaller. Supports partial shared statistical sensitivity
without mechanism identity.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40499-0

## S29 — Cross-cultural geometric-illusion variation

Segall/Campbell/Herskovits-era primary cross-society geometric-illusion experiments and
later within-population follow-ups.

Use: susceptibility varies materially across cultural/ecological contexts, while simple
single-factor explanations such as a universal carpentered-world account are
insufficient. Used as transport pressure, not as modern effect-size authority.

## S30 — Development, modality and deprivation are not one axis

The combined evidence from S11–S14 and S21 shows that:

```text
chronological age
sensory history
current channel
neural routing
functional information
phenomenal quality
```

must remain separately typed. No one source alone establishes this synthesis.

## Evidence boundary

HD1 does not claim exhaustive closure for every sensory modality. The source set is
sufficient to establish the cross-modal category separations and model pluralism needed
for canonical foundation extraction. Later domain-specific research may extend or revise
mechanistic details without reopening the core firewalls automatically.
