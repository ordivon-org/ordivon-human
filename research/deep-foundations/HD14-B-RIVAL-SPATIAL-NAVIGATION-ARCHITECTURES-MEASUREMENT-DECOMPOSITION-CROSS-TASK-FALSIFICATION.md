# HD14-B — Rival Spatial-Navigation Architectures, Measurement Decomposition & Cross-Task Falsification

## 0. Entry

HD14-A established that `CognitiveMap` is not an unqualified primitive and retained four positive objects/projections:

```text
SpatialRelationRepresentation
ReferenceFrameTransformationProjection
PathIntegrationStateUpdatingProjection
LandmarkSpatialRecalibrationProjection
```

B asks:

> Which spatial-navigation architecture predicts the same selective lesion, developmental, cue-conflict, active/passive, sensory-deprivation and tool-supported cases without silently importing neighboring Human owners?

Scoring keeps separate:

```text
Explains(X)
!= CompatibleWith(X)
!= CanBeExtendedToFit(X)
!= ImportsNeighborOwnerFor(X)
```

# 3. B1 — Unitary Cognitive-Map / Survey Architecture

The strongest form of B1 claims that Humans construct an integrated spatial representation whose relational structure supports flexible orientation, novel routes and shortcut inference.

It is stronger than `people remember routes`.

---

# 4. B1 has real positive evidence

DTD provides evidence that severe selective navigation impairment can occur despite broadly preserved cognition, and classic DTD studies identify poor map-like spatial representation as a major differentiating failure.

Large-scale campus learning also shows people can rapidly acquire coherent spatial relations across previously separated routes after connecting experience.

Thus:

```text
MapLikeSpatialRelationRepresentation
= real Human capability family
```

---

# 5. B1 fails if map formation and map use are collapsed

A selective DTD case could:

```text
learn routes
recognize landmarks
construct cognitive-map-like representations
```

but failed severely when required to translate allocentric map information into egocentrically guided navigation.

Therefore:

```text
MapFormation
!= MapUseForNavigation
```

and a unitary map state does not explain the transformation step.

---

# 6. B1 fails if route/landmark/survey are treated as map stages

Repeated-learning evidence shows landmark, route and survey knowledge can develop in parallel and become increasingly correlated rather than appearing as mandatory sequential stages.

Long-delay evidence shows route and survey knowledge can forget at different rates.

Therefore:

```text
OneMapStrength
cannot replace typed spatial knowledge dimensions.
```

---

# 7. Landmark-dependent shortcuts reject shortcut = metric-map identity

Humans can navigate novel shortcuts successfully when dispersed landmarks are available yet fail in a landmark-poor desert; displaced landmarks attract shortcut responses, and only when landmarks become unreliable do participants fall back on coarse survey knowledge.

Therefore:

```text
ShortcutSuccess
!= AccurateMetricMapProof
```

B1 can survive only by admitting heterogeneous landmark and coarse-survey mechanisms.

---

# 8. Graph knowledge pressures Euclidean map totality

Active-learning experiments show people can acquire labeled graph knowledge supporting novel and shortest routes, while metric survey knowledge responds differently to active-learning components.

Thus:

```text
EnvironmentalGraphRepresentation
!= MetricSurveyRepresentation
```

B1 must specify topology/metric content rather than invoke one map.

---

# 9. Nested environments pressure global-map identity

Humans can maintain local spatial representations in nested environments without fully integrating them into one simultaneous global representation, switching/reorienting among relevant contexts.

Therefore:

```text
Navigation
!= OneAlwaysGlobalMapReadout
```

---

# 10. B1 disposition

```text
B1 Unitary Cognitive Map
= STRONG for flexible relational representation
= FAIL as whole process architecture
= survives only as typed SpatialRelationRepresentation family
```

Its stable positive object is strongly HF8-owned.

---

# 11. B2 — Hippocampal–Entorhinal Place/Grid/Map Architecture

B2 claims that hippocampal-entorhinal mechanisms provide a map-like spatial code, with place/grid/head-direction-like representations supporting location, relational structure, updating and planning.

This is one of the strongest biological rivals.

---

# 12. B2 receives strong map-formation/use evidence

Human fMRI studies report complementary hippocampal and retrosplenial contributions during formation and use of map-like spatial representations.

Hippocampal anatomy predicts individual differences in rapid learning and flexible inference over large-scale spatial layouts.

Goal-directed wayfinding also recruits hippocampal systems more strongly than familiar route following in classic virtual-navigation work.

Thus:

```text
HippocampalContributionToFlexibleSpatialRepresentation
= STRONG POSITIVE EVIDENCE
```

---

# 13. Grid-like coding is a genuine Human neural phenomenon

Human entorhinal grid-like signals occur during:

```text
virtual navigation
imagined navigation
olfactory landmark navigation
perceived self-location drift induced by multisensory bodily illusion
observation/tracking of another navigator
```

and coordinate with broader navigation networks.

This is substantial implementation evidence.

---

# 14. 2026 cortisol perturbation adds causal pressure

A 2026 within-subject cortisol/placebo study in healthy men found cortisol impaired virtual path-integration performance and altered entorhinal grid-like representation, while reported landmark proximity/navigation-pattern measures did not show the same impairment.

Thus:

```text
EntorhinalGridLikeProcess
can contribute causally/selectively to PathIntegration_D.
```

This is stronger than correlational fMRI alone.

---

# 15. But hippocampus/entorhinal cortex is not necessary for every path-integration regime

Patients with hippocampal lesions or larger MTL lesions including entorhinal cortex could point to and estimate distance from the start of short blindfolded paths as accurately as controls in one classic study.

Parallel human/rat work again found human MTL-lesion patients performed accurately on sufficiently short/simple path integration, despite severe memory impairment.

Therefore:

```text
PathIntegration
!= HippocampalEntorhinalProcessOnly
```

and task duration/working-memory demand matters.

---

# 16. Map reading / coordinate transformation can survive hippocampal lesions

Patients with lesions limited to the hippocampus performed normally on map-reading/navigation tasks requiring repeated transformation between hand-held map coordinates and geographical/body movement coordinates when the map remained available.

A larger MTL lesion could impair maximal coordinate-conflict conditions.

Therefore:

```text
MapCoordinateTransformation
!= HippocampusNecessaryByDefinition
```

---

# 17. Remote spatial representations can survive bilateral hippocampal damage

A profoundly amnesic patient with extensive bilateral hippocampal lesions retained broad allocentric knowledge of a long-familiar neighborhood and world geography while losing some local/detail information.

Thus:

```text
Hippocampus
!= PermanentExclusiveStoreOfAllSpatialRelations
```

---

# 18. Grid-like coding is not navigation-specific

2026 developmental fMRI in a non-spatial conceptual 2D knowledge space found entorhinal grid-like codes that strengthened with age and tracked inferential reasoning and knowledge assimilation.

Earlier work also found grid-like signals in controlled visual-space tracking and imagined/non-self navigation.

Therefore:

```text
GridLikeCode
!= SpatialNavigationSemanticOwner
```

It is better interpreted as a reusable coordinate/relational coding mechanism family.

---

# 19. Olfactory grid-like navigation further weakens visual-spatial identity

Human participants can learn a complex odor-landmark environment and show aligned grid-like responses in entorhinal and piriform cortex.

Therefore:

```text
EntorhinalGridLikeCode
is modality-flexible
```

and does not identify visual navigation as its owner.

---

# 20. B2 disposition

```text
B2 Hippocampal–Entorhinal Map Architecture
= STRONG implementation architecture
= strong for flexible relational learning / some PI regimes
= NOT necessary for every map-reading or short-PI regime
= grid code extends beyond spatial navigation
= NOT a semantic owner by anatomy alone
```

---

# 21. B3 — Path-Integration / Self-Motion-Primary Architecture

B3 treats navigation as fundamentally maintaining/updating self-position and heading from self-motion, with other cues correcting that estimate.

---

# 22. B3 has strong psychophysical support

Humans use:

```text
optic flow
proprioception/body cues
vestibular information
```

for path integration, with translational and rotational components showing distinct effects.

A loop-closure paradigm found visual optic flow and proprioception made roughly equal independent contributions while vestibular-only information was insufficient in that task.

Thus:

```text
PathIntegrationStateUpdating
= real multimodal mechanism family
```

---

# 23. Vestibular pathology supports self-motion contribution

Chronic bilateral vestibular loss is associated with spatial-memory/navigation impairment and hippocampal changes.

More specifically, bilateral vestibulopathy can selectively impair recombination of novel routes in real space while familiar route retracing remains normal.

Therefore:

```text
Vestibular/SelfMotionEvidence
matters especially for flexible spatial recombination in some regimes.
```

---

# 24. B3 cannot generate all map-like knowledge alone

Classic shortcut work found path integration in landmark-poor environments did not produce accurate novel shortcut navigation.

Therefore:

```text
PathIntegration
!= SufficientMetricCognitiveMapGenerator
```

---

# 25. B3 cannot explain landmark/route/graph learning alone

Landmark identity, route ordering, graph decision structure and survey relations require stored/perceived environmental relations beyond current self-motion state.

Thus B3 must import:

```text
HF20 landmark/scene evidence
HF7 spatial memory
HF8 relational representation
HF9 graph/route inference
```

---

# 26. B3 disposition

```text
B3 Path-Integration/Self-Motion Primary
= STRONG local online-state architecture
= causal/selective evidence exists
= FAIL as navigation totality
```

`PathIntegrationStateUpdatingProjection` remains a major unresolved residual.

---

# 27. B4 — Landmark–Route / Graph Navigation

B4 emphasizes environmental reference features, learned route relations and topological/graph structure rather than one global metric survey map.

---

# 28. Landmark guidance is often behaviorally dominant

Novel shortcuts can be strongly pulled by displaced landmarks, and landmark-rich environments permit shortcut behavior that fails in landmark-poor environments.

Landmarks can also correct path-integration error.

Thus:

```text
LandmarkReferenceUse
= major navigation mechanism family
```

---

# 29. Graph knowledge is real and not route replay

Participants can acquire labeled graph knowledge that supports novel and metrically shortest routes under detours, even when the exact route has not been traveled during learning.

Therefore:

```text
GraphKnowledge
!= RouteReplay
```

---

# 30. B4 fails selective landmark agnosia and landmark-free homing

Landmark recognition can be selectively impaired while other object recognition remains spared.

Conversely, people can perform short blindfolded homing/path-integration without landmark input.

Thus:

```text
LandmarkNavigation
!= NavigationNecessaryCoreByDefinition
```

---

# 31. B4 cannot explain heading/global-axis representation alone

Stable heading/facing-direction representation can generalize across locations and visual city versions relative to environmental axes.

This requires orientation/reference-frame structure beyond learned landmark-action associations.

---

# 32. B4 disposition

```text
B4 Landmark–Route/Graph Architecture
= STRONG for environmental structure / learned paths
= graph representation is real
= FAIL as online state / heading / nonlandmark totality
```

---

# 33. B5 — Allocentric–Egocentric Dual / Reference-Frame Architecture

B5 treats navigation as interaction between environment-centered and self-centered spatial representations.

This family predicts important dissociations.

---

# 34. B5 has strong behavioral and lesion support

Participants can preferentially use allocentric or egocentric reference frames and switch to the nonpreferred frame when instructed.

Posterior-parietal lesions can preserve broad allocentric distance/proximity judgments while impairing familiar route navigation dependent on egocentric access.

A DTD map-following case preserved allocentric map construction but failed allocentric-to-egocentric guided navigation.

Thus:

```text
AllocentricContent
!= EgocentricActionUse
```

---

# 35. B5 is too coarse if egocentric = one thing

Egocentric representations can be body-, head-, gaze/viewer- or current-observer-centered.

Congenital blindness also changes some allocentric↔egocentric switching costs without eliminating all spatial representation.

Therefore:

```text
Egocentric
!= OneProcess
```

---

# 36. B5 requires transformation, not just two stores

If allocentric representation can be intact while map-following fails, the architecture needs an operation:

```text
ReferenceFrameTransformation
```

not merely two independent representation banks.

---

# 37. B5 disposition

```text
B5 Allocentric–Egocentric Dual
= STRONG representational distinction
= FAIL as complete architecture without transformation/updating/cue integration
```

---

# 38. B6 — Reference-Frame Transformation Architecture

B6 treats the core navigation problem as transforming spatial relations among frames so stored/environmental relations can guide current orientation and action.

---

# 39. Selective map-following DTD is B6's strongest case

One DTD patient could build maps, recognize landmarks and follow learned routes yet showed dramatic impairment when allocentrically encoded map information had to guide egocentric navigation in a novel environment.

This is close to a direct selective transformation deficit.

Therefore:

```text
ReferenceFrameTransformationProjection
= real ownership-relevant process candidate
```

---

# 40. PPC/RSC evidence supports transformation roles

Posterior-parietal lesion evidence links PPC to egocentric access/use of remote spatial knowledge.

Retrosplenial activity varies with spatial orientation/reference-frame demands and global heading representations.

This converges with a transformation/interfacing architecture.

---

# 41. But B6 cannot create the spatial content it transforms

Reference-frame transformation presupposes:

```text
source spatial content
source/target frame specification
current self state
perceptual/action anchor
```

Therefore B6 imports HF8/HF20/HF11.

---

# 42. B6 cannot explain path-integration state formation alone

Online translation/rotation updating from optic flow/proprioception can occur before any allocentric-to-egocentric conversion is required.

Thus:

```text
ReferenceFrameTransformation
!= PathIntegrationStateUpdating
```

---

# 43. B6 disposition

```text
B6 Reference-Frame Transformation
= STRONG residual process candidate
= selective case evidence
= not whole navigation ontology
```

---

# 44. B7 — Reliability-Weighted Cue Integration / Recalibration

B7 treats navigation as combining uncertain spatial cues according to estimated reliability/common cause, then recalibrating when discrepancies appear.

---

# 45. Landmark + path-integration behavior strongly supports B7 locally

Visual landmarks and path-integration cues can be integrated to reduce response variability while competing to determine homing direction under conflict.

2025 naturalistic landmark-correction work shows correction magnitude depends on confidence in the current self-motion estimate.

Thus:

```text
ReliabilitySensitiveSpatialCueUse
= real mechanism family
```

---

# 46. But Bayes-optimality is not universal

A loop-closure path-integration paradigm found optic flow and proprioception contributed roughly equally and independently, without evidence of ideal Bayesian combination.

Complex landmark environments show striking individual differences; adding more landmarks can impair some participants.

Therefore:

```text
SpatialCueIntegration
!= UniversalBayesOptimalIntegration
```

---

# 47. Cue integration cannot explain representation loss by itself

DTD, landmark agnosia and allocentric-map-processing deficits can arise from missing/poor representations rather than merely poor reliability weighting.

B7 must import representation and memory systems.

---

# 48. Reorientation is target-relative, not just reliability-weighted

The same geometric/landmark array can support local-object reorientation differently from remote-city direction judgments.

Thus cue utility depends on target semantics/environment structure in addition to precision.

---

# 49. B7 disposition

```text
B7 Reliability-Weighted Cue Integration
= STRONG local estimation/recalibration architecture
= FAIL as representation/memory/planning totality
```

`LandmarkSpatialRecalibrationProjection` survives but is strongly HF20-centered.

---

# 50. B8 — Embodied / Enactive Sensorimotor Navigation

B8 emphasizes that navigation is performed by a moving body with vestibular, proprioceptive, efferent and active-sampling information rather than by detached map reading.

---

# 51. Active-learning decomposition strongly supports B8 locally

Walking/podokinetic cues can improve metric survey accuracy relative to passive visual exposure.

Yet decision making rather than idiothetic input can be the dominant active component for acquiring graph/route knowledge.

Thus embodiment matters, but its contribution depends on the target knowledge.

---

# 52. Vestibular loss demonstrates long-term body–navigation coupling

Bilateral vestibular loss is associated with hippocampal atrophy and selective spatial/navigation deficits, including worse novel-route recombination with preserved familiar-route retracing.

Therefore:

```text
BodySelfMotionSystem
can alter long-term spatial architecture.
```

---

# 53. Blindness and sensory substitution reject visual-navigation identity

Congenitally blind Humans can navigate using auditory/tactile sensory-substitution devices and show navigation-related cortical recruitment.

Spatial updating across sensory substitution can produce allocentric/egocentric spatial representations without normal visual experience.

Thus:

```text
Navigation
!= VisualFormat
```

---

# 54. But embodiment is not sufficient for all navigation knowledge

Visual-only exposure can support above-chance survey knowledge.

Map reading can guide navigation with minimal memory demand and without requiring hippocampal integrity in limited-lesion cases.

Imagined navigation and nonspatial conceptual inference can recruit grid-like codes without actual locomotion.

Therefore:

```text
EmbodiedMovement
!= NecessaryForAllSpatialRepresentationOrInference
```

---

# 55. B8 disposition

```text
B8 Embodied/Enactive Navigation
= STRONG grounding/acquisition architecture
= crucial in some regimes
= FAIL as navigation totality
```

---
