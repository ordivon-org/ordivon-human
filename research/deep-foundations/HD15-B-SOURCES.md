# HD15-B — Primary Evidence Ledger

## B01 — Samad, Chung & Shams 2015 — Bayesian sensory inference of ownership

`Perception of body ownership is driven by Bayesian sensory inference.`
PLoS ONE 10(2):e0117178.
PMID: 25658822.
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0117178.

A visual/proprioceptive/tactile Bayesian causal-inference model predicted rubber-hand ownership without tactile stimulation and stronger ownership under synchronous stroking; experiments confirmed these predictions.

Use:

```text
OwnershipInference can be modeled as common-cause inference over uncertain bodily evidence.
```

Limit:

```text
local ownership computation != whole bodily-self architecture
```

---

## B02 — Chancel, Ma & Ehrsson 2022 — uncertainty-sensitive common-cause inference

`Uncertainty-based inference of a common cause for body ownership.`
eLife 11:e77221.
PMID: 36165441.
DOI: 10.7554/eLife.77221.

Psychophysical detection paradigm manipulated visuotactile asynchrony and visual noise. Ownership probability increased with uncertainty and was better fit by an uncertainty-aware causal-inference model than a non-Bayesian alternative.

Use:

```text
SensoryReliability changes OwnershipInference.
```

## B03 — Chancel & Ehrsson 2023 — proprioceptive uncertainty

`Proprioceptive uncertainty promotes the rubber hand illusion.`
Cortex 165:70–85.
PMID: 37269634.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cortex.2023.04.005.

Tendon vibration increased proprioceptive noise. Ownership-illusion probability increased and was captured by Bayesian causal inference, particularly through the inferred common-cause prior.

Use:

```text
ProprioceptiveUncertainty
!= noise-only nuisance;
it changes self-body common-cause inference.
```

## B04 — Chancel, Iriye & Ehrsson 2022 — neural causal-inference variables

`Causal Inference of Body Ownership in the Posterior Parietal Cortex.`
Journal of Neuroscience 42(37):7131–7143.
PMID: 35940875.
DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0656-22.2022.

Thirty healthy participants completed trial-wise ownership detection under varying visuotactile asynchrony during fMRI; posterior-parietal responses were fit with Bayesian causal-inference variables and predicted ownership decisions.

Use:

```text
BCI variables have neural implementation evidence.
BrainRegion != semantic owner.
```

## B05 — D’Angelo, Lanfranco, Chancel & Ehrsson 2026 — causal alpha perturbation

`Parietal alpha frequency shapes own-body perception by modulating the temporal integration of bodily signals.`
Nature Communications 17:53.
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-67657-w.
Published 2026-01-12.

Across psychophysics, EEG, tACS and computational modeling, parietal individual alpha frequency predicted body-ownership and visuotactile-simultaneity temporal binding windows/sensitivity. Causally increasing/decreasing alpha frequency narrowed/widened those windows and altered sensitivity. Bayesian modeling linked alpha frequency to uncertainty in temporal evidence.

Use:

```text
TemporalIntegrationPrecision
causally contributes to
OwnershipInference.
```

Limit:

```text
shared temporal integration mechanism
!= ownership semantic owner
```

## B06 — O'Kane, Chancel & Ehrsson 2024 — hierarchical part/full-body ownership

`Hierarchical and dynamic relationships between body part ownership and full-body ownership.`
Cognition 246:105697.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2023.105697.

Local part ownership depended mainly on local synchrony and could occur relatively independently of full-body ownership. Full-body ownership depended nonlinearly on the number of synchronously stimulated parts and fed back onto nonstimulated part ownership.

Use:

```text
PartOwnership != FullBodyOwnership
Local→global integration is nonlinear
Global→local contextual feedback exists
```

## B07 — Kondo et al. 2020 — scrambled body

`Scrambled body differentiates body part ownership from the full body illusion.`
Scientific Reports 10:5274.
PMID: 32210268.
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-62121-9.

Synchronous visuomotor stimulation of spatially scrambled hands/feet induced body-part ownership without corresponding full-body ownership, whereas normal configuration supported both.

Use:

```text
GlobalBodyStructure adds constraints beyond local part synchrony.
```

## B08 — Kalckert & Ehrsson 2012 — ownership/agency double dissociation

`Moving a Rubber Hand that Feels Like Your Own: A Dissociation of Ownership and Agency.`
Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 6:40.
PMID: 22435056.
DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040.

Passive synchronous movement preserved ownership but abolished agency; active synchronous movement with anatomically incongruent hand orientation preserved agency while diminishing ownership.

Use:

```text
BodyOwnership != SenseOfAgency
```

This is a direct destructive row for unitary bodily-self theories.

## B09 — Ma & Hommel 2015 — active noncorporeal proxies

PMID: 26094223.

Participants reported ownership for actively controlled virtual balloons/squares whose visual changes covaried systematically with hand movements.

## B10 — Tsakiris et al. 2010 — structural/corporeal constraint

`Hands only illusion: multisensory integration elicits sense of ownership for body parts but not for non-corporeal objects.`
Experimental Brain Research.
PMID: 19820918.
DOI: 10.1007/s00221-009-2039-3.

Passive visuotactile synchrony produced ownership more readily for plausible body-part targets than arbitrary objects.

## B11 — Ferri et al. 2013 — predictive expectation without delivered touch

`The body beyond the body: expectation of a sensory event is enough to induce ownership over a fake hand.`
Proceedings of the Royal Society B 280:20131140.
PMID: 23804622.
DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2013.1140.

Approach of an experimenter's hand toward a plausible rubber hand, without actual tactile contact, produced ownership-related questionnaire/autonomic effects; rotated hand/wood controls reduced the effect.

Use:

```text
DeliveredCrossmodalCorrelation != necessary in every ownership regime.
Prediction/expectation and structural constraints participate.
```

## B12 — Fotopoulou et al. 2011 — perspective-dependent somatoparaphrenia

`Mirror-view reverses somatoparaphrenia: dissociation between first- and third-person perspectives on body ownership.`
Neuropsychologia 49(14):3946–3955.
PMID: 22023911.
DOI: 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.011.

Two somatoparaphrenic patients repeatedly switched ownership/disownership judgments of the same plegic hand within seconds as direct first-person and mirror third-person views alternated.

Use:

```text
BiologicalParthood fixed
while OwnershipAttribution changes with perspective.

FirstPersonBodyRepresentation
!= ThirdPersonBodyRecognition
```

Cortex 188:53–67.

DOI: 10.1016/j.cortex.2025.04.014.

Double-blind sham-controlled crossover N=104 manipulated dlPFC/TPJ activity while prospective action-choice and retrospective outcome-valence cues were varied in binding tasks.

```text
ProspectiveAgencyEvidence
!= RetrospectiveAgencyEvidence
```

## B15 — Mariano et al. 2026 — explicit/implicit agency dissociation in VR

`My avatar moves with me, so I am the one acting: Avatar responsiveness supports implicit sense of agency in virtual reality.`
PLoS ONE 21(6):e0351839.
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0351839.
Published 2026-06-22.

Two experiments, total N=70. Explicit agency was greater for active than passive actions in both still-avatar and responsive-avatar experiments. Intentional binding appeared only with responsive avatar movements and only at the shortest 200 ms action-outcome delay.

Use:

```text
ExplicitAgencyJudgment
!= IntentionalBinding

AvatarResponsiveness
can affect one agency-related readout
without equivalently affecting another.
```

## B16 — Itatani et al. 2025 — ownership and vicarious agency under minimal control

`Body ownership and vicarious Agency: Behavioural consequences in a virtual reality rock concert.`
Consciousness and Cognition 133:103903.
DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2025.103903.

Participants first experienced synchronous or asynchronous embodiment as a virtual rock performer. Greater prior ownership in the synchronous condition predicted later vicarious agency and imitation of avatar behavior during a stage performance despite little actual control of the later performing avatar.

Use:

```text
ActualMotorControl != necessary for every agency-like self-attribution
BodyOwnership can bias downstream agency attribution.
```

## B17 — Pyasik et al. 2026 — online postural coupling under full-body ownership

`The role of body ownership in postural control: A virtual full body illusion study.`
Cognition 272:106509.
PMID: 41812534.
DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2026.106509.

During first-person virtual embodiment, participants' center of pressure shifted coherently with an avatar's single-leg-squat phases; no equivalent effect occurred during third-person observation, and no prolonged post-exposure effect remained.

Use:

```text
FullBodyOwnership can alter online sensorimotor/postural representation
without becoming identical to postural control.
```

# Evidence synthesis

The evidence supports three local architectures more strongly than any total bodily-self theory:

```text
BodyOwnershipCausalInferenceProjection
PartWholeOwnershipIntegrationProjection
AgencyCueIntegrationProjection
```

and directly falsifies strong universal forms of:

```text
one minimal-self integrator
fixed structural-body template
motor comparator as total agency mechanism
active sensorimotor contingency as necessary ownership mechanism
one implicit agency meter
```

The 2026 causal-alpha study is particularly important because it turns one computational component of ownership from correlational plausibility into causal manipulation evidence, while simultaneously showing that the manipulated mechanism is shared with a more generic visuotactile simultaneity process. That is strong scientific reality but weak semantic-owner specificity.

No source in this ledger establishes HF24, HOC11, or a reopen condition for HF0–HF23.
