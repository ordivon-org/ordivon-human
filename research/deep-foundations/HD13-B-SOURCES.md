# HD13-B — Primary Evidence Ledger

## B1 ANS / approximate numerosity

### S01 — Barth et al. 2005
`Non-symbolic arithmetic in adults and young children.`
PMID: 15876429. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2004.09.011.

Adults and preschoolers performed approximate arithmetic on large nonsymbolic sets.

Use:
```text
ApproximateArithmetic = real capability family
```

### S02 — Hyde, Khanum & Spelke 2014
`Brief non-symbolic, approximate number practice enhances subsequent exact symbolic arithmetic in children.`
PMID: 24462713. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2013.12.007.

Short approximate-number practice improved subsequent exact symbolic arithmetic relative to nonnumerical magnitude controls in the reported experiments.

Use as positive transfer evidence only.

### S03 — Park & Brannon 2013
`Training the approximate number system improves math proficiency.`
PMID: 23921769.

Approximate-arithmetic training improved symbolic addition/subtraction in the reported adult experiments.

### S04 — Au et al. / first-grade training 2018
`Testing the Efficacy of Training Basic Numerical Cognition and Transfer Effects to Improvement in Children's Math Ability.`
PMID: 30333768.

Six weeks of training improved ANS acuity but did not transfer to exact calculation or broader math ability.

Use:
```text
ANSAcuityGain != SymbolicMathTransferByDefinition
```

### S05 — Lindskog et al. 2021
`Failure to replicate the benefit of approximate arithmetic training for symbolic arithmetic fluency in adults.`
PMID: 33280814.

Four experiments, total N=318, found approximate arithmetic training no more effective than control training for symbolic arithmetic fluency.

### S06 — Lau et al. 2021
`Kindergarteners' symbolic number abilities predict nonsymbolic number abilities and math achievement in grade 1.`
PMID: 33630621. DOI: 10.1037/dev0001158.

N=622 longitudinal modeling favored a refinement direction: earlier symbolic ability predicted later nonsymbolic/mixed ability and arithmetic more strongly than ANS-first predictions.

### S07 — 2026 large cohort co-development
`Co-developmental trajectories of comparison, ordering, and arithmetic skills for children in grade 2 to grade 4.`
PMID: 41875220. DOI: 10.1093/chidev/aacag029.

N=9,552 and N=3,189 cohorts. Earlier arithmetic predicted later comparison/order more strongly than reverse paths.

Use:
```text
DevelopmentalDirection != OneWayANSFoundation
```

### S08 — infant ANS longitudinal study 2023
`Number sense at 12 months predicts 4-year-olds' maths skills.`
PMID: 36869432. DOI: 10.1111/desc.13386.

Positive evidence that early approximate numerical sensitivity can predict later symbolic math beyond measured inhibition/intelligence in this sample.

### S09 — nonsymbolic arithmetic compositionality 2023
`Is Nonsymbolic Arithmetic Truly "Arithmetic"? Examining the Computational Capacity of the Approximate Number System in Young Children.`
PMID: 37303302. DOI: 10.1111/cogs.13299.

Children failed to reliably reuse a nonsymbolic arithmetic result as input to a subsequent nonsymbolic operation in the tested paradigm.

Use:
```text
ApproximateArithmeticLikePerformance != FullFunctionLikeArithmetic
```

# B2 OTS / subitizing

### S10 — Piazza et al. 2011
`Subitizing reflects visuo-spatial object individuation capacity.`
PMID: 21679934.

Subitizing capacity covaried with visual object individuation.

### S11 — Burr et al. 2011
`Evidence for a shared mechanism used in multiple-object tracking and subitizing.`
PMID: 21968785.

Tracking load reduced subitizing capacity in a resource-specific manner.

### S12 — vanMarle et al. 2016
`Attaching meaning to the number words: contributions of the object tracking and approximate number systems.`
PMID: 27981702. DOI: 10.1111/desc.12495.

N=198 preschoolers; both OTS and ANS measures predicted early cardinal knowledge, inconsistent with ANS-only and OTS-only models.

# B3 Triple-Code / multi-code

### S13 — Malone et al. 2019
`Learning correspondences between magnitudes, symbols and words: Evidence for a triple code model of arithmetic development.`
PMID: 30797098. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2018.11.016.

N=166 children. Learning mappings from magnitudes to verbal/visual symbols predicted arithmetic beyond other measured predictors.

### S14 — Rockhill et al. 2024
`Investigating the Triple Code Model in numerical cognition using stereotactic electroencephalography.`
PMID: 39625888. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0313155.

13 epilepsy patients; format/modality-sensitive numerical activity with strong bilateral parietal engagement across formats.

### S15 — Sokol et al. 1991
`Cognitive representations and processes in arithmetic: inferences from the performance of brain-damaged subjects.`
PMID: 1845392. DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.17.3.355.

Single-patient evidence distinguishes individual facts, general rules and procedures; fact retrieval used abstract internal representations not reducible to input/output format.

### S16 — van Harskamp et al. 2002
`Are multiplication facts implemented by the left supramarginal and angular gyri?`
PMID: 12062890.

Patient preserved multiplication facts but had impaired subtraction facts with intact quantity manipulation, contradicting a simple operation→Triple-Code mapping.

# B4 exact-integer conceptual development

### S17 — Sarnecka & Carey 2008
`How counting represents number: what children must learn and when they learn it.`
PMID: 18572155. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2008.05.007.

Cardinal-principle knowers showed qualitatively stronger successor/unit-change knowledge than subset-knowers.

### S18 — Sarnecka & Wright 2013
`The idea of an exact number: children's understanding of cardinality and equinumerosity.`
PMID: 23672476. DOI: 10.1111/cogs.12043.

Exact cardinality, successor and equinumerosity are interrelated conceptual achievements.

### S19 — Schneider et al. 2021
`Counting and the ontogenetic origins of exact equality.`
PMID: 34801862.

Exact set matching improved with cardinal-principle knowledge but was not perfectly guaranteed immediately after acquisition.

Use:
```text
ExactIntegerDevelopment = multi-relation / staged
```

# B5 language / symbolic access / cross-cultural

### S20 — Pica et al. 2004
`Exact and approximate arithmetic in an Amazonian indigene group.`
PMID: 15486303.

Munduruku participants performed large approximate comparison/addition beyond number-word range but failed exact arithmetic beyond the small exact lexical range.

### S21 — Frank et al. 2008
`Number as a cognitive technology: evidence from Piraha language and cognition.`
PMID: 18547557. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2008.04.007.

Exact one-to-one matching could succeed without exact number words under external support; memory-demanding exact matching degraded.

### S22 — da Silva Sinha et al. 2026
`Numerical cognition in speakers of an Amazonian language with exactly twenty number words.`
PMID: 42053677. DOI: 10.1007/s00426-026-02300-x.

Awety speakers possessed a conventional exact counting system to twenty and corresponding exact performance within range.

### S23 — Rousselle & Noël 2007
`Basic numerical skills in children with mathematics learning disabilities: a comparison of symbolic vs non-symbolic number magnitude processing.`
PMID: 16488405. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2006.01.005.

Mathematics-disabled children were impaired on symbolic digit magnitude but not nonsymbolic collection comparison in the tested sample.

### S24 — Castro Cañizares et al. 2012
`Symbolic and non-symbolic number magnitude processing in children with developmental dyscalculia.`
PMID: 23156905. DOI: 10.5209/rev_sjop.2012.v15.n3.39387.

DD children were slower on symbolic magnitude but showed similar symbolic/nonsymbolic distance/congruity patterns, supporting an access-deficit interpretation in that sample.

### S25 — Decarli et al. 2022
`Severe Developmental Dyscalculia Is Characterized by Core Deficits in Both Symbolic and Nonsymbolic Number Sense.`
PMID: 36282938. DOI: 10.1177/09567976221097947.

Severe DD selected with average domain-general skills showed deficits in both symbolic and nonsymbolic tasks.

### S26 — Skagerlund & Träff 2016
`Number Processing and Heterogeneity of Developmental Dyscalculia: Subtypes With Different Cognitive Profiles and Deficits.`
PMID: 24598147. DOI: 10.1177/0022219414522707.

General-DD and arithmetic-fact-DD groups showed different symbolic/nonsymbolic profiles.

Use:
```text
Dyscalculia != OneSymbolicAccessPhenotype
```

### S27 — Chang et al. 2026
`Latent Neurocognitive Mechanisms Underlying Quantity Discrimination in Children with and without Mathematical Learning Disabilities.`
PMID: 41663251. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2385-24.2025.

Drift-diffusion/dynamic monitoring plus fMRI revealed symbolic-format response-caution and post-error-adjustment differences; supports multidimensional MLD and extends symbolic-access models with control/metacognitive processes.

# B6 generalized magnitude / embodiment / sensorimotor

### S28 — Dormal et al. 2006
`Numerosity-duration interference: a Stroop experiment.`
PMID: 16095549. DOI: 10.1016/j.actpsy.2005.06.003.

Numerosity interfered with duration judgments while duration did not symmetrically interfere with numerosity.

### S29 — Agrillo et al. 2010
`Time and numerosity estimation are independent: Behavioral evidence for two different systems using a conflict paradigm.`
PMID: 24168275. DOI: 10.1080/17588921003632537.

A different auditory conflict paradigm found no mutual interference.

Use:
```text
MagnitudeInteraction != OneGeneralMagnitudeMechanism
```

### S30 — Anobile et al. 2016
`A shared numerical representation for action and perception.`
PMID: 27504969. DOI: 10.7554/eLife.16161.

Finger-tapping adaptation altered subsequent visual numerosity estimates across simultaneous/sequential presentations.

### S31 — Togoli et al. 2020
`The shared numerical representation for action and perception develops independently from vision.`
PMID: 32580065. DOI: 10.1016/j.cortex.2020.05.004.

Action adaptation affected auditory numerosity estimates in both sighted and congenitally blind participants.

### S32 — Kanjlia et al. 2016
`Absence of visual experience modifies the neural basis of numerical thinking.`
PMID: 27638209. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1524982113.

Congenitally blind adults recruited the canonical IPS/frontoparietal numerical network despite absent visual experience, with additional occipital recruitment.

### S33 — Crollen et al. 2021
`Early visual deprivation does not prevent the emergence of basic numerical abilities in blind children.`
PMID: 33477011. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2021.104586.

Blind children showed comparable or stronger basic numerical competence despite different memory associations.

### S34 — Anobile et al. 2026
`Sensorimotor numerosity uniquely supports arithmetic development in children.`
PMID: 42181107. DOI: 10.1177/20416695261443209.

Action-numerosity estimation precision explained substantial variance in mental calculation beyond age, reasoning, nonnumerical motor skills and visual numerosity precision in the reported cross-sectional study.

# B7 domain-general control / measurement

### S35 — Cheng et al. 2020
`Visual form perception predicts 3-year longitudinal development of mathematical achievement.`
PMID: 32556792.

Visual form perception statistically explained the longitudinal ANS–math relation in the reported data.

### S36 — Cheng et al. 2020 / training mediation
`Short-term numerosity training promotes symbolic arithmetic in children with developmental dyscalculia: The mediating role of visual form perception.`
PMID: 31599035. DOI: 10.1111/desc.12910.

Arithmetic gains following numerosity training were statistically fully mediated by visual-form-perception improvement.

### S37 — Soto-Calvo et al. 2015
`Identifying the cognitive predictors of early counting and calculation skills: Evidence from a longitudinal study.`
PMID: 26218332. DOI: 10.1016/j.jecp.2015.06.011.

Phonological/visuospatial memory and nonsymbolic approximate discrimination contributed to early calculation growth.
