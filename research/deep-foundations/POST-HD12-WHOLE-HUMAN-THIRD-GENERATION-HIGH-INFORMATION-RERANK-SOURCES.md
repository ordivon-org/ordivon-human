# Post-HD12 Third-Generation Re-Ranking — Primary Evidence Ledger

## N01 — Dehaene & Cohen 1997 — arithmetic double dissociation

`Cerebral pathways for calculation: double dissociation between rote verbal and quantitative knowledge of arithmetic.`
Cortex 33(2):219–250.
PMID: 9220256. DOI: 10.1016/S0010-9452(08)70002-9.

Two acalculic patients showed opposite deficits in rote verbal arithmetic facts versus quantitative numerical knowledge.

Use:

```text
ArithmeticFactKnowledge != QuantitativeMagnitudeKnowledge
Calculation != OneUnitaryFaculty
```

---

## N02 — Stanescu-Cosson et al. 2000 — exact vs approximate networks

`Understanding dissociations in dyscalculia: a brain imaging study of the impact of number size on the cerebral networks for exact and approximate calculation.`
Brain 123(Pt 11):2240–2255.
PMID: 11050024. DOI: 10.1093/brain/123.11.2240.

Healthy-participant fMRI/ERP results distinguished greater verbal/angular involvement for small exact/rote calculation from bilateral parietal/frontoparietal involvement in approximation and large-number quantitative processing.

Use:

```text
ExactCalculation != ApproximateCalculation
```

without claiming two exhaustive modules.

---

## N03 — exact/approximate dissociation in global aphasia 2009

`Dissociation of exact and approximate calculation in severe global aphasia.`
PMID: 19452030.

Severe language impairment coexisted with relatively preserved numerical comprehension/approximation and impaired exact calculation in the reported patient.

Use:

```text
LanguageImpairment != ApproximateQuantityLossByDefinition
```

---

## N04 — Pica et al. 2004 — Munduruku exact vs approximate arithmetic

`Exact and approximate arithmetic in an Amazonian indigene group.`
Science.
PMID: 15486303.

Munduruku participants with number words only for a small exact range could compare/add large approximate numerosities but failed exact arithmetic beyond the small exact lexical range.

Use:

```text
ApproximateQuantityRepresentation
!= ExactSymbolicNumberRepresentation
```

and:

```text
LargeExactNumberLexicon
is not required for large approximate arithmetic.
```

---

## N05 — Frank et al. 2008 — Piraha exact matching under support

`Number as a cognitive technology: evidence from Piraha language and cognition.`
Cognition 108(3):819–824.
PMID: 18547557. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2008.04.007.

Piraha speakers without an exact number-word system could perform exact matching for large visible sets when one-to-one external support was available, while memory-demanding matching produced inaccuracy.

Use:

```text
NoExactNumberWords != NoExactQuantityPerformanceUnderAllSupports
ExactMatching != ExactNumberConceptByDefinition
```

---

## N06 — da Silva Sinha et al. 2026 — Awety numerical system

`Numerical cognition in speakers of an Amazonian language with exactly twenty number words.`
Psychological Research 90(3):83.
PMID: 42053677. DOI: 10.1007/s00426-026-02300-x.

The study documented a conventional Awety counting system to twenty and found reliable exact numerical performance within that range; nonsymbolic number performance was comparable to Portuguese-speaking controls on tested tasks.

Use:

```text
AmazonianLanguage != RestrictedExactNumberSystemByDefinition
```

and require language/culture-specific measurement rather than broad ethnic inference.

---

## N07 — Ren et al. 2025 — symbolic/nonsymbolic integration task dependence

`Integration of symbolic and non-symbolic numerical information in children: Task dependence and its link to math abilities.`
Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 256:106263.
PMID: 40252638. DOI: 10.1016/j.jecp.2025.106263.

N=119 children aged 8–10.

One comparison task showed mixed-format symbolic/nonsymbolic costs while another simultaneous discrimination paradigm showed facilitation for matching symbolic/nonsymbolic quantities; derived integration measures did not straightforwardly predict standardized mathematics achievement.

Use:

```text
SymbolicIntegration != OneStableTraitByDefinition
SymbolicNumber != NonsymbolicNumerosity
```

---

## N08 — Decarli et al. 2022 — severe dyscalculia and basic number sense

`Severe Developmental Dyscalculia Is Characterized by Core Deficits in Both Symbolic and Nonsymbolic Number Sense.`
Psychological Science.
PMID: 36282938. DOI: 10.1177/09567976221097947.

Children with severe dyscalculia were selected with average domain-general skills and matched on IQ, age and visuospatial memory; deficits appeared in both symbolic and nonsymbolic number-sense tasks.

Use:

```text
NumericalDeficit != LowIQByDefinition
```

---

## N09 — weaker numerosity encoding in dyscalculia 2024

`Weaker number sense accounts for impaired numerosity perception in dyscalculia: Behavioral and computational evidence.`
PMID: 38949566.

Psychophysical and computational analyses supported weaker numerosity encoding rather than greater reliance on continuous non-numerical visual properties as the sole explanation.

Use:

```text
PoorNumerosityJudgment != VisualFeatureInterferenceOnly
```

---

## N10 — longitudinal MLD predictors 2025

`Early cognitive predictors of mathematical learning disabilities: A longitudinal study from kindergarten to second grade.`
PMID: 41207131.

N=226 followed longitudinally; symbolic comparison, number identification and executive working memory predicted later MLD status in the reported model.

Use:

```text
MLD != OneNonsymbolicNumberSenseDeficitByDefinition
```

---

## N11 — Chang et al. 2026 — latent MLD quantity mechanisms

`Latent Neurocognitive Mechanisms Underlying Quantity Discrimination in Children with and without Mathematical Learning Disabilities.`
Journal of Neuroscience 46(10):e2385242025.
PMID: 41663251. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2385-24.2025.

Symbolic and nonsymbolic quantity discrimination showed format-dependent latent differences in response caution and post-error adjustment, with symbolic latent processes more strongly predicting broader math ability in the reported data.

Use:

```text
ObservedQuantityPerformance != NumericalRepresentationOnly
```

and preserve HF3 control/metacognition.

---

## N12 — developmental delay heterogeneity 2024

`Delayed development of basic numerical skills in children with developmental dyscalculia.`
PMID: 38274693.

N=480 across grades 2–4. Many dyscalculia-group numerical differences resembled developmental delay, while dot enumeration showed a more specific exception.

Use:

```text
Dyscalculia != OneQualitativeNumericalDeviation
DevelopmentalDelay != SelectiveDeficit
```

without task-specific evidence.

---

## P01 — Luther et al. 2026 — psychopathology comparison source

`Computational phenotypes underlying effort-based decision-making and negative symptoms in a transdiagnostic severe mental illness sample.`
Molecular Psychiatry.
PMID: 41691110. DOI: 10.1038/s41380-026-03474-x.

N=920 across schizophrenia, first-episode psychosis, bipolar disorder, depression, clinical-high-risk, other clinical and healthy groups.

Different diagnostic groups were best fit by different effort/reward computational profiles; simple equifinality to negative symptoms was not supported.

Use only for ranking:

```text
PsychiatricEndpoint != OneMechanismByDiagnosis
```

Psychopathology remains a powerful falsifier axis but not a current ordinary-function owner leader.
