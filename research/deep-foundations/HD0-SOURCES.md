# HD0 — Evidence Ledger

## Use rule

HD0 is a direction search, not a domain review. Sources below establish that candidate
bridges remain scientifically live, heterogeneous, or actively revised. They do not by
themselves define a Human ontology.

---

## S01 — Active sensing in human olfaction

**Sagar, V. et al. (2025). _The human brain modulates sniffs according to fine-grained
perceptual features of odours_. Nature Human Behaviour.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02327-x`

Use: shows a concrete perception-action loop in which sensory sampling behavior (sniff)
is modulated by perceptual properties, making one-way `signal → percept` accounts
insufficient as a universal grammar.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02327-x

---

## S02 — Developing human cortex remains mechanistically unresolved

**Keefe, M. G., Steyert, M. R. & Nowakowski, T. J. (2025). _Lineage-resolved atlas of
the developing human cortex_. Nature 647, 194–202.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41586-025-09033-8`

Use: prospective lineage tracing of 6,402 progenitor cells reveals developmental cell
relationships and switches not resolved by prior snapshot atlases. Supports HD0's claim
that neural implementation/development remains an active reconstruction target.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09033-8

Related:
Nano, P. R. et al. (2025), _Integrated analysis of molecular atlases unveils modules
driving developmental cell subtype specification in the human cortex_, Nature
Neuroscience 28, 949–963. DOI `10.1038/s41593-025-01933-2`.

## S03 — Language universals under phylogenetic/geographic control

**Greenhill and collaborators (2025/2026). _Enduring constraints on grammar revealed by
Bayesian spatiophylogenetic analyses_. Nature Human Behaviour 10, 126–136.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02325-z`

Use: tests 191 proposed universals against Grambank data for 2,430 languages and finds
robust support for roughly one third, while also finding recurrent preferred patterns.
Supports plural, falsifiable accounts of language constraints rather than assuming a
traditional universal list is settled.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02325-z

---

## S04 — Comparative language-network anatomy

**Becker, Y. et al. (2025). _Long arcuate fascicle in wild and captive chimpanzees as a
potential structural precursor of the language network_. Nature Communications 16,
4485.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41467-025-59254-8`

Use: high-resolution tractography identifies a chimpanzee arcuate-fascicle connection
into middle temporal cortex previously treated as more strictly human-specific. Supports
comparative/evolutionary pressure on language-origin claims and gradual-precuror models.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59254-8

---

## S05 — Human cultural uniqueness remains theoretically live

**Morgan, T. J. H. & Feldman, M. W. (2025). _Human culture is uniquely open-ended rather
than uniquely cumulative_. Nature Human Behaviour 9, 28–42.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-024-02035-y`

Use: argues that cumulative change and high-fidelity transmission are not uniquely human
and proposes the open-ended scope of inherited cultural variation as a stronger candidate
feature. Used as a rival hypothesis, not settled ontology.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02035-y

---

## S06 — Culture outside humans and developmental dependence on social learning

**_Culture is critical in driving orangutan diet development past individual
potentials_ (2025). Nature Human Behaviour.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02350-y`

Use: empirically calibrated agent-based simulations based on long-term wild data indicate
that social learning is required for orangutan diet repertoires to reach adult-like
breadth on observed developmental timescales. Challenges Human=culture simple binaries.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02350-y

---

## S07 — Emotion regulation is culturally moderated

**Chen, M. S. et al. (2025). _Emotion regulation and mental health across cultures: a
systematic review and meta-analysis_. Nature Human Behaviour 9, 1176–1200.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02168-8`

Use: 249 articles, 150,474 participants, 37 countries/regions; associations between
reappraisal/suppression and mental-health outcomes vary with cultural moderators and
sample characteristics. Supports culture-sensitive affect/regulation models.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02168-8

---

## S08 — Human relationship concepts across modern/historical cultures

**Cheng, X. et al. (2025). _The conceptual structure of human relationships across
modern and historical cultures_. Nature Human Behaviour 9, 1162–1175.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02122-8`

Use: combines >20,000 modern participants with historical language material spanning
roughly 3,000 years; finds shared relational dimensions/categories alongside cultural
variation. Supports a dedicated relational-representation pressure beyond generic
`interaction`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02122-8

---

## S09 — Social interaction becomes developmentally embodied

**Carollo, A. (2026). _How social interactions become embodied in development_. Nature
Reviews Psychology.**

Use: synthesizes developmental evidence on caregiver interaction, attachment, bodily
regulation and socio-emotional development. Used to identify a cross-scale
relation→development→body bridge, not as a universal attachment theory.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-026-00592-9

---

## S10 — RDoC as dimensional, multi-unit perturbation framework

**US National Institute of Mental Health. Research Domain Criteria (RDoC).**

Use: RDoC studies functional constructs across normal-to-abnormal dimensions and
multiple units of analysis (including genes, circuits, physiology, behavior and
self-report), while explicitly treating development and environment as essential
contexts. HD0 uses this as evidence that pathology can be a construct-falsification
surface rather than merely a diagnostic taxonomy.

Sources:
- https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/about-rdoc
- https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/rdoc-matrix

## S11 — Heritability is method-, measure- and context-dependent

**Ebeltoft, J. C. et al. (2025). _The genetic and environmental composition of
socioeconomic status in Norway_. Nature Communications 16, 4461.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41467-025-58961-6`

Use: >170,000-person Norwegian cohort and four heritability approaches show materially
different genetic/environmental variance estimates across measures and methods. The
paper explicitly emphasizes that heritability is a population statistic, is
context-dependent, and does not imply immutability or individual determination.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58961-6

## S12 — Disability/functioning is contextual

**World Health Organization — International Classification of Functioning, Disability
and Health (ICF).**

Use: WHO's framework explicitly treats functioning/disability as occurring in context
and includes environmental factors. HD0 uses it as an external check on the existing HF
relational capability grammar.

Source: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health

## S13 — Psychology itself remains construct-fragmented

**Anvari, F. et al. (2025). _Defragmenting psychology_. Nature Human Behaviour 9,
836–839.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02138-0`

Use: explicit contemporary critique of proliferation and low reuse of psychological
constructs/measures. Supports HD0's bridge-first rather than construct-accumulation
research strategy.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02138-0

## S14 — Predictive coding / active inference empirical status

**Hodson, R., Mehta, M. & Smith, R. (2024). _The empirical status of predictive coding
and active inference_. Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 157, 105473.**

DOI: `10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105473`

Use: review finds only modest/incomplete direct empirical support and emphasizes formal
comparison with alternative models. Used as a grand-theory guard.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38030100/

---

## S15 — Active inference becoming more explicitly falsifiable, still unsettled

**Lageman, J., Fahrenfort, J. J. & Slagter, H. A. (2026). _Prediction in action: toward
an empirical science of active inference_. Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews,
106817.**

DOI: `10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106817`

Use: derives/compares more specific predictions for decision and motor-control domains,
while concluding that existing evidence remains promising but insufficient for strong
closure. Supports HD1 comparison rather than adopting active inference by fiat.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42285188/

---

## S16 — Teaching, leadership and opaque culture across forager societies

**Garfield, Z. H. & Lew-Levy, S. (2025). _Teaching is associated with the transmission
of opaque culture and leadership across 23 egalitarian hunter-gatherer societies_.
Nature Communications 16, 3387.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41467-025-58764-9`

Use: comparative ethnographic evidence distinguishes instrumental from opaque cultural
transmission and connects teaching with leadership/social influence. Supports culture as
transmission architecture, not merely a static norm set.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58764-9

---

## S17 — Generative AI can express culturally conditioned tendencies

**Lu, J. G., Song, L. L. & Zhang, L. D. (2025). _Cultural tendencies in generative AI_.
Nature Human Behaviour 9, 2360–2369.**

DOI: `10.1038/s41562-025-02242-1`

Use: demonstrates that language/context can alter measured cultural tendencies in model
outputs. HD0 uses this as evidence that AI is not a neutral external comparator and can
itself carry culturally structured training/context effects.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02242-1

# Source boundary

HD0 intentionally does not attempt exhaustive reviews of perception, evolution,
emotion, language, culture, development, psychopathology or genetics. Each admitted deep
route must construct its own fuller primary-evidence ledger before any foundation-level
revision is considered.
