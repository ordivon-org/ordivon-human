# HD13-B — Rival Numerical-Cognition Models, Measurement Decomposition & Cross-Task Falsification

## 0. Entry

```text
NumericalCognition
= heterogeneous research umbrella
```

```text
QuantitativeRelationRepresentation
!= HF24
```

B asks:

> Which model family explains the same cross-cultural, developmental, lesion and task battery without silently redefining `number`, using task score as mechanism, or importing neighboring owners as invisible auxiliary modules?

# 3. B1 — ANS-primary / analog numerical magnitude

The strongest ANS claim is not `all mathematics is ANS`.

A defensible ANS family posits noisy approximate representations of nonsymbolic numerosity with ratio-sensitive discrimination, available early and across species.

This family has substantial empirical content.

---

# 4. B1 succeeds on large approximate numerosity

Humans can compare large nonsymbolic quantities without counting.

Preschoolers can perform approximate addition/subtraction-like operations on large sets before formal arithmetic instruction.

Young children can even perform approximate multiplication-like tasks before symbolic multiplication instruction.

Thus:

```text
LargeApproximateNumerosity
is a real Human capability family.
```

B does not reduce this to symbolic arithmetic.

---

# 5. B1 survives one developmental prediction

Infant approximate-number performance can predict later mathematical ability even after some general-control covariates are modeled.

This is positive evidence that early approximate numerosity is developmentally relevant.

But:

```text
EarlyPredictor
!= ExclusiveDevelopmentalFoundation
```

---

# 6. B1 fails the simple unidirectional-development story

A large longitudinal kindergarten study comparing mapping versus refinement predictions found earlier symbolic number ability was the strongest predictor of later nonsymbolic ability, mixed symbolic/nonsymbolic comparison and arithmetic.

Therefore:

```text
ANS → SymbolicNumber
```

is not the only viable developmental direction.

Retain:

```text
SymbolicLearning can refine later nonsymbolic performance.
```

---

# 7. Arithmetic can shape later symbolic magnitude/order

A 2026 large-cohort longitudinal analysis found paths from earlier arithmetic to later digit comparison and ordering were stronger than the reverse paths.

Therefore:

```text
FoundationalNumberProcess → Arithmetic
```

cannot be assumed as the sole developmental direction.

Numerical systems co-develop and can be mutually reorganizing.

---

# 8. B1 training evidence is mixed

Some experiments report that brief or repeated approximate-number/arithmetic practice improves subsequent exact symbolic arithmetic.

Other randomized/controlled child studies improve ANS acuity without transfer to exact calculation or broader mathematics.

Four adult experiments failed to replicate a reported symbolic-arithmetic benefit of approximate-arithmetic training.

Therefore:

```text
ANSTrainingGain
!= ReliableSymbolicMathTransferByDefinition
```

and:

```text
TrainingTransfer
cannot identify foundational ownership alone.
```

---

# 9. Visual-form mediation is a serious ANS pressure

In one dyscalculia numerosity-training study, arithmetic improvement was statistically mediated by improved visual form perception.

This means:

```text
NumerosityTrainingEffect
may include HF20 visual-perceptual learning.
```

Thus ANS-task improvement is not automatically evidence of an abstract numerical mechanism.

---

# 10. Nonsymbolic arithmetic is not fully symbolic-like computation

Children can produce approximate solutions to nonsymbolic arithmetic tasks.

But 2023 work found children could not reliably reuse the result of one nonsymbolic computation as an input to a subsequent nonsymbolic operation in the tested paradigm.

Therefore:

```text
ApproximateArithmeticLikePerformance
!= FullCompositionalArithmeticFunctionByDefinition
```

This limits claims that ANS already contains the structure of symbolic arithmetic.

---

# 11. B1 cannot supply exact-integer content alone

Noisy approximate magnitude does not by itself encode:

```text
exact successor
exact equinumerosity
stable arbitrary large integer identity
formal exact equality
```

Exact-integer conceptual development requires additional representational/symbolic structure.

Therefore:

```text
ANS
!= ExactIntegerConceptByDefinition
```

---

# 12. B1 disposition

```text
B1 ANS-primary
= STRONG local account of large approximate numerosity
= PARTIAL developmental contributor
= REJECT as whole numerical-cognition substrate/owner
```

---

# 13. B2 — Object Tracking / parallel individuation

B2 treats small collections through limited-capacity representations of distinct individuals rather than noisy large-set magnitude.

This is strongest in the subitizing range.

---

# 14. B2 has real resource evidence

Subitizing capacity covaries with visual object-individuation capacity.

Concurrent multiple-object tracking consumes subitizing capacity approximately as tracked-object load increases.

Therefore:

```text
SmallSetEnumeration
can depend on object individuation resources.
```

HF20 is an immediate neighboring owner.

---

# 15. B2 has lesion support

Neuropsychological work distinguishes lesion patterns associated with small-set enumeration versus large-set counting.

Classic simultanagnosia evidence likewise supports:

```text
Subitizing != SerialCounting
```

Thus OTS-like machinery is not merely a verbal theory.

---

# 16. B2 is not pure exact-number machinery

Small-set exact labeling is configuration/pattern sensitive.

OTS can individuate `three objects` without necessarily representing the abstract integer three as such.

Therefore:

```text
ObjectIndividuation
!= ExactIntegerConcept
```

and:

```text
Subitizing
!= OTSIdentityByDefinition
```

---

# 17. B2 contributes to cardinal learning but does not own it

Longitudinal preschool work directly compared ANS-only, OTS-only and dual-system models.

Both OTS- and ANS-related measures predicted early cardinal knowledge; later cardinal-principle acquisition was more strongly associated with ANS in that dataset.

Thus:

```text
CardinalityLearning
!= OTSOnly
!= ANSOnly
```

---

# 18. B2 breadth failure

OTS does not naturally explain:

```text
large approximate numerosity
arbitrary exact integers
number words / numerals
ratio/proportion
arithmetic facts
calculation procedures
formal symbolic mathematics
```

without extensive neighboring-owner imports.

---

# 19. B2 disposition

```text
B2 OTS / parallel individuation
= STRONG small-set mechanism family
= IMPORTANT developmental scaffold
= NOT numerical-cognition totality
```

---

# 20. B3 — Triple-Code / multi-code

The Triple-Code family distinguishes approximately:

```text
analog/quantity code
verbal code
visual-Arabic code
```

with task-dependent translation/use.

Unlike one NumberSense, this architecture anticipates major representational dissociations.

---

# 21. B3 developmental support

A developmental paired-associate study found children's learning of mappings between nonsymbolic magnitudes, words and visual symbols predicted arithmetic performance beyond other measured predictors.

This supports:

```text
CrossCodeMapping
is developmentally important.
```

---

# 22. B3 current intracranial support

2024 stereotactic EEG work found format/modality-sensitive activity across parietal, temporal and frontoparietal regions while bilateral parietal regions were strongly engaged across numerical formats.

Therefore:

```text
NumericalFormatSpecificity
can coexist with
cross-format parietal commonality.
```

This is positive evidence for multi-code/layered organization.

---

# 23. B3 fits arithmetic-fact vs quantity dissociations

The architecture naturally accommodates:

```text
verbal arithmetic-fact retrieval
!= quantitative magnitude manipulation
```

and:

```text
symbol form processing
!= represented quantity
```

better than ANS monism.

---

# 24. But B3's three codes are too coarse

HD13-A/B require distinctions not cleanly captured by only three codes:

```text
OTS / object individuation
cardinality vs ordinality
successor/equinumerosity
ratio/proportion
fact vs rule vs procedure
operation-specific fact memory
symbolic order vs magnitude
number-line strategy
response-control/metacognition
```

Therefore:

```text
ThreeCodes
!= CompleteNumericalArchitecture
```

---

# 25. Patient evidence pressures strict Triple-Code task mapping

Acquired-calculation cases can preserve multiplication facts while subtraction facts are impaired despite intact quantity comprehension/manipulation, contrary to a simple mapping of multiplication→verbal facts and subtraction→quantity manipulation.

Other single-patient work shows facts, general rules and procedures can be independently represented/disrupted.

Thus:

```text
ArithmeticOperation
cannot be mapped one-to-one to one code.
```

---

# 26. B3 codes largely coincide with existing owners

The most stable Triple-Code distinctions naturally allocate to:

```text
visual / sensory quantity evidence → HF20
verbal number-word / notation systems → HF23
arithmetic-fact memory → HF7
represented numerical content → HF8
calculation/transformation → HF9
```

So B3 is highly useful architecture without yet producing peer-owner deletion harm.

---

# 27. B3 disposition

```text
B3 Triple-Code / multi-code
= STRONG architectural rival
= better than one-code monism
= incomplete and mostly cross-owner
= NOT HF24 by itself
```

---

# 28. B4 — exact-integer conceptual bootstrapping / counting-principle

B4 asks how Humans acquire exact integer concepts rather than only approximate quantities.

Its key objects include:

```text
count list
cardinal principle
successor relation
exact equality / equinumerosity
one-to-one correspondence
```

---

# 29. B4 explains conceptual discontinuities ANS does not

Children can memorize number words before understanding exact cardinality.

Generalized successor and exact-equality knowledge can emerge later than count-list familiarity.

This supports a real conceptual reconstruction rather than continuous improvement in approximate acuity alone.

---

# 30. Exact equality follows counting-system knowledge imperfectly

2021 set-matching work found exact matching improves with cardinal-principle knowledge but is not perfectly guaranteed immediately after cardinal-principle acquisition.

Therefore:

```text
ExactIntegerConceptDevelopment
= staged / multi-relation
```

rather than one sudden word-to-ANS mapping.

---

# 31. Cross-cultural evidence supports the role of exact symbolic/counting systems

Munduruku evidence separates large approximate arithmetic from exact arithmetic beyond a small lexical range.

Piraha evidence shows exact visible matching can still succeed under one-to-one external support without an exact number-word system.

Awety evidence shows a conventional exact counting system and corresponding exact performance within its learned range.

Thus:

```text
Language/CountingSystem
matters materially for exact number,
```

but:

```text
ExactPerformance
!= ExactNumberWordPossessionByDefinition.
```

---

# 32. B4 ownership pressure is immediate

Exact cardinality, successor, order and equality are relations represented by the Human.

HF8 already owns relational/conceptual representation.

HF23 owns the symbolic count system.

HD4/HF6 own transmission/acquisition dynamics.

Therefore:

```text
ExactIntegerBootstrapping
may be a developmental composition
rather than a new peer owner.
```

---

# 33. B4 disposition

```text
B4 exact-integer bootstrapping
= STRONG account of exact-number conceptual development
= FAIL as large approximate / sensorimotor / arithmetic totality
= strong HF8/HF23/HF6/HD4 ownership pressure
```

---

# 34. B5 — language / symbolic-access accounts

B5 includes several distinct claims:

```text
exact number depends materially on linguistic/symbolic systems
symbolic arithmetic facts can be language coded
some dyscalculia reflects poor access from symbols to quantity
symbolic learning can reorganize numerical processing
```

These are not identical.

---

# 35. Symbolic-access dyscalculia is a real phenotype

Some studies find children with mathematical difficulties perform normally on nonsymbolic quantity comparison but poorly on Arabic-digit magnitude tasks.

Number-line work likewise reports dyscalculia profiles with greater symbolic-access errors but relatively spared analog nonsymbolic mapping.

Thus:

```text
SymbolicAccessDeficit
is a real candidate phenotype.
```

---

# 36. But symbolic-access deficit is not universal dyscalculia

Other carefully selected severe dyscalculia samples show deficits in both symbolic and nonsymbolic number processing despite matched IQ/visuospatial memory.

Subtyping studies find different dyscalculia profiles: some symbolic-only, some symbolic+nonsymbolic.

Therefore:

```text
Dyscalculia
!= SymbolicAccessDeficitByDefinition
```

---

# 37. 2026 latent modeling strengthens but narrows B5

Current MLD drift-diffusion/neuroimaging work finds important symbolic-format differences in response caution and post-error adjustment, while latent symbolic processes predict broader math ability better than nonsymbolic processes in that sample.

This supports symbolic-access/control importance.

But it also shows:

```text
SymbolicDifficulty
!= SymbolToMagnitudeMappingOnly
```

because HF3 control/metacognitive processes contribute.

---

# 38. Symbolic representation can refine nonsymbolic performance

Longitudinal evidence that earlier symbolic number ability predicts later nonsymbolic ability rejects a simple one-way:

```text
preverbal magnitude → symbolic code
```

model.

The relation is developmental and bidirectional/interactive.

---

# 39. Language is not sufficient for exact-number concept

Children can know count words before exact successor/equinumerosity relations.

Exact visible matching can occur under external support without an exact number lexicon.

Thus:

```text
NumberLanguage
!= ExactNumberConcept
```

and:

```text
LanguageSupport
!= SemanticOwner
```

---

# 40. B5 disposition

```text
B5 language / symbolic-access
= STRONG for exact symbolic systems and some clinical/developmental profiles
= NOT universal numerical architecture
= mostly HF23 + HF8 + HF7 + HF3 + HD4 composition
```

# 41. B6 — generalized magnitude / embodied-sensorimotor

B6 contains two related but distinct ideas:

```text
multiple magnitude dimensions share computational/neural resources
numerical content is grounded in action/body/spatial experience
```

These should not be collapsed.

---

# 42. Action↔perception numerosity gives strong positive commonality

Finger-tapping adaptation can bias subsequent perceived visual numerosity.

Related work finds action-to-auditory numerosity adaptation in both sighted and congenitally blind participants.

Therefore:

```text
ActionNumerosity
and
PerceptualNumerosity
interact across modalities.
```

This is one of B's strongest positive commonality results.

---

# 43. Vision is not required for broad numerical competence

Congenitally blind adults and children can develop strong numerical abilities.

The canonical frontoparietal math/number network remains engaged in congenital blindness, although cortical recruitment can reorganize.

Thus:

```text
VisualExperience
!= NecessaryNumericalFoundation
```

and embodied accounts must allow multiple sensory/action routes.

---

# 44. Finger-number embodiment is experience-sensitive

Early blind participants can use different counting strategies and show different finger-number interference than sighted/late-blind participants.

Therefore:

```text
FingerNumberAssociation
!= UniversalNumericalEssence
```

It can be learned/situated embodiment.

---

# 45. 2026 sensorimotor numerosity strengthens B6 locally

Children's precision in estimating self-generated action numerosity showed a strong relation to mental calculation even after several covariates and visual-numerosity precision were modeled in the reported study.

This supports:

```text
SensorimotorNumerosity
= real quantitative evidence regime.
```

But correlation does not establish ownership.

---

# 46. Generalized magnitude is not one undifferentiated mechanism

Numerosity can interfere with duration in some paradigms, sometimes asymmetrically.

Other conflict paradigms find time and numerosity estimates independent.

HD13-A also found numerosity and area judgments have separable developmental signatures despite interference.

Therefore:

```text
SharedMagnitudeInteraction
!= OneGeneralMagnitudeMechanismByDefinition
```

---

# 47. Advanced mathematics further limits simple embodiment

Professional mathematical reasoning can develop in people blind from birth/childhood and recruit the canonical math-responsive frontoparietal network.

This shows sensory scaffolding can vary while abstract mathematical reasoning remains possible.

Thus:

```text
EmbodiedScaffold
!= RepresentationalOwner
```

---

# 48. B6 disposition

```text
B6 generalized-magnitude / embodied-sensorimotor
= STRONG evidence for quantitative grounding and cross-domain interactions
= REJECT as one undifferentiated magnitude owner
= retains unresolved cross-modal numerosity residual
```

# 50. MLD current evidence strongly supports B7 contribution

# 51. Working memory contributes developmentally


# 52. Visual form can mediate apparent numerical training effects

A numerosity-training study in dyscalculia found arithmetic improvement fully mediated statistically by improvement in visual form perception.

This is a direct warning:

```text

may alter HF20 evidence quality
without improving a core number representation.
```

---

# 53. B7 cannot eliminate local numerical mechanisms

Severe dyscalculia can show symbolic and nonsymbolic deficits after matching IQ and visuospatial memory.

Infant ANS performance can predict later math beyond inhibition/general intelligence in one longitudinal sample.

Therefore:

```text
NumericalCognition
!= DomainGeneralControlOnly
```
