# HD3 — Primary Evidence Ledger

## Evidence rule

HD3 uses primary empirical/formal evidence to distinguish social-cognitive mechanisms from
persistent relational state. A source can establish one dissociation without licensing a
universal social ontology.

## S01 — Empathy and Theory of Mind dissociate

Kanske, P. et al. (2015). **Dissecting the social brain: Introducing the EmpaToM to reveal
distinct neural networks and brain–behavior relations for empathy and Theory of Mind.**
NeuroImage. PMID `26254589`.

Use: empathy/affect sharing and mental-state reasoning show distinguishable behavioral and
neural structure in a large task paradigm.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26254589/

## S02 — Strong empathizers are not automatically better mentalizers

Kanske, P. et al. (2016). **Are strong empathizers better mentalizers? Evidence for
independence and interaction between the routes of social cognition.** PMID `27129794`.

Use: empathy and mentalizing capacities are partly independent; strong affect sharing can
interfere with mentalizing in emotionally intense contexts.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27129794/

## S03 — Alexithymia and autism are separable in empathy-for-pain

Bird, G. et al. (2010). **Empathic brain responses in insula are modulated by levels of
alexithymia but not autism.** Brain. PMID `20371509`.

Use: demonstrates that alexithymia and autism status can contribute differently to one
affective-empathy paradigm; rejects global autism=absent-empathy inference.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20371509/

## S04 — Empathy and compassion training dissociate

Klimecki, O. M. et al. (2014). Primary empathy-versus-compassion training study. PMID
`23576808`.

Use: empathy-focused practice and compassion-focused practice produced distinct subjective
and neural consequences, supporting `Compassion != EmpathicDistress`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23576808/

## S05 — Empathy and concern predict prosociality differently

Jordan, M. R. et al. (2016). Primary factor/behavior study. PMID `27668802`.

Use: empathic sharing and concern are psychometrically/behaviorally separable and relate
differently to prosocial behavior.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27668802/

## S06 — Spontaneous and explicit belief use dissociate after TPJ damage

Primary acquired-lesion study of left posterior temporoparietal junction. PMID `27317925`.

Use: spontaneous use of another person's belief can be impaired while explicitly prompted
belief reasoning remains more available. Supports process-sensitive mentalizing.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27317925/

## S07 — Lesion evidence for mental-state attribution network

Primary lesion-mapping study. PMID `33030812`.

Use: causal lesion evidence constrains brain systems involved in mental-state attribution
without identifying one universal Theory-of-Mind module.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33030812/

## S08 — Inverse planning as a computational model of action understanding

Baker, C. L., Saxe, R. & Tenenbaum, J. B. (2009). **Action understanding as inverse
planning.** Cognition. PMID `19729154`.

Use: Bayesian inverse-planning formalization infers goals/beliefs from observed action under
an approximate rational action model.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19729154/

## S09 — Infants infer value from action cost

Liu, S., Ullman, T. D., Tenenbaum, J. B. & Spelke, E. S. (2017). **Ten-month-old infants
infer the value of goals from the costs of actions.** Science. PMID `29170232`.

Use: infants expect agents to prefer goals associated with greater prior action cost,
consistent with structured latent-value inference but not full adult belief reasoning.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29170232/

## S10 — Great apes show false-belief-sensitive anticipation

Krupenye, C. et al. (2016). **Great apes anticipate that other individuals will act
according to false beliefs.** Science. PMID `27846501`.

Use: comparative pressure against treating all false-belief-sensitive prediction as uniquely
human; anticipatory looking remains distinct from explicit language-mediated reasoning.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27846501/

## S11 — Standardized false-belief development across five cultures

Callaghan, T. et al. (2005). **Synchrony in the onset of mental-state reasoning: evidence
from five cultures.** PMID `15869697`.

Use: standardized classic false-belief performance shows a broad common developmental
milestone while not proving identical developmental timing/mechanism everywhere.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15869697/

## S12 — Parent mind-mindedness and cross-cultural ToM development

Hughes, C., Devine, R. T. & Wang, Z. (2018). Parent–child mind-mindedness study across UK
and Hong Kong samples. PMID `28160284`.

Use: parental mental-state discourse/mind-mindedness predicts child ToM and explains some
between-sample differences, pressure against maturation-only accounts.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28160284/

## S13 — Contemporary cross-cultural developmental variation

2026 Turkish preschool Theory-of-Mind pattern study. PMID `42126196`.

Use: current evidence that developmental sequences/timing can differ across cultural and
linguistic contexts.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42126196/

## S14 — Naturalistic joint attention is dynamic and multimodal

Primary parent-child dual head-mounted eye-tracking study. PMID `41641498`.

Use: joint attention emerges through dynamically coupled gaze/manual activity in natural
interaction rather than one fixed gaze template.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41641498/

## S15 — Touch can become a cue of third-party jointness

Thiele and collaborators (2025). Primary developmental joint-attention study. PMID
`40892583`.

Use: older infants can use proximal touch as a cue to third-party social connectedness,
where younger infants rely more strongly on eye contact. Supports developmental cue
plurality.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40892583/

## S16 — Tactile initiation of joint attention in autistic toddlers

2025 prospective toddler study. PMID `40327262`.

Use: autistic toddlers showed fewer visual/auditory joint-attention initiations but more
tactile initiations in the declared task, pressure against `low visual JA = no JA`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40327262/

## S17 — Infant declarative pointing shares attention/interest

Liszkowski, U. et al. (2004). Primary 12-month-old pointing study. PMID `15595371`.

Use: infant pointing can function to share attention/interest rather than merely request an
instrumental outcome.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15595371/

## S18 — Trust updates across repeated interaction

King-Casas, B. et al. (2005). **Getting to know you: reputation and trust in a two-person
economic exchange.** Science. PMID `15802598`.

Use: reciprocity/history changes future trust behavior and partner-specific prediction,
showing trust as an adaptive relation rather than fixed trait.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15802598/

## S19 — Direct social experience shapes trust learning

Primary repeated social-experience/trust study. PMID `23087604`.

Use: prior partner experience changes later learning/trust policy.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23087604/

## S20 — Attachment intervention changes infant stress recovery through caregiving

Berlin, L. J. and collaborators (2019). Randomized attachment-based parenting intervention.
PMID `30716550`.

Use: intervention increased maternal sensitivity, which indirectly improved infant cortisol
recovery after mild stress, providing causal pressure for relation/caregiving→regulation.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30716550/

## S21 — Attachment and infant cortisol trajectories

2026 primary attachment/cortisol study. PMID `42293297`.

Use: attachment security relates to different stress/cortisol trajectories while not
establishing `attachment = cortisol profile`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42293297/

## S22 — Adult partner co-regulation under stress

Primary dyadic coping / psychosocial stress study. PMID `23127351`.

Use: positive partner dyadic coping predicts faster cortisol recovery with moderation by
attachment-related individual differences. Supports `CoRegulation != ChildhoodOnly`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23127351/

## S23 — Attachment relation history and secure-base representations differ

Longitudinal secure-base script study. PMID `27302650`.

Use: caregiving/attachment history relates to later secure-base representations; supports
`AttachmentRelation != AttachmentRepresentation`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27302650/

## S24 — Attachment-like relations to professional caregivers develop over time

2024 daycare caregiver primary study. PMID `38194729`.

Use: attachment-type behavior toward professional caregivers can develop during the first
months of care, pressure against attachment as one immutable person trait or genetic/family
relation only.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38194729/

## S25 — Relationship concepts across modern and historical cultures

Cheng, X. et al. (2025). **The conceptual structure of human relationships across modern
and historical cultures.** Nature Human Behaviour 9, 1162–1175. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-025-02122-8`.

Use: >20,000 participants plus historical language material identify recurrent relation
dimensions/categories alongside meaningful cultural variation. Supports relation as a
first-class representation without one universal taxonomy.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02122-8

## S26 — Genetic relatedness, cultural kinship and residence jointly structure support

Primary Tamil social-support network study. PMID `31303166`.

Use: cooperation/support reflects genetic relatedness, culturally classified kin and
residence; strong falsifier of `kinship = genetics`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31303166/

## S27 — Ancient-genomic evidence of changing social/kin organization

2025 Çatalhöyük ancient-genome study. PMID `40570118`.

Use: early co-burials are more often genetically related whereas later social/burial units
can include unrelated individuals, demonstrating social-unit organization beyond a fixed
biological-family mapping.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40570118/

## S28 — Neolithic social units can contain genetically unrelated individuals

2021 ancient-genome/kinship study. PMID `33857427`.

Use: additional historical pressure for `social household/unit != genetic kin set`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33857427/

## S29 — Loneliness and social isolation are separable

Shen, C. et al. (2025). **Plasma proteomic signatures of social isolation and loneliness
associated with morbidity and mortality.** Nature Human Behaviour 9, 569–583. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-024-02078-1`.

Use: the study operationalizes objective social isolation and subjective loneliness as
separate exposures in 42,062 UK Biobank participants and finds overlapping but non-identical
proteomic associations. Supports the construct firewall without making proteomics the
relationship ontology.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02078-1

## S30 — Social-isolation and loneliness trajectories can diverge longitudinally

2025/2026 South Korea longitudinal primary study.

Use: objective isolation and subjective loneliness show distinct trajectories, supporting
`Loneliness != SocialIsolation`.

## S31 — Perceived empathy gap changes social-risk behavior

2025 Nature Human Behaviour multi-study/field-experiment programme (N≈5,192).

Use: people can underestimate peers' empathy; correcting the belief increases social-risk
taking/network expansion. Supports social-belief reflexivity without proving actual
relationship quality.

## S32 — Human social connection to AI is anthropomorphism-sensitive

Folk, D., Heine, S. J. & Dunn, E. (2025). **Individual differences in anthropomorphism
help explain social connection to AI companions.** Scientific Reports 15, 36548. DOI
`10.1038/s41598-025-19212-2`.

Use: two experiments (total N=1,274; one preregistered) found that interacting with a
chatbot can change reported Human social connection and that individual anthropomorphism
moderates this effect. Supports `HumanConnectionToAI != MutualExperiencedRelationship`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-19212-2

## S33 — Same response generator, different Human/AI partner label, different closeness

Kleinert, T. et al. (2026). **AI outperforms humans in establishing interpersonal
closeness in emotionally engaging interactions, but only when labelled as human.**
Communications Psychology 4, 23. DOI `10.1038/s44271-025-00391-7`.

Use: two double-blind randomized preregistered studies (N=492) using a text-based Fast
Friends procedure found that AI-generated responses labelled as Human could produce high
reported closeness, while an AI label reduced but did not eliminate relationship building.
This supports `PartnerOntologyBelief can change HumanRelationState`; it does not establish
reciprocal AI experience.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00391-7

## S34 — Perceived AI warmth/competence changes trust

2026 Scientific Reports trust-game study, N≈400.

Use: social-perception variables influence Human trust toward AI; `perceived warmth !=
actual benevolent experience` remains mandatory.

## S35 — Anthropomorphism, perceived integrity/ability and AI trust are non-identical

2025 chatbot-trust primary study. PMID `39636606`.

Use: personalization/anthropomorphism, perceived ability/integrity and trust/usage show
non-identical relations.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39636606/

## S36 — Double-empathy pressure: observer–narrator neurotype fit

Cheang and collaborators (2024/2025). Primary empathic-accuracy study. PMID `38757626`.

Use: non-autistic participants show lower empathic accuracy for autistic narrators in some
emotion domains, supporting relational rather than one-sided deficit models.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38757626/

## S37 — Mixed-neurotype interaction pressure

Jones and collaborators (2024). Primary autism interaction study. PMID `38149622`.

Use: mixed-neurotype interactions are evaluated differently, supporting interaction-level
fit as a social variable.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38149622/

## S38 — Double-empathy effects are not universally symmetric

2026 primary empathic-accuracy study. PMID `42271534`.

Use: matching effects are weaker/context-sensitive in some datasets; rejects turning the
double-empathy hypothesis into a universal reverse-deficit slogan.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42271534/

## S39 — Cognitive and affective social cognition can dissociate with autistic traits

2024 primary high-autistic-traits study. PMID `38298152`.

Use: cognitive versus affective social-cognition surfaces show different relations to
autistic traits.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38298152/

## S40 — Self–other processing is component-specific

2026 EEG study. PMID `41781632`.

Use: empathy/self-other effects differ across sensitivity, response bias and ERP indices;
there is no one scalar self-other process.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41781632/

## S41 — Causal stimulation dissociates self–other mechanisms

Primary HD-tDCS perspective-taking study. PMID `30272127`.

Use: right TPJ/dmPFC contributions differ across self–other tasks, supporting
`SelfOtherControl_D != SelfOtherControl_E`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30272127/

## S42 — Self–other distinction and empathy under cTBS

Primary causal stimulation study. PMID `32335328`.

Use: further causal pressure against a single undifferentiated empathy/self-other system.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32335328/

# Evidence boundary

HD3 deliberately mixes social-neuroscience, developmental, comparative, cultural,
relationship and Human–AI perturbations because the admission question is about **scale and
construct boundaries**, not one disciplinary theory. None of these sources proves a
universal relationship taxonomy, a unique ToM architecture, a universal autism deficit, or
machine relational experience.
