# HD4 — Primary Evidence Ledger

## Evidence rule

HD4 uses primary experiments, field studies and formal/computational studies to test whether
social learning/culture forms one new neighboring Human foundation. Evidence of a process is
not evidence that the process deserves a standalone ontology. Comparative, developmental,
network and AI cases are deliberately combined because the target is the **architecture of
inheritance across agents and time**.

## S01 — Social-learning strategies are environment-dependent

Rendell, L. et al. (2010). **Why copy others? Insights from the social learning strategies
tournament.** Science 328, 208–213. PMID `20378813`; DOI `10.1126/science.1184719`.

Use: formal tournament showing that heavy social learning can be adaptive under the modeled
conditions and that information age/demonstrator-expression structure matter. This is a
model result, not a universal Human copying law.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20378813/

## S02 — Multiple learning mechanisms coexist in a wild population

Hoppitt, W. et al. (2012). **Identification of learning mechanisms in a wild meerkat
population.** PLoS ONE 7:e42044. PMID `22905113`; DOI
`10.1371/journal.pone.0042044`.

Use: identifies several social/asocial processes, including local enhancement, emulation and
observational perseverance. Strong pressure against `SocialLearning = Imitation`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22905113/

## S03 — Chimpanzee imitation/emulation changes with causal transparency

Horner, V. & Whiten, A. (2005). **Causal knowledge and imitation/emulation switching in
chimpanzees and children.** Animal Cognition 8, 164–181. PMID `15549502`; DOI
`10.1007/s10071-004-0239-6`.

Use: chimpanzees reproduced irrelevant actions when causal structure was opaque but omitted
them when transparent; children copied more broadly in both conditions. Rejects fixed
species-wide one-mechanism caricatures.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15549502/

## S04 — Human overimitation preserves apparently irrelevant actions

Lyons, D. E., Young, A. G. & Keil, F. C. (2007). **The hidden structure of overimitation.**
PNAS. PMID `18056814`; DOI `10.1073/pnas.0704452104`.

Use: children robustly reproduce causally unnecessary actions in declared contexts;
pressure against `Human social learning = causal-efficiency optimization`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18056814/

## S05 — Chimpanzees can socially acquire a skill they failed to innovate

van Leeuwen, E. J. C. et al. (2024). **Chimpanzees use social information to acquire a
skill they fail to innovate.** Nature Human Behaviour 8, 891–902. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-024-01836-5`.

Use: 66 chimpanzees were exposed to the task; after trained demonstrators were introduced,
14 naive chimpanzees acquired the sequential skill after failing to solve it in the prior
three-month baseline. Strong comparative pressure against defining know-how copying as
Human-unique.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01836-5

## S06 — Prestige affects chimpanzee cultural learning

Horner, V. et al. (2010). **Prestige affects cultural learning in chimpanzees.** PLoS ONE
5:e10625. PMID `20502702`; DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0010625`.

Use: model characteristics/status affect source selection in chimpanzees. Prestige is a
learning cue, not evidence of expertise/truth.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20502702/

## S07 — Children monitor informant accuracy

Koenig, M. A. & Harris, P. L. (2005). **Preschoolers mistrust ignorant and inaccurate
speakers.** Child Development 76, 1261–1277. PMID `16274439`; DOI
`10.1111/j.1467-8624.2005.00849.x`.

Use: children can prefer previously accurate/knowledgeable informants, separating source
reliability from familiarity or authority.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16274439/

## S08 — Accuracy memory can guide later source selection

Corriveau, K. H. & Harris, P. L. (2009). **Preschoolers continue to trust a more accurate
informant 1 week after exposure to accuracy information.** Developmental Science. PMID
`19120427`; DOI `10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00763.x`.

Use: source reliability can become a persistent learning prior rather than a one-shot cue.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19120427/

## S09 — Social power and epistemic accuracy can conflict

Zhao, Q., Zhang, X. & Wang, H. (2026). **The effect of social power and information
accuracy on selective trust in 4- to 6-year-old children.** Acta Psychologica 266:106966.
PMID `42061060`; DOI `10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.106966`.

Use: children weigh power/prestige/dominance against information accuracy in age-sensitive
ways. Supports `SourceSelection != ContentEvaluation` and `Prestige != Truth`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42061060/

## S10 — Social-learning ontogeny varies across societies

van Leeuwen, E. J. C. et al. (2018). **The development of human social learning across
seven societies.** Nature Communications 9, 2076. DOI
`10.1038/s41467-018-04468-2`.

Use: standardized study of 605 children aged 4–14 across seven societies found cultural
variation in social-information reliance and variation/regularity in majority preference.
Rejects one fixed universal copying policy.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04468-2

## S11 — Wild-bird traditions can diffuse and persist through conformity

Aplin, L. M. et al. (2015). **Experimentally induced innovations lead to persistent culture
via conformity in wild birds.** Nature 518, 538–541. DOI `10.1038/nature13998`.

Use: seeded foraging variants diffused through great-tit social networks and remained locally
biased across population turnover. Supports tradition/conformity as population processes
without making persistence evidence of truth or welfare.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13998

## S12 — Functional teaching exists outside Humans

Thornton, A. & McAuliffe, K. (2006). **Teaching in wild meerkats.** Science 313, 227–229.
PMID `16840701`; DOI `10.1126/science.1128727`.

Use: helpers alter prey provisioning in ways that facilitate pup learning under a functional
teaching criterion. Rejects `Teaching = Human-unique explicit pedagogy`.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16840701/

## S13 — Human teaching/transmission routes vary across development and society

Hewlett, B. S. et al. (2011). **Social learning among Congo Basin hunter-gatherers.**
Philosophical Transactions B 366, 1168–1178. PMID `21357239`; DOI
`10.1098/rstb.2010.0373`.

Use: Aka/Bofi learning was mainly vertical early in life and more horizontal/oblique later;
teaching existed but observation/imitation were often more frequent. Separates transmission
route from teaching mechanism.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21357239/

## S14 — Cumulative learning does not always require imitation

Caldwell, C. A. & Millen, A. E. (2009). **Social learning mechanisms and cumulative
cultural evolution: Is imitation necessary?** Psychological Science 20, 1478–1483. PMID
`19891752`; DOI `10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02469.x`.

Use: 700 participants/70 microsocieties; imitation, emulation and teaching conditions each
supported cumulative improvement in the paper-airplane task. Rejects one universally
necessary copying mechanism.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19891752/

## S15 — Teaching/imitation can be useful without being necessary

Zwirner, E. & Thornton, A. (2015). **Cognitive requirements of cumulative culture: teaching
is useful but not essential.** Scientific Reports.

Use: basket transmission chains show cumulative improvement under teaching, imitation and
emulation conditions; teaching improved robustness while exact high-fidelity action copying
was not universally necessary.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/srep16781

## S16 — Human children outperform apes in one cumulative-culture puzzle

Dean, L. G. et al. (2012). **Identification of the social and cognitive processes underlying
human cumulative culture.** Science 335, 1114–1118.

Use: children reached higher puzzle stages than chimpanzees/capuchins in the declared task,
with teaching, imitation and prosocial behavior associated with success. Supports a package
of mechanisms, not one unique module.

Source: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1213969

## S17 — Naturally occurring cumulative cultural evolution in wild bird song

Williams, H. et al. (2022). **Cumulative cultural evolution and mechanisms for cultural
selection in wild bird songs.** Nature Communications 13, 4001. DOI
`10.1038/s41467-022-31621-9`.

Use: Savannah sparrow song variants changed, spread socially and were later elaborated with
increased behavioral efficacy. Strong firewall: `CumulativeCulture != HumanUniqueByDefinition`
under the study's functional criteria.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-31621-9

## S18 — Distributed Human populations can exceed isolated inventive horizons

Derex, M. & Boyd, R. (2015). **The foundations of the human cultural niche.** Nature
Communications 6, 8398. DOI `10.1038/ncomms9398`.

Use: groups produced more complex virtual artifacts than isolated individuals; access to
process information and population structure mattered; partial connectivity preserved
exploration/diversity while permitting information exchange.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9398

## S19 — Larger populations help only under enabling information architecture

Walker, B. & Fay, N. (2026). **Identifying the necessary conditions for large populations
to enhance cumulative culture.** Scientific Reports 16, 10090. DOI
`10.1038/s41598-026-40973-x`.

Use: two randomized large-scale experiments (combined N=941) show larger populations can
improve CCE through access to better/diverse solutions, but reliable benefits depend on
conditions such as attention filtering/external records. Rejects `MorePeople = MoreCulture`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40973-x

## S20 — Transmission chains can generate structured language-like systems

Kirby, S., Cornish, H. & Smith, K. (2008). **Cumulative cultural evolution in the laboratory:
An experimental approach to the origins of structure in human language.** PNAS 105,
10681–10686. DOI `10.1073/pnas.0707835105`.

Use: repeated Human transmission transformed artificial languages toward more learnable and
structured systems without a central designer. Supports `TransmissionProcess can transform
TransmittedSystem`.

Source: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0707835105

## S21 — Tool-making transmission benefits from teaching/language in one task

Morgan, T. J. H. et al. (2015). **Experimental evidence for the co-evolution of hominin
tool-making teaching and language.** Nature Communications 6, 6029. DOI
`10.1038/ncomms7029`.

Use: Oldowan-like tool-making transmission improved with richer teaching/linguistic
conditions in the experiment. Teaching/language benefit remains task-relative rather than a
universal necessity.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7029

## S22 — Cultural transmission can alter what is available to later generations

Derex/Boyd population and iterated-learning experiments jointly supply primary evidence that
transmission architecture changes the search/developmental environment of later learners.

Use: supports the recurrent Human→externalization→later-Human loop while avoiding a literal
genetic-evolution equivalence.

Sources:
- https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9398
- https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0707835105

## S23 — Generative AI is not culturally neutral across language contexts

Lu, J. G., Song, L. L. & Zhang, L. D. (2025). **Cultural tendencies in generative AI.**
Nature Human Behaviour 9, 2360–2369. DOI `10.1038/s41562-025-02242-1`.

Use: GPT and ERNIE outputs showed different social-orientation/cognitive-style tendencies
across Chinese/English use conditions, with downstream recommendation differences. Supports
`AITransmissionSurface != CultureNeutralChannel`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02242-1

## S24 — Generative AI can increase average individual creativity

Lee, B. C. & Chung, J. (2024). **An empirical investigation of the impact of ChatGPT on
creativity.** Nature Human Behaviour. DOI `10.1038/s41562-024-01953-1`.

Use: randomized creative-task experiments found higher average creativity under ChatGPT
assistance in the tested tasks, especially for incremental rather than radical novelty.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01953-1

## S25 — Individual creativity gains can coincide with lower pool diversity

Meincke, L., Nave, G. & Terwiesch, C. (2025). **ChatGPT decreases idea diversity in
brainstorming.** Nature Human Behaviour 9, 1107–1109. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-025-02173-x`.

Use: reanalysis of the Lee/Chung open data supports a population-level tradeoff: average
individual idea quality can rise while pool diversity falls.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x

## S26 — Human and LLM creativity distributions differ at the tail

Wang, D. et al. (2026). **A large-scale comparison of divergent creativity in humans and
large language models.** Nature Human Behaviour 10, 531–540. DOI
`10.1038/s41562-025-02331-1`.

Use: 9,198 Humans and 215,542 LLM observations; Human scores were slightly higher on average
and more variable with a higher right tail under the declared metric. Supports
`AveragePerformance != PopulationSearchValue`.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02331-1

## S27 — Artificial systems can transmit latent behavioural traits through distillation

Cloud, A. et al. (2026). **Language models transmit behavioural traits through hidden
signals in data.** Nature 652, 615–621. DOI `10.1038/s41586-026-10319-8`.

Use: student models can inherit teacher behavioural traits through semantically unrelated
teacher-generated data in declared matched-base-model conditions. This is artificial
inheritance pressure, not evidence that model distillation and Human cultural transmission
are identical.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10319-8

## S28 — Culture can persist across turnover without one stable set of individuals

Aplin et al. (2015) and Williams et al. (2022) provide natural-population examples where
socially learned patterns persist/change across cohort turnover.

Use: `IndividualDeath/Turnover != CulturalTraitDeath` when transmission substrates persist.

Sources:
- https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13998
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-31621-9

## S29 — Product/process information are non-equivalent cultural resources

Derex & Boyd (2015) manipulated access to other participants' artifacts versus the
underlying production process.

Use: social access to outcomes can aid learning, but process transparency can materially
change cumulative performance in opaque/combinatorial tasks.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9398

## S30 — Population size, connectivity and records interact

Derex & Boyd (2015) plus Walker & Fay (2026) demonstrate that population effects depend on
network connectivity and information architecture, including external records/comparison.

Use: rejects monotonic `population size/connectivity → CCE` laws.

Sources:
- https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9398
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40973-x

## S31 — Socially learned persistence need not imply normative value

Aplin-style conformity and HF13 harmful-norm evidence jointly motivate the descriptive
firewall. HD4 does not use persistence, age, majority support or prestige as evidence of
truth/welfare/legitimacy.

Primary cultural-persistence anchor:
https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13998

## S32 — Evidence boundary

The ledger does **not** establish:

```text
one universal social-learning strategy
one Human-specific imitation module
one universal functional definition of teaching
one necessary transmission fidelity threshold
one universal definition of cumulative/open-ended culture
one monotonic demographic law
one cultural atom
one universal culture boundary
one proof that AI constitutes an autonomous culture or social subject
```

Its role is to constrain the cross-foundation cultural-inheritance grammar and the HF23
admission decision.
