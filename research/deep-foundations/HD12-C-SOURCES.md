# HD12-C — Primary Evidence Ledger

## S01 — Grube et al. 2010 — cerebellar degeneration

`Dissociation of duration-based and beat-based auditory timing in cerebellar degeneration.`
PNAS 107(25):11597–11601.
PMID: 20534501. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0910473107.

Primary lesion evidence.

Use:

```text
cerebellar degeneration selectively impairs absolute single-interval timing
while beat-based relative timing can remain preserved.
```

Pressure:

```text
AbsoluteIntervalTiming != BeatBasedRelativeTiming
```

---

## S02 — Grube et al. 2010 — cerebellar cTBS

`Transcranial magnetic theta-burst stimulation of the human cerebellum distinguishes absolute, duration-based from relative, beat-based perception of subsecond time intervals.`
Frontiers in Psychology 1:171.
PMID: 21833234. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00171.

Causal perturbation selectively impaired single-interval timing relative to beat tasks.

Use:

```text
local cerebellar absolute-timing mechanism is real
```

without implying one timing Foundation.

---

## S03 — Breska & Ivry 2018 — cerebellar/Parkinsonian double dissociation

`Double dissociation of single-interval and rhythmic temporal prediction in cerebellar degeneration and Parkinson's disease.`
PNAS 115(48):12283–12288.
PMID: 30425170. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1810596115.

Visual temporal-orienting task:

```text
cerebellar degeneration
→ impaired single-interval prediction, preserved rhythmic prediction

Parkinson's disease
→ preserved single-interval prediction, impaired rhythmic prediction
```

This is a decisive cross-regime selective dissociation.

---

## S04 — Mangels, Ivry & Shimizu 1998 — cerebellar vs prefrontal timing

`Dissociable contributions of the prefrontal and neocerebellar cortex to time perception.`
Cognitive Brain Research 7(1):15–39.
PMID: 9714713. DOI: 10.1016/S0926-6410(98)00005-6.

Use:

```text
prefrontal long-duration deficits co-occurred with WM/attention/strategy deficits;
cerebellar timing deficits were more pervasive and less strategy-sensitive.
```

Supports a real local timing mechanism distinct from generic HF3 support.

---

## S05 — Gooch et al. 2010 — cerebellar suprasecond lesion timing

`Interval timing disruptions in subjects with cerebellar lesions.`
Neuropsychologia 48(4):1022–1031.
PMID: 19962999.

Cerebellar lesions affected estimation/reproduction/production variably while temporal discrimination could remain preserved.

Use:

```text
CerebellarTimingDeficit != OneUniformTaskDeficit
```

---

## S06 — Torta et al. 2010 — action duration vs interval duration in Parkinson's disease

`Dissociation between time reproduction of actions and of intervals in patients with Parkinson's disease.`
Journal of Neurology 257:1356–1361.
PMID: 20352253. DOI: 10.1007/s00415-010-5532-5.

In the off-stimulation/off-medication condition, patients under-reproduced duration of their actions while accurately reproducing ordinary intervals.

Use:

```text
ActionDurationReproduction != IntervalDurationReproduction
```

---

## S07 — Sowiński & Dalla Bella 2013 — auditory-motor mapping

`Poor synchronization to the beat may result from deficient auditory-motor mapping.`
Neuropsychologia 51(10):1952–1963.
PMID: 23838002. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2013.06.027.

Some poor synchronizers showed normal duration-deviation detection and normal rhythm discrimination but poor synchronization to music.

Use:

```text
Beat/RhythmPerception != AuditoryMotorBeatMapping != Synchronization
```

---

## S08 — Bégel et al. 2017 — poor explicit beat tracking with preserved synchronization

`"Lost in time" but still moving to the beat.`
Neuropsychologia 94:129–138.
PMID: 27914979. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2016.11.022.

Single-case evidence showed poor explicit beat tracking can coexist with relatively preserved synchronization.

Use:

```text
ExplicitBeatJudgment != ImplicitBeatSupportForAction
```

---

## S09 — Grahn & Brett / Parkinsonian beat processing line

`Impairment of beat-based rhythm discrimination in Parkinson's disease.`
Cortex 45(1):54–61.
PMID: 19027895. DOI: 10.1016/j.cortex.2008.01.005.

Use:

```text
basal-ganglia dysfunction can selectively pressure beat-based rhythm processing
```

without implying a global timing loss.

---

## S10 — Miller et al. 2016 — dopaminergic medication and beat timing

`The Effect of Dopaminergic Medication on Beat-Based Auditory Timing in Parkinson's Disease.`
Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.
PMID: 26941707.

Medication altered some metric-rhythm discrimination patterns while Beat Alignment Test group differences were not uniform.

Use:

```text
ParkinsonTimingDeficit != OneUniformBeatDeficit
```

---

## S11 — Gundogdu et al. 2026 — selective ADHD timing

`Duration-specific time perception impairments in adolescents with ADHD: links to executive function and symptom dimensions.`
Child Neuropsychology (online ahead of print).
PMID: 42447042. DOI: 10.1080/09297049.2026.2703819.

Use:

```text
reproduction can be selectively impaired
without uniform duration discrimination / long estimation deficit.
```

Supports task/control decomposition.

## S12 — Toplak et al. 2005 — ADHD temporal-information utilization

`Duration judgments in children with ADHD suggest deficient utilization of temporal information rather than general impairment in timing.`
Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology.
PMID: 15590495.

Use as historical selective-task pressure:

```text
long-interval timing difficulty can reflect WM/strategy utilization
rather than one central timing mechanism.
```

---

## S13 — Setti et al. 2015 — aging audiovisual timing

`Impaired timing of audiovisual events in the elderly.`
Experimental Brain Research.
PMID: 26474576. DOI: 10.1007/s00221-015-4466-7.

Older adults showed broader/poorer TOJ and stream/bounce temporal binding while SJ could remain comparatively preserved.

Use:

```text
AgeEffect_TOJ != AgeEffect_SJ
```

---

## S14 — Mioni et al. 2019 — aMCI clock vs memory

`Timing deficiencies in amnestic Mild Cognitive Impairment: Disentangling clock and memory processes.`
Neuropsychologia 127:197–204.
PMID: 31340173.

Patients showed stronger regression toward the mean in multi-duration reproduction but no group difference in clock-time variability; clock variability did not explain the central-tendency difference.

Use:

```text
CentralTendencyIncrease != ClockNoiseIncrease
```

and preserve HF7 memory/prior ownership.

---

## S15 — Guan, Xiong & Yu 2024 — interval-invariant transfer

`Double training reveals an interval-invariant subsecond temporal structure in the brain.`
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 50(12):1225–1235.
PMID: 39480346. DOI: 10.1037/xhp0001254.

Double training enabled complete transfer to an untrained interval.

Use:

```text
interval-invariant temporal representation/component is plausible
```

but not sufficient to establish a timing Foundation.

---

## S16 — Wang et al. 2022 — supramodal conceptual subsecond representation

`A supramodal and conceptual representation of subsecond time revealed with perceptual learning of temporal interval discrimination.`
PMID: 35739220.

Double training enabled complete cross-modal transfer between auditory and visual temporal interval discrimination under the studied conditions.

Use:

```text
AbstractTemporalRelationRepresentation
can transfer across modality
```

and pressure strict modality isolation.

---

## S17 — Bratzke, Seifried & Ulrich 2012 — asymmetric transfer

`Perceptual learning in temporal discrimination: asymmetric cross-modal transfer from audition to vision.`
Experimental Brain Research 221(2):205–210.
PMID: 22772383. DOI: 10.1007/s00221-012-3162-0.

Use:

```text
cross-modal temporal transfer exists in some designs
but is not automatically symmetric.
```

# C synthesis

Cross-regime evidence supports:

```text
multiple real timing mechanisms
+
transferable temporal relation representation
+
shared/nonshared calibration and precision factors
```

but not:

```text
one peer Human temporal owner.
```

Best allocation:

```text
abstract temporal representation → HF8
perceptual evidence/calibration → HF20
control/metacognition → HF3
retrospective memory/prior → HF7
action timing/mapping → HF11
passage experience → HF2/HF21
```

with narrow timing projections retained as deep scientific research objects.
