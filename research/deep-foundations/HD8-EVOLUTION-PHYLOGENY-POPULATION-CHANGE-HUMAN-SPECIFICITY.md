# HD8 — Evolution, Phylogeny, Population Change, Natural Selection and Human Specificity

## 0. Why HD8 exists

Human Foundations had already used:

```text
evolutionary adaptation
comparative animal evidence
homology / convergence
fitness
evolutionary function
human-specificity
cultural evolution
archaic ancestry
```

but no canonical layer owned the inferential grammar connecting them.

HD8 asks:

> **How do populations and lineages change across generations, what licenses an adaptation or
> Human-specificity claim, and how can evolutionary explanation constrain Human modeling
> without becoming progress, purpose, optimality or normative authority?**

---

# 1. Evolution is not progress

```text
Evolution != Progress
```

Evolutionary change has no built-in direction toward greater complexity, intelligence,
fitness in every environment, welfare or moral improvement.

---

# 2. Evolution is population/lineage change, not individual learning

At minimum:

```text
EvolutionaryChange_D
= change in inherited population/lineage state across generations under D
```

Therefore:

```text
EvolutionaryChange != WithinLifetimeLearning
EvolutionaryAdaptation != PhysiologicalAdaptation
```

HF6 remains the within-lifetime/developmental-change owner.

---

# 3. Population is not one essence

Use a declared reproductive/demographic/genetic sampling criterion:

```text
Population_D = set/distribution of organisms or lineages under criterion D
```

```text
Population != NaturalKindByDefinition
```

Population boundaries can be porous and model-relative.

---

# 4. Lineage is not population

```text
Lineage != Population
```

A lineage tracks descent through time; a population is a contemporaneous or time-bounded
sampling/reproductive unit under a model.

---

# 5. Species is not population

```text
Species != Population
```

Multiple structured populations can belong to one species, and species concepts themselves
are question/theory dependent in difficult boundary cases.

---

# 6. Species concept is not one universal test

Biological, phylogenetic, ecological and other species concepts emphasize different
criteria.

```text
SpeciesCriterion_D != SpeciesCriterion_E
```

HD8 does not solve all species philosophy; it prevents one criterion from silently becoming
universal.

---

# 7. Generation is not one fixed clock interval

```text
Generation != FixedYearsByDefinition
```

Generation time varies among populations, sex classes and historical conditions and matters
for molecular/demographic inference.

---

# 8. Population state is multidimensional

A minimum evolutionary state can include:

```text
PopulationEvolutionState_t = {
  allele/haplotype/genome-frequency distributions,
  population size and effective size,
  age/sex/reproductive structure,
  geographic structure,
  migration/gene flow,
  mating structure,
  environment/ecology,
  inherited cultural/environmental states when relevant,
  uncertainty
}
```

---

# 9. Census size is not effective population size

```text
CensusPopulationSize != EffectivePopulationSize
```

Unequal reproductive success, sex ratio, fluctuations and structure alter the amount of
genetic drift relative to census count.

---

# 10. Effective population size is model-relative

```text
Ne_D != CensusCount
```

Different estimators can target different historical/evolutionary aspects of effective size.

---

# 11. Allele-frequency change is not selection by definition

```text
AlleleFrequencyChange != NaturalSelectionByDefinition
```

Mutation, drift, migration/admixture, selection, segregation/recombination structure and
sampling can all alter observed frequencies.

---

# 12. Selection is not evolution totality

```text
NaturalSelection != EvolutionTotality
```

Selection is one mechanism/process family among several.

---

# 13. Drift is not measurement noise

```text
GeneticDrift != MeasurementNoise
```

Drift is a real stochastic population process caused by finite reproduction/sampling of
lineages across generations.

---

# 14. Drift is not unimportant residue

```text
Drift != NegligibleByDefinition
```

Founder events, bottlenecks and small effective size can make stochastic change dominant.

---

# 15. Mutation is not adaptive innovation by definition

```text
Mutation != Adaptation
```

Mutation generates new variation without regard to future usefulness under ordinary
Darwinian models.

---

# 16. Recombination is not mutation

```text
Recombination != Mutation
```

Recombination reshuffles inherited variation; mutation changes sequence state.

---

# 17. Migration is not selection

```text
Migration/GeneFlow != NaturalSelection
```

Alleles can rise locally because carriers migrate, without local selective advantage.

---

# 18. Admixture is not adaptation

```text
Admixture != Adaptation
```

Admixture introduces ancestry/variation. Later selection may act on some introgressed
variants.

---

# 19. Population replacement is not selection at the locus

Ancient DNA forces:

```text
AlleleFrequencyShiftFromPopulationTurnover
!= LocusSpecificSelection
```

Demographic ancestry change can move allele frequencies even if the allele has no fitness
effect.

---

# 20. Selection signal is not selection proof

```text
SelectionStatistic != HistoricalSelectionProof
```

Demography, admixture, linked selection, ascertainment and model misspecification can produce
or erase candidate signatures.

---

# 21. Ancient DNA changes the evidence position

Present-day genomes infer history backward.
Ancient DNA can directly sample temporal frequency/ancestry states.

```text
PresentDaySignature != DirectHistoricalObservation
```

Neither is error-free; their inferential structures differ.

---

# 22. Selection can be directional without fixation

```text
PositiveSelection != InevitableFixation
```

Changing environments, migration, tradeoffs, competing loci and finite time can prevent
fixation.

---

# 23. Purifying selection is not absence of evolution

```text
PurifyingSelection != EvolutionaryStasis
```

It changes the fate/frequency of deleterious variation while conserving some functional
features.

---

# 24. Balancing selection is not directional selection

```text
BalancingSelection != DirectionalSelection
```

Multiple alleles can remain under heterozygote advantage, frequency dependence, fluctuating
selection or other mechanisms.

---

# 25. Sexual selection is not natural-selection opposite

```text
SexualSelection != NonNaturalSelection
```

It is selection mediated by mating/reproductive success; survival and mating components can
trade off.

---

# 26. Kin/inclusive-fitness models are not altruism identity

```text
KinSelectionExplanation != MoralAltruism
```

Evolutionary consequences of helping relatives do not define the actor's conscious motive or
moral status.

---

# 27. Group/multilevel selection is model-level dependent

```text
SelectionAtLevel_D != SelectionAtLevel_E
```

Covariance in reproductive success can be decomposed at gene/individual/group levels under
different formalisms; HD8 does not force one universal unit of selection.

---

# 28. Gene is not the unit of selection totality

```text
Gene != UnitOfSelectionTotality
```

Selection can be represented at alleles, organisms, kin-structured and multilevel units
depending on the causal/model question.

---

# 29. Fitness is not welfare

```text
EvolutionaryFitness != Welfare
```

Fitness concerns differential contribution to future generations under a declared model;
welfare concerns well-being/value and belongs to HF14/HF15.

---

# 30. Fitness is not moral worth

```text
Fitness != MoralWorth
Fitness != HumanValue
```

No descriptive reproductive statistic authorizes a hierarchy of persons.

---

# 31. Fitness is not current goal

```text
EvolutionaryFitness != CurrentHumanGoal
```

A person need not desire reproduction or descendants for a trait/process to have an
evolutionary history.

---

# 32. Absolute and relative fitness differ

```text
AbsoluteFitness != RelativeFitness
```

A lineage can increase in absolute number while losing relative representation, or vice versa.

---

# 33. Fitness is environment-relative

```text
Fitness_D(EnvironmentA) != Fitness_D(EnvironmentB)ByDefinition
```

A genotype advantageous under one ecology can be neutral or costly under another.

---

# 34. Fitness is frequency-dependent in some systems

```text
Fitness(genotype) != ContextFreeConstant
```

The payoff can depend on frequencies of competitors, mates, pathogens or social strategies.

---

# 35. One fitness component is not total fitness

```text
Survival != Fertility != MatingSuccess != OffspringSurvival
```

Evolutionary fitness aggregates components only under explicit life-history assumptions.

---

# 36. More offspring now is not necessarily greater long-run lineage fitness

```text
CurrentOffspringCount != Lifetime/ReproductiveFitnessTotality
```

Survival, quality, future reproduction and descendant reproduction can matter.

---

# 37. Adaptation requires historical selection evidence

HD8 uses a strong definition:

```text
Adaptation_D
= trait/state whose historical form/frequency was shaped by selection because it affected
  fitness under D
```

Therefore:

```text
CurrentUsefulness != AdaptationByDefinition
```

---

# 38. Adaptation is not optimality

```text
Adaptation != OptimalDesign
```

Historical constraint, tradeoffs, pleiotropy, limited variation and changing environments can
produce suboptimal outcomes.

---

# 39. Adaptation is not perfection

```text
Adaptive != Perfect
```

Selection only compares available heritable alternatives in historical contexts.

---

# 40. Adaptation is not global benefit

```text
AdaptiveIn_D != BeneficialIn_E
```

HbS/malaria and many immunity examples make this explicit.

---

# 41. Selected allele can carry severe costs

A variant can be maintained because benefit under one genotype/environment outweighs costs
under another.

```text
Selected != Harmless
```

---

# 42. Evolutionary tradeoff is not design failure

```text
Tradeoff != MalfunctionByDefinition
```

Selection can favour compromises across competing fitness components.

---

# 43. Maladaptation can arise from environmental change

```text
PreviouslyAdaptive != CurrentlyAdaptive
```

Historical selection does not guarantee fit to modern environments.

---

# 44. Current function is not historical adaptation cause

```text
CurrentFunction != HistoricalSelectiveFunctionByDefinition
```

A structure can acquire new uses after originating.

---

# 45. Exaptation / co-option

Use:

```text
Exaptation_D
= current useful role that was not the historical selective reason for the trait's origin
```

Therefore:

```text
CurrentBenefit != OriginalAdaptation
```

---

# 46. Byproduct is not adaptation

```text
CorrelatedByproduct != AdaptationByDefinition
```

Pleiotropy/developmental architecture can produce traits not directly selected.

---

# 47. Constraint is not selection

```text
Developmental/PhysicalConstraint != Selection
```

Some forms persist because alternatives are inaccessible, not because every detail is
selected.

---

# 48. Historical contingency matters

```text
SameCurrentEnvironment
!= SameEvolutionaryOutcomeByDefinition
```

Initial variation, demographic history and prior adaptations alter reachable paths.

---

# 49. Convergence is not shared recent ancestry

```text
ConvergentEvolution != Homology
```

Similar traits can evolve independently under similar pressures.

---

# 50. Lactase persistence is a Human convergence case

African and European populations evolved adult lactase persistence through distinct
regulatory variants/haplotype histories.

```text
SamePhenotypeClass
!= SameMutation/History
```

---

# 51. Parallelism/convergence distinction is scale-relative

```text
Convergence_D != Parallelism_EByDefinition
```

How similar the developmental/genetic pathways must be is a declared comparative criterion.

---

# 52. Homology is historical correspondence, not superficial similarity

```text
Homology != PhenotypicSimilarity
```

Homology claims depend on common ancestry of the compared character/state under a phylogeny.

---

# 53. Homologous structure is not identical phenotype

```text
HomologousTrait != IdenticalTraitState
```

Homologous systems can diverge strongly after common ancestry.

---

# 54. Homologous function is not identical experience

HF21/HF15 remain authoritative:

```text
HomologousDefensiveFunction != IdenticalPhenomenology
```

Evolutionary continuity supports some comparative inference, not direct subjective identity.

---

# 55. Analogy/function similarity is not homology

```text
FunctionalSimilarity != Homology
```

Convergent systems can perform similar functions with independent origins.

---

# 56. Phylogeny is not a ladder

```text
PhylogeneticTree != ProgressLadder
```

Extant chimpanzees are not ancestors of Humans; both descend from common ancestral
populations.

---

# 57. `Primitive` is not lower value

```text
AncestralTrait != InferiorTrait
DerivedTrait != SuperiorTrait
```

Ancestral/derived describe historical character states, not quality.

---

# 58. Derived is not Human-specific

```text
DerivedInClade != HumanSpecific
```

A derived state can be shared across apes, primates or mammals.

---

# 59. Human-specific requires a comparator set

Use:

```text
HumanSpecific_D(feature)
```

where D declares:

```text
comparison species/taxa
measurement level
developmental stage
population scope
threshold
available evidence
```

---

# 60. Human-specific is not Human-universal

```text
HumanSpecific != HumanUniversal
```

A derived feature can occur only in some Human populations/individuals.

---

# 61. Human-universal is not Human-specific

```text
HumanUniversal != HumanSpecific
```

A near-universal Human feature can have homologous/precursor forms in other species.

---

# 62. Human-specific is not no precursor

```text
HumanSpecificDerivedState
!= NoAncestral/AnimalPrecursor
```

Quantitative elaboration of an ancestral system can produce a derived Human state.

---

# 63. Difference of degree is not difference of kind by default

```text
LargeQuantitativeDifference != CategoricalNoveltyByDefinition
```

Threshold/nonlinearity may create qualitative consequences, but must be shown.

---

# 64. Absence in one comparator is not Human uniqueness

```text
AbsentInChimpanzeeSample
!= HumanSpecificByDefinition
```

Other apes, monkeys, mammals or convergent taxa can retain related capacities.

---

# 65. Failure in one task is not species incapacity

```text
TaskFailure_D != SpeciesCapabilityAbsence
```

Motivation, ecology, apparatus and development matter.

---

# 66. Positive nonhuman evidence can falsify strong uniqueness claims

If a uniqueness criterion is:

```text
Only Humans can X
```

then one well-supported nonhuman case of X defeats that criterion even if Humans remain far
more capable in scale or flexibility.

---

# 67. Chimpanzee social-learning evidence

Chimpanzees acquired a sequential skill socially after months of failure to innovate it
independently.

Therefore:

```text
SocialLearningBeyondIndependentInnovation
!= HumanUnique
```

---

# 68. Bumblebee evidence is an even stronger phylogenetic warning

Bumblebees learned a two-step behaviour socially that naive bees did not independently solve.

Thus a capacity can arise in very distant lineages:

```text
SimilarFunctionalCapacity
!= SharedRecentCognitiveMechanism
```

---

# 69. Orangutan cultural repertoire evidence

Data-calibrated models of wild Sumatran orangutans indicate adult-like diet breadth develops
reliably only with social learning.

Therefore:

```text
CulturallyDependentRepertoire
!= HumanUniqueByDefinition
```

---

# 70. Human culture can still be exceptional in scale/open-endedness

Rejecting one uniqueness criterion does not imply:

```text
HumanCulture = NonhumanCulture
```

Differences can remain in breadth, open-ended generativity, symbolic externalization,
institutional accumulation and cross-domain recombination.

---

# 71. Uniqueness is criterion-by-criterion

```text
HumanUnique_D1
can be false while
HumanUnique_D2
remains open
```

There is no single `Human uniqueness` bit.

---

# 72. Molecular Human-specificity is also layered

Comparative primate single-cell studies show:

```text
many homologous/conserved cell types/programs
+
smaller lineage-divergent regulatory programs
```

Therefore:

```text
HumanSpecificRegulation
!= HumanSpecificCellTypeTotality
```

---

# 73. Conserved architecture can produce derived regulation

```text
ConservedCellType
+ DerivedRegulatoryState
```

is a valid evolutionary outcome.

No need for entirely new biological modules.

---

# 74. Human-specific gene is not Human-specific trait

```text
HumanSpecificGene/duplication
!= HumanSpecificPhenotypeByDefinition
```

Functional/developmental evidence is required.

---

# 75. Human-specific regulatory element is not complete Human-specific mechanism

```text
HumanSpecificCRE
!= CompleteTraitExplanation
```

A derived regulatory element participates in networks and development.

---

# 76. Comparative organoid evidence is model evidence

```text
OrganoidSpeciesDifference
!= InVivoSpeciesDifferenceByDefinition
```

Organoids improve causal accessibility but remain models requiring external validation.

---

# 77. Phylogenetic comparative inference needs non-independence correction

Species are not IID data points.

```text
CrossSpeciesCorrelation
!= IndependentReplicates
```

Shared ancestry must be modeled.

---

# 78. Correlated trait evolution is not direct causation

```text
PhylogeneticCorrelation(A,B)
!= ACausesB
```

Temporal ordering, confounders and mechanistic evidence remain needed.

---

# 79. Ancestral-state reconstruction is inference

```text
ReconstructedAncestorState
!= DirectHistoricalObservation
```

Confidence depends on phylogeny, transition model and sampled taxa.

---

# 80. Extant species are not frozen ancestors

```text
ChimpanzeeToday != HumanChimpLCA
```

Both lineages evolved since divergence.

---

# 81. Fossils do not map one-to-one to modern species categories

```text
MorphologicalSimilarity != DirectAncestorByDefinition
```

Phylogenetic placement and population continuity require multiple evidence streams.

---

# 82. Gene tree is not species tree

```text
GeneTree != SpeciesTreeByDefinition
```

Incomplete lineage sorting, introgression and recombination can yield different local
genealogies.

---

# 83. Incomplete lineage sorting is not introgression

```text
ILS != Introgression
```

Shared ancient variation can persist across lineage splits without later gene flow.

---

# 84. Introgression is not incomplete lineage sorting

Archaic-human analyses explicitly need statistics/models distinguishing the two.

```text
SharedArchaicLikeHaplotype
!= IntrogressionProofByDefinition
```

---

# 85. Human evolution is reticulate, not purely branching

Neanderthal/Denisovan-modern-human admixture requires:

```text
PhylogeneticTree
+ GeneFlowEdges
```

for many genomic questions.

---

# 86. Archaic ancestry is not one event totality

```text
ArchaicIntrogression != OneUniversalPulseByDefinition
```

Timing and direction can vary among lineages/populations.

---

# 87. Introgressed is not adaptive

```text
IntrogressedVariant != AdaptiveVariantByDefinition
```

Much archaic functional variation has been removed or remains neutral; some loci show
adaptive retention.

---

# 88. Adaptive introgression is two claims

A strong claim requires:

```text
Evidence of introgressed origin
+
Evidence of positive selection / fitness benefit after introgression
```

Neither alone is sufficient.

---

# 89. EPAS1 is the canonical strong example

Tibetan high-altitude EPAS1 haplotype structure is best explained by Denisovan-like
introgression plus later selection.

```text
SourceOfVariation != SelectiveProcess
```

Introgression supplied variation; selection changed its frequency.

---

# 90. Adaptation can use standing variation

```text
Adaptation != NewMutationRequired
```

Selection can act on variants already present before an environmental shift.

---

# 91. Adaptation can use introgressed variation

```text
AdaptationSource
∈ {new mutation, standing variation, introgressed variation, ...}
```

The source of variation and the selective mechanism are separate axes.

---

# 92. Convergent adaptation can use different variants

Lactase persistence demonstrates:

```text
SimilarSelectivePressure
→ DifferentGeneticRoutes
→ SimilarPhenotypeClass
```

This is incompatible with one-gene-one-adaptation thinking.

---

# 93. Similar genetic change can support different phenotypic outcomes

```text
SameVariant != SameEffectAcrossPopulation/Environment
```

HD7 G×E/context dependence remains in force.

---

# 94. Selection is historical-context dependent

Ancient lactase-persistence trajectories show dairying alone does not fully identify why
selection intensified.

```text
CulturalPracticePresent
!= SelectiveMechanismFullySpecified
```

Disease, famine, settlement density and demography can mediate fitness consequences.

---

# 95. Culture can become an evolutionary environment

```text
CulturalState_t
→ changed diet/pathogen/social/reproductive environment
→ changed genetic fitness landscape
```

HD4 supplies cultural transmission; HD8 supplies population-genetic consequence.

---

# 96. Gene–culture coevolution is not genes reacting to culture only

```text
Genes → CultureOnly
```

is insufficient.

Use:

```text
GeneticVariation ↔ Development/Behavior ↔ CulturalEnvironment
```

across generations.

---

# 97. Culture is not just environment in every model

Culture itself has inheritance/transmission dynamics.

```text
CulturalInheritance != PassiveEnvironmentalExposure
```

HD4 remains the cultural-process owner.

---

# 98. Gene–culture coevolution is not simultaneous change by definition

```text
GenesChanged + CultureChanged
!= GeneCultureCoevolutionProof
```

Evidence must support reciprocal causal coupling across generations.

---

# 99. Lactase persistence is evidence for coupling, not a universal template

Different cultural/genetic histories can produce other forms of coevolution.

```text
OneGeneCultureCase != GeneCultureTheoryTotality
```

---

# 100. Niche construction

Use a minimal definition:

```text
Organisms/collectives modify environmental states
→ those states alter future selection/development/ecology
```

---

# 101. Niche construction is not intentional engineering by definition

```text
NicheConstruction != ConsciousDesign
```

Environmental modification can be unintended or nonhuman.

---

# 102. Niche construction is not all organism–environment interaction

```text
AnyEnvironmentalEffect != NicheConstructionByDefinition
```

The modified environment must persist/be causally relevant to later organismal/ecological
conditions under the declared model.

---

# 103. Human built environments can be niche construction

Agriculture, settlement, cooking, sanitation and infrastructure can alter:

```text
pathogens
diet
energy access
mobility
mating/population structure
pollution
thermal environment
```

but each requires evidence for downstream evolutionary consequences.

---

# 104. Technology can change selection without biological intention

```text
TechnologyAdoption
→ changed survival/reproduction/exposure
→ changed selection pressures
```

No designer need intend genetic consequences.

---

# 105. Medicine can relax or redirect selection pressures

```text
TreatmentEnvironment != AncestralEnvironment
```

but `relaxed selection` does not mean disease disappears or Human evolution stops.

---

# 106. Human evolution has not stopped

```text
Culture/Medicine != EndOfEvolution
```

Mutation, reproduction, drift, migration and selection continue; the fitness landscape
changes.

---

# 107. Faster cultural change does not eliminate genetic change

```text
CulturalEvolutionFast
!= GeneticEvolutionZero
```

Timescales interact rather than replace one another.

---

# 108. Genetic evolution does not explain current cultural detail by itself

```text
GeneticEvolution
!= SpecificCultureContent
```

HD4/HF23 remain necessary.

---

# 109. Evolutionary explanation and proximate mechanism differ

```text
WhyHistoricallySelected
!= HowMechanismWorksNow
```

A trait can have a well-supported evolutionary history while its present neural/physiological
mechanism remains unknown, and vice versa.

---

# 110. Development is not evolutionary explanation

```text
Ontogeny != Phylogeny
```

Developmental generation of a trait and lineage history are separate causal questions.

---

# 111. Evolution is not explanation solvent

```text
EvolutionaryStory != ExplanationByDefault
```

A plausible adaptive narrative without comparative/genetic/historical evidence is not a
strong evolutionary explanation.

---

# 112. Adaptationism must be falsifiable

Before calling a Human trait an adaptation ask whether evidence distinguishes:

```text
selection
from
constraint / drift / byproduct / exaptation / gene flow / cultural transmission
```

---

# 113. Current utility evidence alone is weak for adaptation

```text
TraitHelpsNow
!= SelectedBecauseItHelpedHistorically
```

Historical link is required.

---

# 114. Cross-cultural recurrence is not adaptation proof

```text
CrossCulturalCommonness != GeneticAdaptationByDefinition
```

Shared development, convergent culture or institutions can produce recurrence.

---

# 115. Cross-species recurrence is not homology proof

```text
SameBehaviorAcrossSpecies != HomologyByDefinition
```

Convergence, common ancestry and independent learning must be distinguished.

---

# 116. Comparative evidence is level-specific

For any claim specify whether evidence supports similarity of:

```text
molecular pathway
cell type
anatomy
computation/function
behavior
learning mechanism
phenomenology
social institution
```

Similarity at one level does not force identity at another.

---

# 117. Behavior similarity is not cognition identity

```text
SameBehavior != SameCognitiveMechanism
```

Bumblebee/chimpanzee parallels are therefore uniqueness falsifiers for the behavior/capacity,
not proof of identical mental architecture.

---

# 118. Molecular similarity is not behavioral identity

```text
ConservedGenePathway != SameBehavior
```

Developmental/system context matters.

---

# 119. Human lineage-specific molecular changes can exploit older systems

A human-specific gene/regulatory change can act through ape/primate-conserved developmental
machinery.

```text
DerivedComponent + ConservedSystem
→ DerivedPhenotype
```

---

# 120. Novelty is often recombination/redeployment, not creation from nothing

```text
EvolutionaryNovelty != NoPrecursor
```

New combinations, duplicated genes, regulatory changes and changed developmental timing can
modify ancestral structures.

---

# 121. Human-specificity is often mosaic

For one Human system:

```text
ancestral components
+ ape-derived components
+ hominin-derived components
+ modern-human-specific changes
+ cultural-developmental layers
```

can coexist.

---

# 122. No single date marks `the Human` totality

```text
HumanTraitSet != OneOriginEvent
```

Different features arose at different lineage/population times and some continue evolving.

---

# 123. Anatomically modern is not behaviorally modern totality

```text
AnatomicalModernity != Cultural/CognitiveModernityByDefinition
```

Archaeological/cultural traits have heterogeneous temporal/geographic trajectories.

---

# 124. Genetic modernity is not cultural modernity

```text
GenomeSimilarityToPresentHumans
!= PresentDayCulture/Capability
```

Culture and institutions dramatically change realized capability.

---

# 125. Archaic hominin is not nonhuman moral category

```text
ArchaicHomininClassification != MoralStanding
```

HF15 remains moral-standing owner; extinct lineage taxonomy does not settle experience or
status.

---

# 126. Interbreeding does not erase lineage distinction automatically

```text
GeneFlow != NoPopulationStructure
```

Distinct populations/lineages can exchange genes while remaining partially differentiated.

---

# 127. Hybridization is not evolutionary failure

```text
Hybridization != BreakdownByDefinition
```

Gene flow can introduce deleterious, neutral or beneficial variation.

---

# 128. Introgressed ancestry can be selected against

```text
Introgressed != Beneficial
```
