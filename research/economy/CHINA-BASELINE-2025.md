# China 2025 Descriptive Baseline

## Purpose

This baseline establishes the national context for HUMAN-ECON-001 using the National Bureau of Statistics' 2025 household income and consumption release.

It is not a model of an “average person” and does not estimate household saving behavior.

## What the baseline establishes

### 1. Labour income remains central

Wages and salaries account for more than half of average disposable income. For many working-age people, human-capital and employment decisions are therefore likely to dominate the early economic trajectory.

This does not establish that education or job switching causes the highest return. It establishes that a research program focused only on portfolio return would omit the largest average income source.

### 2. Property income is not the average household's dominant income source

Property income accounts for 8.0% of average disposable income. This reinforces the need to separate:

```text
wealth accumulation during labour-dominant stages
from
income generated after substantial productive assets exist
```

### 3. The mean is not a sufficient baseline

Median disposable income is 83.5% of the mean. Any threshold based on average income risks describing a person above the centre of the distribution.

### 4. National averages cannot reveal saving or fragility

Subtracting average consumption from average income yields 13,901 yuan, but this does not mean the typical household saved 32.05% of income because:

- the income and consumption distributions are not jointly observed in the release;
- households can finance consumption from debt, transfers, or asset sales;
- asset purchases and repayments are not represented as ordinary consumption;
- household size and equivalence needs differ;
- measurement error and category definitions differ;
- averages can be driven by different households.

### 5. Necessary and adjustable expenditure cannot be inferred from category names

Food and residence together represent about half of average consumption, but:

- some food spending is flexible and high-value;
- some housing costs are fixed while others can change after relocation;
- education, transport, and health can be necessary, capability-building, or low-value depending on the case;
- expenditure amount does not reveal quality produced.

The empirical study must classify adjustment cost, necessity, and outcome contribution separately.

## What the baseline does not establish

It does not show:

- net worth or liquid wealth distribution;
- household debt service;
- housing concentration;
- wealth controlled by each household member;
- income volatility;
- transitions across years;
- response to unemployment or health shocks;
- consumption quality or subjective well-being;
- active saving versus capital gains;
- family transfers received under conditions of control.

## Data escalation

### CHFS

Use for:

- financial and non-financial assets;
- housing and business wealth;
- liabilities and credit constraints;
- income and expenditure;
- insurance and social protection;
- family transfers and employment.

### CFPS

Use for:

- longitudinal income and expenditure;
- education and capability proxies;
- health and functional state;
- family dynamics and relationships;
- migration;
- subjective and non-economic well-being.

### NBS

Continue to use for:

- national and urban/rural calibration;
- current nominal and real changes;
- official definitions and sampling context.

## Current conclusion

China's 2025 aggregate baseline supports a labour-and-consumption-centred first stage, but it cannot answer the research question. The next valid unit is the joint household trajectory:

```text
income + consumption + assets + liabilities + liquidity
+ commitments + health + capability + family conditions
across time
```
