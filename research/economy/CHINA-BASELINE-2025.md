# China 2025 Public-Average Calibration

## Purpose

This file provides a small set of official national averages for magnitude checks in HUMAN-ECON-001.

It does not model an average person, prove a causal mechanism, or select an individual strategy.

## What the averages calibrate

### 1. Labour and business remain the dominant average income channels

Wages and business income together account for 73.3% of average disposable income, while property income accounts for 8.0%.

This does not prove that any person should prioritize employment or entrepreneurship.

It makes one investment-first hypothesis harder to defend:

> For a person with little capital, optimizing financial return alone begins from a much smaller average income channel than labour and business value creation.

### 2. Absolute magnitude matters more than percentage display

A 12,000-yuan recurring annual improvement is about the same scale as the official mean income–consumption difference of 13,901 yuan.

To generate 12,000 yuan from capital alone requires:

- 240,000 yuan at 5%;
- 150,000 yuan at 8%;
- 120,000 yuan at 10%.

These return rates are illustrative assumptions, not forecasts.

The comparison explains why the dominant engine changes with the capital base.

### 3. The mean cannot define a personal strategy

The median is materially below the mean, and urban/rural averages differ substantially.

Therefore the averages may calibrate order of magnitude but may not supply:

- a personal saving target;
- a required income;
- an emergency-fund duration;
- an asset allocation;
- a definition of sufficient life quality.

### 4. Consumption cannot be classified by category name alone

Food, residence, transport, education, and health may represent:

- necessary maintenance;
- current life quality;
- future capability investment;
- status consumption;
- avoidable lock-in.

The same category can serve several mechanisms. E4 must classify function and future effect rather than infer value from spending amount.

## What the averages do not establish

They do not identify:

- who created or controlled the income;
- individual capability or market access;
- value created versus value captured;
- wealth, debt, or ownership distribution;
- business and career transition paths;
- causal effects of education, migration, tools, or investment;
- which path is best for one person.

## Use rule

Public averages enter this program only when they answer one of five questions:

1. Is the proposed mechanism large enough to matter within a finite life?
2. Which base does a percentage multiply?
3. What order of magnitude must a path overcome?
4. Does a first-principles claim conflict with an obvious public baseline?
5. Is attention being spent on a small channel while a much larger channel remains changeable?

They are calibration, not authority.
