# HUMAN-ECON-001 — Sustainable Personal Economic Autonomy

**Status: active. E0 and E1 first evidence round completed 2026-08-02.**

## Research question

> From the point at which a person can independently decide income, consumption, debt, saving, work, and investment, how can they accumulate more durable wealth without materially degrading present quality of life, while expanding security, refusal power, exit capacity, and future choices?

This is a household-finance and human-development question, not an investment-return contest.

## Current answer in one sentence

A durable path is more likely to come from preserving a quality-of-life floor, building liquid resilience, limiting inflexible commitments and correlated failure modes, expanding transferable earning capacity, maintaining a persistent whole-balance-sheet surplus, and only then converting long-horizon surplus into diversified productive assets.

This is a provisional mechanism map, not a universal prescription.

## First-round findings

1. **Income, consumption, and wealth are separate but interrelated.** None can represent economic autonomy alone.
2. **Liquidity has independent causal value.** A household can be wealthy on paper and unable to absorb a near-term shock.
3. **Gross saving is not net wealth creation.** Automatic saving can coexist with more unsecured or mortgage debt.
4. **Fixed commitments change risk capacity.** Infrequently adjustable obligations amplify moderate income shocks and reduce exit capacity.
5. **Human capital is part of the risk system.** Age is not a sufficient portfolio rule; income volatility, labour flexibility, social insurance, and correlation with asset returns matter.
6. **Quality of life must be measured directly.** Lower expenditure is not automatically better, and higher expenditure is not automatically better.
7. **Autonomy is a distinct outcome.** It concerns the feasible actions available after obligations, constraints, and shocks—not merely net worth.
8. **Chinese aggregate data show why microdata are necessary.** Average income exceeds average consumption, but the median is below the mean and urban/rural gaps are large; aggregate differences cannot identify which households accumulate or remain fragile.

## What E0–E1 do not yet establish

The first round does not yet estimate:

- the optimal saving rate for any person;
- a universal emergency-fund duration;
- whether education, migration, employment, entrepreneurship, or investment has the highest expected return for a specific Chinese subgroup;
- the causal effect of individual consumption categories on life quality and future earnings;
- an optimal asset allocation;
- a personal financial-freedom number.

Those require joint microdata, subgroup definitions, causal designs, and explicit individual conditions.

## Active frontier

The next empirical round should use CHFS and CFPS to study:

1. which households have high net worth but low liquidity;
2. how commitment burden predicts consumption cuts and recovery after income shocks;
3. whether early-stage wealth growth is more strongly associated with earnings growth, active saving, asset returns, housing gains, or family transfers;
4. which expenditures predict present well-being and later capability after controlling for income and prior state;
5. which combinations of income concentration, asset concentration, debt, and family support produce the largest loss of option value.

Public Git will contain code, variable maps, synthetic fixtures, and aggregate results—not redistributable survey microdata.
