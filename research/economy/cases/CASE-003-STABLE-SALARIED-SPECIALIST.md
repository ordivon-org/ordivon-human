# CASE-003 — Stable Salaried Specialist with Shrinking Options

> **Evidence status:** closed composite structural pressure test.
>
> **Primary modules:** E0, E2–E6, E8, and E9. Other E sections test guardrails and false recommendations; they are not equally diagnostic.

> Return to the [case index](README.md) or apply the framework through the [practice guide](../PRACTICE-GUIDE.md).

## 0. Case boundary

This synthetic case contains no identifying dates, age, location, profession, employer, salary, asset amount, or household structure.

It tests whether E0–E9 can distinguish apparent financial stability from genuine autonomy.

## 1. Generalized starting state

| Dimension | Structural condition |
|---|---|
| income | stable and sufficient for ordinary life |
| capability | valuable inside the current organization, partly transferable outside it |
| dependence | one employer supplies most income, proof, network, and professional identity |
| surplus | recurring, but increasingly absorbed by fixed commitments and lifestyle expansion |
| capital | meaningful relative to ordinary expenses, but not enough to replace labour income |
| ownership | mostly financial claims; little controlled productive ownership |
| time | work and obligations leave limited space for external proof or experiments |
| bargaining | adequate while the current role remains valued, weak if the role disappears suddenly |
| risk | career plateau, organization-specific capability, and commitment ratchet |
| autonomy | higher than in precarious work, but lower than income and net worth suggest |

## 2. E0 diagnosis

The first unsupported transition is:

```text
stable salary and recurring surplus
→ portable bargaining power, protected options,
and some production that persists beyond current employment
```

The bottleneck is not immediate income survival. It is the conversion of stability into durable options and ownership before commitments consume the surplus.

## 3. Action ranking

1. preserve the life-quality floor and current income while the path is tested;
2. stop adding low-value fixed commitments;
3. make current capability externally legible;
4. test whether a higher-value or more portable market exists;
5. allocate part of surplus to continuity and option capital;
6. run one bounded ownership experiment;
7. avoid premature resignation or concentrated entrepreneurship.

## 4. E1 — maintain capability rather than only role performance

The specialist may perform well while learning little that transfers.

A capability review should separate:

- organization-specific process knowledge;
- general professional judgment;
- technical or operational depth;
- communication and leadership;
- market knowledge;
- tool-dependent execution;
- knowledge that has become obsolete.

### Capability audit

Choose one important work result and answer:

1. What part of the result depends on the current organization?
2. What part would remain useful elsewhere?
3. Can the result be explained without confidential details?
4. Can the person reproduce it with different tools or constraints?
5. What evidence would another employer or client accept?

### Failure signal

If nearly all value depends on internal systems, title, or relationships, the person has less transferable capability than income suggests.

## 5. E2 — compare complete paths

### Path A — deepen the current specialist track

Strong when the field remains valuable and the person can gain scarce judgment, responsibility, or certification with real access value.

### Path B — move to an adjacent market

Strong when current capability is portable but underpriced or locked inside one organization.

### Path C — management

Strong only when the person wants and can create value through coordination, judgment, and responsibility. It is not an automatic promotion from specialist work.

### Path D — independent service or product

Strong only after a repeated external problem and bounded demand evidence.

### Path E — remain and simplify

May dominate when the current role is healthy, sufficiently paid, still developing capability, and alternatives do not justify disruption.

The model should not force career change merely because ownership is incomplete.

## 6. E3 — build external proof without violating obligations

The specialist should produce a small external proof surface:

- a generalized case explanation;
- a public technical or professional note;
- a talk or workshop;
- a credential only where it changes access;
- an open contribution;
- a bounded external project permitted by contract;
- references from people who directly observed the work.

The proof should demonstrate a result and judgment, not disclose employer property.

### Evidence threshold

External proof becomes meaningful when it produces one of:

- credible interview interest;
- an invitation to contribute;
- a paid or responsibility-bearing trial;
- a stronger internal negotiation position;
- an external reference independent of the current manager.

## 7. E4 — evaluate the total return from work

The current role should be assessed by:

```text
cash
+ capability growth
+ proof
+ market access
+ schedule quality
+ future ownership opportunity
− health and commitment cost
```

A salary increase may be weak if it requires more permanent availability, narrows capability, or creates commitments that remove refusal power.

### Market test

Without resigning, test the external market through a bounded set of conversations, applications, or paid trials.

The purpose is not to collect offers for status. It is to discover whether current capability is portable and what gap matters most.

## 8. E5 — stop the commitment ratchet

The case becomes fragile when every income increase is converted into:

- larger housing or transport obligations;
- expensive recurring services;
- debt based on peak income;
- dependent family expectations;
- a lifestyle that requires the current employer.

### Commitment review

Classify each major recurring cost by:

- essential life value;
- restoration or relationship value;
- capability value;
- reversibility;
- income needed to sustain it;
- effect on refusal power.

The objective is not to freeze life quality. It is to prevent temporary salary from becoming permanent compulsion.

## 9. E6 — first ownership experiment

The specialist should search current work for repeated knowledge that can be legally and ethically separated from employer property.

Possible forms include:

- a general method;
- a curriculum or workshop;
- a reusable diagnostic;
- a narrow professional service;
- a tool built from public or original material;
- direct relationships formed outside the employer’s protected customer base.

### Experiment rule

Start manually with a small number of users. Do not build a platform before the repeated problem and rights are clear.

### Ownership evidence

Ownership becomes productive when:

- the person controls the reusable core;
- external users repeatedly value it;
- maintenance is lower than the value created;
- it does not depend entirely on the current employer’s identity or data.

## 10. E7 — system-building remains conditional

Do not hire, automate, or scale until the ownership experiment demonstrates repeated demand.

The first system test is whether another user can receive the normal result through a documented process without extensive live explanation.

If not, the asset remains a personal service or proof artifact, which may still be valuable.

## 11. E8 — capital by function

A plausible functional structure is:

1. continuity sufficient to support a deliberate job transition;
2. option capital for verified training, interviewing, or a bounded experiment;
3. diversified long-horizon claims that reduce employer dependence;
4. small controlled productive capital only after evidence;
5. liability reduction where commitments materially weaken refusal power.

No universal percentages follow.

### Attention threshold

Complex active investment is weakly ranked if it displaces the external proof and ownership transition that can change recurring income and control.

## 12. E9 — bounded validation cycle

### Phase A — option audit

- map fixed commitments;
- separate transferable from organization-specific capability;
- identify one proof gap;
- define a safe transition boundary.

### Phase B — external legibility

- produce one public or shareable proof artifact;
- conduct several external market conversations;
- obtain one independent review or reference.

### Phase C — ownership test

- manually deliver one legally separable result;
- test repeated demand;
- measure preparation, support, and maintenance.

### Phase D — decision

- remain and deepen if the current role still compounds capability;
- negotiate or move if the external market values the capability more strongly;
- continue the ownership experiment only if demand repeats;
- simplify commitments if they block all reasonable transitions.

## 13. Evidence threshold

The case advances when most of the following appear:

- external proof independent of the current employer;
- a realistic alternative role, buyer, or professional network;
- continuity sufficient for a deliberate transition;
- fixed commitments no longer absorb all surplus growth;
- one bounded ownership experiment with real users;
- no sustained decline in life quality.

## 14. Competing actions rejected

### Resign immediately to recover freedom

Rejected because nominal freedom without demand, continuity, and proof can reduce autonomy.

### Stay indefinitely because the salary is stable

Rejected when capability, market access, and refusal power are steadily shrinking.

### Build a complex side business

Rejected before a manually delivered repeated problem exists.

### Optimize the financial portfolio as the primary solution

Rejected when the main risk is dependence on one employer and fixed commitments rather than small allocation differences.

## 15. Model pressure points

### High income can coexist with weak autonomy

The case tests whether the model correctly discounts income that depends on one organization and supports irreversible commitments.

### Stability can justify patience

The model must not convert every stable worker into an entrepreneur. Remaining employed may dominate while capability and ownership are built gradually.

### Ownership and employment can coexist

The transition need not be a binary move from employee to founder. Small independent claims may improve bargaining before they replace income.

## 16. Initial verdict

```text
portable proof, option capital, and bounded ownership
>
resignation, lifestyle expansion, or portfolio complexity
```

The case supports the E-series only if it can improve options without requiring the person to abandon a functioning role before stronger evidence appears.
