# CASE-006 — Asset-Rich but Autonomy-Poor Owner

> **Evidence status:** closed composite structural pressure test.
>
> **Primary modules:** E0 and E5–E9. Other E sections test guardrails and false recommendations; they are not equally diagnostic.

> Return to the [case index](README.md) or apply the framework through the [practice guide](../PRACTICE-GUIDE.md).

## 0. Case boundary

This synthetic case contains no identifying dates, age, location, asset amount, family structure, business, property, provider, or legal arrangement.

It tests whether E0–E9 can distinguish high accounting wealth from usable economic autonomy.

## 1. Generalized starting state

| Dimension | Structural condition |
|---|---|
| net worth | high relative to ordinary living costs |
| asset form | concentrated in illiquid property, private enterprise, family arrangements, or complex claims |
| cash flow | positive but uneven, partly dependent on active management or one operating source |
| control | nominal ownership exists, but information, transfer, exit, and day-to-day control are uneven |
| time | substantial attention is consumed by administration, maintenance, disputes, providers, or operating decisions |
| commitments | lifestyle, dependants, staff, properties, or institutions require continuing cash and supervision |
| liquidity | lower than headline wealth suggests |
| concentration | several assets share the same business, location, currency, counterparties, or family failure mode |
| governance | records and authority are distributed across people and entities; no simple operating view exists |
| autonomy | the owner can afford many goods but cannot easily stop managing, relocate, refuse, or simplify |

## 2. E0 diagnosis

The first unsupported transition is:

```text
large nominal ownership
→ usable, understandable, transferable,
and low-maintenance command over time and choices
```

The binding constraint is not further accumulation. It is the conversion of complex concentrated claims into usable autonomy without destroying valuable control or relationships.

## 3. Action ranking

1. establish a reliable view of claims, obligations, control, and cash requirements;
2. protect essential life and near-term liquidity;
3. identify which assets produce net value after attention and maintenance;
4. reduce one complexity or concentration surface at a time;
5. clarify delegation, information, and exit rights;
6. convert some concentrated value into independent claims where justified;
7. deliberately use resources to reduce compulsory management and improve life;
8. continue accumulation only where marginal value exceeds autonomy cost.

## 4. E1 — retain enough capability to govern

Delegation does not remove the need for understanding.

The owner should retain sufficient capability to:

- understand the main source of each cash flow;
- recognize leverage and guarantees;
- identify who has authority and access;
- compare provider incentives;
- detect missing records or unexplained complexity;
- know what cannot be exited quickly;
- ask informed questions without performing every task.

### Governance capability audit

For each material claim, can the owner state:

1. what is owned;
2. who controls normal decisions;
3. who receives residual value;
4. what continuing work and cost are required;
5. how value can be transferred or exited;
6. what ordinary event can impair it;
7. where the authoritative records exist?

If not, the asset is not fully governable despite legal title.

## 5. E2 — define the desired relation to wealth

### Path A — active owner-operator

Valid when direct involvement remains meaningful, high-value, and chosen.

### Path B — governed ownership

Valid when competent operators can act under clear information, authority, incentives, and review.

### Path C — simplified diversified ownership

Valid when control advantage is weak and complexity consumes more value than it creates.

### Path D — partial liquidity and deliberate use

Valid when resources can improve time, health, relationships, mobility, or chosen projects more than continued accumulation.

### Path E — stewardship or transfer

Valid when the owner’s objective includes family continuity, institutional purpose, or contribution beyond personal consumption.

The model should not assume that the correct destination is passive financial ownership. Different assets may require different relations.

## 6. E3 — replace narrative wealth with inspectable evidence

The owner may hold stories such as:

- the business is highly valuable;
- the property is safe;
- the family arrangement is understood;
- the provider handles everything;
- the asset can be sold when needed.

These claims require evidence.

A compact ownership record should show:

```text
claim
→ legal and practical control
→ cash flow
→ maintenance and attention
→ liabilities and guarantees
→ concentration
→ transfer and exit path
→ unresolved unknowns
```

The purpose is not constant valuation. It is to expose whether the asset performs its intended function.

## 7. E4 — measure net economic contribution

Gross income and headline appreciation can conceal:

- unpaid owner labour;
- maintenance;
- taxes and fees;
- vacancies or idle capacity;
- disputes;
- provider and governance costs;
- capital calls;
- concentration risk;
- inability to use value when needed.

A useful comparison is:

```text
cash and option value produced
− money, attention, fragility, and compulsion required
```

An asset can be financially profitable while reducing autonomy.

## 8. E5 — examine commitments supported by wealth

High wealth can produce a commitment ratchet:

- multiple residences or facilities;
- staff and service providers;
- recurring family or institutional expectations;
- debt and guarantees;
- complex entities;
- lifestyle systems requiring continuous coordination.

### Commitment test

For each major obligation, ask:

- does it serve essential life, relationship, purpose, or production?
- who would maintain it if the owner stopped?
- can it be reduced or exited?
- which income source must continue to support it?
- does it create chosen value or merely preserve prior decisions?

Simplification is not automatically desirable. Some commitments carry deep personal or social value. The trade-off must be explicit.

## 9. E6 — ownership quality over nominal title

The case tests the full ownership bundle:

- residual income;
- residual control;
- information;
- transfer;
- exit;
- exclusion;
- maintenance responsibility;
- liability and loss exposure.

### Weak ownership patterns

- another party controls information and access;
- assets cannot be transferred without family or institutional conflict;
- the owner bears losses but lacks normal control;
- records are incomplete;
- rights depend on informal promises;
- value disappears without the owner’s personal relationships;
- the asset cannot produce without continuing owner labour.

### Ownership improvement

Improve one weak dimension at a time through clearer records, authority, contracts, portability, independent information, or reduced exposure.

## 10. E7 — govern rather than personally operate everything

The goal is not universal delegation. It is to separate:

- decisions where owner judgment creates value;
- routine administration;
- professional specialist work;
- operator authority;
- independent review;
- emergency escalation.

### Delegation test

For one recurring responsibility:

1. define the expected result;
2. identify information needed;
3. grant bounded authority;
4. establish an independent check;
5. specify escalation;
6. test operation without owner intervention;
7. review whether owner attention actually falls.

### Negative delegation

Delegation fails when it creates more providers, reports, meetings, and opaque dependencies without reducing owner work or risk.

## 11. E8 — capital by function

The whole system should distinguish:

1. **continuity capital** — usable under ordinary disruption;
2. **operating capital** — required for active enterprises and assets;
3. **option capital** — mobility, restructuring, learning, and chosen projects;
4. **controlled productive claims** — areas where the owner has real information or control advantage;
5. **independent diversified claims** — exposures that do not share the dominant failure;
6. **protection and liability reduction** — insurance, reserves, guarantees, and structural separation where appropriate;
7. **deliberate life use** — resources converted into time, health, relationships, creation, and contribution.

No universal allocation or product recommendation follows.

### Concentration rule

Retain concentration while:

```text
marginal controlled creation value
>
marginal continuity, liquidity,
diversification, and autonomy value
```

Reduce concentration when control advantage weakens, internal reinvestment opportunities decline, or one failure can impair the owner’s entire life system.

## 12. E9 — bounded validation cycle

### Phase A — ownership map

- list material claims and obligations;
- identify legal and practical controllers;
- map cash requirements, maintenance, and guarantees;
- record unknowns rather than estimating them away.

### Phase B — function and concentration test

- assign each claim a primary function;
- identify duplicated failure modes;
- identify assets that create less usable value than their complexity suggests;
- define essential liquidity and continuity needs.

### Phase C — one simplification or governance repair

Choose one:

- consolidate records;
- clarify authority;
- remove an unnecessary provider or entity;
- reduce one liability;
- delegate one routine operation;
- create one independent information channel;
- convert one concentrated claim into a more usable form where appropriate.

### Phase D — autonomy decision

- retain complexity that produces chosen value;
- simplify where maintenance exceeds control advantage;
- convert some wealth into time or life quality;
- continue active creation where it remains meaningful;
- stop accumulation by inertia when it no longer expands the feasible action set.

## 13. Evidence threshold

The case progresses when most of the following appear:

- material claims and obligations are understandable;
- essential liquidity does not depend on forced sale;
- owner attention falls for at least one recurring responsibility;
- one concentration or liability surface is reduced or deliberately retained;
- delegation improves information rather than obscures it;
- the owner can stop or relocate for a bounded period without system failure;
- resources are deliberately used for a valued life outcome;
- no important relationship or purpose is damaged through blind simplification.

## 14. Competing actions rejected

### Continue maximizing returns by default

Rejected when the binding constraint is complexity, liquidity, attention, or chosen use rather than insufficient wealth.

### Liquidate everything into simple financial assets

Rejected because controlled businesses, property, relationships, or purpose-linked assets may have value that cannot be replaced by simplicity alone.

### Delegate all decisions

Rejected because opaque delegation can reduce control and increase agency risk.

### Personally inspect every detail

Rejected because total supervision converts ownership into compulsory administration.

### Preserve every inherited or historical structure

Rejected when its only justification is inertia and its maintenance consumes autonomy.

## 15. Model pressure points

### Wealth and autonomy can diverge sharply

The case directly tests the E0 distinction between accounting net worth and a feasible action set.

### Simplification has real costs

The model must account for taxes, contracts, relationships, control, identity, and purpose. Complexity cannot be deleted merely because it is hard to govern.

### Spending can be the correct economic action

Converting wealth into health, time, relationships, or meaningful work can dominate further accumulation when basic economic power is already sufficient.

### Governance is productive only when thin

A larger reporting and advisory system can become another maintenance burden.

## 16. Initial verdict

```text
usable control, liquidity, simpler governance,
and deliberate life use
>
further accumulation by inertia
```

The case supports the E-series only if it can recommend active ownership, delegation, simplification, diversification, or spending conditionally—without treating any one of them as the universal endpoint.
