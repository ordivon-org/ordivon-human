# CASE-002 — Precarious Worker with Fragmented Income

> **Evidence status:** closed composite structural pressure test.
>
> **Primary modules:** E0–E5, E8, and E9. Other E sections test guardrails and false recommendations; they are not equally diagnostic.

> Return to the [case index](README.md) or apply the framework through the [practice guide](../PRACTICE-GUIDE.md).

## 0. Case boundary

This is a synthetic structural case, not a disguised biography. It contains no dates, location, age, employer, platform, occupation, or exact financial amounts.

The case tests whether E0–E9 can distinguish a genuine continuity problem from premature advice about investing, entrepreneurship, or long-term ownership.

## 1. Generalized starting state

| Dimension | Structural condition |
|---|---|
| income | obtained through irregular shifts, temporary assignments, platform work, or short contracts |
| dependence | one buyer, intermediary, or scheduling system controls much of the available work |
| capability | practical experience exists, but it is not expressed as transferable proof |
| surplus | inconsistent; ordinary disruptions regularly consume what was retained |
| time | fragmented by unpredictable scheduling and recovery needs |
| health | variable work intensity may impair sleep, recovery, or attendance |
| capital | small and primarily needed for continuity |
| ownership | no material productive asset beyond personal capability and work history |
| bargaining | weak because refusal may immediately reduce essential income |
| attention | divided between finding work, completing work, and reacting to short-term financial pressure |

## 2. E0 diagnosis

The first unsupported transition is not “surplus to investment.” It is:

```text
fragmented labour and hidden practical capability
→ reliable exchange with enough continuity to refuse the worst offers
```

The binding constraints are:

1. unstable access to work;
2. weak external proof of transferable capability;
3. insufficient continuity capital;
4. schedule fragmentation that blocks deliberate improvement.

The model therefore ranks stability and proof above speculative return, broad retraining, or product creation.

## 3. Action ranking

1. protect essential functioning and the ability to keep working;
2. reduce dependence on a single work source where feasible;
3. make one existing practical capability legible;
4. seek a more predictable or higher-value adjacent exchange;
5. build a small continuity reserve;
6. only then fund longer learning or ownership experiments.

This ranking changes if an immediate health, housing, legal, or caregiving crisis is the true binding constraint.

## 4. E1 — restore a usable learning base

The worker may possess substantial practical competence while lacking stable time to develop it.

The first foundation intervention is not an intensive course. It is a repeatable minimum:

- one protected recovery block;
- one short weekly capability block;
- one record of completed work and problems solved;
- one limit on additional commitments that destroy attendance or recovery.

### Foundation evidence

The base is improving when the person can maintain a small learning and documentation loop across several ordinary work disruptions.

### Failure signal

If even a minimal loop repeatedly fails because of exhaustion, illness, transport, care obligations, or unsafe work, the correct action is to repair continuity rather than prescribe more discipline.

## 5. E2 — choose an adjacent direction

A complete career reinvention may be too expensive and slow. The first direction test should begin near existing evidence.

Possible adjacent value surfaces include:

- reliability and coordination;
- customer or client handling;
- equipment or process knowledge;
- quality checking;
- scheduling and dispatch;
- documentation;
- training new workers;
- resolving recurring exceptions.

The person should identify which result is both repeatedly needed and more transferable than the current job label.

### Direction test

Choose one adjacent capability and test it through:

1. a short independent learning sequence;
2. one improved result in current work;
3. one external review or interview;
4. one application or exchange outside the dominant intermediary.

### Direction falsifier

Stop the track when it requires a large commitment before any credible access evidence, or when employers and buyers do not distinguish the capability from ordinary labour supply.

## 6. E3 — convert work history into proof

A fragmented work record often appears weaker than the actual capability it contains.

The person should create a small proof record around a recurring result, such as:

- preventing a common error;
- handling a difficult customer situation;
- improving handover quality;
- reducing missed steps;
- training another worker;
- reorganizing a repeated task.

The proof should state:

```text
starting problem
→ action taken
→ constraint
→ observable result
→ what remained uncertain
```

It must not disclose confidential or personal information.

### First stronger exchange

The next exchange may be:

- a more predictable assignment;
- responsibility for a higher-value task;
- a direct referral;
- a role with clearer hours;
- a small service outside the dominant platform;
- a paid trial in an adjacent role.

The objective is not immediate high income. It is evidence that the person can be selected for a result rather than only for availability.

## 7. E4 — stabilize captured value

Income growth has little autonomy value when every gain is offset by volatility, transport, equipment, unpaid waiting, or health loss.

Evaluate work sources by:

- net income after direct work costs;
- predictability;
- schedule control;
- capability growth;
- proof gained;
- dependence on one intermediary;
- recovery burden.

A slightly lower nominal rate may dominate when it creates reliable hours, lower hidden costs, and time for capability growth.

### Practical experiment

Compare the current dominant work source with one alternative over a bounded set of assignments. Record total time, direct costs, cancellations, recovery burden, and proof gained.

### Stop rule

Do not leave the current income source before the alternative demonstrates enough reliability, unless the current source violates a safety or health boundary.

## 8. E5 — protect continuity and avoid coercive commitments

The main spending question is not a universal saving rate. It is which costs preserve the ability to work and transition.

High-priority uses may include:

- reliable transport or communication needed for work;
- required equipment;
- health and recovery;
- fees tied to a verified access opportunity;
- a small reserve against missed work;
- reducing an obligation that forces acceptance of harmful work.

Low-priority uses include status commitments, high-cost debt for uncertain training, and tools purchased before a real use path exists.

## 9. E6 — ownership remains secondary but not absent

The first ownership surface may be modest:

- a reusable checklist;
- a direct contact network where permitted;
- a training note;
- a repeatable service process;
- a reputation attached to a specific result.

These assets matter only when they reduce dependence or support a stronger exchange.

Do not build a product or business merely because ownership is theoretically desirable.

## 10. E7 — system-building is not yet the bottleneck

Delegation, automation, hiring, and scale are premature unless the person already has repeated direct demand.

The only useful system test at this stage is whether a simple process makes work access or delivery more reliable without adding maintenance.

Examples:

- a repeatable application process;
- a client intake template;
- a scheduling and follow-up routine;
- a compact proof portfolio.

## 11. E8 — capital by function

The ranking is:

```text
continuity
→ verified access option
→ capability maintenance
→ low-maintenance long-horizon ownership
→ controlled experiment
```

High-variance activity is weakly ranked because the capital base is small and a reset can directly reduce refusal power.

No product-level allocation follows from the public case.

## 12. E9 — bounded validation cycle

### Phase A — continuity map

- identify essential work-enabling costs;
- identify the dominant work dependency;
- record hidden work costs and schedule failures;
- define the minimum recoverable weekly rhythm.

### Phase B — proof extraction

- select one repeated higher-value result;
- create several anonymized result records;
- obtain feedback from an external employer, buyer, or experienced practitioner.

### Phase C — alternative exchange

- test one adjacent work source;
- seek one responsibility-bearing trial;
- compare net value and predictability rather than only nominal pay.

### Phase D — decision

- retain the current mix if the alternative is weaker;
- shift gradually if reliability and proof improve;
- redesign the direction if buyers do not recognize the result;
- return to continuity repair if health or obligations dominate.

## 13. Evidence threshold

The case progresses when most of the following appear:

- one stable work-enabling routine;
- one transferable capability expressed through concrete proof;
- one exchange outside the dominant intermediary;
- improved predictability or bargaining;
- a small continuity reserve that survives an ordinary disruption;
- no material decline in health or essential functioning.

## 14. Competing actions rejected

### Begin active investing as the primary strategy

Rejected because small capital and continuity risk make capability and reliable exchange much larger channels.

### Enroll immediately in a long expensive retraining program

Rejected unless a short access test shows credible demand and the downside is survivable.

### Launch a business immediately

Rejected because direct demand, reliable delivery, and continuity are not yet established.

### Maximize total hours

Rejected when additional hours reduce reliability, health, or the ability to move toward stronger exchange.

## 15. Model pressure points

### Stability can be productive rather than merely defensive

A more predictable work arrangement may create time and refusal power that later enable capability and ownership.

### Practical capability may be hidden by fragmented credentials

The model must not equate weak formal proof with absence of useful competence.

### Continuity and capability can conflict

The person may need immediate income from low-growth work. The model should permit a mixed strategy rather than demanding a clean transition.

## 16. Initial verdict

```text
continuity and transferable proof
>
long retraining, entrepreneurship, or return optimization
```

The case supports the E-series only if a bounded adjacent-path test can improve exchange without assuming uninterrupted time, exceptional support, or immediate exit from current work.
