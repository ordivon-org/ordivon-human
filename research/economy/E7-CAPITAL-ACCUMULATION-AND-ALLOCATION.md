# E7 — Capital Accumulation and Allocation

## 1. Question

> Once a person has a quality-preserving surplus or productive ownership, how should capital be accumulated and allocated across continuity, capability, concentrated creation, and diversified productive claims?

E7 is downstream of E0–E6.

It does not begin from an asset list or universal portfolio percentage.

## 2. Capital is stored and transferable command

Capital is a claim that can be redeployed across time into:

- consumption and continuity;
- learning and capability;
- tools and experiments;
- products and organizations;
- debt reduction;
- other productive assets;
- future autonomy.

Capital differs from current income because it survives the period. It differs from capability because it can usually be transferred. It differs from ownership because some capital is only a liquid option awaiting deployment.

## 3. Sources of capital

Capital can arise from:

```text
quality-preserving surplus
+ retained business profits
+ asset returns
+ sale or licensing of created claims
+ external transfers
− losses, taxes, costs, and withdrawals
```

The source matters because risk may remain attached.

Examples:

- wealth earned from one employer may still be correlated with employer equity;
- profits retained in one business are both capital and concentrated business exposure;
- a house may store wealth but be difficult to redeploy;
- family transfer may be conditional or controlled by another party.

## 4. Allocation serves functions, not labels

E7 retains five functional capital pools.

### C1 — Continuity capital

Purpose:

- maintain essential life during interruption;
- prevent forced sale or expensive borrowing;
- preserve the ability to wait and choose.

Required properties:

- usable within the relevant time;
- low expected loss over that horizon;
- not controlled by the same party causing the shock;
- sized against the actual failure process rather than a universal month count.

### C2 — Option and exploration capital

Purpose:

- fund retraining, relocation, prototypes, experiments, and search;
- buy time for uncertain but reversible paths.

Required properties:

- bounded loss;
- staged release;
- clear learning objective;
- stop or pivot condition;
- separation from essential continuity capital.

### C3 — Controlled productive capital

Purpose:

- fund a product, business, tool, distribution route, or system where the person has meaningful information or control advantage.

Required properties:

- explicit mechanism of value creation;
- credible marginal use of additional capital;
- control or contractual rights that support capture;
- bounded personal liability;
- evidence that capital rather than demand or execution is the constraint.

### C4 — Diversified long-horizon productive claims

Purpose:

- convert surplus into broad claims on production;
- reduce dependence on one project, operator, country, platform, or own labour;
- compound with low personal maintenance.

Required properties:

- diversification across independent failure modes;
- tolerable costs and taxes;
- horizon compatible with volatility and illiquidity;
- governance simple enough not to consume disproportionate attention.

### C5 — Protection and liability reduction

Purpose:

- remove asymmetric losses;
- reduce expensive or coercive debt;
- insure risks that would otherwise destroy the path;
- separate personal survival from business failure.

A liability reduction may dominate a new asset when it produces a high certain saving, releases collateral, or restores refusal power.

These pools are functional. One instrument may serve more than one function; one label may conceal several risks.

## 5. The marginal allocation question

For the next unit of capital, ask:

```text
Which available use creates the largest increase in terminal outcomes
per unit of capital, time, attention, dependence, and failure risk?
```

The relevant comparison is among current feasible uses:

- preserve continuity;
- remove costly liability;
- invest in capability;
- fund an owned opportunity;
- buy diversified productive claims;
- consume for present life;
- retain optionality.

A high expected percentage return does not dominate when:

- the base is small;
- the opportunity consumes scarce attention;
- failure destroys continuity;
- the return is highly correlated with labour or business income;
- the estimate depends on unavailable information or control.

## 6. Capital allocation is a whole-system decision

Financial assets cannot be allocated independently of:

- human capital;
- labour-income volatility;
- business ownership;
- housing and location;
- debt and fixed commitments;
- public insurance and family support;
- ability to adjust work;
- time horizon;
- control and legal access.

A person's existing economic exposure may already be concentrated even when the financial portfolio appears diversified.

Examples:

```text
job in one industry
+ equity in employer
+ house in the same city
+ professional network in the same industry
```

or

```text
founder labour
+ company equity
+ personal guarantees
+ customer concentration
+ identity tied to company success
```

## 7. Risk capacity is not age

Age may affect horizon, but risk capacity depends on:

- essential commitments;
- liquid continuity capital;
- labour flexibility;
- income volatility;
- correlation of income with assets;
- health and ability to recover;
- social insurance;
- business concentration;
- reversibility of the investment;
- consequences of permanent loss.

Recent lifecycle research reinforces that labour-income correlation and social insurance can cause optimal allocations to differ substantially even among people of similar age.

E7 therefore rejects age-only allocation rules.

## 8. Concentrated creation versus diversified preservation

### Concentrated creation capital

Can dominate when:

- the person has control or unique information;
- small capital can unlock a large production bottleneck;
- the opportunity cannot be replicated through public assets;
- ownership preserves a large share of created value;
- failure is bounded and learning transfers.

### Diversified preservation capital

Can dominate when:

- capital already materially supports life and autonomy;
- no exceptional controlled opportunity exists;
- human and business capital are already concentrated;
- marginal owner reinvestment has declined;
- low-maintenance compounding has high value;
- permanent loss would close important options.

The allocation can rationally evolve from concentrated creation to diversified preservation as the capital base grows.

## 9. Reinvestment in an owned business

Retained profits should not be reinvested automatically.

The owner must compare:

```text
marginal value created by another unit inside the business
versus
marginal value of liquidity, capability, debt reduction, or diversified claims
```

Reinvestment is strongest when:

- demand is validated;
- unit economics are positive;
- the bottleneck is genuinely capital;
- the owner retains meaningful control and capture;
- additional capital does not merely subsidize inefficiency;
- downside remains bounded.

Distribution or diversification is stronger when:

- capital accumulates without a high-value internal use;
- growth requires worsening terms or dependencies;
- owner wealth is excessively concentrated;
- the business can use external financing without unacceptable control loss;
- life and future experiments need independent capital.

## 10. Financial leverage

Debt changes the allocation problem by introducing fixed claims and timing constraints.

Financial leverage is admissible only when:

1. the financed mechanism has positive expected net value;
2. cash-flow timing can service fixed claims under ordinary adverse states;
3. collateral failure does not destroy unrelated essential assets;
4. the liability does not force acceptance of harmful work or terms;
5. the exposure is not already duplicated through labour, business, or housing;
6. exit and restructuring remain possible;
7. the person is compensated for losing flexibility.

Leverage is especially dangerous when used to fund volatile market exposure with no control advantage.

## 11. Active allocation and the attention threshold

Active selection or trading should be admitted only when:

```text
expected advantage × capital base
>
fees + taxes + spread + error risk
+ value of attention and displaced higher-leverage work
```

This test explains why an activity can have positive expected percentage value but still be economically irrational for one person.

The attention threshold rises when:

- capital is small;
- capability and ownership opportunities are large;
- the person lacks information advantage;
- decisions are frequent;
- activity increases emotional volatility or repeated reset risk.

The threshold falls when:

- capital is large;
- allocation decisions are infrequent and consequential;
- the person has a genuine structural edge or control right;
- governance cannot be delegated cheaply.

## 12. Inattention can be rational

Observation and adjustment consume resources.

A low-maintenance allocation may dominate a theoretically better but attention-intensive strategy after:

- monitoring cost;
- transaction cost;
- behavioral error;
- tax friction;
- opportunity cost;
- stress and life-quality cost.

Therefore:

```text
more frequent decision
≠
more control
```

and

```text
more optimization
≠
more terminal value
```

## 13. Rebalancing is a failure-control action

Rebalancing is justified when drift materially changes:

- concentration;
- liquidity;
- liability coverage;
- horizon fit;
- risk correlation;
- ability to survive an ordinary failure;
- access to a clearly superior controlled opportunity.

Calendar activity without a changed failure mode is not automatically useful.

A good rebalance rule should avoid:

- precision unsupported by the model;
- frequent taxable or costly turnover;
- selling productive claims merely because they rose;
- preserving a target percentage after the underlying life or business state changed.

## 14. Illiquidity

Illiquidity may be compensated by:

- higher expected productive return;
- control rights;
- protection from impulsive action;
- access to opportunities unavailable in liquid markets.

It also imposes:

- inability to fund shocks or options;
- bargaining weakness at forced exit;
- valuation uncertainty;
- maintenance and legal burden;
- concentration.

Illiquidity is acceptable only when the person retains enough independent continuity and option capital.

## 15. Currency, jurisdiction, and institutional risk

A claim's value depends on the institutions through which it can be used.

Relevant exposures include:

- currency of future consumption;
- custody and provider risk;
- capital controls and legal access;
- taxation;
- property and contract enforcement;
- geographic concentration;
- political and policy change.

The program does not prescribe a jurisdictional allocation. It requires these dependencies to be made visible rather than treating all nominally diversified assets as institutionally independent.

## 16. Allocation by stage

| Stage | Capital priority | Main allocation error |
|---|---|---|
| dependence | preserve essential function and fund capability conversion | risking scarce capital before an income engine exists |
| labour-dominant | continuity, capability, and low-cost diversified ownership | excessive attention to small-return optimization |
| stable surplus | option capital, controlled ownership, and diversified claims | allowing lifestyle or one bet to absorb all surplus |
| scalable ownership | fund validated bottlenecks and extract independent capital | leaving all wealth inside the operating system |
| capital-dominant | preserve autonomy through diversification, governance, and low maintenance | concentration, complexity, and avoidable permanent loss |
| high autonomy | align capital with life, contribution, stewardship, and chosen risk | accumulation without additional valued options |

## 17. E7 decision protocol

For the next meaningful allocation, record:

1. current stage and binding constraint;
2. terminal outcome the allocation should change;
3. function of the capital pool;
4. expected absolute—not only percentage—magnitude;
5. required time and attention;
6. relationship to labour and business exposure;
7. control and information advantage;
8. liquidity and exit path;
9. ordinary adverse state and permanent-loss state;
10. maintenance, tax, legal, and provider costs;
11. simpler alternative;
12. evidence that would trigger continuation, extraction, diversification, or exit.

No total portfolio score is required.

## 18. E7 result

Capital allocation is not a standalone investment problem.

It is the downstream allocation of prior capability, captured value, surplus, and ownership across:

```text
continuity
+ future options
+ controlled productive opportunities
+ diversified productive claims
+ protection from asymmetric loss
```

At low capital, the main error is often spending scarce attention on small financial-return differences while capability and ownership remain changeable. At high concentrated wealth, the main error reverses: continuing to seek creation-level upside when diversification, governance, and preservation would expand autonomy more.

The correct allocation therefore changes with the base, the person's non-financial exposures, control advantage, and the consequences of failure.
