# E9 — Conditional Strategy Map

## 1. Purpose

E9 synthesizes HUMAN-ECON-001 into a state-dependent decision map.

It does not prescribe one saving rate, asset allocation, career, business model, or wealth target.

Its output form is:

```text
starting state
→ binding constraint
→ dominant mechanism
→ order-of-magnitude test
→ action family
→ life-quality and dependence cost
→ failure boundary
→ evidence and exit rule
→ next-stage transition
```

## 2. Universal decision order

For any economic action:

1. identify the terminal outcome;
2. identify the current stage and binding constraint;
3. identify which wealth mechanism changes;
4. estimate absolute magnitude and time to usefulness;
5. account for life quality, attention, maintenance, and dependence;
6. expose the ordinary and terminal failure modes;
7. preserve reversibility where uncertainty is high;
8. scale only after mechanism-specific evidence;
9. convert gains into durable ownership and broader autonomy;
10. stop when another mechanism becomes dominant.

## 3. State S0 — Economic dependence

### Typical state

- little independently controlled income;
- small capital base;
- weak proof and market access;
- decisions partly controlled by family, institution, or another provider.

### Binding constraint

```text
private ability has not become independently exchangeable
```

### Dominant mechanism

- capability conversion;
- proof;
- market access;
- independent control over first income.

### Action families

- learn a capability connected to valued demand;
- produce inspectable work;
- use education or credentials where they grant real access;
- enter stronger learning and market environments;
- use tools and AI to accelerate practice and production;
- run low-cost market tests.

### Usually secondary

- optimizing small investment returns;
- complex tax or portfolio structures;
- business scale before demand and proof.

### Failure boundary

Do not risk essential capability, education access, health, or the small capital needed to reach independent exchange.

### Transition evidence

```text
credible result
+ paying or independently controlled demand
→ S1
```

## 4. State S1 — Labour-dominant independent income

### Typical state

- income exists but mostly stops with current labour;
- one employer, client, or market is material;
- surplus is small or inconsistent;
- capital return is small in absolute terms.

### Binding constraint

```text
captured value per unit of time and dependence on one buyer
```

### Dominant mechanism

- improve capability-demand fit;
- strengthen proof and bargaining;
- enter higher-value markets;
- create reliable surplus;
- begin low-maintenance diversified ownership.

### Action families

- move from routine execution toward scarce judgment, integration, or responsibility;
- improve language, distribution, network, and market access;
- make work externally legible and transferable;
- remove low-value fixed commitments;
- use tools to reduce low-value labour;
- separate a small bounded experiment from continuity capital.

### Magnitude test

Compare any portfolio optimization with plausible recurring income change:

```text
capital required = recurring annual income change / expected return difference
```

### Failure boundary

Avoid repeated reset, expensive leverage, and commitments that eliminate refusal power.

### Transition evidence

```text
reliable captured value
− quality-preserving life and capability cost
= recurring surplus
→ S2
```

## 5. State S2 — Stable surplus

### Typical state

- income reliably exceeds the quality-preserving cost of life;
- ordinary failure is survivable;
- capital can fund experiments and long-horizon claims.

### Binding constraint

```text
surplus is not yet converted into a larger future production base
```

### Dominant mechanism

- capability investment with a market conversion path;
- option capital;
- controlled productive ownership;
- diversified productive claims;
- liability reduction where it releases autonomy.

### Action families

- build reusable code, product, media, intellectual property, or distribution;
- productize repeatable services;
- fund staged experiments;
- accumulate diversified long-horizon claims with low maintenance;
- remove liabilities with high certain cost or coercive collateral;
- preserve present-life spending with high dual return.

### Failure boundary

Do not allow one experiment, lifestyle ratchet, or business obligation to absorb all future options.

### Transition evidence

```text
part of output or income persists beyond current labour
+ residual rights are controlled
→ S3
```

## 6. State S3 — Scalable ownership

### Typical state

- product, distribution, business equity, or system produces material value;
- owner labour remains important but not strictly proportional to output;
- concentrated ownership and reinvestment may have high value.

### Binding constraint

```text
turn a valuable asset into an owner-independent and transferable production system
```

### Dominant mechanism

- replication;
- distribution;
- organizational reliability;
- control and governance;
- selective reinvestment;
- extraction of independent capital.

### Action families

- strengthen unit economics and recurring demand;
- remove founder bottlenecks;
- document and systematize critical operations;
- acquire or partner for scarce complements;
- preserve direct customer and data access;
- test bounded owner absence;
- diversify a portion of wealth when marginal control value declines.

### Concentration rule

Continue concentrated reinvestment only while:

```text
marginal controlled productive value
>
marginal value of continuity, options, and diversification
```

### Failure boundary

Avoid personal guarantees, unbounded fixed costs, one-platform dependence, and scaling before repeatability.

### Transition evidence

```text
meaningful residual income or enterprise value
survives owner substitution
+ independent capital base exists
→ S4
```

## 7. State S4 — Capital- and system-dominant wealth

### Typical state

- owned claims materially support life and further growth;
- labour income is no longer the sole engine;
- permanent loss and governance become first-order.

### Binding constraint

```text
preserve and redeploy productive claims without allowing concentration, complexity, or governance failure to destroy autonomy
```

### Dominant mechanism

- diversification across independent failure modes;
- simple governance;
- capital allocation;
- liability and institutional-risk management;
- selective high-conviction creation only where control advantage remains.

### Action families

- convert excess concentrated operating wealth into broader claims;
- separate operating assets from continuity and family capital;
- delegate standardized allocation and administration;
- retain control only where judgment creates real value;
- simplify providers, entities, and processes;
- align currency and liquidity with future uses.

### Active-management threshold

```text
expected advantage × large capital base
>
all financial, error, attention, and governance costs
```

### Failure boundary

Avoid irreversible concentration, hidden leverage, opaque delegation, and structures too complex to understand or exit.

### Transition evidence

```text
essential life and chosen projects
no longer require accepting ordinary market offers
→ S5
```

## 8. State S5 — High economic autonomy

### Typical state

- essential life is supported without compulsory ordinary labour;
- work may continue for creation, meaning, influence, or stewardship;
- additional wealth has diminishing effect on basic option value.

### Binding constraint

```text
convert resources into a finite valuable life rather than continue accumulation by inertia
```

### Dominant mechanism

- deliberate use of time;
- health and capability preservation;
- chosen creation;
- stewardship, contribution, and relationships;
- simple durable governance.

### Action families

- reduce wealth-maintenance labour;
- choose projects by meaning and marginal social or personal value;
- preserve enough capability to understand delegated systems;
- transfer ownership and knowledge deliberately;
- spend on high-value life, time, and contribution;
- accept chosen risk only where the downside does not recreate compulsion.

### Failure boundary

Do not let wealth management, status, fear of loss, or endless accumulation become the new controller.

## 9. Path P1 — Professional capability compounding

### Mechanism

```text
capability
→ proof
→ higher-value work
→ bargaining and access
→ surplus
→ diversified ownership
```

### Best fit

- low initial capital;
- strong learning capacity;
- markets that reward demonstrated expertise;
- preference for lower operating complexity.

### Main risk

Income remains labour-dependent and may plateau without ownership or distribution.

### Exit or transition

Begin ownership conversion when surplus and repeated market knowledge reveal a reusable asset or when diversified claims can materially reduce labour dependence.

## 10. Path P2 — Tool-leveraged independent production

### Mechanism

```text
capability
+ software, AI, and automation
→ higher output per person
→ productized service or product
→ direct ownership
```

### Best fit

- digitally replicable output;
- one person can verify and integrate tools;
- low initial organizational need;
- direct access to users.

### Main risk

Tool advantage diffuses; distribution, trust, and maintenance become the bottleneck.

### Exit or transition

Scale only after demand and repeatability. Preserve portability and direct customer access.

## 11. Path P3 — Entrepreneurship and organization

### Mechanism

```text
valuable problem
→ product
→ distribution
→ coordinated people and capital
→ residual enterprise claim
```

### Best fit

- opportunity requires complementary specialists and capital;
- scale materially increases value;
- founder can retain sufficient control or capture.

### Main risk

High variance, concentration, fixed costs, dilution, and founder life-quality collapse.

### Exit or transition

Sell, delegate, restructure, or diversify when marginal control value falls below concentration and maintenance cost.

## 12. Path P4 — Income-led diversified capital accumulation

### Mechanism

```text
stable labour or business surplus
→ broad low-maintenance productive claims
→ long-horizon compounding
```

### Best fit

- no exceptional controlled ownership opportunity;
- preference for simplicity;
- stable surplus and long horizon;
- low desire for organizational maintenance.

### Main risk

Slow absolute progress when surplus is small; attention may be wasted optimizing minor return differences.

### Exit or transition

No required exit. The path can gradually reduce labour dependence as the base becomes material.

## 13. Path P5 — External transfer

### Mechanism

```text
family transfer, inheritance, grant, or windfall
→ larger starting capital base
```

### Required analysis

- actual control;
- conditions and revocability;
- obligations to others;
- concentration and institutional access;
- capability to govern the resource.

A transfer changes stage but does not prove internal wealth-formation capability.

## 14. Path P6 — Concentrated speculation

### Mechanism

```text
capital
→ exposure to uncertain price change without material control
→ rapid gain or loss
```

### Potential role

- bounded optional exposure;
- learning about a market;
- expression of a genuinely evidenced edge.

### Main risk

- repeated reset;
- attention capture;
- leverage;
- luck interpreted as skill;
- no creation of transferable capability or productive ownership.

### Admission rule

Speculation must remain inside a bounded failure domain and compete against the opportunity cost of capability, ownership, and diversified claims.

## 15. Cross-cutting decision matrix

| Starting condition | Dominant next question | Usually weak default |
|---|---|---|
| no independent income | how does capability reach paying demand? | portfolio optimization |
| income but no surplus | how can captured value rise or commitments fall without destroying capability? | forced maximum saving |
| stable surplus but no ownership | what reusable or diversified claim should surplus acquire? | idle accumulation without purpose |
| product but no distribution | how does it reach repeated demand? | adding features or staff |
| revenue but owner is bottleneck | what system or role removes founder dependence? | scaling customer volume first |
| concentrated business wealth | where does control still justify exposure? | either total reinvestment or immediate total exit |
| large diversified base | what risks, complexity, or governance still threaten autonomy? | high-frequency active management |
| high autonomy | what use of time and resources is actually valued? | accumulation by inertia |

## 16. What no longer survives as universal advice

The program rejects universal claims such as:

- save one fixed percentage;
- hold one fixed number of months of expenses;
- risk should depend mainly on age;
- entrepreneurship is always superior to employment;
- passive assets are always superior to private ownership;
- concentrate to become rich and diversify to stay rich as an unconditional rule;
- all debt is harmful or all leverage is productive;
- higher income necessarily creates autonomy;
- lower consumption necessarily improves the trajectory;
- more financial decisions create more control;
- reaching one wealth number completes the problem.

Each rule may be useful under specific states and mechanisms. None survives without conditions.

## 17. Final HUMAN-ECON-001 synthesis

The complete first-principles model is:

```text
terminal outcomes
=
present life quality
+ durable economic power
+ economic autonomy

wealth formation
=
capability
→ value creation
→ value capture
→ quality-preserving surplus
→ productive ownership
→ leverage
→ compounding

continuity constraint
=
ordinary failure must not destroy essential life,
future capability, or the ability to continue

allocation rule
=
place the next unit of time, money, attention, and control
where it changes the binding constraint by the largest durable amount
relative to maintenance, dependence, and failure risk
```

## 18. E9 result

Personal economic strategy is a changing control problem, not a fixed financial plan.

The person should repeatedly ask:

```text
What is the current binding constraint?
Which mechanism can change it by a meaningful absolute amount?
What must remain intact if the attempt fails?
What durable claim or option will remain if it succeeds?
When does a different mechanism become dominant?
```

The end state is not maximum capital.

It is a life in which resources, productive systems, and institutions increasingly serve chosen action rather than determine it.
