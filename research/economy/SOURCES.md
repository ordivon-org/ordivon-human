# HUMAN-ECON-001 Sources and Evidence Hierarchy

## Evidence hierarchy

The active economic-autonomy program uses sources in this order:

1. **first-principles identities and constraints** — finite time, resource conservation, ownership, control, replication, and compounding;
2. **public averages** — only for order-of-magnitude calibration;
3. **mature theory and historical cases** — to expose mechanisms and counterexamples;
4. **granular data** — admitted only when a later decision genuinely depends on it.

Data availability does not choose the research question.

## Primary public baseline

### National Bureau of Statistics of China

National Bureau of Statistics. **2025年居民收入和消费支出情况.** Published 2026-01-19.

<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202601/t20260119_1962321.html>

Used for:

- public per-capita income and consumption scale;
- average income-source shares;
- urban/rural order-of-magnitude comparison;
- broad consumption composition.

Not used to infer individual causality or a typical household saving rate.

## Supporting conceptual sources

These sources do not determine the E0–E3 structure. They are retained because they clarify mechanisms that can challenge or refine first-principles reasoning.

### Income, consumption, and wealth are distinct

OECD. **OECD Framework for Statistics on the Distribution of Household Income, Consumption and Wealth.** 2013.

<https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264194830-en>

### Financial well-being includes control and freedom of choice

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. **Why financial well-being?**

<https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/about/>

### Liquidity can matter independently of accounting wealth

Ganong, P. and P. Noel. **Liquidity vs. Wealth in Household Debt Obligations.** NBER Working Paper 24964, 2018.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w24964>

### Illiquid wealth can coexist with little usable cash

Kaplan, G., G. L. Violante and J. Weidner. **The Wealthy Hand-to-Mouth.** NBER Working Paper 20073, 2014.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w20073>

### Commitments change risk response

Chetty, R. and A. Szeidl. **Consumption Commitments and Risk Preferences.** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2007.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w12467>

### Active saving and valuation gains are not the same

Fagereng, A., M. B. Holm, B. Moll and G. Natvik. **Saving Behavior Across the Wealth Distribution: The Importance of Capital Gains.** NBER Working Paper 26588, 2019.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w26588>

### Returns to wealth are heterogeneous

Fagereng, A., L. Guiso, D. Malacrino and L. Pistaferri. **Heterogeneity and Persistence in Returns to Wealth.** NBER Working Paper 22822, 2016.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w22822>

## Current exclusions

The active program does not currently require:

- CHFS or CFPS respondent-level data;
- a household panel model;
- a personal financial score;
- a proprietary market dataset;
- a detailed consumption classifier;
- a causal estimate for every stage transition.

Those may be admitted later only when a concrete unresolved decision cannot be answered through a simpler mechanism model and public calibration.
