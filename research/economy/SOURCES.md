# HUMAN-ECON-001 Sources and Evidence Hierarchy

## Evidence hierarchy

The economic-autonomy program uses sources in this order:

1. **first-principles identities and constraints** — finite time, resource conservation, ownership, control, replication, and compounding;
2. **public averages** — order-of-magnitude calibration only;
3. **mature theory and historical cases** — mechanisms and counterexamples;
4. **granular data** — only when a later decision genuinely depends on it.

Data availability does not choose the research question.

National Bureau of Statistics. **2025年居民收入和消费支出情况.** Published 2026-01-19.

<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202601/t20260119_1962321.html>

Fagereng, A., M. B. Holm, B. Moll and G. Natvik. **Saving Behavior Across the Wealth Distribution: The Importance of Capital Gains.** NBER Working Paper 26588, 2019.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w26588>

## Ownership, control, and the firm

### Ownership allocates residual control under incomplete contracts

Hart, O. and J. Moore. **Incomplete Contracts and Ownership: Some New Thoughts.** *American Economic Review*, 2007.

<https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.97.2.182>

Hart, O. **Incomplete Contracts and Control.** *American Economic Review*, 2017.

<https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.107.7.1731>

These support the distinction between nominal possession and residual control rights when future uses cannot be fully specified.

### Organizational ownership and authority

Van den Steen, E. **Interpersonal Authority in a Theory of the Firm.** *American Economic Review*, 2010.

<https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.1.466>

Used to challenge the assumption that organization is merely many independent market contracts.

## Entrepreneurship, concentration, and exit

### Entrepreneurial wealth is concentrated

Gentry, W. and R. G. Hubbard. **Entrepreneurship and Household Saving.** NBER Working Paper 7894, 2000.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w7894>

### Concentrated private business ownership does not automatically earn a premium

Moskowitz, T. and A. Vissing-Jørgensen. **The Returns to Entrepreneurial Investment: A Private Equity Premium Puzzle?** NBER Working Paper 8876, 2002.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w8876>

### Private-business returns are dispersed and persistent

Boar, C., D. Gorea and V. Midrigan. **Why Are Returns to Private Business Wealth So Dispersed?** NBER Working Paper 29705, revised 2023.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w29705>

### Concentration may support effort and private benefits

Elenev, V. and T. Landvoigt. **Asset Pricing with Optimal Under-Diversification.** NBER Working Paper 31121, 2023.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w31121>

### Wealth before entry and flexible exit matter

Wang, C., N. Wang and J. Yang. **A Unified Model of Entrepreneurship Dynamics.** NBER Working Paper 16843, 2011.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w16843>

These sources support a stage-dependent concentration judgment rather than unconditional entrepreneurship or diversification rules.

## Capital allocation, human capital, and attention

### Illiquid human or business capital changes liquid portfolio choice

Schwartz, E. and C. Tebaldi. **Illiquid Assets and Optimal Portfolio Choice.** NBER Working Paper 12633, revised 2010.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w12633>

### Labour flexibility changes risk capacity

Bodie, Z., R. Merton and W. Samuelson. **Labor Supply Flexibility and Portfolio Choice in a Life-Cycle Model.** NBER Working Paper 3954, 1992.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w3954>

### Age-only lifecycle allocation can fail

Scott, J., J. Shoven, S. Slavov and J. Watson. **Life Cycle Portfolio Choice with Human Capital and Social Security.** NBER Working Paper 34966, 2026.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w34966>

The paper models labour-income correlation and social insurance and finds large allocation differences across otherwise similar lifecycle positions.

### Observation and adjustment have costs

Abel, A., J. Eberly and S. Panageas. **Optimal Inattention to the Stock Market with Information Costs and Transactions Costs.** NBER Working Paper 15010, 2009.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w15010>

Used to support low-frequency, state-dependent review rather than permanent market attention.

## Behavioural and execution counterexamples

### Frequent individual trading can reduce net performance

Barber, B. and T. Odean. **Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth: The Common Stock Investment Performance of Individual Investors.** *Journal of Finance*, 2000.

<https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html>

### Overconfidence can drive excessive trading

Daniel, K. and D. Hirshleifer. **Overconfident Investors, Predictable Returns, and Excessive Trading.** *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 2015.

<https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.29.4.61>

### Attention and sleep can change trading performance

Han, H., D. Hirshleifer, J. Sheng and Z. Sun. **Trading in Twilight: Sleep, Mental Alertness, and Stock Market Trading.** NBER Working Paper 33477, 2025.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w33477>

### AI-generated portfolios are not automatically diversified or superior

Carlin, B., R. Israelsen and C. Wazzan. **AI Managed Household Portfolios: A Preliminary Report.** NBER Working Paper 35153, 2026.

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w35153>

The preliminary study reports concentrated, attention-driven recommendations and no statistically significant abnormal return under its methodology. It is a counterexample to treating model-generated activity as financial authority.

## Current exclusions

The completed first cycle does not require:

- CHFS or CFPS respondent-level data;
- a household panel model;
- a personal financial score;
- a proprietary market dataset;
- a detailed consumption classifier;
- an asset-allocation optimizer;
- a behavioural tracking platform;
- a causal estimate for every stage transition.

A later study may admit new evidence only when a concrete unresolved decision cannot be answered through the existing mechanism map, public calibration, and bounded counterexamples.
