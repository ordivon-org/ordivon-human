# HUMAN-ECON-001 Sources

Accessed 2026-08-02. Official frameworks and primary research are used as evidence inputs, not imported as universal prescriptions.

## Official frameworks and Chinese data

### S1 — National Bureau of Statistics of China

National Bureau of Statistics. **2025年居民收入和消费支出情况.** Published 2026-01-19. Reports national, urban, and rural disposable income, median income, income sources, consumption expenditure, category shares, and household-survey sampling method.  
<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202601/t20260119_1962321.html>

### S2 — OECD income, consumption, and wealth framework

OECD. **OECD Framework for Statistics on the Distribution of Household Income, Consumption and Wealth.** 2013. Treats income, consumption, and wealth as separate but interrelated dimensions for joint micro-level analysis.  
<https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264194830-en>

### S3 — OECD joint distribution

Balestra, C. and F. Oehler. **Measuring the joint distribution of household income, consumption and wealth at the micro level.** OECD Papers on Well-being and Inequalities No. 11, 2023. Documents partial correlation and multidimensional material deprivation.  
<https://doi.org/10.1787/f9d85db6-en>

### S4 — CFPB financial well-being

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. **Why financial well-being?** Defines the construct through day-to-day control, shock absorption, goal progress, and freedom of choice.  
<https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/about/>

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. **Measuring financial well-being: A guide to using the CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale.**  
<https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/financial-well-being-scale/>

### S5 — CHFS public data

China Household Finance Survey and Research Center. **2021年中国家庭金融调查（CHFS）数据公开发布.** 2026. Documents 2021 public data, 22,027 households, 29 provinces, and balance-sheet coverage.  
<https://chfs.swufe.edu.cn/info/1041/4051.htm>

### S6 — CHFS cross-wave update and 2025 trial data

China Household Finance Survey and Research Center. **2011–2021年CHFS公开数据更新公告.** 2026. Standardizes household, individual, and master datasets across six public waves.  
<https://chfs.swufe.edu.cn/info/1041/4171.htm>

China Household Finance Survey and Research Center. **中国家庭金融调查2025年数据（试用版）西财绿色通道开放.** 2026. Documents 23,277 households across 29 provinces and current restricted availability.  
<https://chfs.swufe.edu.cn/info/1041/4141.htm>

### S7 — CFPS

Institute of Social Science Survey, Peking University. **China Family Panel Studies introduction.** Describes the nationally representative biennial longitudinal survey of communities, families, and individuals covering economic and non-economic well-being.  
<https://www.isss.pku.edu.cn/cfps/en/about/introduction/index.htm>

Institute of Social Science Survey, Peking University. **CFPS Data User Agreement.** Prohibits redistribution and identifiable-data exploration.  
<https://www.isss.pku.edu.cn/cfps/en/data/DataUserAgreement/index.htm>

## Household finance and economic mechanisms

### S8 — Liquidity versus wealth

Ganong, P. and P. Noel. **Liquidity vs. Wealth in Household Debt Obligations: Evidence from Housing Policy in the Great Recession.** NBER Working Paper 24964, 2018.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w24964>

### S9 — Wealthy hand-to-mouth households

Kaplan, G., G. L. Violante and J. Weidner. **The Wealthy Hand-to-Mouth.** NBER Working Paper 20073, 2014.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w20073>

### S10 — Consumption commitments

<https://doi.org/10.3386/w12467>

Beshears, J. et al. **Does Pension Automatic Enrollment Increase Debt? Evidence from a Large-Scale Natural Experiment.** NBER Working Paper 32100, revised 2024.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w32100>

### S12 — Auto-IRA and whole balance sheets

Bloomfield, A., N. Dao, K. M. Lee and S. Slavov. **How Do State Auto-IRA Policies Affect Household Balance Sheets?** NBER Working Paper 35373, 2026.

Campbell, J. Y. and T. Ramadorai. **Household Finance in Retrospect and Prospect.** NBER Working Paper 34621, 2026. Reviews computational lifecycle models, administrative data, microeconometric causal inference, and behavioral approaches.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w34621>

### S14 — Practical lifecycle portfolio choice

Choi, J. J., C. Liu and P. Liu. **Practical Finance: An Approximate Solution to Lifecycle Portfolio Choice.** NBER Working Paper 34166, 2025.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w34166>

### S15 — Human capital and social insurance

Scott, J., J. B. Shoven, S. Slavov and J. G. Watson. **Life Cycle Portfolio Choice with Human Capital and Social Security.** NBER Working Paper 34966, 2026. Shows that labour-income correlation and social insurance alter simple age-based risk logic.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w34966>

### S16 — Active saving versus capital gains

Fagereng, A., M. B. Holm, B. Moll and G. Natvik. **Saving Behavior Across the Wealth Distribution: The Importance of Capital Gains.** NBER Working Paper 26588, 2019.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w26588>

### S17 — Heterogeneous returns to wealth

Fagereng, A., L. Guiso, D. Malacrino and L. Pistaferri. **Heterogeneity and Persistence in Returns to Wealth.** NBER Working Paper 22822, 2016.  
<https://doi.org/10.3386/w22822>

## Measurement cautions

- Aggregate means do not identify joint household states.
- Cross-sectional associations do not identify the effect of changing one person's decision.
- Retirement-account balances are not net wealth unless debt and liquidity are also observed.
- Housing and business valuations contain substantial measurement and liquidation uncertainty.
- Survey expenditure uses different recall periods and can contain recall error.
- Household assets do not imply equal member authority or exit access.
- Structural model recommendations depend on institutional and preference assumptions.
- Results from the US and UK require explicit transport analysis before use in China.
