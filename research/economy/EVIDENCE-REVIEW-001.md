# Evidence Review 001 — What Already Survives Contact with Data

## Scope

This review tests the first mechanism claims behind sustainable personal economic autonomy. It prioritizes official statistical frameworks, primary household-finance research, natural experiments, administrative data, and nationally representative surveys.

It does not attempt to settle optimal portfolio allocation or estimate a personalized strategy.

## Evidence grades

- **A — causal or quasi-causal:** randomized experiment, natural experiment, discontinuity, or strong administrative design;
- **B — longitudinal/administrative descriptive:** repeated household or population records with strong measurement but no complete causal identification;
- **C — cross-sectional descriptive:** useful for distribution and association;
- **D — structural/theoretical:** mechanism is explicit but results depend on assumptions and calibration;
- **E — normative framework:** defines what should be measured, not what action causes an outcome.

## Finding 1 — Income, consumption, and wealth cannot substitute for one another

**Evidence:** E/C.

The OECD framework treats household income, consumption, and wealth as separate but interrelated dimensions of material well-being. Later joint-distribution work finds that they are only partially correlated and that deprivation identified by assets or consumption extends beyond income poverty. [S2, S3]

Implications:

- current income does not reveal accumulated buffer;
- consumption can exceed income through debt or asset drawdown;
- high wealth can be illiquid or inaccessible;
- the same income supports different quality and autonomy under different commitments;
- analyses must observe the joint distribution, not three independent averages.

**Retained claim:** every economic-autonomy study needs a whole balance sheet plus consumption and income flows.

## Finding 2 — Financial well-being includes security and freedom of choice

**Evidence:** E.

The CFPB's consumer-derived framework defines financial well-being through control of ordinary finances, shock absorption, progress toward goals, and freedom to make choices that support enjoyment of life. It explicitly notes that income, net worth, and credit score alone are insufficient. [S4]

This is not imported as a universal scale. The important retained result is conceptual:

```text
security and choice are outcomes
not merely side effects of net worth
```

**Retained claim:** economic autonomy must be measured directly through feasible actions and shock response.

## Finding 3 — Liquidity has independent causal importance

**Evidence:** A/B.

Ganong and Noel compare mortgage modifications that mainly changed long-term debt principal with modifications that mainly reduced near-term payments. Principal reduction increased housing wealth without improving liquidity and had little effect on default or consumption; payment relief improved liquidity and produced large effects. [S8]

Kaplan, Violante, and Weidner document “wealthy hand-to-mouth” households across several countries: households with substantial illiquid assets but little liquid wealth. Their portfolio structure predicts strong consumption response to transitory income changes. [S9]

These studies do not imply that wealth is unimportant. They establish that timing and mobilizability are separate from accounting value.

**Retained claim:** liquid resilience is a first-class state variable and cannot be replaced by net worth.

## Finding 4 — Gross saving can overstate household improvement

**Evidence:** A.

A UK natural experiment on automatic pension enrollment found that additional pension saving was partly offset by increases in unsecured debt, and was also accompanied by changes in mortgage borrowing. [S11]

A 2026 study of Oregon's Auto-IRA policy similarly reports increases in retirement ownership and assets alongside changes in checking/savings balances and higher credit-card debt. [S12]

The exact magnitude and institutional setting do not transfer automatically to China. The robust methodological lesson does:

```text
visible account accumulation
is not identical to
whole-balance-sheet wealth creation
```

**Retained claim:** evaluate changes in assets, liabilities, and liquidity jointly.

## Finding 5 — Fixed commitments amplify moderate shocks

**Evidence:** A/D.

Chetty and Szeidl model consumption commitments as expenditures with adjustment costs and infrequent revision. Their framework predicts greater local sensitivity to moderate shocks and is supported by unemployment event-study patterns. [S10]

Commitments may include housing, debt, care responsibilities, business costs, or other obligations, but empirical studies must define them rather than label all recurring expenditure as harmful.

**Retained claim:** commitment burden and adjustability can change risk capacity even when net wealth and average expenditure are unchanged.

## Finding 6 — Human capital changes financial risk capacity, but age is not enough

**Evidence:** D.

Lifecycle portfolio models traditionally treat future labour income as a major non-tradable asset. Recent work shows that optimal risk exposure depends on the volatility and market correlation of labour income, the value of social insurance, and the flexibility to adjust work—not just age or remaining working years. [S13, S14, S15]

The strongest version of “young people should hold more risky assets” therefore fails as a universal rule. A young person whose income, employer equity, city, and professional network all depend on the same cyclical sector may already hold concentrated economic exposure.

**Retained claim:** income process, labour flexibility, social insurance, and correlation structure belong in risk analysis.

## Finding 7 — Active saving and asset revaluation must be separated

**Evidence:** B.

Norwegian administrative data show that the relationship between saving and wealth changes materially depending on whether capital gains are included. Net saving rates can appear relatively flat while gross saving including gains rises strongly with wealth because wealthier households hold appreciating assets. [S16]

Other Norwegian administrative evidence finds persistent heterogeneity in individual returns to wealth, including within asset classes. [S17]

This means that observed wealth growth can reflect:

- active saving;
- leverage;
- asset allocation;
- persistent return differences;
- market regime;
- transfers;
- measurement and valuation.

**Retained claim:** do not infer superior discipline or a reproducible strategy from wealth growth without decomposition.

## Finding 8 — Aggregate Chinese averages conceal the target problem

**Evidence:** C.

Official 2025 statistics report national per-capita disposable income of 43,377 yuan, median income of 36,231 yuan, and consumption expenditure of 29,476 yuan. Wage income contributed 56.6% of disposable income and property income 8.0%. Food plus residence accounted for about 51.0% of reported consumption. Urban disposable income was about 2.31 times rural income, while urban consumption was about 1.77 times rural consumption. [S1]

The difference between average income and average consumption is not a household saving rate. The two averages do not identify whether the same households have the surplus, whether assets are liquid, or how debt and transfers change the balance sheet.

**Retained claim:** national aggregates are calibration context; CHFS and CFPS are required for household trajectories and heterogeneity.

## Finding 9 — China has complementary but differently suited microdata

**Evidence:** official data documentation.

CHFS records employment, financial and non-financial assets, housing, business assets, liabilities, income, expenditure, social insurance, and insurance. The 2021 public data were released and standardized across six public waves in 2026; the 2025 trial data are currently limited to eligible users at Southwestern University of Finance and Economics. [S5, S6]

CFPS is a biennial longitudinal survey covering individuals, families, and communities, with economic activity, education, family dynamics, migration, health, and non-economic well-being. Its public data require registration and use under a data agreement; the data may not be redistributed through this repository. [S7]

**Retained data division:** use CHFS for the detailed household balance sheet and CFPS for longitudinal links among economic state, health, education, relationships, and life quality.

## Hypothesis ledger after Review 001

| Hypothesis | Current judgment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity is unusually valuable at low liquid wealth | supported | causal debt-modification evidence and wealthy hand-to-mouth evidence |
| Reducing inflexible commitments can raise autonomy more than equal cuts to flexible spending | provisionally supported | commitment mechanism and unemployment response; direct autonomy measure still absent |
| Early-stage wealth growth is usually dominated by earning-capability growth rather than investment alpha | unresolved | plausible balance-sheet arithmetic, but requires Chinese lifecycle decomposition |
| Avoiding ruin matters more than maximizing average return | retained as a constraint, not yet an estimated ranking | liquidity and correlated-failure evidence support the mechanism |
| Some expenditure raises both present life quality and future capability | unresolved | requires category-level longitudinal or causal evidence |
| High income can coexist with low autonomy | conceptually supported, empirical China estimate pending | commitment, liquidity, control, and concentration can offset income |
| Financial freedom is continuous rather than binary | retained as a measurement model | CFPB security/choice structure and option-set logic |
| Automatic saving always increases net wealth | rejected | debt offsets observed in natural experiments |
| Net worth is sufficient for shock capacity | rejected | liquidity evidence |
| Age alone determines appropriate financial risk | rejected | human-capital and income-correlation models |

## New research implications

The first empirical analysis should not begin with stock returns. It should estimate:

1. the joint distribution of liquid wealth, net worth, debt, income, and commitments;
2. shock response conditional on liquidity and commitment burden;
3. active saving versus valuation and transfer components of wealth change;
4. the contribution of earnings growth relative to asset returns at different initial wealth levels;
5. quality-of-life and capability changes associated with different expenditure categories;
6. control over assets and support, where data permit.

## Limits

- Most causal household-finance evidence in this review comes from the United States or United Kingdom.
- Structural lifecycle results depend on preferences, income processes, taxes, pensions, and market assumptions.
- Chinese aggregate statistics do not expose household joint distributions.
- CHFS and CFPS measurements contain recall error, nonresponse, attrition, valuation uncertainty, and household-authority ambiguity.
- Economic autonomy is not fully observed in existing surveys; some option-set measures will need proxy validation or a bounded new module.
