# HOC6 Evidence Ledger

## 1. Mathieu et al. 2000 — shared mental models and team process

John E. Mathieu, Tonia S. Heffner, Gerald F. Goodwin, Eduardo Salas, Janis A. Cannon-Bowers.
`The influence of shared mental models on team process and performance.`
Journal of Applied Psychology. 2000;85(2):273–283.
DOI: 10.1037/0021-9010.85.2.273
PMID: 10783543.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10783543/

Design:

```text
56 undergraduate dyads
computer-based flight-combat simulation
separate task- and team-based mental-model similarity measures
```

Retained pressure:

```text
greater convergence in task/team mental models was associated with later team processes/performance;
team process mediated the reported relation to team effectiveness.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
critical representational alignment can aid coordination;
shared-model value operates through coordination process rather than identical minds by definition.
```

Do not infer that every team needs globally identical mental models.

## 2. Mills & Healey 2018 — running repairs in dialogue

Gregory J. Mills, Patrick G. T. Healey.
`Running Repairs: Coordinating Meaning in Dialogue.`
Topics in Cognitive Science. 2018.
DOI: 10.1111/tops.12336
PMID: 29687611.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29687611/

Interactive maze/reference-task experiments manipulated positive and negative feedback signals in live dialogue.

Retained pressure:

```text
feedback about understanding/misunderstanding changes how interlocutors coordinate meaning;
repair dynamics are part of constructing shared reference rather than a mere post-hoc error count.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
Repair can update future communication/common-ground policy.
NoRepair != MutualUnderstanding.
```

## 3. Zeng et al. 2026 — LVLM versus Human common-ground behavior

Peter Zeng, Weiling Li, Amie J. Paige, Zhengxiang Wang, Panagiotis Kaliosis, Dimitris Samaras, Gregory J. Zelinsky, Susan E. Brennan, Owen Rambow.
`LVLMs and Humans Ground Differently in Referential Communication.`
Proceedings of ACL 2026, pages 9061–9087.
DOI: 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.410

ACL Anthology:
https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.410/

Design:

```text
89 director–matcher pairs
356 dialogues across four repeated rounds
human-human, human-AI, AI-human and AI-AI conditions
novel picture-reference task
```

Retained pressure:

```text
LVLMs in the study did not interactively generate/resolve references in the same smooth common-ground pattern as Human partners;
fluent language generation does not establish Human-like common-ground adaptation.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
LLMFluency != HumanLikeCommonGroundCapability
Human–AI coordination should explicitly represent grounding/repair needs.
```

## 4. Interpersonal influence / trust contagion in Human–Human–AI teams, 2025

`Interpersonal influence matters: Trust contagion and repair in human-human-AI team.`
Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans. 2025;5:100194.
DOI: 10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100194

Publisher record:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882125000787

Mixed experiment manipulated AI reliability and a Human confederate's trusting/neutral/distrusting stance in a cooperative resource-allocation game.

Retained pressure:

```text
one Human teammate's expressed trust stance influenced another participant's reliance/self-reported trust toward the AI;
positive and negative contagion were asymmetric;
team social influence can repair or distort trust calibration depending on actual AI reliability.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
Trust can propagate through team structure.
TeamSocialConsensus != CalibratedTrust.
```

## 5. Trust repair strategy differences across Human–AI and Human–Human teams, 2026

`Restoring Trust in Human-Centered AI: Differential Impacts of Trust Repair Strategies in Human–AI Versus Human–Human Teams.`
International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction. Published online 2026-06-02.
DOI: 10.1080/10447318.2026.2676762

Publisher record:
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2026.2676762

Design:

```text
3 × 2 between-subjects experiment
134 valid participants
repair strategy: commitment / apology / no repair
team type: Human–AI / Human–Human
urban risk-management task
```

Retained pressure:

```text
trust-repair effectiveness is contingent on repair strategy and team relation/context;
Human–AI trust repair should not be assumed identical to Human–Human repair.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
TrustRepair is an episode/protocol, not a magic apology flag.
HumanAITrust != HumanHumanTrust by default.
```

## 6. Purpose versus performance trust violations in Human–AI teams, 2025

`Purpose Outweighs Performance: Trust Drops More When AI Teammates Fail to Cooperate, but Explanations Can Repair It.`
International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction. 2025.
DOI: 10.1080/10447318.2025.2539449

Publisher record:
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2025.2539449

Game-theoretic Human–AI teaming experiment manipulated performance versus cooperative-purpose violations and trust-repair responses.

Retained pressure:

```text
different violation interpretations can produce different trust losses;
repair effects can depend on what dimension participants believe was violated.
```

HOC6 use:

```text
TrustViolationCase needs typed target dimension/attribution.
One generic error count is insufficient for trust repair policy.
```

# 8. Evidence ceiling

The cited studies support specific relational mechanisms under particular tasks and populations.
They do not establish one universal teamwork score, one ideal trust level, or one collaboration protocol.

```text
HOC6
= Ordivon operational synthesis
  + scoped relational evidence
  + explicit task/role/dependence/history/authority boundaries.
```

`CoordinationReadiness`, `TrustCalibrationProfile`, `RelationshipOperationalView`, `CoordinationLiabilitySet` and `NextBestCoordinationAction` must be validated per consumer.
