> After the strongest possible reduction into HF21 + HOC4/HOC5/HOC6 + ExperientialProjectionProtocol, does a characteristic, reusable and substantive affective-regulation action family remain?

---

# 1. Strongest rival — direct HF21 projection + HOC5

The strongest no-new-HOC model is:

```text
HF21
→ identify affective episode/appraisal/experience/expression/action-readiness

HOC5
→ declare goal
→ choose means/action
→ evaluate goal progress
```

At first glance this can encode any regulation episode.

But encoding is not the admission test.

The question is whether repeated consumers can act safely without reconstructing the same missing middle layer:

```text
what aspect of affect is being regulated?
what is the regulation goal?
when in the affective process is intervention occurring?
what strategy family is being used?
what endpoint counts as success?
what immediate/delayed tradeoff occurred?
did strategy fit person/situation/intensity/controllability/social context?
should strategy continue, switch, stop, or no regulation occur?
is another person/Agent co-regulating?
```

HOC5 does not contain these affect-specific semantics.

Therefore the rival can represent the event only by repeatedly rebuilding a de facto emotion-regulation layer inside each consumer.

That is precisely the kind of repeated practical reconstruction HOC exists to canonicalize.

---

# 2. Why this differs from rejected post-HOC9 protocols

## Lived Experience

After target declaration, generic remainder was:

```text
ask / measure / compare / preserve first-person evidence / route
```

No generic substantive intervention remained.

## Identity

After self-model projection, actual action routed to:

```text
goal / role / habit / health / learning / institution
```

No generic identity action survived.

## Supported Decision Participation

After role/support/authority/competence decomposition, generic remainder was:

```text
compose / record / check / route
```

## Affective Regulation

After all target/context decomposition, substantive actions still recur:

```text
change situation
redirect attention
reappraise
accept/tolerate
problem-solve

modulate expression
delay action
alter bodily regulatory input
switch/stop strategy
allow emotion without active reduction
```

Therefore:

```text
AffectiveRegulationResidual
!= evidence/routing protocol only
```

# 3. StrategySituationFit survives the direct attack

A strategy cannot be classified as generically effective from its label alone.

The 2026 coordinated ambulatory analysis of 12 datasets reported:

```text
N = 1,511 Humans
161,513 measurement occasions
```

and found substantial between-person variability in the effectiveness of every studied strategy.

Most importantly, roughly 75–80% of observed effectiveness variance was explained by the intensity of the affective state being regulated.

Thus:

```text
ObservedStrategyOutcome
= strategy
× Human
× episode intensity/history
× situation/context
× timing
× goal
× measurement horizon
```

not:

```text
ObservedStrategyOutcome
= intrinsic StrategyQuality
```

This gives `StrategySituationFitCase` a direct operational role.

---

# 4. Flexibility survives only after a destructive rewrite

Round 7 proposed a provisional `RegulationFlexibilityProfile`.

Round 8 attacks it.

A 2026 daily diary + ecological momentary assessment study directly shows:

```text
strategy switching / variability
!= flexibility
```

When context remained stable, greater strategy switching predicted worse momentary outcomes in the studied samples.

Therefore remove the naive model:

```text
more strategies
more switching
more variability
→ more flexible
```

and retain only:

```text
RegulationFlexibilityEvidenceView
```

which requires evidence that:

```text
context/goal changed
→ regulatory allocation adapted
→ the adaptation was directionally appropriate for the declared endpoint
```

Thus:

```text
FlexibilityEvidence
= ContextSensitivity + AdaptationEvidence + OutcomeEvidence
```

not a person trait scalar.

The destructive result strengthens the family while narrowing one object.

---

# 5. Strategy choice depends on social context and regulation goal

Daily-life research shows suppression, reappraisal and distraction are selected differently depending on:

```text
who is present
relationship closeness
hedonic versus instrumental goal
interpersonal conflict-management goals
```

This matters because HOC5's ordinary goal representation does not supply the affective-strategy mapping.

A consumer needs both:

```text
HOC5 GoalStateView
```

and:

```text
HOC10 StrategySituationFitCase
```

The dependency does not imply identity.

---

# 6. Regulation goals can intentionally preserve or increase unpleasant emotion

Classic instrumental-emotion experiments provide an important falsifier against `regulation = feel better`.

Participants anticipating confrontational tasks could prefer activities expected to increase anger, and anger could improve performance for the confrontational task under the studied conditions.

Therefore:

```text
EmotionRegulationGoal
!= maximize positive valence
```

Possible regulation goals include:

```text
decrease
increase
maintain
tolerate/allow
express accurately
conceal strategically
delay action
recover
preserve task/relationship outcome
```

This is why HOC10 requires `RegulationGoalProfile` rather than `CalmnessTarget`.

---

# 7. Context can reverse the value of a commonly preferred strategy

Person-by-situation research on cognitive reappraisal provides a strong counterexample:

```text
same broad strategy
+ different stressor controllability
→ different association with adjustment
```

A strategy that is useful when the situation itself cannot be changed can be poorly matched when direct situation modification is feasible.

Therefore:

```text
Reappraisal != AlwaysGood
SituationModification != AlwaysAvailable
Acceptance != AlwaysGood
Suppression != AlwaysBad
```

This is an operational selection problem, not merely an evidence protocol.

---

# 8. Endpoint separation survives and is action-critical

HF21 already freezes:

```text
RegulationOfExpression
!= RegulationOfExperience
!= RegulationOfPhysiology
!= RegulationOfAction
```

Round 8 asks whether this distinction is only foundational or operationally consequential.

It is consequential.

Example:

```text
Human suppresses visible expression
→ meeting social/display goal

but
felt distress remains high
physiological activation may differ
later relational/cognitive cost may remain
```

If the consumer records only:

```text
GoalCompleted = true
```

it loses the affective outcome structure.

Therefore retain:

```text
AffectiveRegulationOutcomeProfile
```

as a canonical HOC10 object.

---

# 9. Co-regulation survives HOC6 reduction

HOC6 can model:

```text
relationship
role
dependence
trust
communication
support
```

but interpersonal emotion regulation asks an additional process question:

```text
Is A intentionally or functionally changing B's affective experience/expression/action-readiness through social interaction, toward which target, and with what outcome?
```

The 2025 interpersonal emotion-regulation synthesis treats this as a distinctive process spanning social, developmental, clinical, organizational and other research traditions.

Thus:

```text
RelationshipSupport
!= AffectiveCoRegulation
```

while:

```text
CoRegulationCase
requires HOC6 relational topology.
```

This is composition with a stable affective owner, not reduction of the owner.

---

# 10. Co-regulation is not automatically good support

A supporter can:

```text
validate
help reappraise
redirect attention
encourage approach
encourage avoidance
amplify anger
suppress expression
create reassurance dependence
```

Therefore:

```text
CoRegulationOccurred
!= RegulationSuccess
```

and:

```text
SupportiveIntent
!= BeneficialAffectiveOutcome
```

HOC10 must record immediate and delayed outcomes plus relational/dependence effects.

---

# 11. Clinical evidence does not own the family

Recent transdiagnostic meta-analysis includes hundreds of studies and tens of thousands of clinical/control participants, showing large overall differences in self-reported emotion-regulation difficulties and strategy-use patterns across mental disorders.

An umbrella review of emotion-regulation interventions also finds direct intervention targeting across multiple populations.

This establishes high clinical relevance.

But clinical evidence is not used to define the family because emotion regulation also appears in:

```text
daily life
work
education
relationships
caregiving
development
sport/social interaction
Human-Agent interaction
```

Therefore:

```text
AffectiveRegulation != MentalDisorderConstruct
```

and:

```text
DysregulationEvidence != Diagnosis
```

---

# 12. Cultural transport narrows but does not destroy the family

A 2025 meta-analysis across 249 articles, 150,474 participants and 37 countries/regions found cultural moderators in associations between reappraisal/suppression and mental-health outcomes.

Therefore reject:

```text
one universal strategy ranking
```

but retain:

```text
StrategySituationFitCase(..., cultural_context)
```

The family transports as a typed target-strategy-outcome grammar, not as invariant effect sizes.

---

# 13. Developmental transport also requires coordinates

Adolescent emotion-regulation-flexibility research uses inconsistent definitions and measures.

Daily-life adolescent work shows strategy switching varies with:

```text
age
controllability
emotion intensity
co-regulatory support
```

Therefore:

```text
AdultRegulationProfile
!= Child/AdolescentRegulationProfile by default
```

but the same operational grammar remains usable when developmental stage and support regime are explicit.

---

# 14. No-active-regulation is a first-class outcome

Another destructive case:

```text
negative affect present
```

does not imply:

```text
regulation required
```

Appropriate responses can include:

```text
allow emotion
observe without intervention
seek information
act on the external problem
wait
express emotion
```

Therefore HOC10 must include:

```text
NO_ACTIVE_REGULATION
ALLOW_EXPRESSION
```

as legitimate actions.

This protects the family from becoming an affect-suppression engine.

---

# 15. Emotion support is a boundary, not synonym

Critical HCI work on technology-mediated emotion intervention argues that care/support can be valuable without treating emotion itself as a state to optimize or control.

Therefore:

```text
EmotionSupport
!= EmotionRegulationByDefinition
```

If the action is:

```text
listen
validate
provide presence
preserve space for emotion
```

without an authorized affective-change target, the substantive owner may be HOC6/support rather than HOC10 regulation.

HOC10 should be invoked only when the use question actually concerns affective regulation, adaptation or regulation outcome.

---

# 16. Human-Agent perturbation confirms both value and risk

Recent studies already implement:

```text
robot-facilitated reappraisal
AI-assisted visual reappraisal
AI/robot emotional-support mechanisms
```

showing that Agent systems can become active regulation supports rather than passive observers.

This creates a recurrent operational need to distinguish:

```text
HumanSelfRegulation
AgentSupportedHumanRegulation
AgentDrivenIntervention
Joint/RelationalCoRegulation
```

and:

```text
SupportGain != InternalHumanChange
```

The HOC10 support boundary therefore follows the existing HOC1/HOC3 attribution discipline.

## Architecture E — episode/context-bound HOC10

Retain.

```text

```

Thus A10 can pass with strict architecture constraints.
