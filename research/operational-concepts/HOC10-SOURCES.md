# HOC10 Evidence Ledger

## 1. Wenzel et al. 2026 — daily-life strategy effectiveness is strongly person/situation dependent

Mario Wenzel, Theda Radtke, Thomas Kubiak, Zarah Rowland.
`A critical test of emotion regulation strategy effectiveness in daily life: Individual differences and situational contexts.`
Emotion. Online ahead of print, 2026.
DOI: 10.1037/emo0001687
PMID: 42241085.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241085/

Design:

```text
coordinated analysis of 12 ambulatory datasets
N = 1,511 participants
161,513 measurement occasions
```

Retained pressure:

```text
all studied strategies showed substantial between-person variability in effectiveness;
strategies favorable on average could be ineffective/counterproductive for some Humans;
approximately 75–80% of effectiveness variance was explained by intensity of the affective state being regulated;
persistent negative affect changed strategy-use patterns.
```

HOC10 use:

```text
StrategySituationFitCase is first-class.
AverageStrategyEffect != IndividualStrategyEffect.
Affective intensity/history must be explicit.
```

## 2. 2026 — variability/switching is not flexibility

`Variability is not flexibility: Emotion regulation switching predicts increased momentary distress when context is stable.`
Journal of Abnormal Psychology / Journal of Psychopathology and Clinical Science lineage, 2026.
PMID: 41910550.
DOI: 10.1037/abn0001108.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41910550/

Design:

```text
Study 1: daily diary, N=92, 1,149 diaries
Study 2: EMA, N=158, 12,217 observations
```

Retained pressure:

```text
strategy switching predicted worse momentary outcomes when context remained stable;
context must be measured directly rather than inferred from strategy variability;
variability/switching cannot serve as generic flexibility proxies.
```

HOC10 use:

```text
MoreSwitching != MoreFlexibility.
RegulationFlexibilityEvidenceView requires context + adaptation + outcome evidence.
```

# 18. Evidence ceiling

The evidence supports:

```text
context- and person-dependent strategy effects;
meaningful regulation-goal differences;
endpoint separation;
context-sensitive strategy allocation;
interpersonal/co-regulation as a distinct process;
clinical and nonclinical practical relevance;
strong cultural/developmental transport constraints;
real Human-Agent regulation-support cases.
```

It does **not** establish:

```text
one universal adaptive strategy
one universal maladaptive strategy
one global emotion-regulation skill score
one universal flexibility measure
that negative emotion should be reduced
that suppression is always harmful
that reappraisal/acceptance is always beneficial
that frequent strategy switching is flexibility
that successful Agent support proves independent Human learning
that emotion inference grants intervention authority
```
