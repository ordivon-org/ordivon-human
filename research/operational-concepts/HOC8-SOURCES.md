# HOC8 Evidence Ledger

## 1. Chi, Feltovich & Glaser 1981 — expert/novice problem representation

Michelene T. H. Chi, Paul J. Feltovich, Robert Glaser.
`Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices.`
Cognitive Science. 1981;5(2):121–152.
DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

Publisher:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

Retained pressure:

```text
experts grouped/represented physics problems more by deeper physical principles;
novices relied more on surface/literal features in the studied tasks.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
Expertise can change ProblemRepresentation.
Expertise is not just more facts or faster execution.
```

---

## 2. Novick 1988 — expertise and structural analogical transfer

Laura R. Novick.
`Analogical transfer, problem similarity, and expertise.`
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. 1988;14(3):510–520.
DOI: 10.1037/0278-7393.14.3.510
PMID: 2969945.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2969945/

Retained pressure:

```text
experts showed greater spontaneous positive transfer when problems shared structural rather than surface features in the tested arithmetic domain;
novices were more vulnerable to misleading surface similarity.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
expertise can alter structural transfer/search;
Expertise != UniversalTransfer.
```

---

## 3. Hinds, Patterson & Pfeffer 2001 — expert knowledge can be harder to teach

Pamela J. Hinds, Michael Patterson, Jeffrey Pfeffer.
`Bothered by abstraction: The effect of expertise on knowledge transfer and subsequent novice performance.`
Journal of Applied Psychology. 2001;86(6):1232–1243.
DOI: 10.1037/0021-9010.86.6.1232
PMID: 11768064.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11768064/

Retained pressure:

```text
experts used more abstract/advanced instructions than beginners;
novices instructed by beginners performed better on the immediate target task;
expert instruction supported better performance on a related transfer task in the reported study.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
Expertise != TeachingCapability.
ImmediateInstructionSuccess != TransferValue.
```

## 4. Rozenblit & Keil 2002 — illusion of explanatory depth

Leonid Rozenblit, Frank Keil.
`The misunderstood limits of folk science: An illusion of explanatory depth.`
Cognitive Science. 2002;26(5):521–562.
DOI: 10.1207/S15516709COG2605_1
PMID: 21442007.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21442007/

Retained pressure:

```text
people systematically overestimated how deeply they could explain many mechanisms/systems;
attempting detailed explanation revealed gaps and reduced self-assessed understanding.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
FeelingOfUnderstanding != DemonstratedUnderstanding.
ExplanationProbe can expose structural gaps.
```

## 5. Fisher & Keil 2016 — expertise does not guarantee calibrated explanatory insight

Matthew Fisher, Frank C. Keil.
`The Curse of Expertise: When More Knowledge Leads to Miscalibrated Explanatory Insight.`
Cognitive Science. 2016;40(5):1251–1269.
DOI: 10.1111/cogs.12280
PMID: 26369299.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26369299/

Retained pressure:

```text
domain knowledge/expertise increased confidence about ability to explain;
expertise could still coexist with limits in actual explanatory detail/self-assessment.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
Expertise != PerfectCalibration.
ExpertiseProfile and HOC2 CalibrationProfile remain separate.
```

## 6. Cruz & Lombrozo 2025 — jargon, satisfaction and comprehension

Francisco Cruz, Tania Lombrozo.
`How laypeople evaluate scientific explanations containing jargon.`
Nature Human Behaviour. 2025;9:2038–2053.
DOI: 10.1038/s41562-025-02227-0.

Publisher:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02227-0

Nine experiments, N=6,698.

Retained pressure:

```text
jargon could increase satisfaction with weak/incomplete explanations while reducing comprehensibility;
generating one's own explanations reduced some inflated evaluations and improved self-calibration in the reported studies.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
ExplanatorySatisfaction != Comprehension.
Jargon != Completeness.
Speaker/reader impressions cannot substitute for understanding evidence.
```

## 8. Ward 2021 — internet knowledge misattribution

Adrian F. Ward.
`People mistake the internet's knowledge for their own.`
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. 2021.
DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2105061118
PMID: 34686595.
PMCID: PMC8612631.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34686595/

Eight experiments, N=1,917.

Retained pressure:

```text
participants using Google showed inflated self-assessment of their own unaided knowledge/thinking/memory and predicted greater future unaided knowledge.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
ExternalAnswerAccess can contaminate self-attribution.
```

## 9. Fisher & Oppenheimer 2021 — division of cognitive labor

Matthew Fisher, Daniel M. Oppenheimer.
`Who knows what? Knowledge misattribution in the division of cognitive labor.`
Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. 2021;27(2):292–306.
DOI: 10.1037/xap0000310
PMID: 33829826.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33829826/

Six primary and two supplemental studies, N=3,262 as reported by the authors.

Retained pressure:

```text
outsourcing/accessing knowledgeable partners can make it harder to distinguish partner knowledge from one's own competence;
clearer attribution can reduce misattribution in studied settings.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
TeamKnowledge != IndividualKnowledge.
EpistemicAttributionCase is operationally justified.
```

## 10. Fisher, Smiley & Grillo 2021/2022 — information without internal learning

Matthew Fisher, Adam H. Smiley, Tito L. H. Grillo.
`Information without knowledge: the effects of Internet search on learning.`
Memory. 2022;30(4):375–387 (online 2021).
DOI: 10.1080/09658211.2021.1882501
PMID: 33557708.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33557708/

Retained pressure:

```text
participants who searched online while studying later showed poorer internal learning assessment than those directly receiving the same information while confidence remained equal/higher in the reported experiments.
```

HOC8 use:

```text
AccessSuccess != InternalizedKnowledge.
Current support regime must remain attached to epistemic evidence.
```

# 12. Evidence ceiling

These studies establish scoped dissociations and mechanisms under particular tasks/populations.
They do not justify one universal expert score, one universal understanding test, or assuming all internet/AI use harms internal knowledge.

```text
HOC8
= Ordivon operational synthesis
  + scoped expertise/understanding/access evidence
  + explicit support/domain/freshness/authority boundaries.
```
