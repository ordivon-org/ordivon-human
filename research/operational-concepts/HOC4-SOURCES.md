# HOC4 Evidence Ledger

## 1. Van Dongen et al. 2003 — cumulative chronic sleep restriction

Hans P. A. Van Dongen, Greg Maislin, Janet M. Mullington, David F. Dinges.
`The cumulative cost of additional wakefulness: dose-response effects on neurobehavioral functions and sleep physiology from chronic sleep restriction and total sleep deprivation.`
Sleep. 2003;26(2):117–126.
DOI: 10.1093/sleep/26.2.117
PMID: 12683469.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/

Design pressure:

```text
48 healthy adults;
14 days of 4 h, 6 h or 8 h time in bed, plus a total-sleep-deprivation comparison;
repeated neurobehavioral, subjective and sleep-physiology measurements.
```

Retained evidence:

```text
4 h and 6 h chronic sleep restriction produced cumulative dose-dependent cognitive-performance deficits;
subjective sleepiness changed acutely but showed much smaller further increases than objective cumulative deficits and did not strongly differentiate the 4 h and 6 h groups later in restriction.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
CumulativeExposure matters.
SubjectiveSleepiness != FullObjectiveImpairmentReadout.
Stable subjective state does not prove stable performance capacity.
```

Do not infer the same dose-response for every age, disorder or real-world schedule.

## 2. Zhou et al. 2012 — subjective/objective mismatch and circadian phase

Xuan Zhou, Sally A. Ferguson, Raymond W. Matthews, Charli Sargent, David Darwent, David J. Kennaway, Gregory D. Roach.
`Mismatch between subjective alertness and objective performance under sleep restriction is greatest during the biological night.`
Journal of Sleep Research. 2012;21(1):40–49.
DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-2869.2011.00924.x
PMID: 21564364.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21564364/

Controlled forced-desynchrony design measured subjective alertness and PVT performance under standard versus sleep-restricted conditions across circadian phases.

Retained pressure:

```text
subjective alertness did not fully reflect objective neurobehavioral impairment under sleep restriction;
the mismatch was especially prominent during biological night in the studied protocol.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
SleepPressure/CircadianPhase/SubjectiveAlertness/ObjectivePerformance must remain separate.
StateMismatch is operationally meaningful.
```

## 3. Marcora, Staiano & Manning 2009 — mental fatigue and physical endurance

Samuele M. Marcora, Walter Staiano, Victoria Manning.
`Mental fatigue impairs physical performance in humans.`
Journal of Applied Physiology. 2009;106(3):857–864.
DOI: 10.1152/japplphysiol.91324.2008
PMID: 19131473.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19131473/

Randomized crossover study, N=16, comparing 90 min of demanding cognitive activity with a neutral-documentary control before cycling to exhaustion.

Retained pressure:

```text
cognitive-task-induced mental fatigue reduced subsequent exercise time to exhaustion;
perceived effort was higher, while measured cardiorespiratory/musculoenergetic responses were largely unchanged under the study conditions.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
fatigue effects can cross operational domains;
performance limitation need not identify one depleted physiological fuel.
```

Do not infer one common fatigue mechanism across all tasks.

## 4. Finkbeiner, Russell & Helton 2016 — breaks, vigilance and wellbeing dissociation

Kristin M. Finkbeiner, Paul N. Russell, William S. Helton.
`Rest improves performance, nature improves happiness: Assessment of break periods on the abbreviated vigilance task.`
Consciousness and Cognition. 2016;42:277–285.
DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2016.04.005
PMID: 27089530.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27089530/

Participants were randomly assigned to several break-content conditions or continuous vigilance.

Retained pressure:

```text
the continuous-vigilance group showed the worst performance;
breaks improved vigilance relative to continuous performance;
some break content changed subjective distress/happiness without corresponding performance change.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
Rest can be an effective intervention for one target.
PerformanceRecovery != SubjectiveRecovery.
Break content is part of the intervention.
```

## 5. Banks et al. 2010 — one-night recovery can remain incomplete

Siobhan Banks, Hans P. A. Van Dongen, Greg Maislin, David F. Dinges.
`Neurobehavioral dynamics following chronic sleep restriction: dose-response effects of one night for recovery.`
Sleep. 2010;33(8):1013–1026.
DOI: 10.1093/sleep/33.8.1013
PMID: 20815182.
PMCID: PMC2910531.

PubMed/PMC:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20815182/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2910531/

Randomized recovery-dose study after five nights restricted to 4 h time in bed; N=159 total.

Retained pressure:

```text
larger acute recovery-sleep doses improved several neurobehavioral measures;
some measured deficits remained after the maximum 10 h time-in-bed recovery condition.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
OneRecoveryEpisode != FullRestoration.
Recovery must be endpoint-specific and longitudinal.
```

## 6. Boukhris et al. 2026 — recovery intervention can create immediate sleep-inertia cost

Omar Boukhris, Haresh Suppiah, Manel Kerkeni, Khaled Trabelsi, Hamdi Chtourou, Matthew Driller.
`The effects of sleep inertia following EEG-monitored short and long naps on perceptual, cognitive, and physical performance in recreational athletes.`
Chronobiology International. 2026;43(2):241–256.
DOI: 10.1080/07420528.2025.2597953
PMID: 41379073.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41379073/

Randomized counterbalanced crossover study, N=17, comparing no nap, 25-min nap opportunity and 90-min nap opportunity with repeated tests during the first two post-nap hours.

Retained pressure:

```text
several performance markers were impaired in the earliest post-nap interval relative to later post-nap testing;
sleep-inertia effects were more pronounced after the 90-min condition for several measures under this protocol.
```

HOC4 use:

```text
RecoveryIntervention != ImmediateReadinessIncrease.
RecoveryActionSpec needs transient-cost / re-entry-latency fields.
```

Do not generalize the nap-duration ranking beyond the studied population/protocol.

# 8. Evidence ceiling

The primary studies establish specific state/performance/recovery relationships under controlled conditions.
They do not justify a universal fatigue detector, universal workload score or universal work/rest schedule.

```text
HOC4
= Ordivon operational synthesis
  + typed state/load/recovery evidence
  + explicit target/consequence/transport boundaries.
```

`SustainableWorkEnvelope`, `RecoveryNeedInference`, `StateRiskAlert` and `NextBestRegulationAction` must be validated in each consumer rather than treated as pre-proven latent constructs.
