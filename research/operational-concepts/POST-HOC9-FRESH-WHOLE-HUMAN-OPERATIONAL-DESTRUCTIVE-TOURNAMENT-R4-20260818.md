# 5. Second deletion witness — Role assignment can remain fixed while role identity changes

HOC6 can establish:

```text
role assignment
role authority
role responsibility
handoff expectations
relationship structure
```

But it cannot infer:

```text
H identifies with this role
H experiences the role as self-defining
H treats the role as temporary/external
H experiences conflict among role identities
```

Minimal counterexample:

```text
same role assignment
same competence
same incentives
same relationship structure

A: "this is part of who I am"
B: "this is only a role I currently occupy"
```

The two cases can differ in:

```text
persistence
response to role loss
behavior under weak monitoring
learning investment
willingness to change
reaction to identity threat
```

Therefore:

```text
RoleAssignment != RoleIdentification
```

and HOC6 alone does not delete the residual.

The empirical literature is mixed and intervention-specific, so this does not prove identity causal primacy.

It does establish a practical question not equivalent to ordinary goal existence:

```text
GoalCommitment != IdentityCongruence
```

# 7. Fourth deletion witness — Self-model truth and self-model causal role differ

HOC8 can treat a self-description as a belief/representation and compare it with evidence.

But the operational question is not only:

```text
Is the self-belief accurate?
```

It can also be:

```text
Which self-representation is currently organizing action?
What happens if evidence conflicts with it?
Is the self-model stale after a capability/role/life transition?
Does changing behavior threaten a valued identity?
```

A self-model can be causally important even when descriptively inaccurate.

Examples:

```text
"I am bad at mathematics"
"I am not an athletic person"
"I am the family caregiver"
"I am still the same professional I was before injury"
```

These are not exhausted by knowledge accuracy.

Thus:

```text
SelfModelAccuracy
!= SelfModelBehavioralRole
```

and:

```text
SelfKnowledgeClaim
!= IdentityCommitment/Salience
```

This weakens full reduction into HOC8.

---

# 8. Fifth deletion witness — Temporal self-continuity is not temporal discounting

Self-continuity research distinguishes:

```text
past→present self-continuity
present→future self-continuity
global temporal self-continuity
```

and reports associations with attitudes, motivation, intentions, behavior and health.

More recent modeling work shows that self-continuity and temporal discounting can share temporal structure without collapsing into the same mechanism; age-related changes in the two can dissociate.

Therefore:

```text
FutureSelfContinuity
!= TemporalDiscounting
```

and:

```text
FuturePlanningDifficulty
!= FutureSelfDisconnection by definition
```

This blocks complete reduction of the future-self question into HOC5 intertemporal planning/choice alone.

---

# 9. Sixth deletion witness — Identity disruption can be a target of intervention

Identity-targeted interventions have been studied in:

```text
physical activity
smoking cessation
severe mental illness / psychosis
acquired brain injury
intellectual disability
```

The intervention literature is heterogeneous and not uniformly strong.

But it repeatedly includes operations such as:

```text
possible-self work
identity challenge
self-reflection
narrative restructuring
adjustment to illness/injury
building empowered social identity
reconstructing an integrated self-narrative
```

This indicates that identity disruption/reconstruction is not merely a synonym for capability, symptom burden, habit or motivation.

The correct inference is modest:

```text
Identity-related state can be intervention-relevant
```

not:

```text
Identity intervention always works
```

Survivor pressure: YES.

Survivor pressure: YES WITH CULTURAL SCOPE.

## 24.6 Group / political / religious identity

Person-side identification can affect action while group-side norms/institutions remain externally owned.

Survivor pressure: YES, but ownership boundary is critical.

## 24.7 Future planning

Present/future self connection can differ even when planning skill and ordinary discounting are held conceptually separate.

Survivor pressure: YES.

## 24.8 Human–Agent personalization

Persistent Agent memory can stabilize, distort or manipulate an identity model.

The need to distinguish self-endorsed versus system-inferred identity is recurrent.

Survivor pressure: STRONG YES.
