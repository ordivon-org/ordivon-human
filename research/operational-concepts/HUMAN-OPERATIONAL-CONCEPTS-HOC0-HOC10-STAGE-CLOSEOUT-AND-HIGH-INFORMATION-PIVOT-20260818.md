# Human Operational Concepts HOC0–HOC10 — Stage Closeout and High-Information Pivot

## 0. Why stop extending this chain now

The correct stopping criterion is information gain, not numerical exhaustion.

Across post-HOC9 R1–R8:

```text
many candidate labels
→ HOC0–HOC9 composition / neighboring owner

Lived Experience
→ ExperientialProjectionProtocol

Identity / Self-Model
→ SelfModelProjectionProtocol

Supported Decision Participation
→ SupportedDecisionParticipationProtocol

Affective Regulation
→ survived destructive reduction
→ HOC10 admitted
```

The pattern is now clear:

```text
new label frequency is high
new generic owner yield is low
```

Most further HOC-number search inside the already scanned candidate vocabulary is therefore expected to have low marginal information value.

The HOC chain should stop here until a fresh search finds another stable substantive owner.

---

# 1. What changed the model most

Only a small number of post-HOC9 results changed the architecture materially.

## 1.1 Protocol-vs-family distinction

Three attractive candidate families failed because their reusable remainder consisted mainly of:

```text
evidence discipline
projection
comparison
composition
routing
update / expiry
privacy / authority guards
```

rather than a characteristic generic action owner.

This produced three unnumbered protocols:

```text
ExperientialProjectionProtocol
SelfModelProjectionProtocol
SupportedDecisionParticipationProtocol
```

## 1.2 Closure falsification found HOC10

Round 7 exposed a different failure mode:

```text
Foundation is rich
→ researchers assume practical space is already owned
→ HOC search routes candidate to Foundation
→ but no operational target / strategy / outcome / next-action grammar exists
```

HF21 Affect/Emotion had exactly this problem.

Round 8 showed the surviving middle layer was substantive, so HOC10 was admitted.

This is the highest-value methodological lesson of this stage.

---

# 2. New search principle

Do not ask:

```text
What should HOC11 be?
```

Ask:

```text
Which Human Foundations are conceptually rich
but still operationally thin?
```

For each Foundation-rich space:

```text
Foundation distinctions
× HOC0–HOC10 consumption
× retained protocols
× neighboring-project ownership
× real consumer decisions
```

Then run:

```text
1. practical-question test
2. owner-completeness test
3. deletion test
4. protocol-vs-family test
5. cross-regime transport test
6. Human-Agent perturbation
7. privacy/governance cost test
```

No numbering is assigned before a survivor passes.

HF20 is unusually rich in practical distinctions:

```text

MultisensoryIntegration

```

Yet canonical HOCs currently contain almost no direct operational reconstruction of:

```text
what should be sampled next?
when should sensing stop?
when should the Human move / look / touch / sniff / inspect to reduce uncertainty?
which modality/source should be trusted under current reliability?
when should cues be integrated versus kept separate?
how should perceptual calibration be checked after tool/body/environment change?
how should sensory substitution / alternate modality support be configured?
how should subjective percept, action success and source truth be kept separate operationally?
```

Existing HOCs consume consequences:

```text
HOC1 capability/readiness
HOC2 confidence/verification
HOC3 perceptual learning
HOC5 decisions/actions
HOC8 knowledge
```

but they do not obviously own the sensing policy itself.

This is structurally analogous to the pre-HOC10 HF21 gap and therefore has the highest expected information value.

Current research also continues to pressure this space:

```text
sampling actions can constrain what becomes perceptually available;
exploration strategy can change later active sensory-substitution performance;
multisensory uncertainty can alter evidence accumulation;
active sampling trades information gain against acquisition cost.
```

Disposition:

```text
HF20 operational consumption gap
= HIGHEST-PRIORITY SEARCH CONTINENT
= NOT HOC11
```

# 8. Why HF15–HF18 are not the next Human operational target

HF15–HF18 have very sparse direct HOC references:

```text
HF15 Moral Standing / Moral Patienthood / Dignity
HF16 Population / Future Generations / Non-Identity
HF17 Normative Pluralism / Social Choice / Governance
HF18 Incentives / Mechanism Design / Markets
```

But this is mostly intentional ownership separation.

Their central practical questions concern:

```text
who/what deserves moral consideration
how welfare/rights aggregate
collective decision legitimacy
institutional choice
mechanism design
strategic incentives
markets/public goods
```

These are not missing Human-side person models.

They primarily belong to:

```text
Normative / Governance / Institution / Finance / World
```

Therefore sparse HOC consumption here is not evidence of a Human HOC hole.
