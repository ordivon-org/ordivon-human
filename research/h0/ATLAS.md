# Human System Atlas

## 1. Purpose

The Atlas is a compact map of the systems needed to explain human functioning and development. It is not an exhaustive taxonomy, clinical classification, personality inventory, or claim that each system is independent.

The primary object is:

```text
human organism
embedded in relationships, institutions, environments, technologies, and history
```

## 2. Atlas architecture

### Internal organization

- **Body** — biological integrity, regulation, energy, sensation, movement, health, and recovery.
- **Cognition** — acquisition, representation, transformation, retention, and control of information.
- **Emotion and motivation** — valuation, salience, approach, avoidance, effort, arousal, and regulation.
- **Identity and values** — relatively persistent self-organization, preferences, commitments, meaning, and narrative.
- **Capability** — reliable ability to achieve classes of outcomes across declared conditions.

### Action organization

- **Behaviour** — observable and inferred action patterns through which internal organization meets the world.

### Situated organization

- **Economy and resources** — material, temporal, legal, and infrastructural option sets.
- **Relationships** — dyadic and networked attachment, support, exchange, recognition, influence, and conflict.
- **Institutions and environment** — rules, norms, organizations, places, ecological conditions, information systems, and historical opportunity structures.
- **Technology and external capability** — artefacts and systems that store, transform, coordinate, or execute human intention.

### Cross-cutting axis

- **Time and history** — development, ageing, cumulative exposure, learning, events, shocks, recovery, and path dependence.

## 3. Minimum distinctions

The Atlas distinguishes six kinds of statement:

| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| structure | visual system, working-memory mechanisms, family relationship |
| state | sleep debt, current anxiety, available cash |
| capacity or capability | maximum aerobic capacity, reading skill, ability to coordinate a project |
| action | sleeping, studying, spending, asking for help |
| context | exam conditions, labour market, available AI tool |
| outcome | health, task completion, autonomy, income, well-being |

Observation is separate from all six. A test score, wearable reading, self-report, or transaction record is evidence about a construct, not the construct itself.

## 4. P0 map

```text
Body
 ├─ supplies energy, sensation, movement, and physiological regulation
 ├─ constrains and supports Cognition
 └─ is changed by Emotion/Motivation, Behaviour, and Context

Cognition
 ├─ represents situations and possible actions
 ├─ supports learning, prediction, control, and metacognition
 └─ contributes to Capability only through practice, context, and execution

Emotion and Motivation
 ├─ assign salience and value
 ├─ mobilize or suppress effort and action
 ├─ alter attention, memory, and decision thresholds
 └─ are regulated by Body, Cognition, relationships, and environment

Capability
 ├─ emerges from Body + Cognition + Emotion/Motivation
 ├─ also depends on knowledge, practice, resources, tools, and context
 ├─ is expressed through Behaviour
 └─ must be distinguished from one performance and one assisted result
```

## 5. Non-reduction rules

- Body does not explain every psychological or social phenomenon merely because all human activity is embodied.
- Cognition is not a synonym for intelligence, test score, education, or rationality.
- Emotion is not noise added to otherwise pure reasoning.
- Motivation is not a moral judgment about effort.
- Capability is not an internal possession independent of tools and conditions.
- Behaviour does not reveal intention without uncertainty.
- Economic success does not measure total human value or freedom.
- Relationships and institutions are not residual environmental variables.
- Technology is not external to real capability merely because it is outside the body.

## 6. Scale transitions

The same phenomenon may require several scales:

```text
molecules and cells
→ organs and neural circuits
→ bodily and mental functions
→ actions and tasks
→ relationships and organizations
→ institutions and population distributions
→ historical change
```

H0 will not create one universal causal graph spanning all scales. Each study selects the smallest scale combination that explains its case.

## 7. Current retention judgment

The four P0 systems are retained because each prevents a distinct failure:

- remove Body: fatigue, illness, sleep, sensory, movement, and ageing constraints become unexplained;
- remove Cognition: representation, learning, memory, reasoning, and control collapse into behaviour labels;
- remove Emotion and Motivation: salience, value, effort, stress, approach, and avoidance become unexplained;
- remove Capability: one observed action or tool-assisted performance is mistaken for what a person can reliably do.

Their internal subdivisions remain provisional. P0 records mature candidates but does not freeze an ontology.
