# H0 Sources

Accessed 2026-08-02. H0 uses official frameworks and primary methodological or experimental research. Inclusion indicates relevance, not complete endorsement.

## Official frameworks and research resources

1. World Health Organization. **International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF).** Functioning and disability are represented in context, including environmental factors.  
   <https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health>

2. National Institute of Mental Health. **Research Domain Criteria Matrix.** Dimensional constructs are studied across multiple units of analysis, with explicit developmental and environmental aspects.  
   <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/rdoc-matrix>

3. OECD. **Measuring well-being and progress.** Current well-being, inequalities, and resources for future well-being are treated as multidimensional and distinct from GDP.  
   <https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/measuring-well-being-and-progress.html>

4. National Institutes of Health. **All of Us Research Program Protocol.** Longitudinal multimodal observations include surveys, health records, physical measurements, biospecimens, and digital health data under tiered access.  
   <https://www.nih.gov/allofus/about/protocol>

5. UK Biobank. **Types of data.** Eight broad categories include imaging, biomarkers, genetics, healthcare records, questionnaires, physical measures, demographic and lifestyle data, and environmental data.  
   <https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/about-our-data/types-of-data/>

6. Halfon, N., & Forrest, C. B. (2017). **The Emerging Theoretical Framework of Life Course Health Development.** Presents health development as unfolding, complex, timing-sensitive, plastic, and environmentally conditioned across the life course.  
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31314301/>

## Longitudinal and individual inference

6. Hamaker, E. L., Kuiper, R. M., & Grasman, R. P. P. (2015). **A critique of the cross-lagged panel model.** *Psychological Methods, 20*(1), 102–116. Demonstrates that stable between-person differences can produce erroneous conclusions about within-person relationships.  
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25822208/>

7. Neubauer, A. B., Koval, P., Zyphur, M. J., & Hamaker, E. L. (2025). **Experiments in daily life: When causal within-person effects do (not) translate into between-person differences.** Uses analytical, simulated, and micro-randomized evidence to study cross-level translation.  
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40029297/>

8. Vohra, S. et al. (2015). **CONSORT extension for reporting N-of-1 trials (CENT) 2015 Statement.** Defines reporting extensions for randomized multiple-crossover trials in individual participants.  
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26272792/>

9. Tate, R. L. et al. (2016). **The Single-Case Reporting Guideline In BEhavioural Interventions (SCRIBE) 2016 Statement.** Defines reporting guidance for single-case behavioural intervention research.  
   <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27499802/>

## Human–AI capability development

10. Lira, B., Rogers, T., Goldstein, D. G., Ungar, L., & Duckworth, A. L. (2025). **Learning not cheating: AI assistance can enhance rather than hinder skill development.** Two preregistered writing experiments found improved later unassisted writing after AI-supported practice, while showing that examples may explain much of the benefit.  
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02880>

11. Liu, G., Christian, B., Dumbalska, T., Bakker, M. A., & Dubey, R. (2026). **AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance.** Randomized experiments across 1,222 participants found immediate assistance gains alongside lower later unassisted performance and persistence in the studied tasks.  
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721>

The two human–AI studies are not treated as mutually cancelling universal claims. Their different tasks and assistance structures are evidence that “AI use” is not a sufficiently precise intervention variable.
