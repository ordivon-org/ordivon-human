# Methods M0 Framework Composition

> **Naming note:** this document was produced before the original method-first H0 work was reclassified as [Methods M0](README.md). “H0” below is historical wording and does not refer to the current [Human System Atlas](../../research/h0/README.md).

## Judgment

No reviewed framework is a complete general model of human development, and none should be converted into an Ordivon-owned replacement ontology.

Their combination does, however, expose a narrow unfilled question that justifies Ordivon Human:

> How should cross-domain population evidence, longitudinal individual evidence, and human–AI system evidence be combined for one explicit decision without confusing association, mechanism, capability, agency, and value?

## Framework comparison

| Framework or method | Native strength | Retained contribution | Deliberately not imported |
|---|---|---|---|
| WHO ICF | body functions and structures, activities, participation, environmental factors | functioning occurs in context; body condition is not identical to participation or agency | ICF codes as a universal Ordivon schema; health as the sole outcome |
| NIMH RDoC | dimensional cognitive, affective, social, regulatory, and sensorimotor constructs across multiple measurement units | multiple methods and levels; development and environment alter interpretation | psychiatric research scope; its complete construct matrix as a general human ontology |
| OECD well-being | plural current outcomes, inequalities, deprivation, and resources for future well-being | no single economic or subjective measure defines a good life; distributions matter | a national indicator dashboard; aggregation into one personal score |
| NIH All of Us | longitudinal multimodal health data and tiered access | provenance, repeated observation, multimodality, data minimization, and access proportional to sensitivity | a comprehensive personal data lake; collection before a named question |
| UK Biobank | large linked biological, clinical, behavioural, physical, and environmental data | cross-domain linkage and the value of long-term follow-up | scale as proof of causal validity; transplanting its categories as universal fields |
| Life-course research | timing, development, path dependence, cumulative exposure, reciprocal person–environment change | history and developmental timing are first-class axes | a predetermined stage model applied to every question |
| Within/between-person methods | separation of stable group differences from change within people | population differences cannot be substituted for personal change | one preferred longitudinal estimator independent of design and question |
| N-of-1 and single-case methods | repeated individual intervention, randomization or phase logic, carryover and reporting discipline | individual evidence can update population priors when the intervention is repeatable and effects are observable | permanent self-tracking; clinical crossover assumptions for every behavioural question |
| Human–AI experiments | causal comparison of assistance structures, immediate performance, later performance, and persistence | “AI assistance” must be decomposed by interaction design and measured over time | a universal claim that AI either improves or degrades human ability |

## Five-case composition test

### Case A — Sleep, stress, and cognitive performance

Required contributions:

- ICF-like functioning and bodily state;
- RDoC-like cognition and regulation;
- repeated within-person observations;
- explicit measurement error and temporal lag.

No economic, institutional, or genomic layer is automatically required. The model must allow their admission only when the study demonstrates a role.

### Case B — Skill formation, income, time autonomy, and tool access

Required contributions:

- capability and transfer, not only test performance;
- material and temporal resources;
- institutional opportunity;
- cumulative feedback across time;
- plural outcomes because income, autonomy, learning, and health can diverge.

Health-only frameworks are incomplete; well-being indicators alone do not explain the capability-forming mechanism.

### Case C — Health or financial shock and recovery

Required contributions:

- a discrete event and pre-event baseline;
- bodily or material state;
- relationships, institutions, and available buffers;
- recovery trajectory rather than one post-event measurement;
- several outcomes and horizons.

A static trait model misattributes shock response to personality and omits the recovery environment.

### Case D — AI-assisted work and learning

Required contributions:

- assistance mode as a technological context and intervention;
- assisted output;
- later unassisted capability and novel-task transfer;
- persistence, verification, direction, refusal, replacement, and exit;
- delayed effects and dependence.

No reviewed population or health framework supplies this complete distinction. This is the clearest independent Ordivon Human contribution.

### Case E — Relationships, participation, and well-being

Required contributions:

- relationship structure and interaction behaviour;
- participation and subjective experience;
- institutional or cultural context;
- bidirectional and delayed effects;
- within-person and between-person separation.

A social-network count alone cannot identify relationship quality, causal direction, participation, or well-being.

## Composition result

The case matrix supports six conclusions:

1. The project requires a dynamic state-transition structure, not a flat demographic table.
2. Time, history, action, measurement, and outcome are not peer “human levels.”
3. No study should instantiate every available domain.
4. Mature domain frameworks remain authoritative within their intended scope.
5. Ordivon Human's independent value is cross-domain composition, individual updating, and human–AI co-development.
6. This value does not justify a universal schema, data platform, or proprietary vocabulary.
